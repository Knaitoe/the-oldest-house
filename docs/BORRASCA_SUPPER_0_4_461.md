# The church supper (0.4.461)

Part 4 of docs/BORRASCA_LIFE_PROPOSAL.md. The owner, 10 November: "Put on the church supper from the noticeboard. Let the player
help set out plates, take a slice of pie, and hear the adults talk about work and their kids. Prescott's truck brings the folding
tables, and somebody thanks the driver for fixing their porch steps. Seeing that same company name at the depot later would give
you a better sense of how much the town relies on him." And of the whole list: "Most of these should be optional."

## What is there

On the first visit the church's lawn is being set for Sunday's supper. The store's noticeboard already had it: "Church supper
Sunday, six o'clock. Bring a dish."

- **The company's pickup** stands on the verge and the church's grass, in the company's paint, the same truck as the one in the
  square. PRESCOTT is across its tailgate. A hand on the tailgate reads "PRESCOTT ORE SERVICES, on the tailgate. D-422.
  555-0190." The truck in the square is D-417. The depot's dispatch sheet has both: "D-417: feed and linen to the depot, Monday
  and Thursday. D-422: folding tables to the church, Sunday. Bring them back."
- **Two folding tables** ride on edge in its bed.
- **The dessert table** stands laid, with three pies, the plates and a tray of forks. **One bare table** stands beside it. A
  folded checked cloth lies on the grass where each of the other two tables is to go.
- **Five people**, each on their own skin from the common painter:

| Who | Where | To a reader who comes up |
|---|---|---|
| Vern, Prescott's driver, in the company's work shirt | At the truck's tail | "Two more in the bed, if you want to be useful. They're lighter than they look." Then "Mind your fingers. They pinch when they open." When both are up: "That's the lot. I'll be back for them Monday." |
| Mrs. Hollis | Between the tables | "Set them down where the cloths are, hon." When both are up: "Cloths and plates are on the end of the dessert table. Four places to a table." When all are laid: "Well, look at that. You tell your mother who helped." |
| Mrs. Pruitt | Behind the dessert table | "Pecan was my Dana's. Take a slice, hon. I can't watch it sit there till six." After: "Six o'clock for the rest of it, mind." |
| Mrs. Corley | On the grass by the driver | "You're one of the trail children." Then "You keep together up there. All of you." |
| Brother Neal, the pastor | By the church's door | "All welcome, six o'clock. Tell your folks the tables are up." And "The Lord provides, and Mr. Prescott lends the tables." |

The truck is off the gravel on purpose. The friend's walk up the street passes within a hand of the street's east edge there, and
a truck on the gravel would have stood in her way.

## What a reader can do

None of it is asked for, and nothing else in the town waits on it.

- **Carry the tables.** A hand in the truck's bed, or on its sides or tailgate, takes a folding table, one at a time. Asked for a
  second with the first still carried, Vern says "One at a time." A hand on a cloth with a table carried stands the table there:
  "The legs lock with a clack. The table stands where the cloth lay."
- **Lay them.** A hand on the plates takes a stack. A hand on a bare table with the plates carried lays it: a checked cloth and
  four places. There are three bare tables to lay: the one that stood already and the two the reader carried. With the third laid
  the plates are used up, and Mrs. Hollis says so.
- **Take a slice of pie.** A hand on the pies is given a slice of the pecan, once, and the pecan shows the cut. Mrs. Pruitt says
  whose pie it was as the hand reaches, unless she has said it already. The slice is its own food, not the diner's: "A slice of
  pecan pie on a paper plate."

A table or the plates in a pack do not leave the town. The slice does.

## What is overheard

While the reader is within twelve blocks of the lawn, the five talk among themselves: the first line after three seconds and one
every six seconds after, each speaker turned to the one spoken to. It is said once through. It stops when the reader walks off
and picks up where it stopped.

| Who, to whom | What |
|---|---|
| Mrs. Corley, to Vern | "Thank you for the porch steps, Vern. I never even asked." |
| Vern, to Mrs. Corley | "Mr. Prescott likes things kept up, ma'am. It was no trouble." |
| Mrs. Hollis, to Mrs. Pruitt | "Is your Leland on nights again?" |
| Mrs. Pruitt, to Mrs. Hollis | "Nights through July. They pay time and a half for nights." |
| Mrs. Pruitt, to Mrs. Hollis | "Is Katelyn sleeping through yet?" |
| Mrs. Hollis, to Mrs. Pruitt | "Like a lamb. The Lord gives." |
| Mrs. Hollis, to Mrs. Corley | "We'll want more chairs than this." |
| Mrs. Corley, to Mrs. Hollis | "We set too many last year." |
| Brother Neal, to Mrs. Corley | "And how are your two, Mrs. Corley?" |
| Mrs. Corley, to Brother Neal | "Teething, the both of them. Nobody sleeps." |
| Vern, to Brother Neal | "I'll be back for the tables Monday. Mr. Prescott says keep them as long as you like." |
| Brother Neal, to Vern | "Tell him he's in our prayers. Six o'clock, everybody. Bring the children." |

Each line stands on what the street's four houses already show (docs/BORRASCA_HOMES_0_4_456.md), and none of it is explained.
The Corleys are the household with the packed suitcase and the atlas; the steps were mended without her asking. The Pruitts took
what the town gave them after Dana, and Mr. Pruitt is on the company's nights. Katelyn is the newest line in the Hollises' Bible.
The Corleys have two cribs.

## Later

- **The supper is there on the first visit only.** The lawn is cleared when the reader goes out of the town, while it is still
  loaded, so that a reader who comes back on a later day does not see it go. A reader who comes back on the same day finds it as
  they left it; a table they were carrying is back in the truck.
- **At the depot**, the tables are stacked in the yard against the refinery's wall, past the drums: "Folding tables, stacked
  away. The top one has a ring on it where a pie dish stood." The depot's dispatch sheet has the truck's number against the
  church.
- **The account** has the supper if the reader had a hand in it or heard about the steps: "On the church's grass they were
  setting out Sunday's supper." Then, as each is true: "I carried two folding tables off the Prescott truck and set places."
  "Mrs. Corley thanked the driver for her porch steps. She said she had never asked." "Mrs. Pruitt let me have a slice of the
  pecan before six."

## The name on the tailgate

The company's name could not be read on either truck. It was lettered in white across the tailgate, and the pickup's licence
plate is white and in the middle of the tailgate, so the name read PR, a white box, COTT. The firm's mark lay over the left lamp.
The name now stands on a band of dark paint between the lamps, with the mark made small enough to stand inside the band. This
changes the truck in the square as well.

## Multiplayer

Every reader's Drisking is their own (BorrascaSites), and the lawn is laid from that reader's own record: which tables they have
stood, which they have laid, whether they have had their slice. What the church's people say, and every sound, goes to that
reader alone. The game test runs two readers and checks that nothing the first does is done on the second's lawn.

## What I could not check

I have not played it by hand. The game test drives the server's own handlers, and the client proof shows the models, but nobody
has walked up to the truck. In particular I have not seen, from a child's eye height, how easily a hand finds the tables over the
tailgate and the bed's sides, how the five people's lines read from the street, or whether the truck crowds the way to the
church's path.

The sounds are the game's own (picking up an item, placing a block), so their subtitles are the game's: "Item plops" and "Block
placed".

## Choices of mine

These are mine and easy to change: the driver's name (Vern), the pastor's (Brother Neal) and Mr. Pruitt's (Leland); that the pie
is pecan and was Dana's; that the talk is said once and not round again; that the supper is on the first visit only.
