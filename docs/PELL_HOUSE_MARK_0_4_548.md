# The Pell house 26: Gail's third mark (0.4.548)

The proposal's **Being framed** gives Gail a file: "It gains a mark for each incident the reader can't answer. At three marks, Gail takes
the older sibling away mid-visit." And "Clearing a mark. Evidence found and shown to her clears one: the transcript page, the 'DO NOT
SWITCH OFF' note, a tape played while she's in the room." Its **Failure: the older sibling is taken away**: "Gail's third mark. She comes
in the night and takes the older sibling. Their part ends in Gail's car. They are driven to the end of the cul-de-sac and set down
outside the House's door, and the house is not dealt to them again. Tilly's night goes ahead without them."

The owner's open question 2 asks whether three marks should really end the older sibling's part, or whether the journal's gaslighting
is enough on its own. This slice builds it as proposed, so it can be played and judged; taking it out again would leave everything
else as it is.

Before this slice, the file had two marks to give: the knife (0.4.534) and June's hand (0.4.535), each going on the file whatever the
reader told Gail. Nothing came of them but a line in the account.

## The third: a broken thing

- **On the last night,** locked up, at eleven: if an older sibling at the house is on Gail's file twice and has not had their ending,
  June gets up and goes along the hall to Nessa's school photographs. Of the readers on the file twice or more, the one with the most
  marks is the one she will say did it. A reader already blamed for the photograph once, whose third mark came off, is not framed again.
- **The first moment nobody awake can see her or the photographs,** glass breaks. The last photograph hangs crooked, its glass starred
  across the girl's face. Within 24 blocks it is heard: "In the hall, glass breaks." inside the house, "Over at the house, glass
  breaks." from outside it.
- **The one she names** goes on Gail's file a third time, for this, once ("MarkPhoto"), and is blamed for it ("BlamedForPhoto"),
  whether they are at the house now or not. A line they never wrote falls due in their journal (`PellEntries`): "I took the last
  photograph off the wall and broke it."
- To the photograph, June says: "Oh, look at you. Look what they've done to you."
- **If somebody keeps her or the photographs in sight for two minutes,** it keeps: she goes back to her room, and nothing more happens
  tonight.
- **She rings Gail** from the kitchen telephone, her side heard by anybody near, a pause after each line while Gail talks, and goes back
  to her room.
- **The hour holds at eleven** from when she gets up until Gail sets off home. Midnight, and June going for Tilly, wait for it.

## Gail in the night

- **Fifteen seconds after the receiver is down,** Gail's car comes up the street from the end where the House's door is. Anybody asleep
  wakes to it ("Lights cross the wall. Out in the street, a car is pulling in."). She gets out and comes up to the foot of the path; to
  whoever comes near: "Hello, love. June rang me."
- **A hand on her** opens the conversation panel.
  - To the reader June named: "June's told me about the photograph, love." / "That's three things now. The knife, her hand, and this." /
    "I can't leave you here tonight. Unless there's something you want to show me."
  - To anybody else: "It's late, love. I've come for <name>." / "Unless there's something you want to show me."
- **What they can say** is only what they have about them, or have seen:
  - "Read this. It's June's writing.": the last page off the shed's wall, carried.
  - "This was taped over the freezer's switch.": the note off the freezer's power point, carried.
  - "Come and see what's in the shed.": a reader who has watched a tape.
  - "I'll get my things.": the reader June named.
  - "Say nothing."
- **The page or the note** goes into her hands. She reads the page, then turns it over and reads it again: "This is June's writing."
  / "Where did you find this, love?" She holds the note up to the light from the house: "What's she got in there that can't go off?"
- **The tapes.** "Show me, then." She comes a step behind the reader, along the way they walk. If they get more than eight blocks ahead
  of her, she stops ("Gail stops. She will not go any further without you.") and goes back to the foot of the path. In the shed she
  faces the screen. With a tape playing there, she watches it, and after five seconds of it: "Turn it off. Turn it off, love." With no
  tape for thirty seconds: "There's nothing here, love. Come on." and back she goes.
- **Each of these takes a mark off the named reader's file,** whoever shows her. The named reader's record keeps what was taken off
  ("MarksCleared"); the one who showed her keeps what they showed ("ShowedGail").
- **Under three,** she says "All right." / "I'm not taking anybody anywhere tonight." / "I'll be back first thing, and I'll talk to June
  then.", and goes: back to her car, from the shed the way she came, and the car away the first moment nobody sees it. Everybody at the
  house keeps that she came and went without anybody ("GailCame", "GailLeft"). The hour goes on to midnight.
- **Gail keeps what she is given.** The page and the note are this house's: carried away from it, or left with nobody, they go back on
  the wall and over the switch, and torn tape is left where each was while they are gone. The note still carried stays off its switch.

## Taken away

- **The named reader goes with her** ("I'll get my things." / "Come on, love. In you get."), or she has waited a minute and a half: the
  horn ("Out in the street, Gail sounds the horn."), heard by everybody at the house, and the named reader, wherever they are at it, goes
  in her car.
- "Gail opens the car door for you. Up at the house, a light is on in June's room." A beat to look back, the black, and the car sets
  them down at the House's door, as at first light.
- **Their ending lands,** with nothing to read and nothing resolved: Ended, TakenAway, `taken_away_in_the_night`. The house is not
  dealt to them again as it was, and the journal's false entries go on after it, as after Tilly answered (`PellEntries.failed`). If
  they lay down in the garage that night, that night's entry falls due, as at first light.
- **Until the night ends otherwise,** their record says Tilly answered; if Tilly is brought out at first light after all, their record
  says so when the night ends (`PellMark.nightEnded`).
- **Everybody else at the house keeps who was taken** ("GailTook"), and the night goes on without them. With nobody left to bring her
  out, Tilly answers; a reader playing Tilly can still hold out until it is light.
- If the named reader is not at the house when she has waited, she goes without anybody, and their marks stay.

## Decided here (any of it can move)

- Open question 2, built as proposed: three marks end the older sibling's part.
- The broken thing is the last school photograph, on the last night at eleven, and only for an older sibling already on Gail's file
  twice. A reader on it fewer times never meets it.
- June waits for nobody to see; kept in sight for two minutes, it keeps for the night.
- Each piece of evidence takes one mark off, whoever shows it.
- Gail watches five seconds of a tape; waits thirty in the shed for one; stops eight blocks behind; sounds the horn after a minute and
  a half.
- Gail keeps what she is given.
