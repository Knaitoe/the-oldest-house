## What happens

### The believed past

- **On the landing.** After the guide (Alex or Ren) has said the summer at the lake never happened, a sixth answer: "I remember. I had
  the end of the dock." The guide says "You do?", and that they'd like to have it too.
- **The detail.** Ten seconds later or more, the next time the reader is near the guide and in sight (at the sink, at dinner or in the
  open house), the guide asks the name of the dog that swam out to them. Three answers:
  - Made up: "Sailor. It shook itself all over you." The guide says the name back and can see it now, and couldn't before.
  - Admitted: "I don't know. I wanted it to be true." The guide wanted it too, was going to make one up, and says they could start
    from not knowing.
  - Taken back: "There wasn't a dog. There wasn't a summer." The guide knows, and would still have liked the dog.
- **Afterwards, if the name was made up.** While the picture hangs, the dog is one of the claims the guide says on the landing. Taken
  down, the dog goes with the rest of the summer; hung back, it comes back with it. At the kept table (E2), if the dog is still
  remembered and the guide is staying, the guide says Sailor would have had the scraps by now.

### The reassurance

- **At the door.** While somebody is at the door, once the reader has asked Mara "Is it him?" and heard the voice, a new answer: "It's
  him. I'm sure." Mara says nobody can be sure of that, and asks the reader to stand with her at the door. They can say they will, or
  take it back ("No. I shouldn't have said that." Mara: "No. You shouldn't have.").
- **At the door with her.** Mara goes to the front door and waits beside it. With the reader next to her she opens it, if it's shut,
  and calls "Theo?"; from by the gate the voice says "Who else would it be?". Then Ren (or Alex, if Ren isn't in the house) asks
  whether the reader believes it's him or wants it to be. Three answers: believes it, wants it to be, doesn't know.
- **If he is let in (E3).** Before she stops knowing the reader, Mara says "You said it was him. You were right."
- **If the voice is let go quiet instead.** In the kitchen, before she decides anything, Mara says it back: the reader told her it was
  him, she stood at that door because they said so (or waited there and they didn't come), and then they let him stop. Three answers:
  they wanted it to be him and shouldn't have said they were sure; it was him, and that's why they couldn't let him in; or sorry. Only
  then does she decide.
- **If the evening goes on while she waits** (the picture back up, or the past let in), she leaves the door.

## Also in this release: the way up and the way through

Two faults in how the household walk from room to room, found by this slice's cases:

- **The stair.** Nobody had a way up. The guide on the landing (0.4.691) and Mara upstairs (0.4.689) walked straight at their
  marks through the floor and walls, and arrived only when no reader could see them or the place they were going. A reader already
  on the landing could keep the guide downstairs for good. They now go to the foot of the stair, up it a tread at a time, and along
  the landing; and down the same way.
- **The archways.** Walking from the kitchen into the hall in a reader's sight, Mara turned back at the archway and went on turning
  back and forth there: each step on from the point beside the archway took her out of reach of it, so she was sent back to it. A
  point once passed now stays passed, at both archways, the front door and the stair.
