# Taking up a light a reader has set down (0.4.588)

The owner, playing on 14 November, with a picture of a torch on a hall's wall that would not break:

> "users should be able to remove player-placed torches/lights from the house"

New builds only. No line, protocol, layout or Witness change.

## What was there

- **The House lets a reader set a light down** in its gray halls and on the great staircase: a torch, a soul torch, a
  lantern or a soul lantern, on a floor, on a wall or under a ceiling.
- **It refuses every break inside itself,** with a few named exceptions (the loose floorboard, the cave's rubble, a
  torch in the cave or the hotel by the reader who set it). A light set down in a hall was not one of them, so it
  could never be taken up again: struck, it came straight back.
- **The House's own lamps are the same blocks** as a reader's, and nothing kept which was which.

## What it is now

- **The House keeps every light a reader sets down** in the gray halls and on the great staircase, with who set it.
- **A light kept that way can be taken up by any reader,** by breaking it the ordinary way. The game gives the torch or
  lantern back as it does anywhere.
- **The House's own lamps are not kept that way** and do not give, as before.
- **A reader's light stays a reader's when the House moves it.** The halls move lights nobody is looking at, and a
  maze that refolds stands them on its new floor: where the light now stands, it can still be taken up.
- **A light the House takes without breaking is forgotten.** The dark halls take torches and the last sconce lets weak
  flames die. Nothing of the House's that later stands in the same spot is mistaken for a reader's.

Left as they were:

- **The light sink.** A light set there is the room's, which puts it out in twelve seconds. It cannot be taken up.
- **The cave and the hotel.** A torch there can be taken up only by the reader who set it.
- **The fork with the double.** It takes the reader's torches itself.

## Checks

- **A new case,** in a gray corridor, with two readers and the game's own placing and mining:
  - a reader sets a torch on the floor, and the House keeps it as theirs;
  - the floor under it does not give;
  - the House moves the torch while nobody looks, and it is still that reader's where it now stands;
  - the other reader takes it up there, and is given the torch;
  - a lamp the House stood there itself does not give;
  - a second torch, taken by the House without breaking, is forgotten, and a lamp of the House's stood in its place
    does not give.
- **Twenty-seven other cases that touch lights** pass with it:
  - the fork with the double (5), the dark halls (4), the growl that moves a light, the last sconce, the still light,
    Tom's torches, the halls' darkness, and a maze refolding with a lantern in it (15 in all);
  - the cave's own cases (12), where a torch is still its setter's alone.
- **A compile,** and the quick checks.

Not checked:

- **Nobody has taken a torch up in a played game** since the change.
- **One hall.** The case uses a gray corridor. The other halls and the great staircase go by the same rule and were
  not each tried.
- **A lantern hung under a ceiling** by a reader is kept like any other light; no case hangs one.

## For the owner

- **A torch set before this build is not known to the House,** so the one in your picture will still not break in
  that world. One set after it will.
- **Any reader may take up any reader's light.** In the cave and the hotel only the one who set it can. Say if the
  halls should be the same.
- **A lamp of the House's that the House has moved** (it sometimes stands one of its hanging lamps on the floor
  further back) is still the House's and does not give, though it looks like a lantern a reader might have left. Say
  if those should give too.
- **The light sink is left out on purpose.** If a reader should be able to snatch a torch back before the room eats
  it, that is one line.
