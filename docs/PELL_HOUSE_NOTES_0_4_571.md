# The Pell house, the owner's playtest notes: things to do through the day (0.4.571)

From the owner's notes on playing the Pell house on 13 November (the earlier batches: `docs/PELL_HOUSE_NOTES_0_4_566.md` to
`docs/PELL_HOUSE_NOTES_0_4_570.md`). New builds only. No protocol, layout or Witness change.

The owner: "there need to be more idle activities for players in the pell house, too. ways to interact with tilly, for
singleplayer characters. things for characters to do or tasks they're assigned with so they're not just stuck waiting out the
day."

And, of Callum at the table: "he doesn't have food".

## June's jobs

The chore chart on the kitchen wall has a third strip on it now, stuck on before the siblings came, with no name on it. Against
it, in June's pencil, what she wants done:

    LAY THE TABLE.   BISCUIT'S WATER.   TILLY'S STORY.

After her tour, June's last word is now: "Dinner's at six. I'll give you a call. Till then there's a job or two on the chart in
the kitchen, love."

A hand on the chart's first half reads its names; on its second, the jobs, and which of them June has ticked. Nothing has to be
done. The visit goes on exactly as it did whether a job is done or not.

**Lay the table.** From when June is back in her kitchen until she calls them to dinner.

- A hand on one of the cupboards over the bench takes down the plates the table still wants (four, to begin with).
- A hand on the table lays one at the nearest bare place. The plates do not have to be in the hand; they only have to be on the
  reader.
- All four laid, June says "Four. Good. Always four, love." and it is ticked.
- Some laid, she lays the rest at six and says "I'll finish that, love." Nothing is ticked.
- None laid, she lays it as she always did.
- Plates not laid go back in the cupboard when dinner is called. Two readers can share the plates; the cupboard gives no more
  than the table wants.

**Biscuit's water.** From then until the house is locked up for the night.

- A hand on the dog's bowl by the kennel tips the leaves out and picks it up. "Under them it is dry. They have been in it a long
  time."
- A hand on a tap fills it: the laundry's trough, or the kitchen sink.
- A hand on the kennel, or on the ground beside it with the bowl in hand, sets it down where it stood. Set down full, it is
  ticked. "Nothing comes for it." Anywhere else the bowl says where it goes: "It goes down by the kennel."
- There is no dog.
- In the night, a while after the house is locked up, the first moment nobody sees the bowl, it is dry. "The bowl is empty.
  Round it the ground is wet, and printed with bare feet." A reader asleep in the garage does not see it; one standing at the
  garage's window and looking does, and it waits.
- A week in, for a reader who filled it before, the leaves have not come back into it.

**Tilly's story** is the third job, on the first visit only: ticked when the chapter has been read to its end. A week in it has
been rubbed out, and the chart says so. See below.

On the last night the strip has been taken down. "The strip with no name on it has been taken down."

## Being with Tilly

In Nessa's room, while the house waits on its hour (the afternoon, and the evening before bedtime), on the first two visits.

**Telling her what things look like.** The first hand a reader puts on Tilly in Nessa's room, each afternoon and each evening,
gets her asking: "Is it nice? Tell me what it looks like." A week in: "Tell me what's different. Something is." The conversation
panel opens on what that reader can tell her:

- Only things the reader has themselves put a hand on, or been told by June: the stars on the ceiling (always), the border, the
  desk and its magnifier, the height marks, the cane in the wardrobe, the photographs in the hall, the kennel, the garage, the
  pool, the chart.
- A week in: the pool with its cover off and her hair (always), the photographs on the fridge, the knife gone from the block,
  the name June called her.
- Up to four at a time, then the rest.
- Until something has been told, a kind word is offered too ("It's nice."). She knows what it is. "You always say that."
- "Leave it there." closes it.

She answers each thing once. Some of what she says:

- The desk: "A magnifier. Then she couldn't see much either."
- The marks: "An N. I'm not an N."
- The cane: "She had a cane? Nobody said she had a cane."
- The kennel: "I haven't heard a dog. Not once."
- The chart: "I know. I felt the edge of it. I didn't lift it."
- A week in, her hair: "It can't be. It's been a week."
- The name: "She says sorry after. She always says sorry."

What a reader has told her is their own. Two readers each tell her what each has seen. With the panel shut and her question still
open, a hand on her brings it up again.

**Reading to her.** One of Nessa's large-print books is out, lying on top of the shelf, a bus ticket in it: a library book, weeks
overdue. A hand on the shelf, from inside the room, takes it up.

- Tilly: "Is that the one that's out? Read from the ticket. That's where she got to." She goes and sits on the edge of the bed.
- The panel offers the book a passage at a time. Each is said aloud by the reader, so another reader in the room hears it.
- Tilly has a word after some of them.
- The first visit reads five passages, to the end of the chapter, where the fog comes down. "Don't stop there." "Tomorrow, then.
  Promise."
- A week in: "June reads it to me now. She starts from the ticket every night. We never get past the fog." The reader can read
  her the other five, to the end. "Don't tell June we got to the end. She likes to start from the ticket."
- "Stop there." puts it down: "Keep our place." So does the hour (dinner, bedtime), the reader leaving the room, or two minutes
  with nothing read.
- One reader has the book at a time. How far a reader has read is their own.

The book is original, written for this: *The Keeper's Girl*. Maren minds a lamp on a rock. The keeper's one rule is that the sea
calls a name at night, and it is not hers, and she is not to answer to it: "It can't tell one girl from the next. It only knows
who answers." In the fog the voice learns to sound like the keeper. Maren answers it with her own name, three times. In the
morning the keeper calls her by it.

**With a reader as Tilly** there is nobody to ask and nobody to answer. The other reader can still take the book up and read it
aloud, for Tilly's reader to hear.

## Callum's plate

Callum's plate has always been passed over; June's tape says why. It read as a mistake. Now it is spoken of.

At the first dinner, a little after June has served, Tilly: "Isn't Callum having any? I can't hear his fork." June: "Callum's had
his, love." His plate stays laid, and bare. Readers near enough to hear it keep that they did.

## The account

The account at the end has a line for each of these the reader did themselves: laying the table (and "Four places, June said,
always four", if they heard her say it); what was said of Callum's plate; the bowl, and the bare feet if they found it dry; that
Tilly asked what things looked like and was told; and the book, with whether they got to the end.

## How it is built

- `labyrinth/PellJobs`: the chart, the plates and the table, the bowl, what June ticks, and the keeping of what is carried
  (plates and the bowl go back when their job is over, or when whoever has them leaves).
- `labyrinth/PellCompany`: what a reader has seen, what they have told, Tilly's asking and her answers, and the book.
- `labyrinth/PellBowlItem`: the bowl in the hand.
- `PellIdle` has one new errand, listening: Tilly on the edge of the bed for as long as the book is open.
- Three items (the plates, the bowl empty and full), drawn by `tools/draw_pell_people.py`. The chart's two new faces and the
  bowl's three new stages are drawn by `tools/draw_pell_things.py`. The chart was redrawn to take a third row.
- The lines are in `PellTexts` (its last section).
- Each house (0.4.547) keeps its own chart, plates, bowl and book.

## Four things found while building it

- June's "I'll finish that, love." was first written to be said as she laid the table. She is still saying "Dinner!" then, so it
  would never have been said. She now says it when she next has the breath.
- The bowl was first to be drunk when no reader was within fourteen blocks. The garage, where readers sleep, is nearer the kennel
  than that. It now uses the house's own "nobody sees it" (`PellNight.seen`).
- Tilly's asking was her first line on the first visit and her last a week in, so a reader who had spoken to her on the path
  would not have been asked in her room for another five hands. Each reader's first hand on her in the room is now the asking.
- The bowl was first set down from the item's own use. A block put down that way is a block placed, and the House's rule against
  building takes it away again: the bowl would have been counted as set down, ticked on the chart, and nowhere to be seen. The
  case that uses the game's real right-click found it. Setting it down is now the house's own answer to a hand on the ground by
  the kennel (`PellJobs.ground`), as the kennel and the taps already were; the item only says where it goes.

## Run 1601: a sentence said twice when two readers are there

Run 1601 (5a18946d, 0.4.569 and 0.4.570) passed 25 of its 26 jobs. The long-vignettes job failed two of the two-reader conduct
checks (0.4.565), in scenes those releases do not touch: at the Barretts' (Growing Things) Merry "says the same line again" 20
ticks on, and at the Last Stop Marla does 109 ticks on. The same checks failed once in a local run and passed on the next, so
the fault is real and depends on timing: on which reader the check's walk takes to whom first. Three things were found, and
all three are fixed here.

- **Growing Things.** Merry asked for a hand with the washing, and John with the table, on each reader's own first step into the
  yard and the house. Two readers coming in a moment apart were each asked: the whole sentence twice, a second apart, to
  everybody in hearing. Each now asks once for everybody near enough to hear it (14 blocks), and all of them are kept as asked
  (`GrowingThings.ask`). A reader who comes into hearing while it is still being said has it as the others did. One who comes
  near after it has been said is asked, and no sooner than twenty seconds (`ASK_AGAIN`) after the first asking. Merry also no
  longer speaks over the line about the shoots in the lawn: she asked in the same tick that line was shown, in the same place
  on the screen, so it was never read. She waits three seconds (`READ_FIRST`).
- **The Last Stop.** A reader's word to one of the bar's people gets that person's next line, by the reader's own count; the
  bar's own talk has each of them say their next line, by the bar's count. Both counts begin at the first line. So a reader's
  first word to Marla got "We're past close, hon. You look rough. Sit a minute.", and a few seconds later she said it again
  unasked; a second reader's first word got it a third time. The men outside answered a reader with what they went on to call
  out. Each person now keeps what they have said in the last half minute (`ElkLotNight.FRESH`), and says the next of their lines
  that is not among it (`fresh`). A reader's place in a person's lines moves past a line they were not told twice. One line is
  never passed over: each man's first line in the bar, which is said to each reader with that reader's own greeting.
  `PersonalityTailoringTests` caught the first form of this fix taking the greeting from a second reader.
- **The check itself**, at Costume Night (seen once, locally): Hank called a number, turned to the reader nearest him as he said
  it, and went on looking at them as they walked off; the other reader came nearer, and the check faulted him for not looking at
  that one. The check now asks what the person does: after a line nobody asked for, they are turned to the reader they turned to
  as they said it, for as long as they go on looking at them (`LiteraryActor.addressee`), and only otherwise to whoever is
  nearest. An answer is still turned to the reader who asked.
