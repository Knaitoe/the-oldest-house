## The broken nameplate

On either crew victory the nameplate on *Cordelia*'s wheelhouse breaks along its bolt holes:

- **the ferry crosses:** after Vera's roll on the far bank;
- **the station is cleared:** at the muster.

Vera says: "Her name comes off her now. A piece for every name on the roll, here or not." Two things are left on the wheelhouse front where the plate was: a paler strip with four bolt holes, and a sliver of brass still under one bolt.

Every name on the roll gets a piece, in the order the crew signed. That includes:

- readers who died;
- readers left on the station;
- a reader who opened the master bypass.

The eight letters of CORDELIA are shared among the pieces in order. The first pieces take a letter more when they don't divide evenly: four readers carry CO, RD, EL and IA. A piece's tooltip shows its own letters in their places in the name, with dots where the others go. Laid side by side, the main pieces spell the ferry's name.

A reader who isn't online when the plate breaks finds their piece in their hands when they next log in.

**A tap.** Used in the air, a piece knocks softly. Every matching piece from the same crossing within 48 blocks answers:

- the reader who tapped hears a soft knock from the direction of each companion carrying one;
- each companion hears one from the direction of the tap.

Two matching pieces on one reader carry the knock 96 blocks. A tap works anywhere in the same level, the Overworld included, and can be repeated every two seconds.

**The source's ending** breaks nothing: the crew's pieces come only from a crew victory.

## The spare

From the design: *"A small extra fragment breaks away at one bolt. At the closing roll, the survivors nominate one winning clean reader to take it. Vera can give it to the first eligible reader on her original roll if nobody nominates someone."*

When the plate breaks, Vera says: "There's a spare. It broke away at one bolt. It goes to one of you who came through clean. Say who."

- **Who can say.** Every reader of the crew still standing at the station, turned or not, is asked, with the names of the clean readers still standing to choose from.
- **Who it goes to.** Once everyone standing has said, or after ten seconds, the spare goes to the reader named most. A tie goes to the one earlier on the roll.
- **If nobody names anyone**, it goes to the first clean reader on Vera's roll: "Nobody's saying, so it goes by the roll."
- **If no clean reader is standing**, nobody is asked and the spare stays on its bolt.
- **What it is.** A curl of brass with one bolt hole and no letters.

**Handing it on.** On the far bank, or at the muster after the fight, the spare's bearer can hand it to a reader who turned by using it on them. Their own piece then pairs with it, so two matching pieces carry their knock further. The spare can't go to a reader who never turned, or to anyone outside the crossing. It can't be handed over until it has been given out. A main piece can't be handed on.

There are ten seconds after the spare is given out before morning, so it can be handed over in time for the account.

## Each reader's account

In the morning everyone on the roll is resolved, the dead and the absent included. Each gets their own account, "The account: the last crossing", written from their own record. A reader who isn't online gets theirs when they log back in.

The account follows what that reader did and saw across the three days:

- **Arrival and Day One:** the roll, Ellis's name, their first job, their palm on the plate.
- **Night One:** being taken by the hand at the intake, or seeing it, and how they were freed; the second breath; telling Vera, or showing the second print.
- **Day Two and Night Two:**
  - the winch drum;
  - a bypass opened;
  - turning, with where, or watching someone turn;
  - the pull, and whether it failed;
  - the second slot.
- **Day Three:**
  - the plan they said they would work;
  - asking Vera whether she wants to cross;
  - the source through the glass;
  - the Other Breath's offer.
- **Night Three:**
  - the wheel and the pin;
  - the launch rope;
  - the gate and the bar, and who they worked with;
  - the cutoff, and the blows on the source;
  - being let go when it died;
  - the master bypass, held, sided with or denied, and who denied it;
  - the downstream sluice.
- **Dying:** which night, and watching the rest from the far bank.
- **The ending:**
  - **the ferry:** aboard, and with whom, or left on the station; Vera's roll on the far bank, and the names nobody answered for; the late answer after Ellis's name;
  - **the station cleared:** the still water, the muster bell, and being marked down as one who came out of the gallery;
  - **the source:** the ferry coming in by itself, someone aboard calling the roll, every name answered, Ellis's too, and Vera saying she doesn't know who answered them.
- **What was carried home:**
  - the reader's own piece and its letters;
  - who took the spare, or how it came to them;
  - whether they handed it on or were given it;
  - the Other Breath, if they kept it.

It never genders the reader, names a colour or an age, or uses a pronoun for Ellis. The co-op line reads "[name] was on the roll with me."

## The Witness source

**The last crossing**, a survival story: "I worked three days and three nights at a flood station with a crew, for a keeper who called the roll every morning and one name more at the end of it. On the last night the river came up to the ramp, and we tried to get across."

Its outcomes are the ferry, the gallery and the source. Every reader on the roll resolves it, whichever ending they saw. The Other Breath is kept whatever the ending.

## Dealing

The Last Crossing is dealt once to each reader, from the House's ordinary dealing, when all of these hold:

- the reader has walked one of the yard's ordinary crossings;
- they have not finished it;
- they have a crew around them: four to six readers in the House standing together, within 24 blocks of each other.

A reader on their own, or in a pair or three, isn't dealt it. The operator's test door still reaches it.
