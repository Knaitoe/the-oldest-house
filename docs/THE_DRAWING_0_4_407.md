## The place

A village square on a bright summer morning, at ten o'clock all day. Apart from the plain, it is the only vignette in full daylight.

- **The lane:** the reader comes out of the House's door, in a mossy garden wall, at the foot of a lane between hedges. The lane opens onto the square.
- **The square:** flagstones with grass in the joints, round a patch of lawn with an old oak at two corners and benches facing the middle.
- **Round it:**
  - down the west side, the post office, the bank and a house;
  - down the east side, the grocery under its striped awning, a low stone wall with a vegetable plot behind it, and a house;
  - across the north side, the meeting hall between two more houses. The hall's door is the way on.
- **The doors:** every other door is locked. The post office and the bank are closed for the morning, and the houses' people are out in the square.
- **The box:** a black box on a three-legged stool in the middle of the lawn. Its paint is chipped, and one corner is split and bound with string.
- **The wall:** the children pile stones against it.
- **The board:** by the post office door, two pins wide.
  - The typed notice is pinned on the left. It says the drawing is at ten and everyone is to be there. It is the source.
  - The right-hand pin is bare. When a reader's account is ready, they alone see a folded paper under it.

## The people

There are eight villagers on stranger phases 123 to 130, each with a painted skin.

| Who | What they do |
|---|---|
| Mr. Albright | Runs the drawing, with the list in his hand. Friendly and businesslike. |
| Mr. Rudd | The postmaster. Holds the box steady on its stool. |
| Nell Garrow | Comes in late, laughing at herself, with a jug of lemonade. |
| Dale Garrow | Her husband. |
| Bess Garrow | Their daughter, a child. |
| Old Mr. Pike | Has been to every drawing. There has always been one. |
| Mrs. Dunn | Talks about the rain, the price of feed and her son's new tractor. |
| Ricky | Mrs. Dunn's grandson, a boy who gathers stones from the lane and piles them by the wall. |

A reader can talk to any of them before the households draw again (right-click), through the House's conversation panel. They are friendly and ordinary. Asked what the drawing is for, none of them says.

## One morning, in order

The square runs on one shared clock while a reader is in it. When nobody has been there for ten seconds, it starts over.

1. **The morning** (one minute).
   - The villagers come out of the post office, the bank and the houses in ones and twos.
   - A child carries stones from the lane to the pile by the wall, which grows.
   - Someone comes in late from the grocery with a jug: Nell, the first year.
   - They pour each reader there a glass of lemonade: a real item, drinkable, which does nothing.
2. **The call.**
   - Mr. Albright reads the households from his list, one at a time. Whoever draws for each walks up, draws a folded slip from the box and goes back without opening it.
   - At the end of the list he reads each reader's own name, "a new household this year". A reader who comes in after the call has begun is added at the end.
   - The reader walks up and right-clicks the box. If they don't come within fifteen seconds, he draws for them and the slip is put in their hand.
   - **The slip** is a real item. Using it before everyone has drawn only says not yet.
3. **The opening.**
   - Mr. Albright says everyone can look.
   - Every reader's slip is blank. Using it now unfolds it, and it stays blank.
   - A murmur goes round, and someone says which household it is. The one marked says something back.
4. **The household.** Its members come up one at a time and draw again, the one who came late last. That one's slip has a black mark on it. They hold it out and nobody takes it.
5. **The stones.**
   - The crowd turns and steps back. The one marked is left standing alone by the wall.
   - A child presses a smooth stone into each reader's hand: a real item.
   - The grown villagers pick up stones from the pile.
6. **What the reader does** (each reader for themselves).
   - **Throw it:** use the stone. The day cuts to white before anything is seen.
   - **Drop it:** throw it down. It stays where it fell and cannot be picked up again.
   - **Walk away:** walk back into the lane with the stone in hand. The crowd lets them through. Nobody is ever stopped from leaving.
   - **Stand:** do none of these. After thirty seconds the crowd closes in, and the day cuts to white for everyone still there.

When a reader's day cuts, the screen goes white with a high ringing, and they are set at the head of the lane, looking up it. The villagers are gone for them; the square is empty and quiet, and the box is on its stool. The slip is taken back. The stone stays with them unless it was thrown or dropped.

## Every year after

The drawing is held on every visit, before its story is had and after; each visit is a year.

- **The year:** the most any reader there has stood in the square, plus one. Everyone in the square shares it.
- **The village:** one villager fewer each year, and one more house shuttered.
- **The box:** shabbier each year: bound with string, then with wire, then with a bootlace, then worn almost bare.
- **The one marked:** each year it is the one who comes in late with the jug, standing where Nell stood the first year. In order:
  1. Nell;
  2. Mrs. Dunn;
  3. Dale;
  4. Ricky;
  5. Bess;
  6. Old Mr. Pike. From then on only Mr. Albright, Mr. Rudd and Mr. Pike are left, and it is Mr. Pike every year after.
- **The household:** the one marked and whoever of their household is still there draw again.
- **The children:** with both children gone, the pile is already there when the day starts, and Mr. Rudd hands out the stones.
- **The marked one is never a real player.**

## Resolution and the account

**Resolution:** the first whole day, once its account is read after the notice. A whole day means being read from the list, drawing or being drawn for, and being there when the day cuts. There is one source (`the_drawing`, understanding).

The account says only what that reader's own record holds:

- how many times they have stood in the square;
- who poured the lemonade;
- whether they talked with anyone while they waited;
- that their name was read as a new household;
- whether they drew, or were drawn for;
- whether they opened the slip;
- which household it was, and who drew the mark;
- on a later year, whether they remember the one marked from the year before, or talked with them;
- the stone, and what they did with it:
  - that they threw it;
  - that they dropped it;
  - that they walked away with it in their hand;
  - or that they stood and held it while the others went forward.

It ends at the head of the lane with the square empty. Witness credit does not depend on the choice, so no choice is the right one. Each later whole day pins that year's account on the board in its place.

## Multiplayer

- **Each reader's own:** their slip, their stone, their choice and their account. Each is read out as a new household.
- **Shared:** everyone sees the same year, the same village and the same marked household.
- **Choices:** peers see each other's choice in a line and take nothing from it.
- **A peer's throw** cuts only the thrower's day.
- **The marked household:** never a real player.
