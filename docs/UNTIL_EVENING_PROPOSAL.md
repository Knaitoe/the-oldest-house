# Until Evening — a design for a vignette after C.K. Walker's "Whitefall" (November 25)

The owner, 25 November:

> Can you design a vignette based on ck walker's whitefall? Don't script anything, just build a design document

This is that document. **Nothing is built, and nothing will be until the owner has read it.** I read the story in full on the
author's site (ck-walker.com/whitefall) before writing this. Every name, number and line below is mine unless it is marked as
the story's.

## In short

- **A bus terminal in a blizzard, with nothing round it.** The reader comes off a night bus with a few dozen other passengers
  into a brick terminal. A voice on the loudspeaker says the storm will lift by evening.
- **It does not lift.** The passengers split into camps by the bus they came on. Each camp has its own wall, its own vending
  machine, and a count of what is left.
- **The reader's own hunger is the clock.** Food from outside the terminal does nothing there. What the reader eats, gives away,
  trades, steals and burns is the play, and all of it is kept.
- **Time passes when the reader sleeps,** and it passes badly: they lie down on day 2 and the board says day 4.
- **It ends with a plate.** On the last day somebody hands the reader meat and says whose it was. Then the bay doors open.
- **It was one night.** On the bus out, the receipt in the reader's pocket, the clock outside the House and the reader's own food
  bar all say so. Nobody explains it.

## What the story brings, and what is left behind

The story is a living author's and in copyright. As with Borrasca, only its shape is taken: no sentence of it is used, and every
person in the vignette is new. Whether the place keeps the name Whitefall is the owner's call (question 1); the document uses a
name of its own, **Lowry Transfer**, until then.

Taken, as shape:

- **The terminal with no town.** Hard chairs, a ticket window nobody is behind, a departures board whose bays have gone blank, pay
  phones, one vending machine to a wall, a door marked for staff, lights that hum and never dim.
- **The voice.** A stationmaster nobody ever sees, who gives the storm's length and is wrong each time, by more each time.
- **Camps by bus.** People sort themselves by the city their bus left from, and the rule comes fast: a camp eats from its own
  machine and no other.
- **The snow outside as a wall.** Drifts to the top of the windows, a narrow cut leading away from the door, and nobody who walks
  down it is seen again.
- **The slide.** Rationing, sentries, trade, the first theft, the first death, the staff door broken in, the power going, books
  and chairs burned, a camp that stays fed and nobody asks how, and then people eating their dead: first by the dead person's
  leave, then without it.
- **The pay phone home.** A call a day while the quarters last, the line worse each time.
- **The old man's letter.** A man on his way to a daughter he walked out on, writing to her the whole stay.
- **The end.** A plate, a name, the doors, and a dated receipt that makes the whole stay one night. The bus line later says
  there is no such stop.

Left behind:

- The narrator, his girlfriend and their story. The reader is nobody's stand-in and has no written past.
- The bus fight and the harassment on the way there. Nothing sexual is in the vignette at all.
- The baby that stops crying, and the teenager chosen for the pot. Everyone who can be hurt here is grown (question 8).
- Named killings on stage: the barrel, the rope, the hammer. What the fed camp does is found afterwards, never watched.
- Seventy people. The terminal holds about twenty-six.

## Where and how the reader comes in

- **Kind:** a long single stay, dealt among the stories after the quiet rooms, never before depth 10. About 45 to 60 minutes.
  Saved for each reader, so a reader who logs out comes back to the same day on the same chair.
- **Door line:** *"NIGHT SERVICE. ALL PASSENGERS HOLD THEIR OWN TICKETS."*
- **The bus.** The door opens on the aisle of a night coach, moving. Two minutes: seats, dark windows, snow in the headlights,
  the people who will be the reader's camp in the seats round them. The reader has a ticket in hand with their own name on it and
  ONE WAY. The destination line is rubbed away.
- **The last stop.** One halt at a lit kiosk before the terminal. The reader is given two dollars in quarters as change and may
  buy one thing: coffee, a candy bar, or nothing. Whatever they do, they get a **receipt** with the day and hour on it. It is an
  ordinary scrap and nothing draws the eye to it.
- **The commitment.** Until the coach pulls in at Lowry, the reader can walk back up the aisle and out the door they came by. Once
  they step down onto the bay, the coach's door shuts and that way is gone, as with every committed beat.

## The terminal

One building on an outdoor site, built whole, with nothing else on the site but snow.

| Place | What is there |
| --- | --- |
| **The bay** | Four coaches nose-in under a canopy, snowed to the wheel arches by day 2 and buried by day 7. Roll-down bay doors, shut. |
| **The waiting room** | Four walls, a camp to each. Rows of bolted chairs, a machine to a wall, a clock, the board. |
| **The board** | A flap board. Every bay column is blank. Its last row gives the day, and it is the only place the day is written. |
| **The ticket window** | A shutter, a bell, a cash drawer with a roll of quarters in it for whoever thinks to look on day 1. |
| **The phones** | Two on a short wall. One is dead from the start. |
| **The washrooms** | Water runs until the power goes. After that, snow in whatever will hold it. |
| **The front doors** | Glass, drifted over. One leaf opens on the cut: a passage one person wide between walls of snow, bending out of sight. |
| **The staff door** | Steel, locked. Behind it a break room (lockers, a couch, a shelf of paperbacks, a kettle) and an office. |
| **The office** | Route maps with no Lowry on them, passenger lists, and the loudspeaker's microphone on a desk with nobody at it. |

Notes on building it:

- The windows show snow against the glass and nothing else. No horizon is ever drawn.
- The cut is real ground for about forty blocks. Past the bend the snow comes down white and the reader is walking in place.
- The microphone is live. The announcements go on being made while a reader stands beside it and it sits there on the desk.

## Days and sleep

The stay has **eight waking days**. The board numbers them 1, 2, 4, 7, 10, 14, 17, 19. The reader does not choose the jumps.

- A waking day lasts about six minutes of occupied time, and ends when the reader lies down across the chairs of their camp. If
  they do not, the lights' hum drops, the camp beds down round them, and the day ends on its own two minutes later.
- Everything that changes between days changes while the reader sleeps: who is gone, what is burned, what a camp has left, how
  the people look. People are thinner by stages (three paintings each for the reader's own camp).
- In company the day ends when every living reader in the camp has lain down, or at the two-minute limit.
- The loudspeaker speaks once a day, at a time of its own: by evening; by tomorrow evening; a few days; a few days more; ten or
  twelve days; then nothing until the last announcement.

| Board | What the day is |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | Arrival. The first call home. People still sit where they like. |
| **2** | The camps form and are named for their buses. Somebody walks out down the cut. |
| **4** | A man is beaten at four in the morning for opening the wrong machine. Every machine is broken open and counted. Rations and sentries begin. |
| **7** | Trade. The camp with least comes round with cigarettes and soda for nuts. |
| **10** | The first death, of cold, in another camp. They carry her out into the cut. Half rations. |
| **14** | The staff door is broken in. The fed camp takes the room behind it. The power goes that night. |
| **17** | Fires in bins. A camp is raided for its books. A dying woman asks her own people to use her. |
| **19** | The quietest camp is gone in the night and there is a grill going behind the staff door. The plate. The doors. |

## Hunger

- **The reader's own food bar is the gauge,** and in the terminal it obeys the terminal. Food carried in from outside can be eaten
  and does nothing. The bar falls a set amount each day whatever the reader does and rises only by what the terminal holds.
- **It never kills.** At nothing, the reader walks slowly, cannot sprint or hold a watch, and the edges of the view close in; the
  floor is there and no further.
- **What there is.** One machine's worth to a camp: about forty small things (crackers, nuts, a few candy bars, soda). Each is a
  real item with a real count. The reader's camp keeps its box under the chairs and anyone in the camp can open it.
- **The ration.** Each morning the camp's nurse sets out a share on each person's chair. Taking more is possible, at any time, and
  is seen or not seen by the ordinary rule: whoever is awake and looking.
- **On leaving, the bar is put back** to exactly what it was when the reader stepped onto the coach. This is the twist's third
  proof and should not be softened.

## What the reader does

Nothing below is a scripted beat. Each is a thing the terminal lets a reader do, and each is recorded.

- **Eat, or not.** Their share, more than their share, less.
- **Give.** A share set down on somebody else's chair is theirs. The old man is failing, and the nurse gives her own away. A
  person who is fed lasts longer, and who is still alive on day 19 follows from it.
- **Call home.** A quarter a call on day 1, two on day 2, a dollar by day 7. The talk panel, with a voice the reader never
  places: glad on day 1, puzzled on day 2 (*"You called an hour ago."*), and after that more and more of it lost to the line. The
  same quarters buy from the machines while the machines still take them, so every call is a candy bar.
- **Stand a watch.** Sit the night on the end chair. A reader on watch sees what the sleepers miss: a hand in the box, who goes to
  the staff door after dark and is let in, who comes back from the cut (nobody).
- **Trade.** Cigarettes, soda, a coat, a paperback, quarters. The poor camp will take a bad bargain and the reader can give a
  good one.
- **Steal.** Any camp's box can be opened by a reader nobody is watching. Caught, they are beaten down: they wake the next day on
  their own chairs with a day's share gone from their camp, and the camp knows why.
- **Burn.** Once the power is out, a bin fire keeps a camp's corner above freezing and holds for one day on eight things: chair
  slats, paperbacks, the route maps, a coat. A reader can also feed it **pages torn from their own journal**, and those pages are
  really gone. The old man's letter burns as well as anything.
- **Go through the staff door.** Before day 14 by its key (on the ticket window's hook, if looked for). After, by being let in.
- **Walk the cut.** See the endings.

## The people

About twenty-six, in four camps. The reader's camp is written in full; the others are two or three people with names and the
rest as company.

**The reader's camp** (the bus the reader came on):

| | Who | What they are for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Walt Mercer** | A retired millwright on his way to a daughter he has not seen since she was small. Sick before he boarded. | Writes one letter the whole stay. Asks the reader on day 17 to see that it gets there. On day 19 he gives his leave. |
| **Odile** | A student nurse going home for the holiday. | Keeps the count and sets the ration. The first of the camp to go, if nobody feeds her. |
| **Benny Aldana** | Draws in a pad: the same ridge from memory, over and over. | Talks about somebody at home and changes the story on day 17. He is the one with the plate. |
| **Ro** | Left somewhere in a hurry and will not say where. Plays cards for peanut shells. | Says what the camp will not. Plays the reader for shares if asked, and wins. |
| **Mrs. Tran and Daniel** | A mother and her small boy. | The camp feeds the boy first, always. He is never hungry on screen and never harmed (question 8). |

**The fed camp** (takes the staff room on day 14):

- **Hollis Rudd**, in a team sweatshirt: easy, generous with a candy bar on day 2, and the first to say every camp is on its own.
  *"Play nice"* is the story's phrase for him and is not used; his is *"Let's all stay friends."*
- **Dena** and **the big man, Cope**, who do what he asks.

**The poor camp:** **Gil Pruett** and his grown daughter **Marnie**, who trade first, lose a fight on day 4, and are gone by
day 19, some down the cut and some not.

**The quiet camp:** **Ada Voss**, who warns the reader on day 2, offers to pool on day 7, and on day 17 asks her own people to
use her.

**The voice:** the stationmaster. Never seen, never named, and not in the office.

**The drivers:** one, asleep in a coach on day 1 and not there on day 2.

## Day 19

- The quiet camp's chairs are empty and their bin is cold. The staff door is shut and there is smoke under it.
- Rudd comes out for one more: a young man of the poor camp, named and grown. The reader can stand in front of him. If they do,
  they and Ro are beaten down and Walt is hurt getting between; if they do not, the young man goes through the door. Either way
  the reader is on the floor of their own corner within the minute.
- Benny brings a plate from the bin fire. He says it is Walt, and that Walt said yes. If the reader heard Walt give his leave on
  day 17 they know it is true; if they slept through it they have Benny's word.
- **The plate is a real item with one use.** Eat it, set it down, hand it to somebody else, or put it on the fire.
- Two minutes later the loudspeaker says the bay doors are open. The lights come on, all of them, and the room is seen: the bins,
  the broken machines, what is behind the staff door for anyone who goes to look.
- A coach is running in the bay, dug out, with the day-1 driver at the wheel.

## Endings

| Outcome | How | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ate at Lowry** | The reader ate the plate, or ate from the fed camp's table on any day. | The bus out. The account says whom. |
| **Went out hungry** | The reader reached the doors having eaten nobody. | The bus out, at the gauge's floor. |
| **Walked the cut** | The reader went past the bend at any time after day 1. | White, then the hall they came from, as somebody who left. Not a resolution. The door deals again later, from the bus. |

Kept beside the outcome, and used by the account: who the reader fed and who was alive on day 19; whether they took more than
their share; whether they stole and were caught; calls made; what they burned (journal pages, the letter); whether they stood up
on day 19; whether they sat at Rudd's table (below).

**Rudd's table.** On day 14 Rudd makes one offer to any reader who has ever taken more than their share: a chair in the staff
room, warm, fed. The price is the reader's camp's box. A reader who takes it eats well, and the meat is never named. It is the
same outcome as the plate.

## The bus out, and one night

- The reader boards. The driver asks for the ticket and tears it. The seats hold whoever lived: their own camp's survivors, and
  Rudd, two rows up, who nods.
- **The receipt.** It is still in the reader's pocket. Its day and hour are the day and hour of the last stop, and the driver's
  dashboard clock is eleven hours on from it.
- **The clock outside.** The whole stay advances the world's time by one night and no more. A reader who came in at dusk steps out
  of the House's door, whenever they do, into the next morning.
- **The gauge.** Put back to what it was.
- **The journal.** If the reader wrote in it, their pages are there, headed by the House with the board's days: 1, 2, 4, 7 and on.
  Torn-out pages stay torn out.
- The coach's door opens on the hall. Behind the reader, the door they first came by is a plain passage, as every finished
  story's is. No route in the House leads back to Lowry.

## The account, Witness and what is kept

- **Account:** *"The account: one night at Lowry Transfer"*, written from the recorded facts in the usual way: the camp, who was
  fed and by whom, the calls, the fire, the plate. It gives the count of days as the board gave it and the date as the receipt
  gave it, side by side, and draws no conclusion.
- **Witness:** one source, kind **survival**. Resolutions `ate_at_lowry` and `went_out_hungry`. Walking the cut resolves nothing.
  The pool grows by one and the quota follows from it.
- **Keepsakes:**
  - **The torn ticket stub.** ONE WAY, the reader's name, no destination.
  - **Walt's letter,** if the reader took it and did not burn it. Sealed; it never opens. Posted in the manor's mailbox, it is
    gone the next morning, and that is all that ever comes of it.
  - **A quarter,** if one is left. A House pay phone that takes it rings four times and is not picked up.
- **Afterwards (question 7):** on rare nights, a reader who finished the story and sleeps in a House bed hears the loudspeaker's
  two-tone chime, once, far off. Nothing else.

## More than one reader

- Readers who come through the door together came on the same bus and are the same camp. The camp's box, fire and dead are shared;
  the calls, the gauge, the journal and what each one ate are their own.
- The box holds the same forty things however many readers there are. Two readers who both eat their fill starve the camp's
  people between them, and each can see what the other takes only by watching.
- Rudd's offer is made to each reader apart from the others, out of the others' hearing. One reader can sell the box the others
  are eating from.
- Each reader gets a plate. What one does with theirs settles nothing for the others.
- A reader who walks the cut is gone from the others' terminal, and the camp speaks of them as it does of anyone who went.
- The stay is a copy for each group, as with the other private scenes. Nobody arrives in another group's day 10.

## What it needs

- **Site:** one outdoor scene copy. A `transfer` block family: bolted chair rows, vending machine (whole, broken, emptied), flap
  board, pay phone, ticket shutter, bin (cold, lit), staff door, bay door, drifted glass. Snow cut. About 60 textures.
- **Cast:** eleven painted people. Three states of thinning for the six in the reader's camp. The rest of the room from the
  shared crowd painter in winter coats.
- **Coach:** an interior for the ride in and out (the highway work from State Route 9 and Backseat carries over), and four
  exteriors under snow.
- **Screens:** the talk panel for the phone, as it is. Nothing new.
- **Sound, all subtitled:** the lights' hum, the loudspeaker's chime and six announcements, a pay phone's coin and line static in
  four depths, wind on glass, a machine breaking, a bin fire, the bay doors, the coach idling.
- **Systems reused:** the journal's torn pages; the food bar override used by the Goatman's supper; scene copies; unseen changes;
  saved custody for the plate, the letter and the stub.

## How it would be built

Eight parts, each playable and checked before the next:

1. The site and the terminal, empty, with the bus in and the commitment.
2. The people, seated and talking, day 1 and day 2; camps form.
3. Days and sleep; the board; the loudspeaker.
4. Hunger, the box, rations, giving, taking.
5. The phones and quarters; trade; the watch; theft.
6. Day 10 to day 17: deaths, the staff door, the power, fires, the journal, the letter.
7. Day 19, the plate, the doors, the bus out and the three proofs.
8. The account, Witness, keepsakes, more than one reader, the content note.

## Content

Starvation; death by cold; people eating their dead, said and not shown; the reader can do so knowingly. A beating the reader can
take part in or stand against. No harm to a child, nothing sexual, no butchery on screen. The door carries the content note
before the bus, in the manner of Borrasca's trailhead.

## Questions for the owner

1. **The name.** Lowry Transfer, or Whitefall as the story has it? And the title: *Until Evening*, or the story's own?
2. **Length.** One sitting of 45 to 60 minutes with a save, or the eight days split over two or three visits? Splitting weakens
   "it was one night", and I would keep it whole.
3. **The gauge.** The reader's real food bar, overridden and put back (my choice), or a separate one drawn for the terminal only?
4. **Journal pages as fuel.** They would be really lost. Keep that, or make it a line in the account only?
5. **Rudd's table.** In with more than one reader it lets one player sell out the rest. Wanted, or too sour?
6. **Stealing and the beating.** A reader caught wakes the next day short. Should a second catch end the stay instead?
7. **The chime afterwards.** In, out, or only once?
8. **The boy.** Keep Daniel, fed first and never harmed, or have no child in the terminal at all?
9. **The voice on the phone.** Somebody the reader never places (my choice), or drawn from the House's read of the reader?
10. **The plate, unrefused.** Is "went out hungry" enough of an ending for a reader who ate nobody and fed everybody, or does it
    want a line of its own in the Witness account?
