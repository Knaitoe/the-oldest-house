# Borrasca: the children carry their fathers' letter (0.4.424)

The owner, November 9: "Remember too though that the children were named after their progenitor right?"

They were. In the story, a letter near the end says the town's babies come from the place on the mountain, and that they are named
for the men who fathered them: one letter for the Prescotts and another for the Sheriff. Looking back, nearly every child the
narrator grew up with has a name starting with one of those two letters. The renaming in 0.4.421 gave each child a name from their
own decade but missed this rule.

The vignette keeps the story's two lines of fathers, the Prescotts and the Sheriff, with the same two letters: **P** for the
Prescotts and **K** for the Sheriff. The children born at the depot carry them. The older generation does not.

| Was | Now | Letter | Where they appear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shelby Garrity | **Kelly Garrity** | K, the Sheriff's | the friend; her compass and name patch, the bark, her poster, the depot's tag, the room under the depot |
| Cody Maddox | **Patrick Maddox** | P, the Prescotts' | the boy with the bike; his poster goes up after the first visit |
| Brandi Bell | **Kendra Bell** | K, the Sheriff's | the girl who went looking for him; her poster is up by the adult visits |
| Kayla | Kayla | K, the Sheriff's | unchanged: the newest poster on the last night, the next tag in the book |
| Dana Pruitt | Dana Pruitt | none | unchanged: born about 1965, a town girl from before, taken to the depot herself |

Each name is still one a child of that decade would have, and none is a name from the story.

**The doctor's corkboard.** Every baby on the board now has a first name, and every one starts with P or K, in the childhood year
and years later alike. The board doesn't say why.

The Pruitts, whose Dana went up the trail, have a new daughter. The Maddoxes have a new son the spring after Patrick's poster goes
up.

Nothing in this release states the rule outright. The town's reason, and the place where the rule is written down, come with the
reworked lower rooms (rework 4: adult-only stables and the town's motive). Until then the names are there for a reader who notices
them.

The name patch spells KELLY, its letters a little larger so the five fill the cloth. The internal keys keep the old names
(`CastKimber`, `TaggedNell`, `EliTalk1`, `WrenRememberedEli` and so on).
