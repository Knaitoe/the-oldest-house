# Borrasca: what the playtest found broken (0.4.444)

The first batch from the owner's notes of 10 November (docs/BORRASCA_PLAYTEST_2026_11_10.md): the things that were plainly
broken. The texture and sign pass and the larger pieces follow.

## People

- **Kelly, in room 6.** Her words were hidden again on every tick, so each line showed for a moment. They stay up now.
- **One Mr. Dorsey.** A townsperson who had walked out of the loaded area was made again at his post, and the first came back
  later. A new one is now made only when everywhere about the place he was last seen is loaded and he is not in it, and a
  leftover second one is not let back into the world.
- **The Sheriff, after she is taken.** He no longer says "You two". He speaks to a child who came down alone.
- **The Sheriff's body** does not breathe, sway or turn its head. Blood spreads under it for ten seconds after he falls, and has
  dried dark on a later visit.
- **Earl.** The cell's door is shut, and a hand on its bars or door speaks to him through them. Years later he is not in the
  cell: he sits on a flattened box at the mouth of the alley beside the Tipple, and what he, Gus and the town say follows that.
- **The last night at the belt.** Dana is on the belt, at its head, when the night begins, and two operators stand beside it.
  The belt starts when the reader comes up to the yard. The men watch her go in: "There she goes. The Gentleman'll look after
  her now." "He looks after all of them." "My dad used to say that. Same words." Nobody fetches her or walks her out any more.
  Her cot in room 2 is empty. The breaker still stops the belt, before it starts as well.
- **Taken by the Gentleman**, a child is set down on the fort's floor, not on its roof.

## The town

- **Papers.** The papers in the depot's office and by its sorting table, and the stack on the Sheriff's desk, can be read: the
  dispatch sheet (Prescott's truck takes the folding tables to the church), the payroll (who in town is paid, and for what),
  the sign-in sheet, the intake form for 264-A, the unclaimed property tags, and the report forms with their finding already
  printed. Every paper that can be read has its own face: clipboards, green-bar paper, a ruled sheet with its pen, a stamped
  form, manila tags, a stack, the open night book, the pasted note, a pink carbon.
- **Nothing hangs on glass.** The antiques shop's clock and photographs and the society's framed history had been hung over the
  shop's small side windows. Wall is carried across behind any piece that hangs on one, and a test now scans the whole town.
- **The OPEN signs** hang inside their shops against the window, which keeps its glass.
- **The registers** face their keepers. **The pharmacy's shelves** are bottles, jars and boxes, in two arrangements.
- **The lollipop jar** is a glass jar with a lid and lollipops standing in it; nearly empty, two lie in the bottom.
- **The comic spinner** is a pole on a foot with three tiers of wire pockets on each side, covers outward.
- **The arcade cabinets** have one side panel from the floor to the marquee, with the game's badge above the control panel.
- **The two pumps** stopped at different sales. Years later the second is the one that sticks, as Kurt says.
- **The names on the Tree** are paler, larger and bolder, and are never drawn darker than a lit evening.

## Last orders

- The pool table's groups are stripes and **solids**.

## Tests

The feeding cases start from the belt and check the men and their words. The keeper case checks the shut cell, Earl through the
bars, and Earl on the street years later. The town case scans for pieces hung on glass or air, and checks the OPEN signs, the
pumps, the shelves, the register and all ten papers. The second-visit case checks the Sheriff's new line, and the capture case
that the child is in the fort.
