# The plain, in the Sahel (0.4.287)

The owner: "Consider actual regional landscaping and architecture." The plain keeps a photograph taken in the Sudanese Sahel. Its
landscape was the Sahara instead:

- pale sand dunes on both sides;
- a pale sand floor between them.

The Sahel's dunes are the qoz of Kordofan and Darfur: old dunes of red sand, held down by grass and thorn trees, over ground baked hard
between the tussocks. Termite mounds stand up out of it.

This pass gives the plain that look. Nothing in the story moves:

- the notes and the barrel by the door;
- the figure and its ground beyond the ravine;
- the camera and its exposure;
- every rise of the dunes.

New builds only.

## The plain (`PlainSahel`, at the end of the plain's landscape)

- **The ground turns red.** The dunes, the walkable ground, the ravine and the far ground are red sand and red sandstone instead of pale
  sand, in exactly the same shapes. The dunes still rise in steps of two or more, so they can be seen but not climbed. The entry's
  vestibule is left as the door copies it.
- **The walkable ground** has patches of bare, baked earth. Dry brush grows on it, with a tuft still green here and there. It is kept
  two blocks in from the dunes' foot.
- **Termite mounds**, the red earth built up into spires with a lump at the foot, stand out to either side, at least twelve blocks off
  the middle. None stands across the line to the figure.
- **Two thorn trees** stand alone on the plain: flat-crowned on leaning trunks, one with a second, smaller crown off a side limb.
- **The far edge** beyond the room still ends in a ridge too high to see over, now of red sand.
