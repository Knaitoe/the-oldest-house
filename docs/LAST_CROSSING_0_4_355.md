## The third morning

The station holds at first light for about fifteen seconds once Vera is back on her porch, then Day Three starts. Overnight:

- the rise jumped the cable off the last roller, which wants seating again;
- a pull that ran back until it threw the rollers left two of them off their cradles, and each takes three heaves to reset ("If the pull fails, the rollers can be reset from the upper landing in daylight. The crew starts Day Three with a repair job");
- Vera had the launch pin in overnight to dress it, so the pin's sleeve on the upper ramp stands empty and the pin hangs on a rack on the porch wall.

Vera says: "Depth first. And the pin's on the porch rack; I had it in overnight to dress it. Somebody take it down to the ramp."

## The depth and the roll

Vera walks down to the ramp and reads the depth against the sill: "Third mark on the sill. On the next rise she'll clear it." Anyone within sixteen blocks of her remembers it.

Then she takes the roll there, in the order the crew signed on the first day:

- The dead are called too. Nobody answers for them; she waits, and moves on.
- Anyone else gets their name called, and again if it goes unanswered.
- As on Night Two, only a reader who can hear her can answer:
  - within 48 blocks;
  - not below ground while she is above;
  - not shut in behind a door of another building.
- After the last name she calls Ellis. A breath later, "Here." comes up the speaking pipes. Only a reader near one of the pipes' mouths hears it.

Then she asks: "Who's working the launch? Who's looking for the source? And who's still below?" If any names went unanswered she adds: "Nothing from [names]. Still below, or somewhere I can't see." Each reader who heard her can say:

- "The launch.";
- "The source.";
- "Wherever I'm needed."

The question stays open for fifteen seconds, then she walks back to her porch.

## Talking to Vera on Day Three

- **"What needs doing?"** She sets out both plans: "Two ways off this bank. The launch: every roller seated, the pin carried down and set in its sleeve, the wheel tried, one of you on the rope and one on the pin. Or the feed: whatever comes up our pipes comes up it. Trace it down from the gauge and you'll find the old gallery. Get the bar to its gate and the way to the cutoff clear. Do one, or split it. Before dark."
- **"Do you want to cross?"** Only for a reader who asked about Ellis on an earlier day. She puts the clipboard down: "I want to hear everybody who's actually here."
- **"It got a claw in me."** For either carrier who hasn't said so yet, as before.
- **"About last night."** For the reader who turned on Night Two. Anyone near hears them tell it, and Vera says: "Then say it to them, not me. They're the ones who'll be next to you in the dark."

## The launch

The launch is ready for dark once:

- **Every roller is seated.** One the rise jumped seats with a heave; one a failed pull threw takes three.
- **The pin is staged.** The launch pin is carried down from the porch rack and set up in its sleeve over the sluice. Without the pin in hand, the sleeve only says the pin is on the porch. Hung back on its rack, it can be taken again.
- **The wheel is tried.** The sluice wheel in the pit is run through its whole travel.
- **There is a hand for the rope.** The launch rope is a slip line round a bollard at the ramp's edge, its tail on a tripping lever. By day, a reader there says they'll take it tonight. Before Day Three it is only made fast.
- **There is a hand for the pin.** The reader who staged the pin is its hand unless someone at the sleeve says they'll seat it instead. With someone holding the wheel below, the staged pin still seats in practice, as on Day One, and is knocked back out after.

Whoever is at the ramp, in the pit or in the cable house when it comes together, and the rope's and the pin's hands wherever they are, hear that the launch is ready.

On Day Three the cable house's brake and catch stay as the night left them, with "nothing to pull on today".

## The gallery

Each step of the gallery's work is below.

- **The feed.** The live feed has three tagged sections:
  - one by the gauge, in the gauge room;
  - one in the intake, going into the wall beside a rusted iron door;
  - one in the gallery, along the roof to the feed gate.

  A reader traces a section by rubbing the grime off its tag and chalking an arrow beside it. Once all three are traced, everyone who had a hand in it knows the way.
- **The rusted door.** It stays shut until the feed has been followed to it in the intake. Then three shoves with a shoulder grind it open on a stair going down east under the ramp.
- **The gallery.** An old brick room under the ramp, eleven blocks by nine, lit by three lanterns from its roof. The feed runs along its north wall into the feed gate in its east wall. Behind the gate is a flooded chamber, with a strip of thick glass in the wall either side of the gate. A reader who looks through it sees "something at the bottom of it: wrapped in weed, the size of someone crouching. It moves when you breathe."
- **The locking bar.** It hangs on hooks in the intake beside the rusted door and can only be taken down on Day Three. Carried down to the gate and set in the bracket beside it, it is staged for the night.
- **The debris.** Two heaps of fallen brick and silt choke the passage south from the gallery. Each takes four strokes to clear, and is half down after two. Once both are cleared, the passage is clear to the upstream cutoff. The cutoff is a wheel on the alcove wall that "won't move against the feed; with the gate barred it would."

The gallery is ready for dark once the feed is traced, the bar is staged and the way to the cutoff is clear. Whoever is down there, and whoever traced the feed or cleared the passage, hear it.

## The master bypass and the Other Breath

- **The master bypass.** It stands beside the sluice wheel in the pit and is rusted fast by day. To most readers it is just that: "it hasn't been turned in years." A carrier whose palm on the plate showed the second print knows more: "At high water the pressure will come round behind it and it'll turn. You know that the way you know your own hands." ("A reader whose test already exposed them knows the master bypass will be in reach at high water.")
- **The Other Breath.** "The source offers the Other Breath privately after an exposed reading." Twenty seconds into Day Three, a carrier who was exposed at the plate on an earlier day hears the offer, with the second breath, under their own. One exposed at the plate on Day Three hears it at once. Nobody else hears it: "Under your own breath the other one speaks, nearly words: the master wheel, at high water. Hold it open till the water's at the ramp, and breathe like us." Taking it is Night Three's.
- **The downstream sluice.** It stands east of the cable house, where the branches empty into the river. By day it is only looked at; Night Three's crew can shut it against an opened bypass.

As on Day Two:

- either carrier can still crouch by a wet grate and call something up in daylight;
- the branches' bypasses still open and shut;
- the first carrier's second breath comes and goes;
- so does the second carrier's.

## Before dark

Five sixths of the way through the day Vera says: "Last light soon. Launch crew at the ramp: one below on the wheel, one up top on the pin, one on the rope. Gallery crew down with the bar. And whoever has the plate, decide where it stands tonight. It can't be everywhere."

The plate can still be carried anywhere at the station and set in any bracket or stood on any floor, the gallery included. At the end of the day the light stops at the third dusk, and everyone on the roll who is there has worked Day Three. The night is the next half.
