The owner's decisions so far:
- **Tilly is a co-op role only.** A reader plays her only when a second reader is there. A solo reader is always the older sibling, with Tilly as a character.
- **Failures are permanent.** "Yes, we need to aim for permanent failures."
- **The gaslighting carries forward** into the reader's real House journal: "yes, carry the gaslighting forward."
- **The knife scene stays, by inference:** "a fight for the knife, a lunging fist, and a sharp cut to black. Static perfect for player character and heavy breathing as it stabilizes."
- **Nessa's body may be shown.** The earlier "never shown" was Claude's caution, not the owner's rule. The owner's rule is gore: gore is for adult victims only, and nothing of a child is shown that way. Her body is shown still and intact, never wounded or decayed.
- **The house itself carries the story.** "Since this is a small personal vignette, the house has to have a LOT of attention to detail in its unique textures and environmental storytelling." Most of this document is about the house for that reason.

## The vignette: "The Pell house" (working title)

A low brick bungalow on a cul-de-sac in a dry outer suburb, at the end of summer. June Pell fosters. The older sibling (the reader) and their younger half-sister **Tilly** arrive with **Gail Mercer**, the caseworker, the week after their father died.

June already has one foster child, **Callum**, who does not speak. Her daughter **Nessa** drowned in the pool behind the house. Nessa's room is kept exactly as it was, and Tilly is given it.

It is built whole, like the Last Stop and Last orders, in its own slot. It has its own block family and textures drawn in code (see **The house, room by room** and **Art** below). The polish, composition and review passes skip it.

## Who plays whom

- **Alone.** The reader is the older sibling, and Tilly is a character with her own skin, voice and routine.
- **Two readers.** One reader may play Tilly. The other is the older sibling.
  - **Picking roles.** A long cane and a small coat hang on the hook inside the front door, and whoever takes the cane is Tilly.
  - **If nobody takes the cane** while June shows them round, Tilly stays a character, and both readers are older siblings.
  - **Pairs are saved,** as at the lake house (`docs/LAKE_HOUSE_PAIRS_0_4_156.md`). The same two readers are the siblings on every visit until the ending lands. A third reader gets a house of their own.
- **More readers.** (The owner, 13 November: "the house should generate a second instance for a third and fourth player.") A third and fourth reader get a house of their own, a fifth and sixth another, and so on (`docs/PELL_HOUSE_PAIRS_0_4_547.md`). Each pair's house, line, freezer and Tilly are their own, and each pair's accounts name only each other.
  - *Superseded:* this proposal first gave a third or fourth reader a part as **the kids next door**, on the far side of the back fence. With a house to each pair there is nobody left over to play them, and they are not built.

### The older sibling: sees, and is not believed

- **Sleeps apart.** June gives them the converted garage across the yard. Its door to the house locks from the house side.
- **Sees June out at night,** walking the line with a tin and a brush. The garage window is the only window that looks onto that stretch.
- **Is framed** (see **Being framed**). Their journal fills with entries in their own hand that they never wrote.
- **Finds the tapes** in the shed and watches them in order.
- **Deals with Callum,** and with Gail, who has June's version first.
- **On the last night,** breaks the line and brings Tilly out through the gap.

### Tilly: believed, and cannot see

- **June's warmth.** Tilly lives in Nessa's room, wears Nessa's cardigan, and has her hair brushed by June every night. June is gentle with her and nobody else.
- **Partial sight.** Her view is light and shape, never black. Edges and faces dissolve past a few blocks, and strong light blooms.
  - It scales with `Comfort.effects()` like every other effect, with no floor.
  - Tilly is never the joke and never helpless. Her sight is a different way of knowing the house, and she is better at the house than the reader in several ways.
- **The cane.** Its tap sounds, and for a moment it outlines what is in reach: the edge of a step, a doorframe, the pool's coping, the line underfoot. This is how she moves well, not a handicap.
- **Hearing.** Some sounds reach only Tilly, subtitled for her alone:
  - singing through Nessa's wall at night;
  - the freezer's hum, which she can follow to the laundry;
  - the tape hiss from the shed;
  - June on the phone in the kitchen, telling Gail the things the older sibling never hears said.
- **Feeling the line.** She cannot see the line, but a cold falls over her each time she crosses it (a short frost at the screen's edges, no damage).
- **Her own verb: not answering.**
  - **The calls.** June calls her "Nessa", at first by mistake ("Sorry, love"), then more often. She asks things only Nessa would know ("Where did you put the goggles?").
  - **Answering.** Each answer the reader gives to that name moves the rite on, even a soft "yes". The answers are chosen on the conversation panel, never typed in chat.
  - **Not answering.** Silence, or her own name, holds it back.
  - **The pull to answer.** The dimmed view, June's warmth and the hair-brushing make answering the easy, natural thing. Nothing tells the reader it matters until the tapes do, and Tilly hears the tapes only if the older sibling plays them where she can hear.
- **Co-op lines.** Tilly's own lines come to her reader in grey, to say in their own words or not, as Tess's do. June's lines to Tilly never gender the reader ("love", "sweetheart").

## The people

- **June Pell.**
  - **Look:** a cardigan with the sleeves pushed up, reading glasses on a chain, paint at the quick of two fingernails that never quite comes off. On the last night she wears a swimming cap and an old one-piece under an open towelling robe.
  - **Manner:** the competent warmth of a former counsellor. She is never cruel in front of Tilly.
  - **Her lines** are kind and specific, and slightly wrong: "Inside the line after dark, love. It's for the dog."
- **Tilly:** child scale, a sunhat she won't take off outside, her cane, a backpack with a keyring that clicks.
- **Callum.**
  - **Look:** child scale, barefoot, in hand-me-downs too big for him. His hair is always wet, though nobody sees him swim.
  - **Behaviour:** he copies the older sibling's movements a beat late. At night he stands on the line. He takes cutlery and keeps it.
  - **Never hurt on screen.** He is eerie, never gory.
- **Gail Mercer, the caseworker:** a lanyard, a folder held to her chest, kindness under time pressure. She has always just spoken to June.
- **Nessa.**
  - **Before:** seen only in photographs and home tapes, at every size from toddler to the last school photo.
  - **The last visit:** in the freezer.

## The house, room by room

Every surface tells something, and no texture is a vanilla stand-in.
- **Native pixels:** the art is drawn in code at native scale, 16 to a block, with eight-pixel faces, like the Borrasca and Last orders sheets.
- **Two states:** most props have a "lived" state and a "last night" state.
- **Readability:** where a detail is meant to be read, it must read at eye level from where a reader naturally stands. An eye-level render review signs each room off.

### The street and the front

- **The cul-de-sac.**
  - Four other houses, all closed up and quiet.
  - One has a "SOLD" sign sticker on its agent's board.
  - Every lawn is brown from the watering bans. June's is green.
- **The letterbox.**
  - A bent tin box with the house number in stick-on letters, one missing.
  - **Inside:** mail still coming for Nessa, a school newsletter and a library notice, addressed to her. Under them, a sympathy card in its envelope, never opened.
- **The front garden.**
  - A row of child-height stakes with a dead seedling at each.
  - Each stake has a name on a plastic tag in a child's printing, all of them "NESSA".
  - The newest stake has a fresh seedling and a tag in an adult's careful capitals: "TILLY".
- **The driveway.**
  - June's car, with a booster seat in the back. A cardigan is folded on it and a toy is tucked into the seat belt, both Nessa's.
  - An oil stain where a second car used to stand.
- **The path.**
  - Concrete with a child's handprint pressed into one slab and a date scratched beside it.
  - The line crosses the path just short of the step.
- **The front door.**
  - A security screen door with a diamond grille. Its flyscreen is torn at child height and patched with tape.
  - A brass knocker no one uses.
  - A doormat with the line painted under it. Lifting the mat shows the paint runs straight under, unbroken.
- **The bricks.**
  - Brick veneer with weep holes and salt bloom low on the walls.
  - One window on the side has been bricked up, and its newer bricks don't match.

### The line

- **What it is.** A hand-width painted line, unbroken, round the whole property: front garden, side passage, back fence, and the back of the shed.
- **Its route:**
  - it goes under the side gate, which has been planed at the bottom to let it through;
  - through the dog's kennel (below);
  - over the pool fence's footing;
  - across the driveway, where tyre marks have been painted over.
- **How the paint takes, a texture for each surface:**
  - soaked into concrete;
  - beaded on pavers;
  - sitting on grass blades, which are dead beneath it;
  - flaking off deck boards;
  - pooled in the mulch of the garden bed, where it has been laid twice because the first coat sank in.
- **Layers.** It has been repainted many times. At chipped edges older coats show underneath, in slightly different whites and widths. On the second visit it is fresher than on the first. On the last night it is wet in places, and a footprint has smeared it.
- **What it can do.** It is a real thin layer block (`PellLine`). It can be scrubbed out cell by cell with the laundry's scrubbing brush and a bucket (see **The last night**).

### The kennel

- **What's there:** a timber kennel with a pitched roof, a chain clipped to the wall ring at one end and to nothing at the other, and a stainless bowl full of leaves.
- **The name:** a name board over the opening, BISCUIT, with a crayon heart beside it.
- **The giveaway:** the line runs straight through the kennel's floor. The kennel was moved onto it, not the line round it. There is no dog.

### The pool

The pool has two states.
- **Covered** (the first visit):
  - a sagging tarp holds a pond of rainwater and leaves;
  - the pool fence's child gate is padlocked, and the padlock is new on an old latch.
- **Open** (from the second visit):
  - the water is too clear and the tiles too clean;
  - **the waterline band:** one tile is missing and one is replaced with a tile that doesn't match;
  - **the bottom:** a pair of child's goggles rests on the deep end, under the drain grate;
  - **the lane rope:** a float rope divides shallow from deep, and one float is missing;
  - **at night:** the pool light makes the whole yard waver;
  - **the pump:** it runs constantly. The sound carries into Nessa's room, and only there does it sound like breathing.
- **Around it:**
  - a pool chair turned to face the water, not the sun;
  - a beach towel on its back that hasn't moved in a long time, sun-faded on one side only.

## The three visits

Each visit is a stay at the house. Visits follow the House's usual dealing, with the pair kept together.

### 1. The placement

- **Arrival:** Gail drops them off. June shows them round, and Callum watches from the end of the hall.
- **June's rules,** kindly given:
  - inside the line after dark;
  - the pool gate stays locked;
  - Nessa's room is Tilly's now;
  - the garage is the older sibling's, "for your privacy".
- **Settling in:** the siblings unpack. Dinner is at the table, and June serves Tilly first.
- **At night:**
  - the older sibling sees June out with the tin;
  - Tilly hears singing through the wall;
  - Callum stands on the line in the dark, facing in.
- **The morning:** June has made breakfast, and Gail calls to see how it went.
- **No harm comes to anyone.** This visit is the house, read slowly.

### 2. A week in

- **The pool:** the cover is off. June swims lengths at dawn.
- **Tilly:** June starts calling her Nessa "by mistake".
- **The knife.**
  - **The fight:** at night the older sibling finds Callum in the kitchen with the knife from the empty slot, and they fight him for it.
  - **The cut:** a fist lunges, then a sharp cut to black.
  - **After the cut:** static fills the reader's own view while their breathing, heavy at first, settles as the static clears.
  - **Afterwards:** the knife is gone, there's a towel on the floor, and June is in the doorway.
  - **The next morning,** Gail has June's version: the older sibling went for Callum.
  - **Never shown:** what happened in the black, and no wound on anyone.
- **June's hand.** Off screen there is a door, a sound and a cry. June comes out with her hand wrapped. By the time Gail calls, it was the older sibling who slammed the door on it.
- **The tapes:** the shed is open. The older sibling can watch the tapes in order, and the rite becomes readable (below).
- **The freezer:** Tilly can follow its hum to the laundry. It is cold under her hands, and padlocked.
- **The journal:** the first false entries appear in the older sibling's journal (below).

### 3. The last night

- **The scene:**
  - the calendar's circle is today;
  - the pool is full and lit;
  - the line is fresh and wet in places;
  - every knife slot is empty;
  - Callum is on the line at its thinnest point, behind the shed.
- **At midnight** June takes Tilly into the water. To a reader playing Tilly, this is warm and calm. June talks softly and calls her Nessa.
- **The freezer** is unlocked on the last night. The key is on June's lanyard on the robe by the pool chair, so it can be taken while she is in the water. Inside is Nessa, under frosted plastic, in the dress from the photographs, intact and still. The reader knows her from every room.
- **Breaking the line** (below), with Tilly out through the gap, ends the rite.
- **Dawn:** Gail's car at the curb, either way.

## The tapes

- **What they are:** nine original home recordings by an older woman. She is never named, and she speaks to someone off camera who has lost a child.
- **How they play:** on the shed's television, with the film and screen machinery the shop and Movie Night already use.
- **What they teach, in fragments:**
  - **The ring:** one ring, unbroken, holds what is called back inside it.
  - **The keeper:** a child is kept hungry inside the ring to hold what is called back until it can be poured.
  - **The vessel:** another child, alike in the way that matters, is the vessel. It must answer to the name.
  - **The water:** the pouring is done in water, where the first one was lost.
- **Never explained:** nothing says what Callum is, or whether it works.
- **In order:** watching them in order is what turns the house's details into meaning (the line, the kennel, the circle on Callum's floor, the freezer, the name). Out of order they are only a frightening woman talking.
- **For Tilly:** a reader playing Tilly hears a tape only if it plays where she is, so the older sibling has to bring her to the shed, or bring the sound to her.

## Being framed

Two systems carry it.

- **What Gail hears.** Each framed incident (the knife, June's hand, a broken thing) comes to Gail as June's version first.
  - **Answering Gail.** The older sibling answers her on the conversation panel. A calm answer and an angry one go down differently, and the account remembers what was said.
  - **Her file.** It gains a mark for each incident the reader can't answer. At three marks, Gail takes the older sibling away mid-visit (see **Failure**).
  - **Clearing a mark.** Evidence found and shown to her clears one: the transcript page, the "DO NOT SWITCH OFF" note, a tape played while she's in the room.
- **The journal, in the reader's own hand.**
  - **When:** after each framed incident, and once after each night in the garage.
  - **What:** a short entry appears in the older sibling's real House journal (`docs/JOURNAL_DESIGN.md`), in their own handwriting and their own ink, as if they wrote it. Examples:
    - "I took the knife out of the block."
    - "I slammed the door on her hand. Nobody saw."
    - "I told Tilly nobody wants her here."
  - **How it differs from the co-author.** This is not the other hand (`docs/JOURNAL_COAUTHOR_0_4_180.md`), which writes in its own lettering. This is the reader's lettering, which is the horror.
  - **Like the co-author, it only adds:**
    - it never changes the reader's own words;
    - it writes only on pages begun in the House;
    - it never writes on a page the reader has open.
  - **Tearing it out.** The reader can tear such a page out (`docs/JOURNAL_TORN_PAGES_0_4_199.md`), but a torn false page always turns up again:
    - in Gail's folder on the last visit, in her hands;
    - after the vignette, pinned to a wall in a later hall.
  - **Carried forward,** by the owner's decision:
    - the entries made during the visits stay in the journal for good, whichever way it ends;
    - after a failure, new false entries keep coming, rarely (at most one a House day), each a twisted line about a real thing the reader did in a later vignette, in their own hand;
    - after a success they stop, but the old ones stay.

## The last night, in play

- **Breaking the line.**
  - **The tools:** the scrubbing brush and the bucket from the laundry.
  - **The work:** holding use on a cell of the line scrubs it out over a few seconds. The paint fades in stages, on its own texture for each surface.
  - **The gap:** two cells side by side make a gap wide enough to pass Tilly through.
  - **June repaints** on her rounds on the earlier visits, so a gap opened then is gone by morning. On the last night she is in the water, and the line stays as the readers leave it.
- **Callum on the line.** He stands where it is thinnest and does not move for pushing.
  - **What moves him:** food. A plate from the fridge, anything, offered to him. He takes it, sits down on the grass inside the line and eats, and the line behind him is free to scrub.
  - **When the line breaks:** he looks up and says the only thing he says in the whole vignette: "My name's Callum."
- **Tilly in the water.**
  - **The calls:** June calls her Nessa, softly, again and again.
  - **Holding out:** a reader playing Tilly has the breath gauge the caver already has (`docs/TED_CAVER_0_4_71.md`) while June holds her under for the name. They must hold out and not answer.
  - **A character Tilly:** holds out for a fixed time, shorter if she answered to "Nessa" on the earlier visits.
  - **No drowning:** if breath runs out, June lets her up into the air and asks again, and a dazed reader is tempted to answer. Nobody drowns. The failure is the name, not the water.
- **Getting her out.**
  - **Getting to her:** the older sibling has to get into the pool and take Tilly's hand.
  - **The way out:** guide her out of the water and across the yard to the gap, by sound, since she can't see it.
  - **Guiding by sound:** a reader playing Tilly hears the older sibling's footsteps and voice lines positionally. A character Tilly follows the reader's hand.
  - **June:** stays in the water calling the name. She does not chase.
- **The freezer.**
  - **Optional.** Opening it is not needed to succeed, but it changes the account.
  - **Bringing June:** a reader who brings June to the open freezer and says Nessa's name to her, not to Tilly, ends her calling. June sits down by the freezer and stops.

## Endings

Every ending is permanent. There is no second attempt, and the vignette is never dealt again to a reader whose ending has landed.

### Success: both out, and the name kept

- **The condition:** Tilly crosses the broken line with her own name, the older sibling with her, before dawn.
- **What follows:** Gail's car is at the curb. Callum is sitting on the front step with his shoes on.
- **The account** (survival kind) ends on the street, beyond the line. If the reader brought June to the freezer, it adds that she was still sitting there when the car came.
- **The keepsake** is Tilly's spare cane-tip, a small worn rubber thing, owner-bound. A suggested effect, for the owner to decide: while it is carried, the reader hears the next door they need a beat before they see it.
- **In co-op,** both siblings' readers resolve the story, each with their own account.

### Failure: Tilly answers

- **The moment:** in the water on the last night, Tilly answers to "Nessa". That ends the rite.
- **For a reader playing Tilly:**
  - from then on, the House addresses that reader as Nessa in all its writing: accounts, the House of Leaves, letters;
  - their journal's handwriting changes to a younger, rounder hand from that page on;
  - their account of the house is written as Nessa's, about a placement that went well;
  - they are not harmed, and they leave the House as usual.
- **For a character Tilly** (solo, or two older siblings):
  - Tilly stays;
  - the older sibling is in Gail's car at dawn, and Tilly waves from Nessa's window and doesn't come;
  - later in the House, now and then, there's a cane tapping behind a door that will not open, and a voice that answers to a name that isn't hers.

### Failure: the older sibling is taken away

- **The moment:** Gail's third mark. She comes in the night and takes the older sibling.
- **Their part ends** in Gail's car. They are driven to the end of the cul-de-sac and set down outside the House's door, and the house is not dealt to them again.
- **What follows:** Tilly's night goes ahead without them.
  - **A reader playing Tilly** can still hold out alone until dawn. That is a success for Tilly's reader only, and the older sibling's account says they were not there.
  - **A character Tilly** answers.
- **The forged entries** keep coming after this failure, as after the other.

### Aftermath

Like the pool and the flat (`docs/AFTERMATH_0_4_244.md`), the house comes back once in a later hall.

- **After a success:**
  - empty, with a "FOR SALE" sign on the front lawn;
  - the line scrubbed off everywhere, though its ghost shows in the dead grass;
  - the pool drained;
  - in Nessa's room, only the height marks, and the "N" rubbed out.
- **After Tilly answered:**
  - lived in;
  - the line fresh;
  - on the door frame, one more pencil mark, higher, with a later date and an "N".

## Art

The detail above is the specification. In practice:

- **One block family,** `pell` (like `roadhouse` and `cottage`), with one kind for each prop and fitting above, and a stage property for the lived, second-visit and last-night states.
- **Drawn in code** by one new tool, `tools/draw_pell_house.py`, at native 16-pixel scale, with a `--check` that CI runs, like the other art tools.
- **About seventy textures, in groups:**
  - **Outside:** the brick veneer with weep holes and salt bloom, the bricked-up window, the security door's grille, the patched flyscreen, the letterbox, the stakes and tags, the concrete handprint, the kennel and name board, the side gate.
  - **The line:** on six surfaces, in three ages each, plus the scrubbed stages.
  - **The pool:** the waterline band, the mismatched and missing tiles, the coping, the drain grate, the goggles, the lane rope and floats, the tarp, the pump housing, the child gate and padlock.
  - **The shed:** the corrugated iron, the shelves of dated tape spines, the transcript pages on the wall, the calendar in its three states, the tins.
  - **The laundry:** the freezer's lid, frost and seal, the note on the power point, the stool, the chlorine box.
  - **The kitchen:** the fridge door with its photographs, the chore chart with its lifting strip, the knife block in its three states, the lunchboxes, the lino and burn.
  - **The living room and hall:** the recliner, the tape cabinet and spines, the face-down cards, the hall's school photographs (one face each, growing), the empty hooks, the worn carpet path.
  - **Nessa's room:** the wallpaper border and its crooked patch, the glow stars, the raised-dot labels, the talking clock, the textured switch, the large-print spines, the height marks, the second cane.
  - **Callum's room:** the painted-over window, the floor circle, the drawer of cutlery.
  - **The bathroom:** the two toothbrushes, the mirror word, the bath toy.
  - **The garage:** the pegboard with its outlines in three states, the labelled boxes, the camp bed, the bulb and cord.
- **Faces:** every photograph of Nessa is a real eight-pixel face, consistent across ages, so the reader recognises her in the freezer.
- **Skins.** June, Tilly, Callum, Gail, and Nessa as she is in the freezer.
  - **Painter:** all by the shared painter (`tools/paint_character_skins.py`), listed in the catalog and drawn on the cast proof's pages.
  - **Nessa's frozen skin:** pale frost at the lashes and hairline, eyes closed, lips still. Nothing wounded, nothing decayed.
- **Review.** An eye-level render of every room through the production renderer, in the client proof.
  - **Required views:** the line at each surface, the hall of photographs, Nessa's door frame, the freezer opened, the pool at night.
  - **Sign-off:** each is read for whether its story reads at a glance.

## Sound

- **Outside:** cicadas in the day, crickets at night, a sprinkler two streets away, a car that slows at the end of the cul-de-sac and goes on.
- **The house:**
  - the pool pump, constant, louder in Nessa's room;
  - the freezer hum;
  - the brush on concrete at night, and the tin's lid;
  - the tape deck's motor and hiss.
- **People:**
  - June's song, an original lullaby with plain words, heard only by Tilly through the wall;
  - Callum humming the same song, wrong;
  - the cane's tap, distinct on each surface.
- **The knife scene:** the static and the breathing.
- **Subtitles:** all original and subtitled, like every House sound.

## Fit with the House

- **Witness:** one new survival source. Success resolves it once, for each sibling's reader. Neither failure resolves anything. The pool grows by one, and the quota stays derived (87 sources and 66 required, if it is added at today's 86 / 65).
- **Copies:** a copy per pair (the scene-copy system), so every pair's house, line, freezer and Tilly are their own. Built in 0.4.547 (`docs/PELL_HOUSE_PAIRS_0_4_547.md`).
- **New builds only.**
- **Writing rules:**
  - no line of the House's writing genders the reader or names a colour or an age;
  - the height marks carry dates, never ages;
  - no line tells the reader how they feel;
  - speech is never chat; June, Gail and Tilly speak on the conversation panel and in captions.
- **Comfort:** Tilly's sight, the cold at the line, the static and the cut to black all scale with `Comfort.effects()`, with no floor.

## Content lines

- **Children:**
  - children are in danger and never shown hurt;
  - nobody drowns;
  - the knife scene is inferred, never shown: the fight, the fist, black, static, breath.
- **Nessa** is shown, still and intact, under frost. Nothing of her is wounded or decayed, and she is shown with care: the dress from the photographs, June's hairbrush on the lid.
- **Nothing sexual,** anywhere.
- **Tilly's partial sight** is a way of knowing the house, never a weakness played for fear or for laughs.
