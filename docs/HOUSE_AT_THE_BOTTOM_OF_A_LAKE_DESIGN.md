# A house at the bottom of a lake

Status: design only. Nothing here is implemented yet.

The owner chose Josh Malerman's novella *A House at the Bottom of a Lake* (2016) over Stephen King's "The Raft" for a new vignette, supplied the text, and agreed the points below. The guiding rule is that Minecraft's own verbs carry the story: boats, the air bar, mining, sleeping, light levels and armour slots. Each vignette may need new systems of its own, and this one does.

## Source and limits

- **An adaptation, not a transcription.** The novella is under copyright. The vignette takes its premise and shape only, with original names, words and house. No line of the book is quoted in game; the lines in this document are original placeholders.
- **No sexual content.** The book's turning point is the two seventeen-year-olds sleeping together in the house. None of that is adapted. A first date's nerves are as far as it goes.
- **Clean graffiti.** The tunnel's graffiti is strange, not crude.
- **No body in the dresses.** The figure at the end is faceless, featureless wax.

## What the book gives us

Two teenagers on a first date canoe from a clear lake through a tight graffitied culvert into a murky third lake, and find a two-storey brick house standing on the bottom. They dive into it again and again, each time with better gear and a little further in, and keep it to themselves under one rule: "No hows or whys."

When one of them tries to pry an object loose and asks *how*, someone starts walking upstairs. The house follows them home. They go back to introduce themselves, every light in the house comes on, a faceless wax figure is getting dressed upstairs, and then the house is gone.

## Translating it to Minecraft

- **Breath is the vanilla air bar.** Players already read it. Running out is never death: a short choke (as in the caver), then a blackout, and the reader wakes on the raft with the visit's progress kept.
- **The reader's own breathing aids don't work** anywhere in the vignette: no potions of water breathing, conduit power, turtle shells or Respiration. Only the vignette's own gear helps. A general system that suspends a reader's inventory on entering a vignette may come later; it is not part of this design.
- **Gear grows across visits, as in the book:**
  - Visit 1: free diving.
  - Visit 2: a gold diving helmet on a hose from a compressor on the raft.
  - Visit 3 onward: air tanks worn in the chest slot, with the durability bar as the air gauge, refilled at the compressor.

  All of it is lent by the vignette and stays there.
- **The rule is mining.** Every player mines by reflex. Try to mine anything in the house and it doesn't break: it lifts off its place, turns over to show nothing is holding it, and settles back.
  - The first try gets a warning ("Don't ask how.").
  - Trying again breaks the rule for that reader. It is saved, the footsteps upstairs begin, and it shows in their account.
  - Breaking the rule never closes an ending.
- **The uncanny is specific to Minecraft.** Blocks never float in Minecraft, so "the furniture doesn't float" isn't strange here. These are:
  - candles and a hearth burning underwater;
  - dropped items sinking and lying still instead of bobbing up;
  - the reader's bubbles hanging in place;
  - no fish inside the walls;
  - the dining chairs dry in their own pockets of air.
- **The dry chairs are deliberate.** They use the vanilla quirk that a non-waterlogged block holds air. Sitting in one lets a reader breathe: that's the house asking them to stay, and the trigger for the third ending. Every other partial block in the house must be waterlogged, so no unplanned air pockets exist (no vanilla doors or signs inside).
- **Deep water is dark.** Below about fifteen blocks it is black. A lent flashlight is a real moving light that peers can see; it dies once, in the lounge.
- **Smaller translations:**
  - The floating dresses are smooth display entities, capped at a handful.
  - The still life in the lounge is a custom painting that becomes a face.
  - The dead fish is a cod item lying face up just below the surface.
  - The garden under the windowsill is kelp, seagrass and glowing sea pickles.
  - The basement pool, "water inside water", is a visible downward current with a warmer tint.
- **Cut from the book:**
  - the first lake with its speedboats (the trip starts at the clear lake's dock);
  - building the raft (it is already there, tied to the chimney, and nobody says who built it);
  - all the romance.

## The place

An outdoor literary scene in the next slot (95), multi-visit, appended and carved once in saved worlds under the usual loaded, unwatched and vacant guards. It contains:

- **The dock:** a boathouse on the clear lake, with a green canoe (a vanilla boat painted green) and the rental log, which is the source paper.
- **The culvert:** low, two wide, dark and graffitied. The canoe scrapes and slows; paint chips come off.
- **The third lake:** murky, still, about twenty-five blocks deep. A raft is tied to the chimney of the house, with the compressor, a rope ladder down and a bedroll.
- **The house:**
  - outside: the garden, a stone step with handrails, and the half front door (only the left half, swaying);
  - foyer: coatrack, key bowl and a bulb on a string;
  - ground floor: a dining room set for one more than came, a study with an ashtray that won't lift, a lounge with a racked pool table and the still life, a kitchen with two marble islands and a porcelain beaver pepper shaker, and a stair with a red runner;
  - upstairs: a hall, the dressing room, bedrooms, and the eaves as a swim-crawl;
  - below: a hidden closet opening on thirty steps marked like a growth chart, down to the pool and the sauna.

## The visits

1. **The roof.**
   - Canoe through the culvert to the third lake. The roof sits just under the surface.
   - Free dive to the garden, the half door swaying open, and the foyer.
   - Reaching the foyer and getting back up ends the beat ("There's furniture down there.").
2. **The helmet.**
   - The compressor feeds the helmet's hose, drawn like a lead from the raft to the diver, with a fixed length. While the compressor runs, the diver's air holds.
   - The hose snags once on the porch rail, and the diver has to swim back and free it.
   - The ground floor: the dining room, the study, the lounge and its painting, and the staircase.
   - Then the partner takes a turn below and comes up saying there are bedrooms up there, and dresses.
3. **The tanks.**
   - Upstairs, the dressing room, the bedrooms and the eaves.
   - The kitchen, where the rule tempts.
   - The hidden closet, the growth-chart stair and the pool. The black dress sinks toward the reader, and the partner points up.
4. **Between visits: the house follows you home.** The next time that reader wakes in a bed in their own world, they see private dripping from the ceiling and wet footprints leading from the nearest door to the bed and stopping. It happens once. Nothing in the world is changed.
5. **Say hello.**
   - The sauna door shuts on its own. It's hot and steamy but harmless.
   - The footsteps go upstairs, and every lamp in the house lights for that reader alone (see "The lights" below).
   - In the dressing room the wax figure is putting on the black dress. What the reader does next decides their ending.

## Tess, the solo partner

A reader who comes alone has Tess. She is an original character; the name is a working one.

- **Only for a reader alone.** She exists only while her reader is the only living reader in the scene. Spectators never count and never see her.
- **Only that reader sees or hears her.**
- **When a second reader arrives,** she leaves the first moment her reader isn't looking ("I'll watch the compressor"). If that moment never comes within a few seconds, she swims up and away in plain view. She never vanishes on camera.
- **She doesn't come back mid-visit,** only on the next visit her reader makes alone. Her story pauses while they aren't alone.
- **In the canoe** she rides the second seat.
- **Talking:** she talks only above water. Underwater she mouths and gestures (pointing, beckoning, a gloved thumbs-up), with a short italic line on the action bar.
- **Swimming:** she swims authored routes in a swimming pose and can't be pushed, hurt or leashed.
- **The helmet visit:** she tends the compressor and tugs the hose when it snags.
- **The rule:** she is the one who sets it, and she's hurt if the reader breaks it ("Couldn't it just be ours?").
- **The finale:** at the dock she says "Something was in my room last night. The floor was wet. We have to go back and say hello." In the house she goes up toward the footsteps first, but won't meet it without her reader.
- **After the ending, she says goodbye once.** On the reader's next visit alone she is waiting at the dock one last time and ends it, as the book does. Her words depend on the ending:
  - Said hello: "It was ours because we never asked. I don't want to spend the rest of it asking."
  - Left the lights on: "It's still lit, isn't it. I'm not going back down. I don't think you should either."
  - Stayed a little longer: "You didn't come up. I sat on that raft until it got light. I can't do that again."

  After that she is never at the dock again; the canoe waits there alone. A solo reader is never left wondering where she went, and it costs only a few lines. If the reader never comes back alone, the goodbye simply never happens.

## Multiplayer

- **Shared:** the house, the raft and the compressor.
- **Each reader's own:** Tess, the footsteps, the figure, the lit lamps, the lent gear, the rule, the footprints at home, the ending and the artifact.
- **Two readers are each other's partner, with no Tess.** At the helmet stage one of them has to stay at the compressor while the other dives, so that stage is real co-op.
- **Readers at different visits** share the same house; whatever changes for one of them stays private.
- **After "Said hello", the house is gone for that reader.** Every later visit takes them to an empty copy of the third lake, built once and shared by everyone with that ending. That holds even if they come through the door with someone who still has the house, which matches the book: they can't find it again.

## Endings and artifacts

Each ending readies that reader's account. The reader resolves it by reading the rental log, then the account, at the boathouse. Each artifact is bound to its reader and, like every artifact, can be offered to the Mother. For now the artifacts have only their uses outside the House; uses inside the House are deferred.

| Ending | How it's reached | What follows | Artifact, outside the House |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Said hello** | Touch the wax figure while the lights are on. | The lights go out, the reader blacks out and wakes on the empty lake. | **The flashlight that found you.** A handheld light that works anywhere, underwater too, and never runs out. Once in a while its beam catches something at the edge of the dark. |
| **Left the lights on** | Leave through the half door while the house is lit, without touching the figure. | The house stays lit for that reader on every later visit. | **The house light.** Hung anywhere in the Overworld, even underwater, it makes that place home: the reader respawns under it without a bed. It switches itself on as they come home at night, and now and then they hear wet steps near it. |
| **Stayed a little longer** | Sit at your place at the table and stay seated as the lights dim. | The reader blacks out at the table and wakes on the raft at dawn. The account says days have passed; the world's time does not change. The house stays dark, with their place set. | **The gold helmet.** Unlimited breath underwater, but no armour, muffled hearing and a narrower view. Sometimes the house creaks somewhere above. |

## The lights

The finale's "every light came on" happens for one reader at a time, and the house stays lit afterwards for a reader who left the lights on. It is built so it never depends on the client relighting blocks the server sent to one player:

- **How the lamps look:** the house's lamps switch to their lit state for that reader alone, through per-reader block views. The Last Stop already sends single readers their own blocks.
- **The light itself:** while that reader is inside the house, a hidden night-vision effect gives them full brightness. It has no particles and no icon. This is what actually lights the rooms for them, so the moment works even if a client never relights the private lamp blocks.
- **The lights going out:** the effect ends and a short vanilla darkness covers the blackout.
- **Left the lights on:** the same lamps and brightness return on every later visit while the reader is inside the house.
