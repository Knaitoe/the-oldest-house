# Bike night 23: the Hollow (begun, 0.4.493; its plain way, 0.4.494; somebody who misses, 0.4.495; the high way, 0.4.496; the long way, 0.4.497; the way out, 0.4.498; the steep way, 0.4.504; the tight way, 0.4.505; the dark way, 0.4.506)

The proposal (`docs/BIKE_NIGHT_PROPOSAL.md`, "Act three"):

> **The Hollow** is the owner's platforming realm.
> - **The ground:** islands of graveyard turf with their headstones still in them; crooked fences; a bridge of coffin
>   lids; a pumpkin vine the size of a road, curling up and down; stairs of tilted stones.
> - **The riding:** riders jump and swerve from piece to piece. Missing a jump drops them onto the last island they stood
>   on (a checkpoint), with a scare.

This record grows with the Hollow. So far:

## 0.4.493: what it is made of

Nothing is built yet. This is the Hollow's materials, drawn by the graveyard's art tool
(`tools/draw_calder_graves_art.py`) and looked at on the proof's cemetery page:

- **Sod**: the block an island is made of. The cemetery's grass on top; at its sides, the grass's fringe, cut earth and
  roots hanging out of the bottom of it; underneath, dark earth and root ends.
- **Roots**, in three clumps, that hang under a piece of ground. They are in nobody's way.
- **The vine**: its own green, ribbed along its length, as a block and as a half block (for a vine that curls up and
  down by half blocks, which a bike rides without losing its way).
- **A coffin's wood**, and **a coffin's lid** as a half block: its boards, a pale cross let into it, a brass handle at
  each side.
- **A headstone laid flat** as a half block, for a step: its face up, a border cut round it, the lines of its name.
- **Old board fence** ("crooked fences"): upright, leaning to either side, and broken off short. A standing one is a
  fence where it stands, whatever it leans to, and as high to a bike as any fence; a broken one is a bump.

## What the gaps will be laid out from

Measured on November 12 by a probe case that was not kept (the table is in the proposal, under the Hollow): at top
speed on level ground a plain bike's tap of the hop carries it 2.3 blocks and a full hold 3.3; the Comet 3.4 and 4.8;
the Hopper 2.3 and 3.7, two blocks high. A bike is on the ground while any of its 0.6 of a block is.

So, as planned and not yet built: two-block gaps on the plain way (a tap clears them with room), a three-block gap
where a full hold is asked for, a gap of about five level blocks for the run "only the Comet's top speed carries", and
a ledge two blocks up for the "gap only the Hopper's jump clears".

## 0.4.494: the plain way

"Every bike has a way through." This is it: the way any bike takes, from the hill to the cemetery's far side. The five
ways the bikes' own perks open are not built.

**The hill's ground stops** on the row behind the small stones' own trees, ragged off the gate's line, and what is left
of the side railings ends in a pier on each side. From there north, for a hundred and sixty blocks, there is the
course and nothing else. **The cemetery's back railings and its back gate are on the far side of it now**, where its
ground begins again at the height of the hill's top; behind them is the lane.

In the order it is ridden (heights are where a body stands; the hill's top is 3):

**Every gap is two blocks but one, and most of them come down.** On the level a tap of the hop carries a plain bike
2.3 blocks and a held hop 3.3, and a bike is on the ground while any of its 0.6 of a block is. So on the level a tap
clears two blocks only if it is made in the last block before the brink, and a held hop clears three only from the
brink itself. The way was first laid out level, with the three wanting the hop held: the case that rides it passed
once, and the second time its rider came down a few tenths short of the lids and came off against the first of them.
That is too fine a thing to ask on the way every bike is meant to manage.

So the piece past the three is a block lower, and so are the pieces past four of the twos (a block, or half a block):
falling gives a hop time to carry. Three of the twos are level and are the harder kind. The height that loses is got
back by steps (the vine, the stairs), never by a jump. The lids' own gaps of one are ridden over at speed without a
hop.

## 0.4.495: somebody who misses

The proposal: "Missing a jump drops them onto the last island they stood on (a checkpoint), with a scare."

**Islands.** An island is the hill, the far side, or a run of the course's pieces that touch one another and is at
least five blocks long: long enough to get going on again. In the order they are ridden: the hill; the three graves;
the fences; the grave past the lids; the vine; the stairs with the top; the first half of the last stretch; its second
half; the far side. **The lids are not islands.** Each is two blocks long, and they are a bridge: somebody who goes
down from a lid goes back to the fences.

**Each island has one place** where somebody is set down on it: on its own line, eight blocks short of its far end
(the run they need to get going), or a block in from its near end where it is shorter than that. On the hill it is
on the gate's line between the small stones.

**Whose.** A reader's last island is their own, and is the hill until they have stood on another. On a bike it is the
island the bike is on the ground on. Whoever is on a Hopper's pegs has the island of the one pedalling.

**Missing.** Somebody has missed when they are two blocks under the lowest ground within six blocks of them along the
way, so nobody is taken out of a jump they might still land. They are set down at once on their island at its place,
pointing north, the way on, unhurt:

- riding, they are still on the bike, the bike stopped and upright, with whoever was on its pegs;
- on foot, they are on foot.

**The bike.** One let go of over nothing (its rider got off, or came off, in the air) is laid by its rider's island's
place, where they are about to be. It used to go back to the rack in the park, as it still does anywhere else. One
that lies in the Hollow anywhere but on its rider's island when they go down on foot (on a lid, on the next island) is
laid by them too: they could not have walked back to it. "By them" is the nearest free cell on whole ground beside
the place, or up to four rows behind it, and never where they stand.

**The scare** is theirs alone:

- the mist is over everything at once: as near as a block and a half, held for a little over a second, and gone again
  in a second. This is the one sudden change of the whole view in Bike night. It is as much of one as the reader's
  comfort setting lets through, and nothing at all with that at nothing;
- what is in the mist is heard: one new sound, subtitled "Something stirs in the mist" (39 cues);
- and a line: "You go down into the mist. Something in it has the back wheel, and lets go." On foot: "Something in it
  has your ankle, and lets go."

Anybody else sees mist where they went down and where they are.

**That there is mist under the Hollow, and something in it, is Claude's reading.** The proposal has the course "over
nothing", and has the Hollow's failure as "The screen goes to mist." Nothing is drawn down there.

## 0.4.496: the high way

The proposal: "high: a gap only the Hopper's jump clears, or the Rattler with its turbo". This is the first of the
five ways the bikes' own perks open.

**Where.** East of the line. From the second grave a ledge stands two blocks up across two blocks of nothing. A held
hop on the Hopper goes two blocks high; on any other bike, a block and a quarter. From the ledge the high way runs
level over the fences and the lids: three graves, the first two with two blocks of nothing between them, the third a
block lower across two more; a tap clears each. Then it comes down by half-block steps of laid stones to its foot,
which lies along the whole east side of the grave past the lids, level with it. Headstones stand along its east
edge, which leaves two blocks to ride on.

**What it is worth.** Whoever is up there does not weave the fences, does not hold a hop for the three, and does not
cross the lids. Its graves are islands like any others: somebody who goes down from it is set down on it.

**Not the Rattler.** The proposal gives the high gap to the Rattler's burst as well. The burst as built is speed and
not height: it takes the Rattler half as fast again and no higher. So the high way is the Hopper's alone, and the
Rattler's burst is for the way that wants speed, which is not built. If the owner wants the Rattler up here, its burst
would have to lift it.

**Whoever comes off a bike is beside it** (this changes every ridden bike, Borrasca's too). The game sets a passenger
down on top of what they were riding. The case's first run showed what that meant here: a Comet ridden flat out at the
ledge met its face and threw its rider, who was left at saddle height, above the ledge's top, a third of a block
short of it. A person could have stepped onto the high way from there, and the bike had been stood up on the ledge
too (a bike that comes to rest looks a block up for ground). Now whoever gets off, or comes off, is at the bike's own
level, a little to its right if there is room for them there, else to its left, else where it is. The Comet's rider
comes off under the ledge's top, goes down, and is set down on the second grave with the Comet laid by them.

**The foot was two rows long** when the case first rode it: the Hopper came down the steps fast, had two blocks in
which to turn onto the grave beside it, and went off the end. The foot is eight rows now, the length of that grave
and two more.

## 0.4.497: the long way

The proposal: "long: a run only the Comet's top speed carries".

**Where.** West of the vine. Its head lies against the west side of the grave past the lids, level with it. From
there a run of coffin lids, two wide and twenty-eight rows long, goes straight and level to where the stairs begin,
past everything the vine does: up three blocks, two blocks east, down again. Past the run, three rows of steps up to
its foot, which is against the stairs and level with their second pair. Nothing of it is within a block of the vine
or of the gap between the vine and the stairs, so it is no way round anything on the plain way.

**The run gives way.** It is whole and still until somebody is on it (a bike on the ground there, or somebody on
their feet). Six ticks later it begins to go from its near end, and goes at 0.4 of a row a tick. A bike is pedalled
at 0.32 and no faster; the Comet at 0.46; the Rattler goes 0.48 while its card has it. So the Comet stays ahead of it,
the Rattler does if its rider lets the card take it from the first row, and any other bike has the rows go from under
it partway along (by these numbers about a third of the way; the case asks only that it is between the fourth
row and the twenty-second). All of the run goes, whoever is or is not still on it. Five seconds after the last
row it is whole again. Somebody who goes down with it is set down on the grave past the lids: its head is too short
to be an island, and lids never are.

**It is the Rattler's way as well as the Comet's.** The proposal pairs the Rattler with the high gap; its burst is
speed (0.4.496).

**The course is shared**, so the run is: it goes for everybody at once. Two Comets together both get over. Somebody
who arrives while it is going, or gone, waits for it or takes the vine.

## 0.4.504: the steep way

The proposal: "**steep:** a climb only the Hare gets going up in time."

**Where.** East of the stairs (`CalderHollow.STEEP`). A turn off the stairs' first pair, level with it; a run north
beside the stairs, at the height of their foot; four rows of loose earth; a bank of it, three whole blocks up in
three rows; and a head beside the last stretch's first piece, level with it. The stairs take twelve rows to climb
what the bank climbs in three, and the steep way misses the top, where a witch crosses.

**Loose earth** is its own ground: dark, wet, all clods (`calder_hollow_earth`, drawn by
`tools/draw_calder_graves_art.py`). Two things are true of a bike on it (`NightBike.LOOSE`):

- **A wheel sinks.** A bike on loose earth keeps four fifths of its way each tick, beside what any ground takes. It
  is ridden at a crawl however fast it was come onto: the Comet flat out has lost what it came with inside three
  blocks.
- **A wheel digs in at a step.** On firm ground a bike with no way on mounts a block and stops on it: a climb is
  paid for after it is made. On loose earth a bike mounts a whole block only with the way on to pay for it, and is
  otherwise held at the step's foot, keeping what way it has (it is not a wall, and takes nothing more off it).

So the bank is decided by the make and not by the hands. A step wants about .22 of a block a tick of an ordinary
bike. Pushed ten times a second, an ordinary bike on loose earth is going .16 at its fastest; it would take fourteen
pushes a second to be up. The Hare is driven nearly twice as hard and a climb costs it a third: its step wants .13,
and a push every fifth tick, four a second, has it up. Any slower and it has stopped between pushes.

**"In time."** The bank does not wait (`BikeNightHollow.bank`). Eighty ticks after somebody is first on it (a bike
whose middle is over one of its rows, on the ground; or somebody on their feet), all of it slides away at once,
whoever is still on it, and they hear it go. Whoever is on it goes down and is set back on the run under it, as
anybody who misses is. A hundred ticks later it is there again, when nobody is where it was. At the slowest the Hare
can be pedalled up it at all, it is on the bank for about sixty of the eighty.

**The Rattler.** While its card has it, and pedalled as well, the Rattler is up the bank too. The proposal says the
Hare only. I have left it: the high way was to have been the Rattler's as well, and as built is not.

The bank is no island: the run and the loose earth under it are one, and the head is another. The steep way goes in
a reader's record with the others ("steep").

One sound (46 cues).

## 0.4.505: the tight way

The proposal: "**tight:** a twisting path along the vine only the Wasp turns sharply enough for."

**Where.** East of the vine (`CalderHollow.TIGHT`), from the high way's foot to the foot of the stairs, where it
runs onto the steep way's turn. A tendril of the vine, twenty-six rows, five blocks across between walls of vine
three blocks high. Every sixth row from the sixth it is crossed by a wall with an opening one block wide: at its
west side, then its east, then its west, then its east. So the way through swings four blocks across in six along,
four times. Nothing gets over a wall three blocks high.

**It curls up** from its near end behind whoever is in it (`BikeNightHollow.curl`), as the long way's run gives
way: ten ticks after somebody is first on it, and then at .21 of a row a tick, floor, walls and gates. All of it
goes, whoever is still in it, and they go down and are set back as anybody who misses is. A hundred ticks after its
last row it is whole again, when nobody is where it was.

**Why the Wasp.** Nothing is asked for but the bikes as they already are. At a bike's top speed the Wasp turns in
under two blocks and an ordinary bike in three; the line through the gates wants about two. An ordinary bike can be
turned through a gate only below about .19 of a block a tick, and the tendril is gone from under it at that. Going
faster, it meets the second gate's wall square, and its rider comes off as off any wall.

### The case

One (93 in the namespace), three readers, each ridden by the same hands: along the line through the gates, looking
half a row ahead, a push every third tick.

- The way is nothing else's ground; it runs on from the high way's foot and onto the steep way's turn, level with
  each; its walls and its four gates are where they are said to be; none of its rows is anybody's island.
- **A plain bike, pedalled as hard as it goes**, cannot be turned into the second gate: its rider comes off at the

- **The same, going no faster than .18**: goes down with the tendril before the third gate.
- **The Comet, flat out**, is not through it either.
- **The Wasp** is through every gate and on the tight way's foot, in less time than the tendril takes to go, and

- **The tendril itself**: somebody on foot in its first row sets it going; it goes at its pace after its grace,

### Found on the way, and what changed

- The hands matter. Looking a whole row ahead instead of half a row, the same hands put the Wasp into the second

## 0.4.506: the dark way

The proposal: "**dark:** only the Lamp shows where the ground is." And of the Lamp: "It shows the path in the mist
and the ground in the dark", and "The Lamp's light helps whoever rides near it."

**Where.** West of the first two graves (`CalderHollow.DARK`): three islands of sod from the hill's edge to the
third grave. Each is five rows long and stands three rows of nothing on from the last, a block lower than the hill.
Each is set across from the last with one block of its width in line: the second is east of the first, the third
east of the second, and the third grave is two blocks off the third's east end. There is no one line through all
three, and straight on from the middle of an island is nothing. The hill ends square at the dark way's mouth, so the
first gap is the same from wherever along it somebody leaves the hill.

**The bank.** All of it, and the hill's edge at its mouth, lies in a bank of mist (`CalderHollow.dark`,
`BikeNightGraveyard.DARK_MIST`): a hundred and forty of the cemetery's hundred. In the cemetery's thickest somebody
sees seven blocks; in the bank, two and a half, which is less than any of its gaps. It is each reader's own, as the
cemetery's mist is. What is over a hundred of it comes and goes in under a second: a bank has an edge.

**The Lamp.** A Lamp that is throwing its light cuts the bank to under half for its rider and for whoever is within
seven blocks, as it does the cemetery's: they see about eighteen blocks, which is the next island from the last one.
Its dynamo makes light only while the wheel turns. A Lamp that stops cuts nothing, and the bank closes in again in
under a second.

It misses the first gap the werewolf leaps, the goblin behind the cross, and the ghosts of the first two graves.

### The case

One (94 in the namespace), three readers.

### Not built, not seen, and left with the owner

- **Nobody has seen it.** What two blocks and a half of sight is like from a saddle, whether eighteen is enough to

- It can be learned. The islands are where they are every night, and somebody who has gone down off each of them

- Somebody who goes down in the bank has the mist over them as anybody who misses does, on top of the bank.
