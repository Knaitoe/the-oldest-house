# The household: mundane vignettes from the staircase notes (proposal, 1 November 2026)

## The owner's hope

The owner, 1 November: "is it noted anywhere, my hopes for the notes on the grand staircase to lead to vignettes of their own?
Let's design some mundane vignettes for the staircase notes."

It was not noted. The staircase holds 64 loose sheets (`StaircaseNotes.TEXTS`). Five of them "leak" into short pocket rooms with
one chore each (docs/STAIRCASE_LEAKS_DESIGN.md, 0.4.49), and that design says "A leak is a rare thing, not the point of the stairs."
This proposal records the owner's wish as a goal: the sheets lead to vignettes of their own.

Nothing here is built yet.

## What the sheets already are

Read in order, the 64 sheets are one household's life, written by its members in turn: Ruth, E. Marsh, Mara, A. Bell, Jonah and
Sabine. Each sheet is signed by its "I".
- **0 to 47: the house and the car.** Washing up, washing, mending, Sunday lunch, beds, drives home, lay-bys, petrol
  stations.
- **48 to 63: the stairs.** They share an orange on a landing, eat from tins, string a washing line between two brackets, cut the
  last candle in half, count the cups and spoons and check the lamp before they move on. "We were still together."

So the household went down the great staircase before the reader did, and the sheets are what they left on the treads.

The vignettes below let the reader live a day of theirs. They are mundane on purpose:
- nothing attacks and nothing hurts;
- the people are never seen (a coat on a hook, a chair still warm, a voice from the next room), as in the leaks;
- the unease is that it is all so ordinary, behind a door in the side of a 1,280-block shaft.

## How a sheet leads to its vignette

- **Every vignette has three to five source sheets.** Reading one of them to its last line on the staircase leaves a door in the
  shaft wall beside that tread: an ordinary painted house door with a brass knob, set into the stone where there was no door
  before.
- **The door is the reader's.** Others see it, but it is locked to them ("It isn't yours"). A party member can follow the reader
  through.
- **Through it is a small whole scene.** It is built once in a pocket slot, like the leak rooms, and reached by the same fade and
  seam. Leaving by its door, or finishing, brings the reader back to the same tread, facing and phase.
- **Each source sheet opens its vignette once.** A vignette with four source sheets can be tried four times. A failure stands in
  the account until a later success.
- **Followers wait at the tread.**
- **The five leaks stay as they are.** Their sheets are not source sheets for the vignettes.

## Every vignette has a success and a failure

The failures are mundane, but they are real: they are recorded, the household remembers them, and the last landing counts them.

Nothing is gained or lost from the reader's inventory, apart from the plain food that is eaten where it is served.

### 1. Sunday lunch

**Sheets:** The good cups (11), The towel rail (8), The table (17), Sunday lunch (23).

**Place.** A dining room with a serving hatch to the kitchen. The table seats six, and there is a seventh chair against the wall.

**Play.**
- Lay the table for the household:
  - forks first;
  - the handles must match, because Ruth will change two that don't;
  - the good cups out, with the cracked one taken to the flowers on the sill;
  - and a place for the visitor, from the towel rail sheet, who never comes.
- Bowls come through the hatch.
- Sit at the reader's place. Chairs scrape out around the table, unseen, and the household talks on the action bar: the salt, the
  weather, somebody's knee.
- Eat a slice of bread from the board.

**Success.** Sit through the meal until the chairs push back.

**Failure.** Get up while others are still eating. The talk stops and the reader's plate stays. On later vignettes the household
does not lay a place for them.

**Co-op.** Lay the table together. Each reader gets a place, and the household sets one fewer chair against the wall.

### 2. Fresh sheets

**Sheets:** The airing cupboard (15), Fresh sheets (27), The basket (33), Lost and found (45).

**Place.** The upstairs bedroom, the landing and the airing cupboard.

**Play.**
- Make the bed.
- A sheet takes two to fold. When a lone reader takes one end, the other end lifts by itself and folds toward them.
- The striped pillowcase is missing. It turns up on the laundry basket the moment nobody is looking at the basket.
- A sock is caught inside the fitted sheet.

**Success.** The bed made, with the striped pillowcase, and the sock put on the basket for its partner.

**Failure.** Fold a sheet alone without waiting for the other end. The crease stays, and the household notices.

**Co-op.** Two readers fold together, and the other end never lifts on its own.

### 3. The long way home

**Sheets:** On the way home (2), At the lights (10), The turn (13), The bend by the school (43), The handbrake (46). The
passenger seat (6) stays a leak; its radio and pear tree return here.

**Place.** The passenger seat of a small car, driven through a short loop of streets at dusk, built on the Backseat vignette's
moving car. The driver's seat holds an unseen driver: its belt is fastened and the wheel turns.

**Play.**
- Things to do on the way:
  - hold the bread between your knees, and it taps the door at every turn;
  - turn the radio down to hear which house used to have the pear tree;
  - wipe a clear patch at the lights, and a boy teaches his sister to wait for the green man;
  - lean into the bend by the school, and the car leans with you the second time.
- At the junction the reader chooses. The wrong way ends at a field gate with sheep, and nobody minds: there is plenty of road.

**Success.** Home, and the handbrake pulled up twice.

**Failure.** Get out before home. The car drives on with its passenger door open, and is parked outside the front room's window
in every later vignette, as leak 3 already shows it.

**Co-op.** A second reader rides in the back. They can wake the sleeper in the back seat, or let her rest.

### 4. Mending day

**Sheets:** Thursday (4), The hem (18), Mending day (30), The small tin (42), The window latch (47).

**Place.** The front room by the lamp, with the button tin on the arm of the chair.

**Play.**
- Thread the needle. The lamp held close makes a shadow over it, so it has to be moved aside.
- Turn the sleeve inside out before stitching it.
- Choose a button from the biscuit tin and sew it onto the blue coat.

**Success.** The sleeve sewn right side in, and the button on.

**Failure.** Stitch the sleeve without turning it, and it is sewn shut.
- Ruth's voice unpicks it, slowly, without making the reader feel foolish, and they can try once more.
- Leaving with the coat unfinished is the failure that stands.

### 5. First light

**Sheets:** Breakfast (3), For tomorrow (7), The sink (14), The loaf (29), The saucepan (38). The cup set beside a sleeper is
from At first light (49), which belongs to the stairs.

**Place.** The kitchen before dawn. Someone is asleep on the settee in the next room: a blanket rises and falls.

**Play.**
- Boil the kettle twice: the first cup is for warming hands, the second goes beside the sleeper.
- Peel one extra potato, wrap Bell's plate and write his name on the paper.
- Be quiet. Dropped pans, a slammed cupboard and running are loud, as in the stillness, but here the cost is only waking
  someone.

**Success.** The cup set down beside the sleeper and the plate wrapped, without waking them.

**Failure.** Wake the sleeper. The blanket is thrown back, a voice asks what time it is, and the reader has to say.

### 6. Washing day

**Sheets:** After the rain (5), The blanket (21), A stubborn stain (36), The clothes horse (39).

**Place.** The back yard with its washing line, the one seen through the kitchen door in the leaks.

**Play.**
- Hang the shirts, by their shoulders or by the hem; the wind settles the argument.
- Shake the blanket at the back door with someone, and crumbs fall into the grass. The sparrows come.
- Then rain begins to come in over the roofs, and everything has to be brought in before it is soaked.

**Success.** Everything in, dry, and the one damp sleeve folded over the clothes horse.

**Failure.** The rain gets it, and the washing hangs soaked.

**Co-op.** Two readers race the rain together.

### 7. The lay-by

**Sheets:** A longer way (19), The queue (22), The petrol station (28), The lay-by (37).

**Place.** A lay-by off a country road, with the car pulled in and two coffees steaming on the bonnet in the drizzle.

**Play.**
- Eat the sandwiches and fold the wrapping into a small square.
- Count three tractors as they pass on the road.
- Bell is asleep in the car with his head against the glass; the window fogs in the shape of a head.

**Success.** Three tractors counted, the wrapper folded, and back in the car without waking him.

**Failure.** The horn. He wakes, and the account says so.

### 8. The last landing

**Sheets:** the stair sheets, 48 to 63.

**Place.** This one is not behind a door. It is a real landing deep in the great staircase's shaft, built with the staircase in new
worlds: a recess off the treads with the household's camp.
- cups, a kettle, a lamp, three tins;
- a blanket roll;
- a washing line between two brackets with socks on it and a space in the middle;
- a coat spread on the seat by the wall.

**Play.** It opens once the reader has done three of the vignettes above, whether they succeeded or failed.
- Put everything back in the same order: count the cups, count the spoons, check that the lamp will light again.
- Cut the last candle in half with the warm knife, and put one half in the tin.
- Read the morning list: water, food, dry socks, and the names of the people carrying them. The reader's own name is the last
  one on it, in their own hand.

**Success.** The camp is put in order and the list read.
- The next time the reader comes down past that landing, the camp is packed and gone.
- A single cup is left on the step below, dried against a sleeve.
- The household's Witness story resolves.

**Failure.** Keep anything from the camp, even a cup, and the household cannot move on.
- Their things wait on the landing, still out, every time the reader passes.
- The account ends on what was kept.

**The tally.** The camp also counts the vignettes:
- every place the household laid for the reader at lunch;
- the bed they made;
- the sleeper they did not wake;
- the washing they saved.

The account is built from that tally.

## Witness

The household is one personal story, understanding: "The household", resolved at the last landing. The quota follows the pool.

## Literature

- Each vignette's account is a new sheet in the household's voice, written by whichever of them was not in the scene, about the
  reader as if they had always been one of them. It is added to the reader's own staircase originals and their House of Leaves.
- The last landing's account is the morning list, then the sheet "Before we moved on" with one line added at the end in the
  reader's own hand.

House writing never names the reader's gender, colour or age.

## Multiplayer

- Doors belong to their reader, and party members can follow.
- The chores that take two are better with two; the unseen hand only helps a reader who is alone.
- Each reader's tally and story are their own.

## Assets

Built whole with custom textures, drawn by a generator with `--check`, as the leak rooms were, and sharing their household's
layout so the house can be pieced together:
- the dining room;
- the bedroom and airing cupboard;
- the car loop;
- the front room's lamp corner;
- the dawn kitchen;
- the yard;
- the lay-by;
- the landing camp.
