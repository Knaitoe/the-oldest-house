# The hotel grounds, under Lumpy Ridge (0.4.285)

The owner: "Consider actual regional landscaping and architecture." The hotel is the Stanley's, in Estes Park, Colorado. It stands under
Lumpy Ridge in the Rockies, among:

- granite domes and boulders;
- ponderosa pine on the open slopes;
- blue spruce;
- aspen in the draws, bare in winter.

The hotel itself already reads as the Stanley: a white building under a red roof. The grounds behind it did not:

- a regular three-step snow bank at each side;
- nothing beyond the garden.

The blizzard lets a reader see about six blocks. So this pass puts everything close to where a reader walks; a far ridge would never be
seen. Nothing in the story moves. New builds only.

## The grounds (`StanleyGrounds`, called from the grounds builder)

- **The side banks** drift up unevenly from six blocks in from each side instead of rising in regular steps. Pink-grey Lumpy Ridge
  granite breaks through them here and there, with snow lying on top.
- **Ponderosa pine and blue spruce** stand along both sides of the garden, with snow on their boughs. A ponderosa is:
  - a straight trunk of reddish, plated bark, bare for most of its height;
  - a high, open crown of needle tufts;
  - a limb or two below the crown, each ending in a tuft.
- **A clump of bare aspen** stands in the draw behind the groundskeeper's shed.
