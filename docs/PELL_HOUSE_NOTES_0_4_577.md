# The Pell house, the owner's playtest notes: the furniture (0.4.577)

From the owner's notes on playing the Pell house on 13 November (the earlier batches: `docs/PELL_HOUSE_NOTES_0_4_566.md` to
`docs/PELL_HOUSE_NOTES_0_4_575.md`). New builds only. Pictures and models only: no line, placement, protocol, layout or Witness
change, and nothing a reader does in the house is different.

The owner: "the whole pell house is too uniform in its walls and carpets, and the kitchen isn't spaced believably. all of the
textures could be improved."

The kitchen (0.4.572), the walls and floors (0.4.573), the front beds (0.4.574) and June's room (0.4.575) answered the rest of
that note. This is the last sentence of it, for the furniture: what 0.4.575 left as "the furniture's own textures in the other
rooms, where the frames show them flat".

## What was there

Thirty-two frames from eye level, room by room, before anything was changed.

- **Most furniture was a box with a picture on its front.** The kitchen benches, the wall cupboards, the fridge, Nessa's chest
  of drawers, her wardrobe, her bookcase and the bathroom vanity each had their doors, drawers and handles drawn on one flat
  face. Side on, or from across the room, they read as blocks.
- **Nessa's bed was a slab** with a quilt on it and a plain board at its head. The bath was a slab with the inside of a bath
  drawn on its top.
- **Five things did not show what the house says about them** when a reader puts a hand on them:

| Thing | The house says | What was drawn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The toilet | "The toilet. Its lid is down." | The lid up, the bowl showing |
| The washing machine | "The washing machine. Its lid is up, airing." | The lid shut |
| The bookcase | "One is out, lying on top, a bus ticket in it." | A gap on the shelf, and nothing on top |
| The standard lamp | "A standard lamp, its shade tipped toward the chair." | The shade straight on its pole |
| A kitchen chair | "A vinyl chair. One seam has split and been taped." | A whole seat |

- **The bath's rack and its toy** ("On the rack, a bath toy, faded on one side.") were a few pixels on the picture of the
  bath's inside.
- **The washing machine had its dials twice:** on the panel behind the lid, and again on the front of the cabinet.
- **The wall cupboards wore the benches' end panels,** so each had a dark kick along the foot of its ends, high on the wall.

## What changed

Doors, drawers and lids are now separate pieces that stand a little proud of what they belong to, with a shadow in the gap
between them and a real handle on each. Where a line says something about a thing, the thing shows it.

### The kitchen

- **The benches** (the plain bench, and the ones under the sink, the lunchboxes and the knife block): the kick is set back at
  the foot, two doors stand proud of the carcass with a gap between them, and each has a chrome pull across its top.
- **The fridge:** two doors, upper and lower, with a seam between them and a chrome handle on each next to the seam; a kick
  under the lower door. The photographs and magnets are where they were.
- **The wall cupboards:** two doors each, with a chrome knob at the foot of each beside the gap. Their ends and undersides are
  plain boards.
- **The lunchboxes:** a metal catch on the front of each.
- **The chairs:** the seam along the front of the seat is split for most of its length, with two pieces of tape across the
  split.

### The living room

- **The standard lamp:** its shade is tipped, the open foot of it toward the recliner. Lit, it is tipped the same way.
  It was tipped when the house was first furnished. 0.4.566 mended the picture on it, which the owner had called malformed,
  and stood it straight on the pole at the same time; the line was left saying it was tipped. It is tipped again, and the
  picture on it is the mended one: three bands, each with its own rows of the pleated cloth.

### Nessa's room

- **The bed** stands on legs. At its head are two posts with a rail across their tops and three painted boards between them;
  at its foot is a low board. The quilt stops against both.
- **The chest of drawers:** three drawer fronts, each proud of the chest with a gap above and below, each with a wooden knob
  and its label of raised dots; the top overhangs.
- **The desk:** a rail between its back legs, and a drawer under its top with a knob.
- **The bookcase** is a case: sides, a top, a foot and a shelf across the middle, with the back in shadow. The books stand back
  from the front of the shelves. The gap is where it was. The book that is out lies on top, with a bus ticket standing out of
  its pages.
- **The wardrobe:** two doors proud of its case, over a plinth and under a cornice, a wooden knob on each. Open, both doors
  stand out from it on their hinges. One did before.
- **The veneer** the chest, the desk, the bookcase and the wardrobe are made of has a grain with figure in it. The bed is
  painted.

### Callum's room

- **The drawer of cutlery** takes the new veneer on its front. Nothing else in the room changed.

### The bathroom

- **The bath** is a tub: walls and an end round a floor, with the non-slip mat on the floor and the taps on the rim at one
  end. Across the far end is the rack, and on the rack the bath toy, a duck. The side of it toward the window is faded.
- **The toilet:** a pan on a pedestal, the seat and lid down on it with the two caps of the hinge, and the cistern behind with
  its own lid and a button.
- **The vanity:** its door stands proud over a kick, with a knob. Beside the knob is a chrome rail on two brackets with a hand
  towel over it. The towel had been part of the picture of the door.

### The laundry

- **The washing machine:** its lid stands up against the panel behind it, underside out. Under it is the mouth of the tub, the
  steel ring and the agitator. The dials are on the panel only. The cabinet's front has a maker's plate, vents and a kick.

### Not changed

Everything else is as it was: the television cabinet, the recliner, the side table, the mantel and the window seat; the
kitchen table, the stove and the sink's bowl; the mirror; the freezer and the trough; Callum's mattress and toys; everything
in June's room, the hall, the garage, the shed and the yard; every picture on a wall. The frames showed these with shape and
detail already.

No thing was moved, none stops a body differently, and none says anything it did not say before.

## What the frames show now

The same thirty-two frames, and twenty-one close ones. Four of the close ones are of things as the first visit does not
show them: the wardrobe open, a wall cupboard open and the lamp lit.

- **The kitchen from the arch:** the run of benches reads as cupboards with doors and pulls, the fridge as a fridge with two
  doors, and the wall cupboards as a row of doors with knobs. From their end, the wall cupboards are boards from top to foot.
- **The chairs from above:** on each seat a dark split along the front seam with two pale pieces of tape across it.
- **The lamp from the room and from beside it:** the shade leans, its open foot toward the recliner.
- **Nessa's room from the door:** the bed's head stands above the pillow with its three boards; the bookcase has depth, and
  the book on top of it shows from across the room with the ticket in it.
- **The drawers close up:** three fronts, three knobs, three labels of dots.
- **The wardrobe:** two doors, a line of shadow between them, two knobs. Open, a door stands out to each side of the dark
  inside of it, with the rail, the hangers and the cane.
- **The bath from above:** a rim all the way round, the mat down the floor of it, the taps at one end, the rack and the duck at
  the other. From the door the duck is all one bright colour. From the end of the bath by the wall, half of it is pale.
- **The toilet:** the lid is down.
- **The vanity from the bath:** the towel hangs clear of the door.
- **The washing machine:** the lid is up, and the tub shows under it.

The frames show shapes and pictures, not the game's light. The tool that takes them draws each block as the game draws one
held in the hand, lit from the side, so the tops of things come out darker in them than their sides. In the game a top is the
brightest face.

## What the frames found

- **The chair's tape took three tries.** A patch in the middle of the seat read as a label. One strip laid along the seam read
  as a plate let into the seat. Two short pieces across a split that shows between them and past them read as tape.
- **The wall cupboards' dark feet** showed only in a frame taken from below and to one side. They had been there since the
  kitchen was first furnished.
- **The lunchboxes' catches** were first drawn in the white of the fridge and read as buttons. They are metal now, and
  flatter.

## Caught while drawing

- **The washing machine's dials** were on the cabinet's front as well as on the panel. With the lid stood up between them the
  same dials would have shown above it and below it.
- **The duck's faded side** was first the side toward the room. It is toward the window, where the light would fade it: a
  reader sees the bright side from the door, and the faded one by looking along the wall from the end of the bath.
- **The bed's quilt** would have lain in the same plane as the new posts and the foot board, which flickers in the game. It
  stops short of both.
- **A reader's feet in the bath** stand a pixel above the mat. The bath stops a body as the slab did, a pixel below the new
  rim. It was left, so that nothing a reader can do changed.
- **The bed's foot board** stands two pixels higher than what stops a body there, for the same reason.

## For the owner

Nothing here is new story. Four things are choices of mine:

- **The bath toy is a duck.** The line says "a bath toy".
- **The book lying on the bookcase** has a dark cover with a pale label. The line does not say what it is.
- **The tape on the chairs is on all four.** Every chair says the same line, and they are one model.
- **The lamp's shade is tipped again.** If the straight shade of 0.4.566 is the one to keep, the line should lose "its shade
  tipped toward the chair" instead. Say which.
