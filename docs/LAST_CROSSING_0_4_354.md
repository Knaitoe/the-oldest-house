## The turn

At the second dusk Vera says the light's going and sets off over the spillway to the cable house. Two seconds later the Night One carrier turns where they stand. It takes three seconds:

- a long wet breath that catches and cracks, heard by everyone near;
- the second breath coming up under theirs, heard only by them;
- water running off them.

Anyone with them in view during those three seconds knows whose face it was: "*Name* doubles over where they stand. When the head comes up the skin is slick and running wet, the jaw sits wrong, and the face is still *Name*'s." A carrier who turned with nobody watching has that in their record, and so does where they were when it happened.

A carrier away from the station at dusk turns when they come back to it. A carrier who died here doesn't turn.

At the station a turned reader is drawn in their own body gone drowned:

- the skin dulled toward the river's murk;
- the eyes filmed over;
- three gill cuts down each side of the neck.

Whoever sees them still knows who it is. A reader whose skin this client can't read wears the Undertow's skin instead.

## The roll

Vera calls the roll from the cable house's doorway. She opens the door on the way in unless it's barred. Each name is called, then called again if nobody answers, as on the first evening. A reader only hears their name if:

- they're near enough;
- they aren't below ground (the intake, the pit, the passage, the branches);
- they aren't shut in behind a door of a building she isn't in.

A reader who doesn't answer is noted absent ("Nothing. Somebody find them, and don't go looking on your own."). Everyone who heard the name go unanswered remembers whose it was. The turned can answer, though the answer comes out broken.

## The pull

Once the roll is done (or fourteen seconds before the night is half over), Vera calls the pull from inside the cable house. If the drum was lined up on the marks by day, the pull takes a minute of hauling. If it wasn't, it takes a minute and a half, and Vera says so. The catch comes out of the drum's teeth for it.

- **The brake.** Someone takes the brake off and holds it: they stand within reach of it, and the drum takes up the cable a notch at a time.
- **Binding.** It binds on a loose roller until the roller is seated. Halfway through it binds at the jammed fairlead until the jam is cleared. From above, the jam takes eight levers of the marlinspike out of the cable tools. From the flooded passage under the fairlead, one shove frees it, and only a turned reader can breathe down there. Those in the cable house see the cable jump back into the fairlead with nobody at it.
- **Running back.** Let go, the drum runs back over the rollers. Someone who gets to the brake within four seconds catches it. Otherwise it throws two rollers off their cradles and the pull has failed; they wait for daylight to be reset.
- **Taken up.** Hauled to the end, the cable is taken up and the catch drops back in for the morning. Everyone who held the brake remembers, and so does everyone who saw it in the cable house.

A pull not finished by dawn hasn't been done.

## The things from the water

From twenty-five seconds in they come up as they did on Night One: over the spillway's banks, as many as the crew, going for whoever is nearest. Branches left open by day bring them in other ways:

- every other one comes up through the floor grate behind the brake if the winch's branch is open and nobody has shut that grate, a way in below the barred door;
- some come up through the grate into the pit if the ramp's branch is open.

Readers who see one come up a grate remember it. One in the cable house a few seconds knocks a seated roller off again. They pass a carrier by.

## The turned reader

- **Water.** They breathe water, and under it they can see.
- **The hatch.** They can go down through the cable house's hatch into the flooded passage under the rollers, and come back up it. To anyone else the hatch only lifts an inch on black water.
- **Wet grates.** They fold through a wet grate into its branch, at either end of the ramp's branch.
- **The floor grate.** Crouching on it while the winch's branch runs, they go down into the spur, and they come back up through it from below.
- **Prints.** Wherever they come up onto a dry floor (by the hatch, by the floor grate, in the pit) they leave wet prints. Whoever sees the prints remembers them. At dawn they dry to a tidemark and stay.
- **Being seen.** Readers who see them go down or come up remember whose face it was.

## Claws

A turned reader's bare hand is a claw. It hits a reader for two hearts and one of the things for three, with the slasher's splatter and a raking sound. A reader it hits has their clothes torn: a rent across the right shoulder and chest with a line of blood in it, drawn over whatever they wear. The tear stays through dawn. Everyone who saw the claw land remembers whose it was. A bandage for it is Day Three's.

## The call after dark

A turned reader crouched for three seconds anywhere calls. Up to two of the things within thirty-two blocks turn toward where they crouch and take nobody on the way for twelve seconds. The scrape is heard through the pipes by everyone near.

Called from under the water, the things go down after the breath into the branch instead. After five seconds they come up through the floor grate behind the brake, one at a time:

- if the grate is held or pinned shut, they only knock under the iron, and whoever is near the grate feels it;
- if the winch's branch is shut, they're gone.

Whoever shut the grate over them shut them in.

A turned reader can call once every forty-five seconds. Within four blocks of the plate the call catches in the throat.

## Speech after dark

Everything a turned reader says comes out broken: in chat, over their head and in their own conversation panel. They see the same broken line everyone else does, with their name still on it.

- **Night Two:** a rasp breaks one longer word in its throat ("r—amp"). Another word catches on its first sound ("p-pin"). A longer line has a hiss in it, between sentences if there are two. A line of nothing but names or directions still catches on its first sound: "H-here."
- **Choked:** near the plate, or holding a live grate shut, a line falls to a few audible words. The small words go first, long pauses stand where they were, and there are more hisses.

Names (the crew's, Vera's, Ellis's, the ferry's), directions and negations are never broken or dropped. Whoever hears a turned reader speak remembers the rasp. Daylight speech is normal.

## The grate that chokes

At night, with the winch's branch running, the floor grate behind the brake can be held shut by someone standing on it. A second pair of hands drives a pin through its bars, and then it holds by itself until someone works the pin out.

A turned reader holding a live grate shut feels their throat close: a warning after a second and a half, then a heart lost every second, never below one heart. It eases if they step off, if the branch is shut, or if a friend pins the grate and takes the load off them. The plate does the same to a turned reader within four blocks of it, every two seconds and never below two hearts, with a shortened breath at the edge of its reach first. They take the stronger of the two when both apply.

## The grapple and the second slot

From the design: *"In a full six-reader crew, a successful infection grapple can fill the second slot."*

One of the things that reaches a clean reader of the crew with nobody else of the crew within six blocks gets its arms round them and holds them where they stand. While it holds them it doesn't hurt them. Seen, it's remembered. It lets go when:

- a friend hits it;
- they hit it themselves three times;
- someone takes their hand, which in the cable house hauls them up onto the dry ledge.

Held for five seconds, it lets go and goes back into the water:

- in a crew of six with all six still standing at the station, if the second slot is free, it fills the second slot. That reader is told alone that they'll change when the last light goes, and hears the second breath now and then. They stay themselves through tonight and Day Three, and the things pass them by from now on.
- otherwise it leaves a deep cut.

Once filled, the second slot stays filled.

## Dawn

At first light:

- the things go back into the water;
- the turned come back to themselves where they stand, soaked;
- a pull not taken up has failed;
- the prints dry to a tidemark, and the pin stays in the grate;
- tears stay torn.

Vera says "That's the light. Who's here? Come in where I can see you." and walks back to her porch. Everyone on the roll who is there has worked Night Two, and the station holds in the morning light for Day Three.
