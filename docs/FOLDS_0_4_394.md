# Folds (0.4.394)

R3 of [HALLS_TRIALS_AND_TIME_DESIGN.md](HALLS_TRIALS_AND_TIME_DESIGN.md), in the form the owner set on November 8, built when they asked for "the rest of the rules".

- The first proposal was a chance on each crossing. The owner: "R3 seems to undo a lot of the work we've done in creating a steady labyrinth the player can actually navigate - unless the door loops back around every time, and thus can be accounted for."
- Then: "For R3, looping doors need to work for every player. They should teleport every player to the same general location, within a few hallways of each other. Or transfer all to the same spot. But it can't teleport one player while letting the others progress. That could break group play."

## The rule

- **A fold is a door, not a chance.** From depth 12, about one ordinary door in seven of an ordinary hall is a fold, and never more than one in a hall.
- **Where it goes.** It opens back onto a hall already passed on the same route, two to four crossings back.
  - The reader comes out of the door they once took out of that hall, facing into it, under the same fade as other forced moves. The door is heard shutting behind them.
  - Their depth drops to match, as if they had walked back through every door between.
- **It is the same for everyone.**
  - Which door folds, and how far, is decided once for the hall as reached by its route, and kept with the shared halls.
  - Anyone who comes the same way finds the same fold, whatever their own deals there.
  - Taking it again lands where it did before, so it can be learned, drawn and chalked like any door.
- **Friends go through together.**
  - Whoever follows a friend through a fold within thirty seconds lands where the friend did and drops as many crossings, even if the door would not fold for them alone.
  - Whoever follows a friend who went on through a door goes on too. A fold never takes back one reader of a group while the rest go on.
- **Where it never is.**
  - Never in a hall entered straight from a story.
  - Never through a story's, a trial's or the staircase's door. Only a door that would lead to an ordinary hall folds.
  - Never the hall's only way on, and never a door a wedge holds.
  - Never a fold that would land in a story, a trial or a loop, cross a crossing held by the quiet rooms, or land back in the same hall.
- **The first fold** a reader goes through is marked with one grey line: *This hall again, by the door it was left by.* Later folds say nothing.

## Code

- `Folds`:
  - `FOLD_DEPTH` (12), `FOLD_IN` (7), `MIN_BACK` (2), `MAX_BACK` (4).
  - `decide` keeps the hall's fold in the shared state `folds_0394`, keyed like the shared halls by the hall's route key. The first participant to use one of its doors decides; spectators only read. Nothing is kept for a hall shallower than the folds.
  - `landing` is the door the reader came out of `back` crossings ago, checked against their own way back.
  - `plan` and `record` handle friends within `WINDOW` (GroupCrossings' 600 ticks).
  - `landed` pops the folded crossings and calls `LabyrinthDealer.arriveAt` for the hall landed in, so its doors keep what they led to on that route.
- `LabyrinthDoors.use` asks `Folds.use` after the group crossing check. `LabyrinthDoors.fold` makes the move under the fade. Companions following the reader come too, as with any fade.
- `LabyrinthData.returns` gives a reader's way back, latest first.

## Tests

New case: `ExplorationPassTests.foldsAreDoorsKeptForEveryoneAndFriendsGoThroughThemTogether`.

- Four hundred routes twelve-plus crossings deep through real doors of ordinary halls:
  - between one door in twenty and one in five folds, never two in a hall;
  - every fold goes two to four back and lands in an ordinary hall;
  - none folds at depth eleven.
- A second reader on the same route finds the same fold and the same landing, even with other deals behind its doors.
- No fold in a hall entered straight from a story.
- A reader on another route follows the first through the fold within the window and lands at the same door, as many crossings back. After the window their door is their own again.
- Following a friend who went on, a fold takes nobody back, and the follower goes to the friend's hall.
- Landing drops the folded crossings from the way back and counts the fold for the reader.
