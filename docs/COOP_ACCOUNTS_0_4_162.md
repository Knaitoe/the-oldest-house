# Every shared scene, written for being there with others — 0.4.162

The owner asked for "the varying perspectives for multiplayer": every shared vignette's account drafted for every way a reader can have been part of it (`docs/HOUSE_READ_DESIGN.md`, "Accounts for every way of being there"). Before this version only the monkey's paw, the drowned town and the child's room were drafted; in the other shared scenes every reader got the solo account, however many friends were there. Now every shared scene has an account for being there with others.

## Three kinds of shared scene

The scenes split by what happens in them when several readers are there.

- **One reader's act changes the room for everyone in it.** The others there watched it, and keep a watcher's account and the Witness credit, as in the paw.
  - The Tell-Tale floor: whoever takes up the board stops the beating for everyone.
  - The séance: there is one sitting, whoever's hand is at the cupboard.
  - Usher's vault: the lid is chosen once, and every later visit shows what came of it.
  - Mother's den: whoever gives without asking lets her let go, in front of everyone there.
- **Each reader still comes through for themselves.** In 31 scenes the act that ends the scene is personal: reaching the pier, climbing out of the well, reading the survey, crawling back through the squeeze. A friend's act is not the reader's own, so nobody is credited for it, as in the child's room. Instead each reader's own account closes on their party: who came through before them, and who was still in the scene when they left.
- **Scenes that already have their own accounts:** the paw and the drowned town (drafted, 0.4.147 and 0.4.148), the child's room (0.4.149), the Goatman's campers (0.4.150), the lake house in pairs (0.4.156) and the shallows together (0.4.157). They are unchanged.

Private scenes (Backseat, the silo, Borrasca, the copies, the Red Room, Harrigan, the confession, the Holy Rabbit, the Wheel house, the Whale institute, the phone canoe, the diner, the split-feed bedroom and hide-and-clap) have one reader at a time and need no other account.

## Who the closing line names

- **Who counts:** the reader's thread, the readers who went into the scene together or joined it partway. Spectators are never named.
- **Came through first:** the members who finished the scene before this reader, in the order they finished. Members who finished it on another night come after them, by name.
- **Still in it:** the members in the scene when this reader finished who have not finished it, by name.
- **Nobody else in it:** a reader alone, or the last of the party when everyone else left without finishing, gets no closing line.
- **Saved** with the reader's Witness record, so the account reads the same after a reload, and the line agrees in number however many are named: "Ana was", "Ana and Ben were", "Ana, Ben and Cy were".
- **Only in the reader's own words:** the line follows their account in the Witness ledger. It is never added to an aftermath account.

## The four witness scenes

*X* is the reader who acted, by their player name.

### The Tell-Tale floor

- **Witness:** "*X* took the hatchet to the loose board while I stood by the bed. The beating stopped for all of us at once. I read the words underneath over *X*'s shoulder: someone had stayed to care for him."
- **Partway:** "The floor was already beating when I came in, and *X* already had the hatchet. I watched the board come up and the room go quiet. I never learned how long it had been beating before me."
- **Aftermath:** "I had been in that room while the floor still beat under it. When I came back, *X* had taken the board up. The room was quiet, and there were words where the sound had been."

### The séance

The sitting's end follows how it ended: "the family went out through the west door and shut it behind them" (driven out), "the medium had said everything she meant to say" (heard), or otherwise "nothing at the table moved again".

- **Witness:** "I sat at the table for the sitting. It was *X*'s hand the family could not see. From my chair I could see their faces when the cupboard knocked: they were afraid of the room, not of us. I stayed until *the end*."
- **Partway:** "I came in after the sitting had begun. *X* was already at the cupboard, and the family were already listening. I stayed until *the end*. The album was the first thing I read, not the last."
- **Aftermath:** "When I came back the chairs had been pushed in. *X* had finished the sitting without me. The album lay open at its last page, at her name and the children's."

### Usher's vault

The lid closed:
- **Witness:** "I was in the vault when *X* chose the lid. I never put my own hand on it. On every visit after, the scratching beneath it was louder, and the cracks ran further along the wall."
- **Partway:** "I came to the vault after the lid had been chosen; *X* had chosen it. All I knew of the choice was the scratching, and the cracks along the wall."
- **Aftermath:** "I had been to the vault before the lid was chosen. When I came back the sound had followed the stone, as *X* had seen it do."

The coffin left open:
- **Witness:** "I was in the vault when *X* left the coffin open. I chose nothing. On the next visit she was standing outside it, and nobody had moved her."
- **Partway:** "I came to the vault after *X* had left the coffin open. By then she was already standing outside it."
- **Aftermath:** "I had been to the vault while she still lay in the coffin. When I came back, *X* had seen it through, and she was standing outside."

### Mother's den

- **Witness (a dog given back):** "I was in her den when she gave *X* back a dog. It came out alive and went straight to *X*'s side. For a moment, keeping it safe and keeping it forever were different things."
- **Witness (otherwise):** "I was in her den when *X* gave her something and asked for nothing back. She looked at her shelves as if she had never seen how full they were. Then she let something go."
- **Partway:** "I came into her den late, when *X* had already been talking with her a long while. I saw only the end: she let something go."
- **Aftermath:** "When I came back to her den the shelves were still full, but nobody was keeping them. *X* had been there when she let go."
