# Borrasca: the children's names (0.4.421)

The owner, November 9: "Also does Nells name match the naming convention from the story?"

It did not. The story's children carry names that were in fashion for small-town children of their own decade: mostly two
syllables, often surname-style. The vignette's night book dates its pages 1964, 1979 and 1993, which puts the reader's childhood
in the early 1990s. The friend's poster says she was twelve, so she was born around 1981. "Nell" is an old-fashioned name, and
"Eli" and "Wren" sound like names given in the 2010s. Only Dana, the 1979 page's child, fit her decade.

Each child now has an original name from their own decade, none taken from the story:

| Was | Now | Born about | Where they appear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nell Garrity | **Shelby Garrity** | 1981 | the friend; her compass and name patch, the bark, her poster, the depot's tag, the room under the depot |
| Eli Maddox | **Cody Maddox** | 1981 | the boy with the bike; his poster goes up after the first visit |
| Wren Bell | **Brandi Bell** | 1981 | the girl who went looking for him; her poster is up by the adult visits |
| Mara | **Kayla** | about 1996 | the newest poster, fourteen, on the last night; the next tag in the book |
| Dana Pruitt | Dana Pruitt | about 1965 | unchanged, as is Ellie in her letter |

The name patch's raised letters now spell SHELBY: the asset tool gained the S, H and Y glyphs, with smaller letters so all six
fit.

Internal keys keep the old names (`CastKimber`, `TaggedNell`, `EliTalk1`, `WrenRememberedEli`, `NELL_WALL` and so on), and so do
the code comments and the earlier design notes, where they record what was built at the time. Everything a player reads uses the
new names.
