## What it is

Each reader's people are their own. Nobody else sees them: another reader's game is never told they are there. None of them speaks, turns to be spoken to, can be touched, pushed or hurt, or does anything its story has it do. Nothing is saved of them but which of them a reader has already met on the way down.

### On the way down

- Between one camp and the next, a few of the people who will stand at the next camp's fire each stand once on the stair: at most five to a stretch, spread down it, each in the middle of a flight and the middle of the walk, none within forty steps of a camp.
- One at a time. It is set down nineteen to twenty-seven steps ahead of the reader, where nothing of it can be made out, facing up the stair.
- Nothing lights it and it lights nothing. The stair is too dark for a body standing on it to be seen at all, so it is drawn for that reader as a pale body with no light on it: no face, no dress, a thing in its hand only as an outline. It comes out of the dark at about seventeen blocks and is as plain as it gets within thirteen. The book lights twelve.
- It does not move. When the reader comes within six blocks it dims over about half a second and is not there. That reader does not meet it on the way down again.
- A reader who turns back and climbs forty blocks from it leaves nobody standing. It stands again when they come down.

### On the way home

- After the release, on the walk home, the same people stand at the reader's five fires. They are divided among the camps in the order their stories ended: the first by the door, the most recent deepest.
- They stand round the fire, clear of it and of the bedrolls, turned to it. They are there before the reader is near enough to see the camp.
- Once the reader has climbed eight steps past a camp's corner, its people come after them: three abreast, rows two steps apart, the first row ten steps behind.
- They move only while the reader can see neither them nor the place they are going to. Looked at, they are standing still. Each new look back brings the first row a step nearer, to no nearer than three.
- They never go back down the stair. No two of them stand in one place. Those whose place in the column is still below their own fire wait at it, and fall in as the column passes.
- The people of the camps below come up with the reader, so by the door a reader has everyone behind them.
- The shorter climb carries them as far as it carries the reader.
- When the reader walks out of the door at the top, or leaves the stair any other way, nobody is left standing.

### The climb home is longer than it was

The climb up has been shorter than the way down since 0.4.199: a turn climbed carries the reader two more. On the way home it now carries nobody from a camp's landing or past one, so every camp is walked up to and walked out of. By my count about ten of the stair's eighteen turns are walked on the way home, where about six were. A reader on their way down who turns back is carried as before.

## Where what is built differs from the list

- **"the father in his regalia".** The game draws him as a dark figure, plainer by the night, and draws no regalia. He stands as the story's last night has him.
- **"Mother, the size she is in her room".** In her room a reader is a child's height, a little over half their own. On the stair a reader is their own height, so she is made as much taller: 1.85 times a reader. She is the tallest thing on the stair by a head and shoulders.
- **Holloway** has the face he has when he visits the kitchen, which is the cast body he has.
- **"the man from the monitor"** is drawn, as in his story, in the reader's own body.
- **The bent-neck woman and the woman from the road** are drawn by the same hand, and are alike: both tall.

## How it is done

- `StairCompany` is the whole of it on the server: the table, a reader's people in the order their stories ended, where the shapes stand (`sightings`), the way down (`down`), the way home (`home`), the shorter climb (`lifted`, `holds`), and the sweep that leaves nobody standing.
- A figure is one of the cast's bodies (`LiteraryActor`) bound to its reader (`company`). Bound, it is not saved and is passed over by everything that runs the cast in their stories.
- `client.StairShapes` draws a shape. The pale is the overlay a struck body is flashed with; the dimming is the fog, set for that one figure; how dim is measured against the darkest the game's light goes at the reader's brightness setting.
- The bodies themselves are held, not their ids. A body that is not saved is not dropped when its part of the stair is unloaded: the game hides it until that part loads again. Looked up by id it would have been called gone, another set down, and the first left standing when the stair came back.
- One value more is sent with a cast body: that it is one of the stair's people, or one of them as a shape. The reader's own game needs the first as well as the second, or a body that is solid in its story (the second waitress) would have been solid to walk into on the stair. So the protocol number is 194: client and server must both be 0.4.596.
