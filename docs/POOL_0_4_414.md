# Pool (0.4.414)

The first part of Last orders (docs/BAR_GAMES_PROPOSAL.md): a pool table that plays real eight-ball. Up to four players can use the
same table. Nothing in this release puts a table anywhere. The bar in 0.4.416 is the first place to have one. The keepsake that lets a
reader build one at home comes in 0.4.417. Until then the table is only in the game through `/give`.

## The table

- **Size:** two blocks long, placed like a bed. The foot goes where the player clicks and the head one block on, the way they
  face. The foot keeps the table (`PoolTableBlockEntity`). Breaking either half takes both and drops the table as an item, once. In creative, breaking it drops nothing.
- **Look:**
  - The model: green cloth, mahogany rails with three sights along each long side, a dark apron, four legs and leather
    pockets where the rails meet.
  - The art: drawn in code by `tools/draw_bar_art.py`.
  - The balls: drawn on the cloth by the table's renderer wherever they lie, so anyone standing near can see the frame from the
    room.

## Playing

- **Sitting down:** using either half opens the table's screen.
  - Before the break, a player takes a seat ("You take a cue from the rack.").
  - Once the frame has broken, newcomers watch ("The frame's already on. You watch."), and so does a fifth person.
- **Sides:** the first player breaks. Seats go to the side with fewer players, so two players are one against one and four are two
  against two. A side's players shoot in turn.
- **Playing alone:** one player alone plays both sides ("You chalk a cue. Alone, you play both sides.").
- **Leaving:** a player who walks more than ten blocks off, changes dimension or logs out gives up their seat. Everyone else keeps
  their side. If a side has nobody left, whoever is still at the table plays for it.
- **The screen:**
  - **The view:** the table seen from above, with the rails, cloth, pockets and every ball.
  - **Aiming:** on your shot the cue follows the mouse round the cue ball. A faint line runs to the first ball it would meet, and a
    ghost ball shows where it would touch.
  - **Striking:** holding the left button draws the cue back. Full power takes a little over a second, a bar shows it, and letting
    go strikes. A right-click while drawing puts the cue down without a shot.
  - **Ball in hand:** the cue ball follows the mouse and is greyed where it would touch another ball. A click puts it down, a
    right-click picks it up again, then the shot is drawn as usual.
  - **Text:** the top line names both sides and their group. Underneath, the table says what the last shot did and whose shot it
    is.
- **Everyone sees the same shot:** the server plays every shot once and sends it to everyone within 48 blocks: the table before the
  shot, the shot itself and the table after it.
  - Each player's screen and the room's balls play the shot out at the same speed, with sounds at the table. The cue's tip
    strikes, balls knock, a ball comes off the cushion, and a ball drops and runs down the return.
  - If a player's own playback ever differed, the server's table after the shot replaces it when the shot ends.

## The game

`PoolGame` is the whole game in plain Java, with no Minecraft in it, so the server, the screen and the tests all play the same
code.

- **The table:** 254 by 127 units with balls of radius 3. That matches a nine-foot table scaled down: the cloth is twice as long as it
  is wide.
- **Pockets:** four corner pockets and two side pockets, the side mouths a little narrower.
- **The simulation:** fixed steps of 1/240 of a second. Each step the balls roll with friction, bounce off cushions that take away a
  little of their speed, and collide elastically with each other. A ball whose centre crosses a pocket mouth is potted.
- **Same shot, same result:** Java's arithmetic is the same on every machine, so the same table and the same shot end with every ball
  in the same place.
- **The rack:** a seeded triangle with the eight in the middle, one of each group in the back corners, and the rest shuffled.
- **Rules:** standard eight-ball.
  - **The break:** the cue ball must strike the rack. A legal break opens the table, and groups are decided by the first ball
    potted after it.
  - **Fouls:** potting the cue ball, hitting nothing, or hitting the other group's ball (or the eight too early) first. A foul gives
    the other side the cue ball in hand, to place anywhere it touches nothing.
  - **Turns:** potting one of your own balls without a foul keeps the shot. Otherwise the turn passes.
  - **The eight:** potting it after clearing your group wins the frame. Potting it earlier, or scratching while potting it, loses
    the frame.
  - A finished frame takes no more shots. Using the table again starts the next one with whoever is still seated.
- **The table's own player:** a story can seat the table's own player with a skill from 0 to 1.
  - Its aim uses the ghost-ball method: it picks the best pot it can see and aims the cue ball at the point that sends that ball to
    the pocket. Its skill decides how far its aim and power wander.
  - On its turn it shoots after a short pause.
  - A table where nobody but its own player is still seated is put away.
  - This is the hook for the bar's regular in 0.4.416. A story can also listen to every shot and to the frame's end.

## What is new

- **Blocks and items:** the Pool Table block, block entity and item, in a new `BarRegistry` that darts and the bar will join.
- **Sounds:** four subtitled sounds ("Cue strikes the ball", "Pool balls knock", "Ball hits the cushion", "Ball drops into a
  pocket"), synthesized by `tools/generate_bar_audio.py` from Movie Night's building blocks.
- **Protocol 169:** three payloads.
  - `PoolStatePayload`: the table to the players near it, and to whoever opened it.
  - `PoolShotPayload`: one shot, with the table before and after it.
  - `PoolShootPayload`: a player's shot to the server. The server checks it is that player's shot and that they are within ten
    blocks of the table.
- **CI:** the build and quick gates run `draw_bar_art.py --check` and `generate_bar_audio.py --check`.
- **Tests:** two cases in the literary namespace.
  - `BarGamesTests#poolPlaysTheSameShotTheSameWayAndKeepsTheRules`:
    - the same seed racks the same frame, and the same shot ends the same way;
    - the break opens the table and spreads the rack;
    - a pot keeps the shot and decides the groups;
    - a scratch and a wrong first ball are fouls with ball in hand;
    - the cue ball goes down only where it touches nothing;
    - the eight too soon loses and after the group wins;
    - a finished frame takes no shot;
    - the table's own player pots an easy ball.
  - `BarGamesTests#readersTakeTurnsAtATableAndALateComerWatches`:
    - a player puts the table down from the item, foot and head;
    - two players sit from either end;
    - the first breaks, and a shot out of turn is ignored;
    - the turn follows the side whose shot it is;
    - someone who comes after the break watches;
    - a player who walks off loses their seat while the one left keeps their side and shoots for both;
    - the table's own player takes its shot on its turn;
    - broken from its head, the whole table goes and drops one item.

Layout 134 and Witness 85/64/62 are unchanged. Tables are not saved. A server restart leaves the table standing and starts a new frame.
