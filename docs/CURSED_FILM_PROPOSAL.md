# The cursed film — a proposal (October 30)

The owner asked to consider a vignette based on *The Ring*, "focused more on the cursed film element, rather [than] the ghost girl climbing out the tv", with Paul Tremblay's *Horror Movie* as further inspiration. This is a proposal for review, not built yet.

## What each source brings

- **From *The Ring*:**
  - a tape that should not be watched;
  - its images, which are clues to real places and a real death;
  - a call afterwards and a count of days;
  - the one way out: copy it and show it to someone else.

  Nothing climbs out of the screen. The dread is the film itself.
- **From *Horror Movie*:**
  - the cursed film as a made thing, with a production history: a low-budget horror film shot decades ago and never finished;
  - its existence only as leaked fragments, a screenplay and the stories of the people who made it;
  - the narrator, who played its monster.

  The vignette borrows the idea that a film can be unfinished, and that being in it is worse than watching it.

Nothing is quoted from either work. The film, its script and every line are original, as Movie Night's *Teeth of Summer* was. Neither title is used in the game.

## The vignette: "Work print" (working title)

**The place.** A small editing suite and screening room behind a closed rental shop, after hours. It has:
- the shop floor with its returns slot;
- a back room with a CRT and two video decks;
- an edit bay with reels, a splicer, a light table and a wall of continuity photographs;
- a sound booth.

It is built whole, with custom textures, as the Last Stop and the Stanley were.

**The tape.** In the returns slot is an unlabelled tape. The rental card records the borrower in the reader's own handwriting, as the journal parcel and the self-addressed envelope already are.

**Watching.**
- **Playing it.** The reader puts the tape in the deck themselves, with real blocks.
- **The film.** About ninety seconds of an original film, composed shot by shot on the CRT. It is drawn in code with Movie Night's film machinery.
  - The tracking wavers.
  - The shots are deliberately ordinary and wrong: a hallway that is one of the House's own, a door seen from the wrong side, a figure at the far end of a corridor who is closer in each shot, a hand writing on a page, a room the reader has already been in.
- **Private.** Each reader's viewing is their own. A reader who looks away has not watched it.
- **The call.** When the tape ends, the shop's phone rings. It uses the phone screen the mod already has.
  - There is no voice, only a count: *seven*.
  - The count runs down on the reader's own House arrivals, not on real days, so it cannot run out while they are away.

**The film learns them.** This is Tremblay's idea, made playable.
- **The House has been filming.** The mod already records real camera exposures and footage, and the House already keeps the places a reader has been.
- **What changes.** On each later viewing, one shot becomes the reader's own: a hall they walked, from behind, in the same grain. The figure is closer each time.
- **The cue.** By the last viewings, the reader is the figure.

**Finding out what the film was.**
- **The evidence:** the edit bay's script pages, the call sheet, the continuity photographs and the reels.
- **The story.** It tells, in fragments, of a film shot in a house that kept getting longer, by a crew who did not finish it. It ends on a last scene that was written and never shot. Nothing explains the curse outright.

**Three ways it can end**, each a personal resolution:
1. **Dub it.** Copy the tape on the dual deck and leave the copy:
   - in co-op, by handing it to another reader, who must then watch it;
   - alone, by leaving it in a hall.

   The count stops. The account says what that cost and who has it now. *(passed_it_on)*
2. **Finish the film.** Shoot the unshot last scene on the edit bay's set with the House's camera, using the existing filming verbs. Splice it onto the reel and run the whole film once. It ends, the count stops, and the reader is in the credits. *(finished_the_film)*
3. **Let the count run out.**
   - **What happens.** On the seventh arrival, the reader is taken into the film. They wake in the shot of the hallway, with the grain and the tracking lines over their own sight. They walk out of it into the House, alive.
   - **Why alive.** The House's existing rules on death and custody stay as they are.

   The account is the one the film keeps. *(stayed_in_the_film)*

**What it is not.**
- No ghost from the television.
- No jump scare at the screen.
- The tape cannot be destroyed: pulled out and broken, it is back in the returns slot on the next arrival.

## Fit with the House

- **Witness.** It is one new eligible source, an understanding story. The pool grows to 56 sources, and the quota stays 42 (75% rounded up). Still two kinds and three endings.
- **Multiplayer.**
  - Viewings, the count and the reader-shots are private.
  - The dub is the one deliberately shared thing: a copy really passes from one reader to another, as the owner's co-op rules for shared scenes already allow.
- **The journal.** A rubbing of the rental card, and a co-author trigger at the seventh call, are natural later links. They are not required.
- **Writing rules.** The House's writing rules apply to every line: it never genders the reader and never names a colour or an age.

## Questions for the owner

1. **Setting.** The rental shop and edit suite above? Or a motel room with a TV and a VCR, closer to *The Ring*?
2. **The count.** Seven arrivals, as proposed, or seven House days?
3. **Letting it run out.** Is being taken into the film and walking out alive right? Or should it cost more, such as belongings to the Mother as a death does?
4. **The dub alone.** May a solo reader pass it on by leaving the copy in a hall? Or must it go to another real reader?
5. **The reader in the footage.** May the House show the reader in their own past halls, built from real visits?
