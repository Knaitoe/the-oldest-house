# Three vignettes: an Enríquez story, the bomb, and the machine again

Status: design for the owner's review. Nothing here is built until the owner has read it. Each design ends with the decisions it needs, and they are collected at the end.

The owner, 17 November: "Can you design three vignettes; one inspired by Mariana Enriquez's works. One where the player or players face a nuclear apocalypse- ideally source a piece of horror fiction for inspiration for this. And finally, one inspired by I have no mouth and must scream."

## What the House already has

Two of the three authors are already in the House, so these designs take different material:

- **The House on the Corner** (0.4.408) is Enríquez's "Adela's House": a girl taken by a house. *Last Seen* is the other half of that: the ones who come back.
- **The Kept** (0.4.410) already uses the main beats of Ellison's story: the walk through the machine, the cave of ice, the tins with nothing to open them, the mercy, and the lost mouth. *Not Yet* takes what The Kept left: the keeping itself. The machine will not let its people die, it counts the years, and it sets them against one another.
- **The 1995 game.** The earlier five-character plan ([I_HAVE_NO_MOUTH_GAME_ADAPTATION.md](I_HAVE_NO_MOUTH_GAME_ADAPTATION.md)) follows the 1995 computer game's five chapters. Building those would recreate that game, which I won't do, so this design draws only on the short story.

## House rules that apply to all three

- **Rights.** All three sources are in copyright. Only their shape is used: original names, original text, no quotations, and no source titles shown in the game.
- **Multiplayer first.** Each design works for one reader or several. Progress is personal and honest: watching a peer earns nothing, and spectators earn nothing.
- **Retreat.** An unfinished visit can always be walked out of until a reader commits to a beat themselves.
- **The House's ways.** Supplies are finite and never restock. The House foreshadows and does not explain. Every custom sound has a subtitle. These are new builds only.
- **Art.** Textures are drawn in code at Minecraft's own pixel scale. People are painted on the cast's grid.
- **Writing.** No colour or age is named. The text never says how the reader feels. Gender comes only through the House's read. Speech appears in the conversation panel and over heads, never in chat. Every sample line below is a draft, and is flagged as new when it is built.
- **Children.** No child is ever shown harmed. Where a child is in danger, the danger happens out of sight or not at all.
- **Witness.** Each vignette is one new source with one personal resolution, however many choices it offers. If all three ship, the pool goes from 91 sources to 94 and the quota from 69 to 71, still across at least two kinds.

## 1. Mariana Enríquez: *Last Seen*

### The source

"Kids Who Come Back" is the longest story in *The Dangers of Smoking in Bed*. A woman keeps the city's archive of missing children. Then the children start turning up again in the city's parks, looking exactly as they did the day they went missing, not a day older. Some still carry bruises that should have faded years ago. The families and the city turn on them. The story keeps asking why children go missing at all: most of the archive's children ran from homes that hurt them.

**Taken:** the archive, and the woman who knows every face in it; the children back in a park, unchanged; the families and the city; the question of where they were.

**Not taken:** names, text, the story's ending, and the detail of why any one child went missing.

**Why this story.** It suits the House: the House keeps a record of everyone who has come into it. It is Enríquez at her most direct, angry at a city that would rather not see. It happens in the open, in a park, so it is nothing like the Corner's house. And its children come back, where the Corner's girl does not.

### The place

An outdoor scene under the outside sky, about 64 × 96, on its own scene clock.

- **The park:**
  - paved paths, a fenced playground and a dry fountain;
  - plane trees, and silk-floss trees with spiny bottle-shaped trunks;
  - a bandstand, a shut kiosk, benches and lamps;
  - a railing all round. Beyond it, the noise of an avenue, a bus stop and a row of houses.
- **The office:** a low municipal building at the park's edge.
  - **The front desk:** where families come and ask.
  - **The archive behind it:** shelves of folders to the ceiling, a long table, a lamp, a typewriter, and a wall of posters. Each poster is a painted face with where and on what day the child was last seen. The posters name no ages and no colours.
  - **The archivist's desk:** it has one locked drawer.
- **The families' houses:** across the avenue, with doors and windows a reader can see from the park.

### The people

All are on stranger phases and painted on the cast's grid. Every name is original.

- **Nora, the archivist.** She knows every folder by its face.
- **Patricia and Luz.** They work the front desk and talk to the families.
- **The returned.** Eight to ten children at child scale. Each one matches a poster.
  - **They don't eat:** food handed to them stays in their hands.
  - **They are cold:** a touch puffs frost.
  - **They don't blink.**
  - **One scrape** on one knee is still wet.
  - **They don't sleep:** at dusk they stand facing the avenue.
- **The families.**
  - A grandmother who brings a coat.
  - A father who takes his daughter off by the wrist, then brings her back an hour later and leaves her at the gate.
  - A mother who stands across the avenue and never crosses.
  - A family who never comes.
- **A man from the city:** he wants the files closed.

### How it plays

The park runs one day at a time for everyone in it, on one clock, as the Corner's street does. The day is the furthest any unfinished reader there has reached, so friends go on together. A reader's day counts only if they were there for its beat.

1. **The archive.**
   - **Her rule:** Nora shows the reader the archive. "Every one of them has a folder. A folder stays open."
   - **The chore:** she asks for the day's folders to be shelved. Carrying them from the desk to the shelves teaches the reader the faces.
   - **The first one:** Luz comes in. There is a girl at the fountain. The girl is exactly her poster. Nora stops at the edge of the path.
   - **The verb, recognition:** the reader finds the girl's folder on the shelves by her face and brings it to her. She looks at it, then at the reader. "I was waiting where they told me to."
   - **Counts:** for the reader who brought her the folder.
2. **More of them.**
   - **The park:** there are eight children now, standing apart. The reader matches each one to their folder.
   - **The families:** some come, as above.
   - **The city:** a fence goes up round the playground. A notice goes up on the lamp posts: "Do not approach. Do not feed." The man from the city tells Nora, "A child who comes back is a closed file." She does not close any.
   - **Counts:** for a reader who has matched at least four children.
3. **The night.**
   - **The park:** the gates are chained from outside, with the children inside. Out on the avenue, men gather with flashlights and sticks.
   - **What a reader can do:** take a child's hand, as the Corner girl's hand is taken, and lead them through the office's back door into the archive. Each child goes and stands at their own folder. A reader can also keep the door, or simply leave.
   - **The men:** when they come over the railing, any child left in the park is gone. Nobody is shown being hurt. Their lights sweep the archive windows, and one tries the door.
   - **Dawn:** the children in the archive are gone too. Each folder lies open on the table at its first page.
   - **Counts:** for a reader who was there when the men came and was still there at dawn.
4. **After.** This is where the account waits.
   - **The park by day:** empty, the fence down, the notices torn.
   - **The drawer:** Nora's drawer is unlocked. In it is one folder: the reader's own. The face is the reader's, painted from their skin. It says they were last seen at a door, on the evening the House first took them in, by the House's own record. "Yours came in on the first night. I keep it with the others."
   - **The account:** it is in the folder.

### Multiplayer

- **Shared:** the park, the archive, the children, the families, the city and the night.
- **Each reader's own:** credit for each match goes to whoever brought the folder. Each reader also has their own folder in the drawer. With several readers, the drawer holds a folder for each of them, so friends find each other's faces among the missing. The account is each reader's own.
- **Roles:** one reader fetches folders while another stays with a child. At night, one keeps the door while another leads the children in.

### The resolution

One source, of understanding.

Draft Witness line: "Children came back to a park the way they were the day they went, and I took each one the folder with their face in it. The city wanted them gone by morning. My own folder was in her drawer."

### Art and sound

- **Park:** paving, railings, the bandstand, silk-floss trunks, plane trees, the fountain and the kiosk.
- **Office:** shelves of folders, the poster wall, the desk and drawer, the typewriter.
- **The folder:** an item that shows the child's painted face.
- **The cast:** about ten children and seven adults.
- **Sound:** traffic beyond the railings, pigeons, the gate's chain, the typewriter, paper, flashlight switches and men's voices.

### Decisions

1. **The reader's own folder.** It makes the reader one of the House's missing. Recommended: yes. It is the whole point of the last visit.
2. **The Corner's girl.** If a reader has finished The House on the Corner, should the girl with one arm stand among the returned, with her brother, now grown, across the avenue? Recommended: yes, once, and without a word.
3. **The city's history.** The real headscarves painted round the Plaza de Mayo honour the mothers of the disappeared. Painting them on the park's paving would tie the returned to that history. Recommended: no. It is a real symbol, and the park can carry the history without borrowing it.

## 2. The bomb: *Fallow*

### The source

Robert R. McCammon's *Swan Song* (1987) is a horror novel. It shared the Bram Stoker Award for best novel with Stephen King's *Misery*.

- **The opening:** when the war begins, a girl and a big stranger take cover in the basement of a gas station in Kansas.
- **The man of many faces:** through the aftermath walks a man with many faces, a demon the book suggests started the war for its own amusement.
- **The girl's gift:** plants grow at her touch.

**Taken:** the roadside station in wheat country; strangers sheltering together under it; a girl and a big man; the buried days; a walker with other people's faces; and a girl near whom things grow.

**Not taken:** names, text, anything after the first days, and the novel's other people and wonders.

**Why this one.** It puts the reader in the moment itself, in a place with a door and a cellar, which is what a vignette can do. And its horror has a face that comes walking down the road.

**Others considered:**

- **Ray Bradbury, "There Will Come Soft Rains" (1950).** An automated house goes on with its day after its family are shadows burned on its wall. In *The Martian Chronicles* it is dated, as it happens, 4 August 2026. But it is science fiction rather than horror, and its people are gone before it begins. Its shadows are kept here, from Hiroshima rather than from the story.
- **Joe R. Lansdale, "Tight Little Stitches in a Dead Man's Back" (1986).** This is horror: a lighthouse on a dried-up sea bed twenty years after the war, with roses that come out at night. But its central image rests on the narrator's guilt over his daughter in a way the House shouldn't carry.
- **Raymond Briggs, *When the Wind Blows* (1982).** A couple build a refuge by the government leaflets. A refuge of propped doors could be added to *Fallow*; this is decision 4.

### The place

An outdoor scene under the outside sky, about 96 × 128, on its own scene clock.

- **The station:** a gas station and grocery on a two-lane road.
  - **Out front:** two pumps under a canopy.
  - **The store:** a counter and register, coolers, a rack of road maps, a radio on a shelf, a television on a bracket and a coffee machine.
  - **Round the side:** a garage bay with a car up on the lift.
  - **Behind:** the trailer where the keeper lives, and the storm cellar.
- **The storm cellar:** slanted doors in the yard, stone steps, and a cellar of shelves holding jars and tins, a lantern and a cot.
- **The wheat:** flat to a haze that hides the scene's edge. Across the road is a farmhouse behind a windbreak, and telephone poles run along the road.
- **The pads:** two fenced squares of gravel out in the wheat, each with a flat concrete lid, a pole and a light. They are nothing to look at, until the lids slide open.

### The people

All are on stranger phases and painted on the cast's grid, with original names.

- **The keeper:** he runs the station.
- **A woman and her daughter:** travelling, with car trouble. The girl, at child scale, has a bean plant in a paper cup.
- **The woman's man:** short-tempered, and in a hurry.
- **A big man:** a long-haul driver whose rig sits at the pumps.
- **The stranger:** on the first visit he buys a soda, asks the girl her name (she doesn't tell him), asks the time, and walks off into the wheat toward the pads.

### How it plays

1. **The afternoon.**
   - **Before:** an ordinary hour. The keeper asks the reader to mind the store while he works under the car. The girl shows them her plant. The stranger comes and goes.
   - **The tone:** the radio breaks into a long tone, and the television shows a card with nothing on it. The woman's man laughs.
   - **The pads:** then the lids slide open in the wheat. The ground shakes, and the missiles go up on columns of smoke. The keeper says what it means: "That's ours going up. Theirs will be coming for the holes." A clock begins (decision 1).
   - **Getting people down:** the keeper goes to open the cellar doors. The woman runs back to her car. Her man drives off toward town. The big man carries the girl. A reader can take the woman by the hand and bring her down. A reader can hold the cellar doors. A reader can also do nothing.
   - **The light:**
     - Readers still outside when the clock runs out see the whole view go to light. Their shadow is left on the station's wall where they stood, in the pose they were in.
     - Readers in the cellar see the light through the cracks in the doors. The cellar shakes, the station burns and falls in above them, and the doors are buried.
   - **Counts:** either way.
2. **Buried.** Only readers who were in the cellar play this visit. Readers who were outside go straight to the third.
   - **The cellar:** the lantern's oil is finite and dims as the visit goes on. With it are the big man, the girl, the woman if a reader brought her down, and the keeper if he made it.
   - **Time:** days pass, shortened, by the lantern. The ceiling is warm from what is burning above.
   - **The girl's plant:** it grows in the dark, where it shouldn't.
   - **Digging out:** the steps are buried in rubble, dug a stroke at a time the way the cave's rubble is. Readers dig faster together, and the big man helps.
   - **Outside:** ash falls like snow. The wheat is burned to stubble, the farmhouse is gone and the poles are down. Where the pads were there are craters. On the station's wall are shadows: the woman's man at his truck door, anyone who was not brought down, and any reader who was outside.
   - **Counts:** for a reader who dug.
3. **The road.**
   - **The girl's gift:** she pushes her bean into the ash at the cellar steps. While she sits there, the stubble round her starts to grow back.
   - **Readers who were outside:** they come down the road through the ash, and find their own shadow on the wall.
   - **The stranger:** he comes walking from the direction of the pads, pleasant: "Folks ought to stick together. Where's the little one?" Each time a reader looks at him again, he has another face: the keeper's, the woman's man's, the big man's, a reader's own. He offers a ride in a truck that should not run.
   - **The choice:** a reader can keep the girl hidden by standing on the cellar steps and lying to him. He smiles, says he'll find her anyway, and walks on. Or a reader can step aside. Then he goes down the steps and finds nobody: the girl has walked out into the field where the wheat is coming back, and he stands at the edge of it, looking.
   - **Counts, and the account:** for a reader who stays until he has gone. Their account is in the keeper's ledger, singed at the edges, under the counter.

### Multiplayer

- **Shared:** the station, the clock, the strike, the cellar, the dig, the road and the stranger.
- **Each reader's own:** where they were when the light came, which decides their path. Also their shadow, what they told the stranger, and their account.
- **Roles:** holding the doors, bringing people down, digging. One reader can bring another down by the hand.
- **Paths that meet:** readers from the cellar and readers left outside meet on the road. A shadow and the reader it belongs to can stand side by side.

### The resolution

One source, of survival.

Draft Witness line: "I was at a gas station in the wheat when the lids in the field slid open. [I went down the cellar steps. / I was out by the pumps when the light came, and the wall kept my shadow.] A man came down the road with somebody else's face, asking for the girl."

### Art and sound

- **The station:** pumps, the canopy, store fittings, the register, the radio, the television and the lift.
- **The land:** stubble and ash blocks, and the pads with their lids and fences.
- **The strike:** the missiles, as entities with smoke trails; the flash, as a screen effect (Borrasca's flash is a start).
- **The shadows:** a wall decal painted from a reader's skin silhouette in their pose at the light, made the way the first-death portraits are painted.
- **The stranger:** his face changes only when nobody is looking, by the House's usual rule.
- **Sound:** the broadcast tone, the launch, the blast, the cellar settling, digging and the wind.

### Decisions

1. **How long the clock runs.** Recommended: four minutes, a nod to Britain's four-minute warning, or three if it drags.
2. **A reader outside when the light comes.** A real death under the House's death rules, or the House's faint (they wake at the House's door with everything they carried)? Recommended: the faint. The shadow is the cost.
3. **The girl's gift.** Shown as the wheat growing back round her, or left out, so that the stranger is the only thing in it that is not of this world? Recommended: shown.
4. **A refuge for readers who don't make the cellar.** Doors propped against the store's back wall, by the leaflet, as in *When the Wind Blows*. Recommended: not at first.

## 3. The machine again: *Not Yet*

### The source

Harlan Ellison's "I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream" (1967). The Kept took its plot. What it left is the keeping itself:

- the machine will not let its people die;
- it counts the years it has kept them;
- it remakes their bodies;
- it sets them against one another;
- the man telling the story believes it has left his mind alone.

The 1995 game's chapters are not used.

### The idea

Deeper in the same machine as The Kept is a ward, where it mends what it breaks.

- **Death refused:** here it will not let the reader die.
- **What it wants:** a reaction. It wants the reader to get up.
- **The way out:** lie down and stay down while it does everything it can to make you get up, until it loses interest and looks away.

### The place

An interior slot of about 48 × 64, in The Kept's own materials (casing, grating, screens, cable), with new fittings:

- **The ward:**
  - rows of cots under lamps on jointed arms, with curtains hung on rails of cable and a screen at every cot;
  - a long window that shows the machine's copy of outdoors, badly made: a sun that does not move and painted clouds.
- **The counter:** a wall of turning digits that counts the years the machine has kept each reader.
- **The mending table:** a table under a ring of arms.
- **The long room:** the people it has mended too often. They lie in their cots with their eyes open, breathing, and can no longer move. They speak a little. There are two or three, painted on stranger phases.
- **What it hurts with:** a floor that heats, a hatch that drops into a shaft, cold let in, and arms that grip.
- **The far door:** shut until it looks away.

### How it plays

1. **Refused.**
   - **Arriving:** the reader walks in from the House's door, and the screens wake.
     - A reader who let The Kept's four go reads: "You took my four. There is only you now."
     - Anyone else reads: "I have been waiting for someone to keep."
   - **The first fall:** the floor opens under the reader, and they fall. They wake on a cot, mended on the table on the way, and every screen says NOT YET in the machine's hand. The counter starts.
   - **Every death after:** each one is refused the same way. Each mending leaves a seam drawn on the reader's skin, seen by everyone while they are inside. The seams fade when they leave.
   - **The long room:** one of the people there tells the reader the only rule they know: "It wants you up. Don't give it that."
   - **Counts:** for a reader who has been refused once and has heard the rule.
2. **Lie down.**
   - **The invitation:** "Lie down. I will keep you." The reader lies on their cot, in the ordinary sleeping pose.
   - **The trial:** while they lie there, the machine works to get them up.
     - The cot heats.
     - Something calls them by name. Its names come from the House's real record.
     - The screen shows the far door standing open, which may be a lie.
     - It hurts the person in the next cot: "Get up and I will stop." They groan, then say, "Stay down."
     - It hurts the reader, and refuses their death: NOT YET.
   - **Getting up:** always possible. It ends the trial, and the next lie-down starts over.
   - **When it ends:** after a set time that keeps escalating (decision 1), everything goes quiet and the far door opens. It has looked away.
   - **Counts:** for a reader who stayed down to the end.
3. **After.**
   - **The cot:** the reader's cot is made, and the counter is still counting the years. "It kept counting after I left."
   - **The account:** it is in the drawer under the cot. Its last page is in the machine's hand: how many times it mended them.
   - **Lying down again:** it doesn't bother. "Not you. Not now."

### Multiplayer

- **The cots:** readers lie side by side, and the machine uses each reader against the others.
- **Its lies:** it tells each one that another has got up, and shows them on their screen standing at the door. It hurts each one while the others stay down, and offers to stop if they get up.
- **What it shows:** each reader's screen shows what the machine chooses, so readers come out with different stories. The owner's suggestion of friends comparing what they swear they heard happens here.
- **Getting up:** a reader who gets up starts their own trial over. The machine then uses them on the others: "They got up. Do you want to be the one I keep?"
- **The accounts:** each one records what that reader was told, and what really happened. The House knows both.
- **Credit:** each reader's own. Spectators earn nothing, and its lies do not touch them.

### The resolution

One source, of survival.

Draft Witness line: "Deep in the machine there is a ward where it mends what it breaks. It would not let me die. I lay down and stayed down, whatever it did, until it looked away."

### Art and sound

- **Reused:** The Kept's materials.
- **New:** the cot, the cable curtain rail, the lamp arms, the counter's digits, the painted window, the mending arms and the hatch.
- **People:** two or three painted people for the long room.
- **The seams:** an overlay on the reader's skin while they are inside.
- **Sound:** the counter's tick, the mending, the hatch, a name called, and the silence when it looks away.

### Decisions

1. **How long the trial lasts, and how hard it pushes.** Recommended: ninety seconds the first time, with five provocations in rising order.
2. **Whose names it calls.**
   - With several readers: the other readers' names.
   - With one: the companions the House saw them lose, as The Kept's screens read out, or nothing.
   - Recommended: the companions, as The Kept does.
3. **Whether it really hurts the other reader.** Real damage, refused, or only shown? Recommended: real. Both readers feel what their staying down costs.
4. **The same machine as The Kept.** Recommended: the same machine, so it can remember the four.
