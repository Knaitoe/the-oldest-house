# The basement, the jar and the sisters' plan (0.4.325)

The owner asked, with the novel attached, for the basement shrine to start rendering through the camera before the last visit, for evidence of Merry and Marjorie's plot to "save" their parents, for Merry to go to the basement and interact with the effigy, for her to have dialogue there, and for the effigy's blocks to be "dark and ominous, still painted in Minecraft's vanilla style".

## From the novel

The scanned edition was read directly (PDF page numbers count from the first PDF page). Everything written for the game is adaptation, not quotation.

- PDF 140–143: the basement as Merry knew it before the show: the bulkhead stair, the breaker panel by the washer and dryer, the food shelves with her juice boxes and crackers, and Marjorie's growing-things story told down there.
- PDF 286–288: Marjorie's account of the shrine, with drop cloths either side of the cross and pictures against the walls. On the bench altar she says she found a small capped glass jar of granular white powder. She shows Merry an image search of jars and bags of white powder, then says what they will do; Merry writes "What do we do?" and makes new signs.
- PDF 289–293: the last day. Merry speaks only in signs, written on a pocket pad Marjorie gives her. Marjorie brings the jar out from under her bed, now only a quarter full: she says she dumped most of it out. Merry stirs the powder into the sauce while Marjorie keeps their mother busy. The plan as Merry understood it: their mother makes spaghetti, and they spike the sauce. The sisters have plain pasta, Marjorie saying her stomach hurts. The powder knocks out their parents or makes them sick so the girls can run away and take the jar to the police. Their father gets help and one day comes home.
- PDF 294–295: after the supper Merry runs down to the basement with two flashlights to see whether anything her sister told her was true. There is no shrine, only the stored pewter cross by the shelves of food, a dirty rag over its head. She sits on the floor and waits for the growing things.
- PDF 298: the adult Merry wonders whether the jar held only sugar and flour, and whether their father had the powder to polish the cross.

## In the house

**The shrine from the fifth day.** The camera-only shrine now shows through the reader's own rolling camera in the basement from day 5 (`GhostsLore.SHRINE_DAY`), not only on the last visit. On days 5 and 6 the small capped jar of white powder stands on its bench. On day 7 the bench is bare. Without the camera the ordinary room still holds only the stored cross and its rag, keeping the novel's contradiction. Recording the shrine on any of those days counts; the account and footage say whether the jar was on tape.

**The effigy's own blocks.** The shrine and the stored cross have their own textures, drawn at 16 pixels to the block in a few close dark tones each, as vanilla's are:

- bench wood stained almost black;
- drop cloths gone grey-brown with damp, with rust-coloured stains low down;
- pewter blackened with tarnish, and a filthy rag over the head;
- two dim pictures in black frames, a robed man with a raised knife and a lamb tied on a stone;
- three burnt-down candle stubs on the bench.

**Merry's pad.** On the last day only, a pocket pad with a glittery cover lies on Merry's nightstand, one of Marjorie's pens clipped to it. It holds her question ("WHAT DO WE DO?"), the signs she tore off for the day, and at the bottom of a page, in the same capitals, the plan.

**The canisters.** The sugar and flour canisters on the counter by the sink stand closed on every earlier day. On the last day they stand open, lids off, a teaspoon and white dust between them. Nobody has been baking. With the pad, they leave open both what the girls believed and what the adult Merry later suspects.

**The laptop's fourth page.** The last visit's laptop gains an image search: the search words deleted, the results rows of jars and bags of white powder, one glass jar with a metal cap opened large. The substance is not named and no label is drawn.

**Merry and the jar.** After the others are still, Merry gets up off her chair onto the floor behind it, the empty jar in her hand. She goes out of the dining room and down the actual basement stair to the cross. She crouches to set the jar on the floor in front of it, then sits on the floor, knees drawn up, looking at it. After a while she gets up and goes back up and out through the front door, and the house goes dark as before. The jar stays, for that reader, in front of the cross. A reader who left during the supper finds it there when they return.

**What she says down there.** Clicking Merry on the stair or in the basement gets her own lines, spoken, one per click:

- before she sets the jar down: what her sister said was down there, what is actually there, and that she doesn't know where the police are;
- after: that she put it back, whether it will wear off, and that she is waiting for the growing things.

Each list stops on its last line.
