## When it can happen

Once the two men have met (the meeting DONE) and nothing is being taken out of the house (no disturbance, or one that is over with the
jib door shut and nobody left in the wall).

## The three ways

- **Agreement.** A reader who knows how the frame works (has seen the rule, heard Vera's truth, or knows about the third) can say to Dan
  "You could go back through. He'd walk out." He agrees if the party has backed him (the support count, or Rae was told, or that reader
  told him about Rae), and walks to the frame by himself. Otherwise he refuses.
- **Force.** After he refuses, a reader can say "Then we'll take you." Every reader within reach of him holds him; he goes at a pace that
  grows with the holders (one is slow, two is a walk). If nobody has held him for five seconds, he stops and stays where he is.
- **His panic.** If Vera has called the stair (the party went against her), nobody has got him to agree, and he has been in the wall in
  the disturbance, then thirty seconds later Vera calls down to him that the frame is the one place it will not touch him, and he runs
  for it. A reader can call after him "It isn't coming. Stop." and he stops.

## At the frame

If Daniel is still held in it, the frame lets him go: he steps out to the left of it and stands. Dan steps in, turns round, and the
plaster takes him back over five seconds. Readers who can see the frame are told. Then Daniel asks Rae what the old dog was called; she
turns to the nearest reader and tells it (Pilot); he says the name again under his breath. Readers within hearing keep that.

Whoever is in Daniel's room, the parlor or the hall when it begins, Dan's walk starts from there and goes through the real doors.
