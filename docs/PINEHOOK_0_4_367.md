## The crisis

The crisis (`CRISIS`, phase 7) comes 3600 ticks into the night. It comes sooner, 400 ticks after Casey has turned, if Len's envelope is out of the safe by then: *"Early evidence or a direct confession can move the story forward sooner."*

When it comes:

- **Casey.**
  - **Turned:** he stands in the kitchen and tells everyone near that the mask was his: the knocking, the breaker, Jude in the costume. Not the shed. Not the knife.
  - **Not turned, costume still in the rowboat:** he goes down the dock and drops the bundle over the side.
  - **Not turned, boat ready:** he rows away in it on his own.
- **Jude** asks someone to stay with him.
- **Nina** goes for Len, unless she is already held, has given up her knife, or has the envelope. From the living room she goes out of the back door and down to the cleat on the dock. After a witnessed shed she comes out of the pines at the beach's west end instead.

### Nina, step by step

1. **At the cleat.**
   - With nobody near, she casts off the rope after a second, and the slip gate swings open.
   - With a reader within three blocks of the cleat, she can't: she stops, takes out the knife and tells them to move.
   - If the slip is already open, she goes straight in.
2. **The water.** She swims to the slip. If the rope is made fast again behind her, she comes back up at the cleat.
3. **Inside the boathouse.**
   - **A reader is already inside:** she stops and holds them off.
   - **The brace is not across the door:** Len gets out through it as she comes in, and runs up to the cabin.
   - **The brace is across the door:** Len can't get out. She goes for him. He shouts for help every three seconds, heard from forty blocks.
     - A reader who comes in by the water during the attack stops it.
     - So does a reader who lifts the brace off the door outside, and Len runs.
     - Twelve seconds unstopped, and the shouting stops. Len lies on the boards.
4. **The safe.** Five seconds at it:
   - **The envelope is there:** she takes it and goes out through the slip into the lake.
   - **It isn't:** she comes back up to the cabin to find who has it.
5. **Standing off or hunting.** She gives up after a minute and goes back to the living room.

*"A successful interruption remains successful."* Once a reader has stood in her way, she never goes back to the attack.

### What can be done with her

These are said to her face, in the crisis (or in the night, once the reader knows about the envelope):

- **"What did Len do to you?"** She tells them about the car, the petrol station, and a year of paying for it.
- **"Give me the knife."** She refuses while Len still has his hold on her. Once it is gone she gives the knife up, to anyone. Len's hold is gone once:
  - the envelope has been burned;
  - it is out in the open (Casey has been told what is in it);
  - or she has it.
- **"It's burned. I put it in the fire."** Only for a reader who burned it. She gives up the knife.
- **"Everyone knows what's in it. Casey knows."** Only once Casey has been told, by a reader who read it or told him. She gives up the knife.
- **"Here. It's what you came for."** For a reader carrying the envelope. She destroys it: in the fire if she is by it, torn up if not. She is done with Len.
- **"I'll open it for you."** On her way down, or at the cleat. The reader casts off the rope for her. Casting it off by hand while she waits does the same.
- **"Get hold of her."**
  - Within reach, with another reader close by, or Casey beside her once he has turned: she is held, hands behind her.
  - Alone: she cuts the reader (never below a heart) and goes past. At the cleat she then casts off.

*"That is an authored negotiation with risks, not a single charm check."*

### Len, out of his boathouse

Len asks whoever carries his envelope for it back:

- **Given back:** his account wins, unless the envelope was already out in the open.
- **Refused:** it stays with the reader.

### Jude

- **Getting colder.** Cut and never dressed, Jude says how cold he is at 1200, 2400 and 3600 ticks into the night.
- **Staying with him.** In the crisis, a reader who stays with him hears who it was:
  - after a wounding: Nina, by her gloves;
  - after a witnessed shed: Nina, with the knife;
  - after a watched shed: someone behind the shed a long time.
- **Taken across.** With the rowboat ready (oars or paddle) and Casey free, a reader can ask Casey to row Jude across to the store, where there's a phone. The two walk down the dock and go. Jude lives whatever his cut. Casey and the boat are gone.

*"Getting a vehicle or boat ready is one practical branch during the crisis. It can evacuate Jude, move Nina, or let Casey flee. It does not stand in for resolving the characters' conflict."*

Here the boat evacuates Jude or lets Casey flee.

## First light

Once Nina is held, disarmed, has given up or is gone, and Casey has finished whatever he was doing, first light (`DAWN`, phase 8) comes twenty seconds later:

- **Jude:** cut, never dressed and never taken across, he is on the kitchen floor and not breathing. *"Do not kill a rescued person at dawn just to restore the body count."* A dressed or evacuated Jude always lives.
- **Len:** alive and shut in with nothing across his door, he comes up to the cabin.

The family is worked out from what actually happened, in this order:

1. **Nina finished it** (`nina`). Len is dead. Or Nina has the envelope (taken from the safe, or handed to her).
2. **The performance became the story** (`performance`). Jude is dead, Casey never turned, and the envelope was never told. Len and Nina put it on the dead actor in the mask.
3. **Both exposed** (`exposed`). The envelope is out in the open.
4. **Nina finished it** (`nina`). The envelope was burned.
5. **Len protected** (`protected`). Anything else: Len alive, Nina stopped, his envelope private, his account accepted.

Then at first light:

- **Exposed:** Casey says he is driving to the store to tell someone everything. If Casey has gone, Jude says it.
- **Protected:** Len tells it his way. A reader who read the envelope can tell him that isn't all of it. (From 0.4.368 this turns the weekend into both exposed; see `docs/PINEHOOK_0_4_368.md`.)
- **Performance:** Len and Nina say it was the actor. A reader who saw anything (prints, the tape, the timeline, the strap, Jude's account) can say *"That isn't what happened"*, and anyone can say *"That's what happened."* *"Do not declare a false public story inevitable when someone has already shown the truth"*: the family needs the envelope untold and Casey never turned, and each account keeps who challenged it.
- **Nina, with Len dead:** if anyone helped her in, Casey asks who opened the slip. A reader who did can say so.

Thirty seconds after first light, everyone who came this weekend is resolved, the absent and the dead included:

- the Witness source;
- their own account from their own record.

A reader who leaves the cabin in the morning is resolved as they go. A reader who isn't online is owed theirs at their next login.
