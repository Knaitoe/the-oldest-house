# The Last Stop, on the northern plains (0.4.286)

The owner: "Consider actual regional landscaping and architecture." The Last Stop is a roadhouse on a two-lane highway on the northern
plains, in North Dakota or eastern Montana. It already had the right shape:

- the highway;
- a gravel lot with three pickups;
- a false-front board-and-batten bar;
- a stubble field behind a field fence.

But it was closed in by spruce and oak woods, with young spruce along the road. That reads as the northern woods. On the plains the
trees are the ones people planted against the wind, in rows:

- red cedar;
- green ash;
- an old cottonwood by the yard.

Along the road are power poles, and behind the lot, grain bins.

This pass gives the Last Stop that look. Nothing in the story moves: the bar, the pickups, the lot, the elk's route through the gap, the
field fence, the field's repeat, the lane, the posts and the broken rail are all where they were. New builds only.

## The lot and the road (`ElkLot.edges`)

- **A windbreak** closes the scene on both sides of the lot and road, in place of the woods:
  - a close row of eastern red cedar along the edge: dense, narrow cones of dark needles from near the ground;
  - three old plains cottonwoods inside it, with thick trunks under broad, loose crowns.
- **Power poles** stand along the road's near side, either side of the reader's door, in place of the young spruce:
  - creosoted poles in the right-of-way fence;
  - crossarms across the line they carry along the road.
- **Two steel grain bins** stand past the lot's east end: corrugated steel under a shallow cone and cap, with a hatch.

## The field (`ElkLot.field`)

- The treelines either side are a planted windbreak instead of random spruce and oak:
  - a close row of red cedar outside;
  - green ash inside it.
- They are still planted only well inside the field's repeat, so no crown crosses its seam, and they are placed by the field's phase,
  so every repeat is identical.
