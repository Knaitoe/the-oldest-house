# The Last Crossing, slice 2: Night One (0.4.352)

The second of the four slices set out in `docs/YARD_STORIES_PROPOSAL.md`, built from the owner's design "The Last Crossing". Slice 1 (`docs/LAST_CROSSING_0_4_351.md`) is the road, the station, Vera and the roll, and Day One. This slice covers:

- the surge;
- the authored first infection and the rescue;
- the source's mobs;
- the infected reader's private symptoms;
- the plate's reading.

It is still not dealt and adds no Witness source; the operator's test door reaches it. At dawn the station holds in the morning light. Day Two is slice 3.

Protocol 147: the station fitting's new states and the new creature. Layout 112 is unchanged; nothing standing is rebuilt.

From the design:

> *"The first surge comes through the intake when the crew puts the pump under load. If they are still finishing the priming at dusk, Vera calls the valve settings from above so they can start. The reader working the inlet meets the surge. A thing comes through the broken corner of the grate and gets one claw in the intake worker before they can pull back. That contact is the authored first infection. A quick rescue still matters. It breaks the grip, saves the worker from further damage, and keeps the intake from flooding the lower landing. The worker keeps their human form for the rest of the night."*

- **Names:** `LastCrossingNight` (the night), `Undertow` (the source's mobs).
- **Clock:** four minutes, 4800 ticks, from dusk. The sky runs from dusk to first light on the station's own clock, and the morning after holds.

## The surge

Vera's last words at dusk ("That's the light going. Get to your places.") start the night. She walks in to the gauge room and keeps the night there, by the landing.

The surge comes ten seconds after the pump goes under load. That is ten seconds into the night if the intake was primed by day, or ten seconds after it is primed once Vera has called the valve down the pipe. If the intake never primes, the flood forces it a minute into the night. Either way:

- The pipes slam and water roars off into them; the sound carries across the station.
- Everyone in the pump room, above or below, remembers hearing it.
- A hand comes up through the grate's broken corner and takes hold of the reader working the inlet.

**Who it takes.** The reader working the inlet is the intake worker, if they are below near the grate. Otherwise it is whoever of the crew is nearest it. If nobody is below, Vera shouts "Below! Somebody get down to the intake!", and the hand waits under the grate for the first of the crew to come near it.

**What the grip does.** Vera shouts "It's got hold of someone! Get them up!" For the reader it takes:

- One claw in. This is the authored first infection and the run's first slot, and only theirs.
- They are told, and nobody else is: "Something comes up through the broken corner and a claw sinks into you. It holds."
- It holds them where they stand.
- It hurts them a little at a time, never below three hearts.

**Who sees it.** Anyone in the intake who can see the grate, or anyone in the gauge room looking down through the landing, sees the hand and remembers it.

## The rescue

There are two ways to break the grip:

- **Strike the hand.** Four blows from the intake break it, struck by the one it holds or by anyone near. Alone, the one it held fought free; with help, they were struck free.
- **Take the hand from above.** A reader in the gauge room leans over the landing's rail and reaches down (a click on one of its bars). The one held is offered "Take the hand." for five seconds. Taking it pulls them up over the rail into the gauge room, beside the one who reached. Both remember who pulled whom up.

While the grip lasts, the intake fills. Someone on the gauge room's brake slows it. Holding the brake keeps the pump fighting the water; crouching on it latches it for fifteen seconds, which frees the gauge reader to go to the landing. With nobody on the brake, the intake fills twice as fast.

When the grip breaks:

- the needle drops into the service branches, and whoever is at the gauge sees the pressure go out;
- Vera calls "Get them up here, into the light.";
- the carrier runs with water and bleeds for half a minute. Everyone in the gauge room who sees them come up remembers it.

**The lower floor lost.** Left too long (twenty seconds, or ten with nobody on the brake), the lower floor floods to a head's height:

- The grip lets go as the water comes over them.
- Everyone below comes up the stair streaming.
- Vera calls "We've lost the lower floor. Shut the isolation valve by the landing!"

The lower floor stays flooded for the run; Day Two will have the longer way round. The isolation valve by the landing keeps the upper floor dry. Left open for thirty seconds, every other one of the things from the water comes up through the landing instead of over the spillway.

## The carrier

Two seconds after coming free, the carrier alone hears a second breath under their own. They are told "You take one breath, and under it another answers." Then: "You'll change at the next dusk. You know it the way you know your own hands."

After that, every forty-five seconds the second breath comes again, heard by nobody else. Every other time it comes with a line of its own:

- "Your breath comes twice."
- "Under your own breathing, another breath, slower."
- "The water in the channel sounds almost like talk."

At dawn: "The light comes up. You're still yourself, for today." They keep their human form all night.

A carrier who drops out while the hand holds them gets their beat when they come back to the station. A carrier who died here has no beat and no second breath; they watch from the far bank.

**Telling, the plate, or keeping it.** As the design has it, they can tell anyone, ask for the plate, or finish the shift quietly.

- **Vera.** Her panel offers the carrier "It got a claw in me.", said aloud over their head. Everyone within earshot remembers who said it. She answers: "Then you know what the plate will show. Show them or don't, that's yours. Just don't go near the water on your own."
- **The plate.** A palm on it shows fog on the near side and the second print closing round the hand on the far side, to everyone watching. The carrier's record keeps that their reading exposed them; slice 4's bargain needs it. Each watcher keeps whose second print they saw.

## The source's mobs

**Undertow.** The things that come up out of the water at night, what the design calls the source's mobs, have their own skin on a zombie's body: drowned skin, sodden rags, weed and silt in the hair, the eyes gone dark and the mouth hanging.

- They don't burn by day, turn into anything in the water, or break doors.
- They carry and drop nothing and call nobody else.
- They groan through water and drip as they go.
- A reader killed by one is dead: they watch the rest of the crossing from the far bank. The House's ordinary death applies.

**When they come.** From twenty seconds into the night they climb out over the spillway's banks one at a time, every fifteen seconds. They come up west of it toward the pump room and the ramp, and east of it by the cable house's door. There are as many of them as the crew, at least three and at most six. They go for whoever is nearest, or make for the pump room's door. Anything of theirs that leaves the station goes back into the water. The last thirty seconds bring no more. At dawn they all go back into the water.

**Wounds.** A clean reader they hit is cut. They remember it ("It opens a cut across your arm. Only a cut.") and stay clean: no second infection comes on this night.

**The cable house's door.** A heavy bar leans inside the cable house by the door. Dropping it across the doorway shuts the door and stops anything coming through; it stands higher than a jump. Lifting it stands it back by the door. Left open and unwatched, one of them in the cable house for three seconds knocks the seated roller off its cradle again. That is evidence for the morning.

## The first rise

Eighty seconds into the night the water lifts, and the ferry rides up on its sill and strains at its line.

- **Made fast in time.** If the line was staged at the head of the ramp by day, it holds. If someone throws a turn of the loose line round the cleat in the next twenty seconds, or makes the line in hand fast there, it holds too. Everyone at the ramp then sees the ferry settle back against its sill.
- **Not made fast.** Otherwise the ferry swings off its sill and fetches up against the cut, grinding. If the line was loose on the ramp, it snaps. The ferry is loose for the run.

## The rest of the crew

The design gives each reader their own side of the night:

- the gauge reader on the brake, the latch, or the landing;
- the lookout on the cable house's bar, or leaving it to help at the intake;
- the landing hand at the first rise.

Those beyond four can reach the intake first, cover the lookout, or carry the station lamp down into the stairwell. There is a new hook at the foot of the stair, and the lamp hung there at night lights the way up.

## What each reader's record keeps

To slice 1's record, Night One adds:

- being gripped, the infection and its slot, and how the grip broke (fought, struck, hand or flood) and by whom;
- the private beat and the second breaths;
- seeing the claw, seeing someone come up streaming, and seeing the pressure go out at the gauge;
- the brake held or latched;
- the lower floor lost, and the isolation valve;
- the cable house barred or unbarred;
- seeing the things come up, being cut, and seeing the roller fouled;
- the ferry held or seen swing off its sill;
- the lamp hung on the stair;
- saying it aloud, and whose words were heard;
- the plate's exposure, and whose second prints were seen;
- Night One worked;
- death, and watching from the far bank.

## Reset

The night's things go back into the water. Then:

- the lower floor is drained;
- the grate is whole but for its corner;
- the bar leans by the door;
- the isolation valve is open;
- the stair's hook is empty;
- the cable house's door is shut;
- the dead are cleared with the rest of the crew.
