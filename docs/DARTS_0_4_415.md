# Darts (0.4.415)

The second part of Last orders (docs/BAR_GAMES_PROPOSAL.md): a dartboard that plays real darts, thrown in first person, for one to
four players. As with the pool table, nothing in this release hangs a board anywhere. The bar in 0.4.416 is the first place to have
one, and the keepsake that lets a reader make one at home comes in 0.4.417. Until then the board is only in the game through `/give`.

## The board

- **Hanging it:** a dartboard goes on the side of a solid block, face out. It needs that wall behind it and falls off if the wall
  goes. Breaking it drops it once.
- **Look:** a standard board drawn in code (`tools/draw_bar_art.py`) at 128 pixels to the block.
  - The numbers run 20 at the top, 6 at the right, 3 at the bottom and 11 at the left, in the standard order.
  - The singles are black and cream; the doubles and trebles are red and green; the bull is red inside a green outer bull.
  - Thin wires separate the rings and segments, and the numbers sit on a dark ring round the edge.
- **Size:** the board fills its block's face. Its rings keep a standard board's proportions, with the double's outer wire 7 pixels
  from the centre, so the whole board is about twice the size of a real one.

## Playing

- **Taking a place:** using the board before the first dart takes a place, up to four players ("You chalk up 301. Three darts a
  turn."). Anyone who comes after the first dart, or makes a fifth, watches.
- **Choosing the game:** crouching at the board before the first dart switches between 301 and round the clock.
- **Darts:** whoever's turn it is holds three of the house's darts, in their hand if it is free.
  - When the turn is thrown, bust or won, the darts stay where they stuck for two seconds so everyone can see them.
  - Then they are pulled out with a sound, and the next player is handed three.
- **The house's darts:** they belong to the board and never leave it.
  - Dropped, or left behind by a death, they vanish.
  - They are taken back from anyone whose turn it is not, who walks more than ten blocks away, or whose board is gone.
- **Throwing:** hold use to raise a dart, and let go to throw it.
  - The aim settles for about half a second, holds steady for most of a second, then sways more and more the longer it is held.
    Crouching halves the sway.
  - The view sways with the aim, so the dart goes where the crosshair was.
  - The server works out the sway from how long the dart was held, in the same way, so the throw is the same on every screen.
  - A dart held for less than a fifth of a second is not thrown.
  - The dart flies at a little over a block a tick with a slight drop, so a throw from the oche wants a touch of height.
- **Scoring:** a dart that sticks in the board's face scores the ring and number at the exact point it struck.
  - Anywhere else counts as a miss: the wall, the board's edge, a person (harmlessly), or nowhere at all within three seconds.
  - Each dart is called on the thrower's screen: "Treble 20. 121 left." or "Missed. Still on 7."
- **Leaving:** a player who walks off, changes dimension or logs out gives up their place.
  - The others keep their scores.
  - A turn that was theirs passes on.
- **The chalk:** the score is chalked on a slate on the wall to the board's right, for everyone within 32 blocks. It shows:
  - the game being played;
  - each player's name, with what they have left or the number they are on;
  - a mark against whoever is throwing;
  - the darts of the turn (T20, D5, 25, B).

  The server sends it again every five seconds, so someone who walks up mid-game can read it.

## The games

`DartsGame` is the whole game in plain Java:

- **The board's geometry:** from where a dart lands it gives the ring (single, double, treble, outer bull, bull) and number. It uses
  a standard board's wire radii (bull 6.35 mm, outer bull 15.9 mm, treble 99 to 107 mm, double 162 to 170 mm) and number order.
- **301:** each player starts on 301 and counts down to exactly nothing. A dart that would go below nothing is a bust: the turn ends
  and the score goes back to where it began. Reaching nothing exactly wins.
- **Round the clock:** 1 to 20 in order, any ring of the number, then the outer bull or the bull to finish.
- **Turns:** three darts a turn, then the next player.

## The board's own players

A story can seat the board's own players by name, each with a skill from 0 to 1.

- **Throwing:** on its turn, a regular throws real darts from the oche, about two and a third blocks out from the face, a second and
  a half apart.
- **Aiming:** it aims as a sensible player would:
  - in 301, trebles while there is plenty to score, then a finish;
  - in round the clock, its number.

  Its skill decides how far the dart wanders.
- **Keeping the board:** a story's own board keeps playing while only its regulars are at it, so the bar's regulars can play among
  themselves.
- **Listening:** a story can listen to every dart and to the game's end.

This is the hook for the regulars in 0.4.416.

## What is new

- **Blocks and items:** the Dartboard block, block entity and item, and the Dart item and entity, in `BarRegistry`.
- **Sounds:** four subtitled sounds ("Dart thrown", "Dart hits the board", "Dart hits the wall", "Darts pulled from the board"),
  synthesized by `tools/generate_bar_audio.py`.
- **Protocol 170:** `DartsStatePayload`, the game as it stands, sent to everyone near the board.
- **Pool:** the table's frame is now also sent again every five seconds, for the same reason as the chalk.
- **Tests:** three cases in the literary namespace.
  - `BarGamesTests#dartsScoreWhereTheyLandAndKeepTheRules`:
    - the bull, outer bull, trebles, doubles and singles fall where they should, with the wire between 20 and 1 nine degrees round;
    - aiming at any treble lands in it;
    - three darts end a turn and a fourth counts for nothing;
    - going over is a bust and exactly nothing wins;
    - round the clock goes in order, then the bull;
    - a player leaving keeps the others' scores;
    - a game saves and loads as it was.
  - `BarGamesTests#readersThrowRealDartsInTurn`:
    - a reader hangs the board from the item;
    - crouching changes the game and changes it back;
    - two readers chalk up and only the first holds darts;
    - a real dart thrown from the oche lands, scores where it lands and stays stuck;
    - a late comer watches, and a dart that is not the house's, or not their throw, goes nowhere;
    - three darts end the turn and empty the hand, stay visible, are pulled out, and the next player is handed three;
    - a reader who walks off takes their place and darts with them while the one left keeps their score;
    - broken, the board drops once and its game and darts go.
  - `BarGamesTests#theBoardsOwnPlayerThrowsOnItsTurn`: a regular throws its three darts on its turn and a steady hand finds the board;
    then the reader is handed three.

Layout 134 and Witness 85/64/62 are unchanged. Games are not saved: a server restart leaves the board on the wall and starts afresh.
Thrown darts are not saved either.
