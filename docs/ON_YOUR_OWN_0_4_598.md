## The Last Crossing, with one reader

### What was in the way

Reading the three nights for a crew of one found three things.

1. **Two jobs take two readers at the same moment.** On the last night the sluice wheel is held shut at the bottom of the pit while another reader drops the pin from the deck, and the feed gate is held while another drops the bar. On the second night the brake is held through the pull while somebody else clears what binds. And on the first day Vera has the crew practise the launch: "One of you down on the wheel, one up here on the pin."
2. **A reader alone is nearly always the carrier.** The first night's hand takes whoever goes down to the intake, and Vera calls for somebody to go down.
3. **The last night ended at once for a carrier alone.** The source takes the crossing when nobody clean of the crew is standing, checked once a second. With one name on the roll and that reader carrying it, the night was over a second after dusk.

The days were already built so one reader can work them: both sides of the intake by running between them, the drum lined up with the parking catch, the door shoved, the debris cleared, the pin and the bar carried down. The first night too: a reader held by the hand can beat it off alone, and the brake has a latch.

### The short-handed rules

A crew with fewer than four names on its roll is **short-handed**. That is one reader, or two, or three.

- **Vera is the other pair of hands**, where she already stands.
  - *The pull.* When the hand on the brake lets go while the drum is hauling or binding, Vera takes the brake herself. Nothing runs back. She keeps it till the drum is taken up, or till a reader takes it back from her.
  - *The launch.* With the pin up in its sleeve, a reader who has held the wheel shut for three seconds hears her drop it: "Pin's in! Let go the wheel!" She walks over to it from the ramp first.
  - *The rehearsal*, for a reader who is the whole roll. On the first day she goes down to the pin herself and says what to do. The wheel held shut for three seconds and she drops the pin, as she will on the last night. Two or three readers practise it between them, as a crew does.
- **The bar is in reach.** A reader holding the feed gate, with the bar staged in its bracket by day (or carried down in their hand), knocks it down across the gate themselves.
- **As many things from the water as the crew.** One at a time for a reader alone, two for two, three for three. It was never fewer than three.
- **A carrier still counts.** On the last night the source does not take the crossing only because nobody clean is standing, while a carrier of the crew is standing who has not sided with it and has not gone across. They can still stop it.

And a reader who is the whole roll is told so where the story speaks of the crew: in what Vera answers when asked what needs doing, in the account, and in the Witness line.

Everything else is the owner's rule as it stood:

- the ferry's crossing is the crew's only if somebody aboard is clean;
- the master bypass turns only for a turned reader, and the bargain is theirs;
- the night running out gives the crossing to the source.

### What that makes of one reader's crossing

- **Clean** (they never went down to the intake on the first night). They can launch: wheel, Vera's pin, the rope from the deck, and across with her. Or clear the station.
- **Carrying it.** Three roads, and one of them is a win.
  - *Clear the station.* Hold the gate, knock the bar down, wind the cutoff shut, fight the things off the source and kill it. Its death lets them go, and the station is cleared by the one it had taken.
  - *Cross with it.* Vera drops the pin for them as for anyone. The ferry comes in with nobody clean aboard, and what crossed is the source.
  - *Side with it.* The master bypass at high water, held till the bargain is done. Then nobody of the crew is left to stop it.

### New lines

All of these are mine and are for the owner to change.

Vera, to a short-handed crew:

- When she calls the pull, after her own line: "If something binds, go to it. I'll take the brake."
- Taking the brake: "I've got the brake. Go!"
- On the third day, asked what needs doing, and again in her word before dark: "Short as you are, I'll take the pin tonight. You take the wheel."
- Asked at night what to do, after her own answer. Second night: "If you have to leave the brake, I'll take it." Last night: "I'm on the pin. Hold the wheel till you hear me."

Vera, to a reader who is the whole roll:

- At the rehearsal, in place of "Let's try it. One of you down on the wheel, one up here on the pin. Two knocks, and the pin goes in.": "Let's try it. You go down on the wheel and hold it shut. I'll drop the pin from up here."
- Asked what needs doing, after her own answer. First day: "On your own, take it by turns: read the gauge first, then go below." Second day: "On your own, the parking catch will hold the drum while you walk down to read the marks."

What the reader is told:

- Taking the brake back from her: "You take the brake back from Vera."
- Holding the gate with the bar staged: "You throw your weight on the gate. The feed hammers behind it. The bar is in its bracket, in reach of your hand." (A turned reader gets their own line, then "The bar is in its bracket, in reach of your hand.")
- Knocking it down: "You keep your shoulder to the gate and knock the bar out of its bracket. It drops across with a clang."
- When Vera drops the pin in practice: "Above you the pin drops into its socket. The wheel stops fighting."

In the account, for a reader who was the whole roll or was helped:

- "The keeper, Vera, had me sign a roll on the porch and called my name aloud." (was "had us sign", "called every name")
- "I took the winch brake off for the pull, and Vera took it from me when I had to leave it."
- "On the last night I held the sluice wheel at the bottom of the flooded pit until Vera dropped the pin home from the deck."
- "I held the feed gate in the gallery with my shoulder and knocked the bar down across it myself."
- "Vera marked me down as out of the gallery." (was "as one of the ones who came out of the gallery with her")
- "I laid my palm on the plate and let Vera see a second print come up on the far side." (was "let the others see")

The Witness line, for a reader who was the whole roll:

- "I worked three days and three nights at a flood station on my own, for a keeper who called the roll every morning: my name, and one name more at the end of it. On the last night the river came up to the ramp, and we tried to get across." (a crew's says "with a crew, for a keeper who called the roll every morning and one name more at the end of it")

One line is changed for every crew, because the same line is given to a reader alone. It is the hint a reader gets on reading the source:

- "Sign the roll on the porch. Work the station before dark." (was "Work the station with the others before dark.")

### What is not changed

- The nameplate. With one name on the roll the one piece carries the whole name, and the spare still goes only to a reader who came through clean.
