# Bike night 13: the town (0.4.481)

The fourth slice of the proposal's build order (`docs/BIKE_NIGHT_PROPOSAL.md`, "Act two: the town" and "The town's detail"):
Calder Falls north of the park, standing. This is the streets, the buildings and their dressing. Nothing moves in it yet but
the bank's clock and the theater's marquee: the cars, the people on the pavements and the hunt come next.

The owner, on seeing the town as first built, on a grid:

> the neighborhood looks good but would look better with less even streets, some suburb shortcuts, etc

> clutter and decor in alleys would benefit it too. space between buildings, dumpsters, trash

And, while it was being rebuilt: "do you have a path to the cemetery planned?"

## The streets

They are not a grid. Each street is a centre line with a width, and a place on the ground is road, kerb or pavement by how
near it is to the nearest centre line (`CalderTown.surface`). So a street can slant, bend or go round, its corners come out
round, and two streets that meet are joined without a seam.

- **Park Street** runs along the park's north side, and turns into an avenue at each end.
- **Main Street** is the wide one, east and west through the middle of town.
- **Mill Street** goes from the park's gate up to Main Street. Across Main it steps two blocks east and is narrower
  (Mill Street north), and it ends on Elm Street.
- **West Avenue** and **East Avenue** go up the two sides of the town. Each bends in toward the middle part of the way up.
- **Elm Street** slants: its east end is eight rows north of its west end.
- **Elm Court** is a cul-de-sac off Elm Street, with a turning circle at its head and no line down its middle.
- **Church Lane** goes round the foot of the cemetery's hill, and is nearest the cemetery in its middle, at the gate.

Main Street is nine blocks of road across, Park Street and the south of Mill Street seven, and the rest five. A straight
run has a line down its middle; crossings, bends and the court have none.

## What stands on them

- **South of Main Street:** Hale Hardware (rakes fanned in its window, shut), Calder Savings with its clock over its name,
  and the theater, under a marquee out over the pavement. Three one-sheets hang in lit cases down the theater's side on
  Mill Street.
- **North of Main Street:** the Wash-O-Mat (dryers behind the glass, a payphone on the wall), the Video Hut, and the
  barber's with its pole, which turns.
- **Big Top Burgers,** in its own lot between Park Street and Main: neon front and back, an arcade cabinet in the window,
  and a drive-thru lane down its east side inside a chain-link fence. The lot is fenced across, so the lane is the way
  through it.
- **The Calder Food Fair** and its lot, the week's specials on butcher paper in the windows, a cart left out.
- **Calder Jr. High,** dark. Its field has yard lines chalked across it and a scoreboard on the school's wall, switched
  off with the last game still on it. Bleachers stand along the north side, raised, so there is a way under them.
- **Houses:** six round Elm Court, fifteen along the far sides of the two avenues, and one by the empty lot. Each has a
  door, two windows and a porch.
- **The cemetery's railings** along the whole north edge, brick piers and iron between, with one gate under an arch that
  reads CALDER HILL CEMETERY. The gate is shut. What is behind it comes with the graveyard.

## The back ways

The owner asked for "some suburb shortcuts". The readers can take any of them; the three know them all.

- **The gaps between the shops.** Two blocks of space between the hardware store and the bank, the bank and the theater,
  the laundromat and the Video Hut, and the Video Hut and the barber. Each goes through from Main Street to the alley
  behind.
- **The two alleys,** one behind each row of shops, gravel, with a board fence along the far side.
- **The loading dock** in the laundromat's alley: boards up onto it at one end, and a drop off the other. (The proposal:
  "Its back alley has a loading dock to jump off.")
- **Boards gone from the alley fence** behind the Video Hut, which lets out onto Elm Street.
- **The drive-thru lane.** (The proposal: "The drive-thru lane is a way through.")
- **The empty lot** beside the theater, with a worn trail across it, a plank ramp on the trail, and boards gone from the
  fence where the trail meets the alley.
- **The school's field.** The wire is cut and pulled back in two places, one due north of the other, and the line between
  them runs along the middle yard line and under the bleachers. (The proposal: "A hole in the fence is a reader-sized
  shortcut across the football field and under the bleachers.")
- **The cut,** a footpath between the school's fence and the back fences of Elm Court, from Elm Street to Church Lane.
- **The path out of the head of Elm Court,** between two houses, to Church Lane.
- **Two back yards** with boards gone from their fences, one onto the cut and one onto East Avenue.

## The way to the cemetery

Every way north comes out on Church Lane, and Church Lane's pavement touches the cemetery's railings at the gate. Five ways
reach it: the two avenues, the cut, the field by its two holes, and the path out of Elm Court. The cut is the straight one.
From the park's gate it is up Mill Street, across Main, up to Elm Street and into the cut, which comes out a few blocks from
the gate.

It is named where it leaves Elm Street: CEMETERY, on a blade like a street's, brown where theirs are green, lying along the
path. Its post stands beside the cut's mouth and not in it.

## Behind the shops

The owner asked for "clutter and decor in alleys ... space between buildings, dumpsters, trash". Each alley, the grocery's
yard and the back of the burger place has its own:

- dumpsters, two blocks long, shut or with the lid thrown back;
- trash bags, in ones and heaps;
- a packing crate, milk crates, pallets lying and stood against a wall;
- cardboard boxes, whole and flattened;
- oil drums, standing and on their sides; tyres, flat, stacked and leaning;
- what lies on the ground: a newspaper come apart, cans, a burger's box and a cup, a puddle;
- drainpipes down the walls; meters, an air conditioner, vents, and a caged bulb over each back door;
- back doors, roll shutters, DELIVERIES over the dock, NO PARKING;
- paint on the walls: TULLY RULES, RAY WUZ HERE, a skull through a stencil; and a LOST CAT paper.

None of it is in a street. What lies on the ground is ridden over; a dumpster, a crate, a drum and a stack of tyres are
ridden round.

## Halloween

Pumpkins, carved in four faces and lit from inside, by most front doors, and by the grocery's and the burger place's. A bat on every front door.
A paper skeleton taped in a house's window, or a lantern and the cat on the sill, or one candle in a dark room. Paper
skeletons in the Video Hut's door and the barber's window. Toilet paper in the tree outside the school.

## Light

Lamps stand at the corners and along Main Street, one at the head of Elm Court and one by the cemetery's gate. There are
none in the alleys, in the cut or on the field. The neon, the lit windows, the marquee, the clock and the pumpkins light
themselves. The lamps by the gaps between the shops stand at a shop's corner beside the gap, and not in its mouth.

## The clock and the marquee

- **The bank's clock** shows the night's own time, which starts at 11:41 and runs a minute for a minute, and for three
  seconds in every ten the temperature, which starts at 41 and loses a degree every five minutes, down to 33. It runs on
  the night's clock, so it stands still while nobody is in town.
- **The marquee** carries one of three double features, and changes to the next each night. (The proposal: "an original
  double feature that changes on each visit.")

## How it is drawn

Everything the town is built of is the mod's own, drawn by tools at sixteen pixels to the block:

- `tools/draw_calder_town_art.py`: the materials (stucco, tan brick, clapboard, concrete, a tarred roof, shingles, a lot's
  asphalt and its bay line, porch boards, gravel, the field's turf plain, tracked and chalked, a hedge), the fittings (one
  block, `calder_town`, nineteen kinds), and the clock's digits (`calder_digit`).
- `tools/draw_calder_signs.py`: every front, name, notice, window and door, as pictures cut into block-sized squares. A
  board that hangs free reads from both sides.

The only vanilla blocks in the town are the grass, the worn earth of the lot's trail and the trees' trunks.

## Tests

Four cases in `BikeNightTests`, each on the town built whole:

- **The town stands north of the park, on streets that are not a grid, built of its own things.** Every place a street
  takes is laid as the street has it; the widths, the slant, the bends, the circle and the lines; each front and name at
  its place; nothing vanilla built; every wall sign and wall fitting with a wall behind it; the gaps open; each alley's
  clutter; lamps on Main Street and none in the dark places; the field's holes, lines and raised bleachers; the dock and
  the ramps; the way to the cemetery named; the railings whole and the gate shut.
- **Nobody gets off the town's ground, and every way through is open.** A child-sized body is walked everywhere it can
  go from Mill Street, a quarter block at a time. It reaches every street and every back way, and never the edge of the
  ground. With the field's two holes stopped it cannot get onto the field at all.
- **A bike is ridden from the park's gate to the cemetery's by the back ways:** through a gap, along an alley, over the
  dock and off its end, through the alley fence, up the court and out by its path.
- **The bank's clock keeps the night's time, and the marquee changes with the night.**

The visual proof (`client.BikeNightVisualProof`) has four new pages: the town's fittings by kind and stage, the back of a
shop put together as the builder puts one, and the fronts, names and notices flat on, on two pages. Every state of the two
new blocks is required to have its model.

The second case found a real fault on its first run: at the foot of each avenue the fence stopped half a block short of
the last house, and a child could walk round the end of it and off the edge of the town. It now ends on two whole posts
against the house's corner.

## What the look found

The town was built and looked at from a child's eye height in fifty-six places (a local render, not part of the build).
What that showed, and what was done:

- A lamp stood in the mouth of each gap between two shops. They were moved to the shops' corners.
- The drive-thru's board was a blank white box from behind. A lit board now shows its letters on both sides.
- The newspaper on the ground read as a ladder: its lines of print were as dark as the gravel. They are paler.
- The field was a lawn. It has yard lines and a scoreboard now.
- The theater's side on Mill Street was a blank wall the length of the block. It has three one-sheets.
- Nothing said where the cemetery was. The cut is named.

## Not here yet

- **Anything that moves:** cars, people on the pavements, the line outside the theater, trick-or-treaters, the stock boy
  and his chain of carts, the teenagers in the burger place's lot.
- **The hunt:** the three splitting up and taking these back ways, being caught, and the way the chase is steered to the
  cemetery's gate.
- **Things that change:** the dryers turning, the grocery's lights going off aisle by aisle, the neon's one bad letter,
  tyre tracks in the dew on the field (the tracked turf is drawn and not yet laid).
- **Sound.** The town is silent.
- **Behind the gate.** The graveyard.

## Not seen

The town has been looked at only in that local render, which draws the blocks at night without the game's own lighting of
what glows. It has not been seen in a client, ridden through, or seen with anybody in it. Whether the streets are uneven
enough, whether there are enough shortcuts and whether the alleys are cluttered enough are the owner's to judge.
