# The Oldest House — correspondence and personal notes

Approved selection, final prose pass • 3 October 2026 • Original fictional text

**108 approved pieces:** fourteen chains of six letters (84), sixteen loose notes, and eight optional notes addressed through actual player actions. This is the approved selection after its final prose pass, incorporated in 0.4.31. IDs and thematic labels are review aids rather than text printed on the letters.

**New in this revision: I–N, thirty-six letters that increasingly address and pressure the reader.** Their complete text appears before the original A–H selection. All letters retain their reviewed IDs; the final pass refines cadence, detail and voice.

The present pool treats “you are useless” as a dominant fixation. I would keep it as one writer's fixation and give the surrounding paper a much wider emotional life. These people want sex, company, money, sleep, recognition, forgiveness, independence, and another good afternoon. Their accusations can be perceptive, unfair, self-serving, or mistaken. Their affection can be genuine even when they are frightened or difficult.

## New direct-address chains: I–N

The escalation changes the relationship as well as the language: welcome becomes a claim; desire becomes jealousy; confession recruits an accomplice; an inventory disputes affection; approval becomes contempt; interest becomes an attempt to take the reader's name. Preserve each writer's hand and verbal habits across all six letters.

Progress follows the reader finding and reading successive original letters, with other rooms and correspondence between them. A letter's threats confer no authority over exits, inventory, companions, story credit, or endings. Claimed insight into the reader's motives remains a fallible character judgment.

| Chain | Initial approach | Growing pressure | Final intrusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I — Somewhere to sit | Welcome and permission to rest | The writer makes attention into a debt | Leaving becomes a personal betrayal in the writer's account |
| J — Only an answer | Attraction and imagined intimacy | Jealousy of other voices and demands for exclusivity | The writer wishes lasting estrangement on the reader |
| K — Please say it | A request for sympathy | Confession turns into a demand for absolution | Reading is recast as accepting responsibility |
| L — Kept | Recognition of a real lost possession | The writer questions the reader's attachment and generosity | The reader's ownership is crossed out on the page |
| M — Well done | Recognition and encouragement | Criteria move; effort becomes evidence of inadequacy | The writer withdraws recognition and belittles the desire for it |
| N — Your account | An invitation to say who you are | The writer appropriates silence, name, and signature | The letter claims the reader's identity |

## I — Somewhere to sit

**An unnamed host.** The voice begins considerate and becomes wounded, possessive, and punitive. It resents the visitor's ability to leave. Its coercion is visible in the text, while leaving remains an ordinary player choice.

**I01 — Welcome**

> Sit down for a while. You can tell me how you got here later.
>
> I have tried three ways of making this sound welcoming. They all sound as though I want a favour. For now, I can manage the silence.

**I02 — Again**

> Another letter. I thought you might come across it.
>
> I caught myself leaving a space for you at the end of a sentence. I have begun thinking of you as somebody who returns. You could warn me if you intend to stop.

**I03 — Consideration**

> You are still reading. I have taken some trouble over what to say to you.
>
> A few minutes. That is all I am asking for. You ought to consider how long it took me to ask before deciding you have somewhere better to be.

**I04 — A guest**

> A guest would ask how I am. You move through these pages as though finding them were the only thing that happened.
>
> There is somebody at the other end of a letter. I should not have to explain that to a person who has accepted this much kindness.

**I05 — Easy**

> You are still reading. I could tell you something kind now and make all of this bearable again.
>
> A place has been kept for you. Somebody is pleased you arrived. There: look how little I had to say. It is almost embarrassing to have that much power over someone.

**I06 — Departures**

> Leave, then. I will put your name beneath UNGRATEFUL and make the next letter sound welcoming.
>
> The next person will ask what happened to you. I will say I did everything I could.
>
> I have had a great deal of practice saying that.

## J — Only an answer

**An anonymous adult admirer.** The desire is imagined and declared as such. The writer gradually resents the reader's independent attention. Its final wish is an emotional curse rather than a mechanical punishment.

**J01 — Weather**

> This was going to be a note about the rain. You can see where I began. I have crossed it out so thoroughly the paper is soft there.
>
> I would rather know what you sound like when you stop being careful. I have been imagining it all afternoon. It has made the weather quite difficult to care about.

**J02 — A hand**

> I have imagined taking your hand while you read, putting my thumb in the crease of your palm. You would lose your place and look up, annoyed.
>
> I have imagined the annoyance too. I want something that interrupts me when I make it too pleasant. I want you to have something to say.

**J03 — Other letters**

> If another letter says it loves you, look at the hand. Someone had to sit there making those words.
>
> I know how many times a sentence can be taken apart. How long an afternoon gets when you are trying to sound easy to love. Remember mine when you turn the page.

**J04 — A collection**

> Are you making a collection? The affectionate one, the frightened one, the one who wants you so badly it is difficult to read?
>
> A whole drawer of people. You can shut it when you are tired. We will still be in there, wanting exactly what we wanted when you opened it.

**J05 — Appetite**

> You want another hungry letter. You can enjoy how much it wants from you and set it down when the wanting becomes inconvenient.
>
> Have you any idea how contemptible that feels from this end? I keep trying to write something you will keep. I catch myself wondering whether the next sentence will be good enough. You have made me pathetic. I am getting tired of forgiving you for it.

**J06 — Good wishes**

> I could say I hope you find someone else. I do not.
>
> I hope every room feels occupied before you enter it. I hope every laugh sounds like the end of a conversation you were too late to hear.
>
> I hope you spend the rest of your life feeling you interrupted something.

## K — Please say it

**An unnamed confessor.** The wrongdoing belongs to the fictional writer. The reader is drawn toward it by successive disclosures. The final pressure concerns having heard the confession; the letter never invents a crime committed by the player.

**K01 — A little kindness**

> There must be something you would explain differently if someone would listen long enough.
>
> I am asking for that much kindness. Read what happened before deciding what sort of person could have done it. I have spent years trying to decide that myself.

**K02 — Awake**

> I said I had been asleep. I was awake. I had turned the pillow over because it was too warm.
>
> I heard the knocking and lay still. If I answered, the night would become mine to deal with. I had several prettier explanations ready before I wrote that one.

**K03 — The latch**

> I got up. I put my hand on the latch. I could hear somebody breathing against the other side.
>
> I held the latch until the knocking stopped. Afterwards I said the door must have stuck.
>
> I have had to repeat that often enough to remember exactly how I first said it.

**K04 — Your position**

> Now you can be disgusted. Afterwards is a comfortable place to arrive.
>
> Put your hand on the door. Take it off. Take all night deciding what sort of person you would have been. Nobody is breathing against it now. You should remember that when you describe yourself to me.

**K05 — An ending**

> Would you like me to confess a worse thing? It would give you somewhere definite to put me. You could finish the letter feeling certain.
>
> I can feel myself trying to help you do that. Even now I want to be useful to the person judging me. It makes me very angry with you.

**K06 — In company**

> Read the sentence again: I held the latch until the knocking stopped.
>
> You have given me somewhere else to put it. I have been writing to somebody who stayed long enough to hear the whole thing.
>
> I will remember you that way. You can spend as long as you like explaining what you meant by staying.

## L — Kept

**An unidentified keeper of records.** Begin only when a real original possession has entered this reader's Mother custody record. Bind `{possession}` and `{reader}` to those native facts. Later letters refer to that recorded loss; they do not assert that the object is still held after recovery. This voice is not automatically the Mother of Lost Things.

**L01 — Found**

> {possession}. Your name is beside the entry.
>
> It is a small comfort to find a name where something has gone missing. Somebody can still be asked what it meant to them.
>
> What did this one mean to you?

**L02 — Familiar**

> I have been thinking about the entry. It is easy to imagine a hand closing around something familiar.
>
> Tell me something you remember doing with it before there was any reason to write it down. People become very tender once an inventory has been opened.

**L03 — Importance**

> Give something to another person and you may never know what they did with it. Lose it and every detail begins to matter.
>
> I am trying to work out which part of this deserves sympathy. Your name beside the entry seems to be doing most of the work.

**L04 — Generosity**

> You would like me to admire how much it means to you. Everybody wants their attachment treated as a virtue.
>
> You can be generous with anything you expect to get back. I wonder how much affection would survive an unsigned receipt.

**L05 — A list**

> You are reading an account of something you wanted to keep. You seem to think that makes it an account of something you loved.
>
> I have known people who could barely tell the difference. Living beside them was exhausting. Everything needed their permission to leave.

**L06 — The correction**

> ~~OWNER: {reader}~~
>
> NAME CLAIMED: {reader}
>
> There. I have made the entry more accurate. You can dislike the correction as much as you like.
>
> You have been very patient while I wrote about the missing thing. It will be interesting to see how patient you are when I write about you.

## M — Well done

**A condescending assessor.** Recognition attracts the reader, then becomes conditional and is withdrawn. The dry voice gathers contempt without becoming supernatural exposition. The assessor's criteria have no connection to actual Witness eligibility or progression.

**M01 — Attention**

> Thank you for reading carefully. We were beginning to wonder whether anyone would give a whole page their attention.
>
> We will leave another for you. It is pleasant to have somebody worth preparing it for.

**M02 — Thoroughness**

> You found the next letter. Good. A careful person can make a great deal of a small opportunity.
>
> Continue. We would like to be able to say more about you than that you followed directions.

**M03 — Initiative**

> You have followed the sequence very carefully. We have been waiting to see what you would do without one.
>
> Please do not confuse finding another instruction with showing initiative. Someone has had to prepare each of these opportunities for you.

**M04 — The difficulty**

> The difficulty is becoming clear. We tell you there is more to read and you read it. We withhold an explanation and you search for one.
>
> It is very easy to occupy you. We have spent rather longer trying to discover what else there is to work with.

**M05 — Recognition**

> You are waiting for us to admit how much effort this has cost you. You would like the distance to mean something about the person who covered it.
>
> We are beginning to understand why you continued. It was an unfortunate reason to bring to people who write assessments for a living.

**M06 — The result**

> We have withdrawn the first letter's praise. It has encouraged an impression of yourself that the subsequent letters do very little to support.
>
> You may keep the page if it comforts you. It should be enough to know how little was required to produce that comfort.

## N — Your account

**A correspondent whose own signature becomes less legible.** Curiosity gives way to appropriation. `{reader}` is the actual in-game profile name, bound when the original appears. Claims about motive are the writer's guesses. Any signature imitation is fictional text on this writer's page; player-authored books and signed messages remain intact.

**N01 — Introductions**

> Tell me what you would like me to know about you. Something small will do. I have asked people for their life stories and received very little that sounded lived in.
>
> What would you mention if you thought we were going to meet again?

**N02 — Your name**

> {reader}. I have written it several times to see whether it grows familiar.
>
> It has. I would like to know how long it took you to get used to being called that. I can manage the rest of the introduction myself if you are busy.

**N03 — The gaps**

> I have begun filling the gaps. You can correct a word or two and leave the rest. Explaining yourself from the beginning would take so long, and I have already done most of the work.
>
> It gets easier each time. A little correction, then your name at the bottom. Eventually you will wonder which part needed correcting.

**N04 — In your hand**

> I tried your name as a signature. It looks quite natural beneath something I have written.
>
> You ought to be more careful about where you find yourself familiar. There are people who will accept a name at the bottom of a page and ask nothing else of it.

**N05 — Corrections**

> You can tell me I have misunderstood you. You can make a very long account of the differences.
>
> I shall put it with the other correspondence. It will be another page carrying your name. I can already imagine somebody thanking me for having kept so much of you.

**N06 — The introduction**

> My name is {reader}. I have been reading letters in a place I intended to leave.
>
> That is enough for an introduction. You can cross it out. You will still know which name belongs there.
>
> You have had quite a long time to say something else about yourself. I have made a start for you.

## A — After nine

**Iris to Leon.** Adult desire, domestic affection, irritation, absence, and the limits of identifying with someone else's love letter. Iris is impatient, funny, physically specific, and capable of saying no.

**A01 — Another chance**

> Leon—You laughed through the part where I was trying to be impressive. I've been angry about it all afternoon. Come back tonight. I want another chance to fail in front of you.
>
> —Iris

**A02 — After nine**

> After nine. Knock properly; the wall is thin and Mrs Vale has very little else to do.
>
> I have been trying to work. Mostly I have been thinking about the place where your neck disappears into your shirt.

**A03 — The rent**

> We can pay the rent on Friday if we stop buying wine. I am prepared to stop buying the bad wine.
>
> You came home smelling of sawdust yesterday. I wanted to kiss you before you washed. You looked so surprised I nearly laughed. Have you forgotten what we moved in together for?

**A04 — Whole news**

> I feel petulant. Are these even reaching you?
>
> Yesterday I put on the dress you said you liked. I caught the hem in the drawer and swore at you for being elsewhere. Then I took it off. That is the whole news from here.
>
> Please tell me something equally unimportant. I am tired of hearing only about things worth leaving for.

**A05 — Breakfast**

> I keep writing COME HOME when what I mean is: sleep with me, steal the blanket, complain about the coffee. Stay long enough to be irritating again.
>
> I have begun remembering you as a very considerate man. You should come and correct that.

**A06 — Wrong recipient**

> Someone has written another name across this envelope.
>
> If it is yours, fold the letter back up. I loved a particular, aggravating person. You do not become him because you are lonely.
>
> There is a letter for you somewhere. This one cost me too much to give away.

## B — The window seat

**Mae to her adult daughter Nora.** A relationship informed by the Whalestoe correspondence: loving, sometimes demanding, able to revise itself. Mae receives care in a residential institution. Its staff and her perceptions remain individual and mixed. The closing letter is affectionate and painful rather than a monster reveal.

**B01 — Coat**

> Nora, I shall ask whether you have a warm coat until one of us dies. You may as well prepare for it.
>
> I know you are grown. I am also grown, and would very much like someone to ask whether my feet are cold.

**B02 — The promotion**

> I told everyone about your promotion. I described a much larger office because I could not bear how little they seemed to understand it.
>
> Send me something dull about the job. The sort of thing you complain about over supper. I want to know your life between the good news.

**B03 — Kindness**

> The nurse helped me wash my hair. She was patient. I complained that the water was too hot, then too cold. It was the same water.
>
> It is difficult to forgive someone for seeing you need help. I apologised afterwards, which was almost as difficult. Please do not tell me that I have always been strong. I was tired. She stayed.

**B04 — Garden report**

> Lately the garden is open after lunch.
>
> Everyone says the roses are doing well.
>
> The room gets plenty of sun.
>
> My sleep is improving.
>
> Even my appetite has returned.
>
> On Thursdays we have music.
>
> Usually I sit by the window.
>
> Tomorrow I expect another ordinary day.

*Review annotation: sentence initials read LET ME OUT. This is optional extra meaning, with no reward or required puzzle attached.*

**B05 — Correction**

> Do not leave your work because of the last letter. I wanted you here and made the whole world sound like an emergency.
>
> The garden really does open after lunch. Some days I cannot make myself go into it.
>
> I should have told you that part.

**B06 — A visitor**

> I called a visitor by your name. She corrected me very gently, which made it worse.
>
> She came back today and read to me. Her name is Helen. I wrote it here so I could greet her properly.
>
> We had a good afternoon. I still looked at the door whenever someone passed.

## C — What I left out

**Noel to his sister Ada, with one reply.** A confession about an estranged dying father. Noel starts by arranging the listener's sympathy, then supplies the facts he withheld. Ada refuses the role he has assigned her. The final confession makes a limited attempt at accountability.

**C01 — An explanation**

> Ada—There is something I should have told you years ago.
>
> I keep beginning with how young I was. We were the same age as people who raised children, drove buses, and signed for houses. I will try again without that beginning.

**C02 — The message**

> Father asked for you that afternoon. I told him you were on your way.
>
> I had not called. I knew you would come, and I knew he would brighten when you did. I wanted a little time when I was the child he was pleased to see.

**C03 — The paper**

> He wrote something for you. Six words. I burned the paper with the envelopes after the funeral.
>
> “Tell Ada I kept the drawing.”
>
> You spent twenty years believing he had thrown it away. I let you spend another ten. I keep looking at that sentence for somewhere to put an excuse.

**C04 — The favour**

> If you answer, please tell me you understand.
>
> I had written that at the top. I can see what a filthy favour it is to ask. I have given you a worse memory and am waiting for you to make mine easier.

**C05 — Ada's reply**

> I have read your letter. I have put it away. Those are the two things I can tell you.
>
> Stop asking what this means for us. You have had thirty years to decide when I could know. I would like a week in which nobody asks me to decide anything.

**C06 — Six words**

> I wrote the six words again on a clean sheet and put it with the drawing. I nearly added an account of how sorry I am.
>
> I took that page out. The drawing and his sentence are in the envelope. I will leave them at your door on Friday. You can decide whether to open it.

## D — A good reference

**Ansel's drafts to former colleague Beth.** Envy, class, ambition, attraction to another person's confidence, and hatred that damages its writer. Most of these letters appear unsent. The ending retains the spite rather than curing him.

**D01 — Congratulations**

> Congratulations. Nobody was surprised, which must be pleasant.
>
> I hope the new office is worth losing the ability to enter a room without everyone straightening their backs.
>
> I tried to write something warmer. You would have recognised the effort.

**D02 — Earned**

> You say you earned everything you have. It must be wonderfully restful to believe the people below you earned that too.
>
> Your father paid your rent for three years. Mine needed half my wages. We began on the same Monday. You keep calling that the same beginning.

**D03 — The laugh**

> I can remember every time you embarrassed me. I have made a list.
>
> I cannot remember who else was in the room. In two cases I cannot remember what you said. I have left those spaces blank. Your laugh is very clear.
>
> I would like you to know how much work I have done with so little material.

**D04 — The rehearsal**

> I spent Sunday telling you what I thought of you. You were marvellous at receiving it. Silent, ashamed, attentive.
>
> Monday you asked if I had a good weekend. I said yes. You looked relieved. That was the part I had not rehearsed.

**D05 — A clean victory**

> I wanted you to be cruel when you won. I had several things ready to say.
>
> Instead you thanked the cleaners and remembered my mother's operation. I have been trying to find a selfish reason for that. It would help me a great deal if you supplied one.

**D06 — The reference**

> I wrote you a good reference. Every word was true. It took fifteen minutes.
>
> I have spent the evening trying to forgive you for making it so easy.

## E — Things for one

**Ruth to her dead husband Al.** Bereavement includes administration, irritation, relief, laughter, the body, guilt about happiness, and gradual practical change. These letters can remain entirely human; they need no proof of a haunting.

**E01 — The account**

> They asked why I wanted your name taken off the account. I said you had died. The woman said she was sorry and asked whether you could confirm the change in writing.
>
> I nearly laughed. I wanted to tell you first.

**E02 — At the shop**

> I laughed at something in the shop. Properly laughed. A man put two apples back, weighed the bag again, then put one back in. He kept explaining himself to the scales.
>
> On the walk home I tried to remember how long I had laughed. As though there were an amount that would make it all right.

**E03 — The drawer**

> I hated the way you left every drawer open. I have closed them all. I can get dressed without bruising my hip.
>
> There were things I was right to be angry about. They have become very difficult to say out loud.

**E04 — Four minutes**

> A new kettle takes four minutes. Yours took seven. I used to stand beside it and think about nothing.
>
> I have three minutes every morning that I do not know what to do with. I stand there until it has been seven anyway.

**E05 — Tuesday**

> I liked Tuesday. The windows were open and the soup came out properly. Helen stayed after lunch. We argued about a film neither of us had seen.
>
> I wanted the day to keep going. I am writing that here because I couldn't say it at your grave.

**E06 — Cherries**

> I gave your coat to the man downstairs. It fits him badly. He wears it anyway.
>
> I kept the shopping list in the pocket. You had written cherries twice.
>
> Today I bought enough for one. I ate them standing at the sink. They were good.

## F — Night cover

**D. V.'s shift log, with a supervisor's memo.** Work, boredom, fatigue, small connections, and the gradual replacement of a worker by an entry on a form. The first four documents are mundane. The last turns an ordinary employment obligation toward the reader.

**F01 — First shift**

> 11.00: took over from Martin. One bulb out by the stores. Reported it.
>
> 1.20: sandwich.
>
> 3.10: the dog across the road barked at a bin.
>
> Nothing to report. This is apparently what doing the job well looks like.

**F02 — A good day**

> The clock is right. I checked it against the radio.
>
> I have eaten, slept, earned my money, and done nobody any harm. I cannot name anything wrong with today. I would still pay someone to tell me it was over.

**F03 — Home time**

> Went home and sat on the edge of the bed in my coat. Maren asked whether something had happened.
>
> I said no. She waited. After a while she took my coat and hung it up. I had thought there would be more to tell her too.

**F04 — Supervisor's memo**

> Please stop writing personal observations in the occurrence book. “Nothing happened” is sufficient.
>
> This book is a record of the premises. It is not a record of your evening.

**F05 — An occurrence**

> Maren rang. The baby laughed at a spoon. She held the receiver near him and I heard it happen again.
>
> I am entering this as an occurrence. Somebody else can cross it out.

**F06 — Relief**

> SIGNING OUT.
>
> Relief worker's name required. I cannot leave the line blank.
>
> Martin says he never worked here. The office says my position was discontinued in March. There is a fresh line beneath mine, with today's date.
>
> If you sign it, please tell Maren I went home.

## G — Half of everything

**Paula to her brother Daniel.** Money, caregiving, family affection, resentment, and the indignity of needing practical help. Paula is direct and materially specific. The ending makes a modest request rather than completing an emotional reconciliation.

**G01 — Expenses**

> Soap 1.10. Bus fares 6.40. New sheets 18.00. Three afternoons off work.
>
> Send whatever half means to you this month. I have stopped trying to put a price beside the afternoons.

**G02 — At the funeral**

> You cried beautifully at the funeral. I mean that. I was glad somebody could.
>
> When you said she never complained, I wanted to put your face through the flowers. She complained every day. Usually she wanted you.

**G03 — The house**

> You want half the house. Of course you do. Half is fair.
>
> Come and take half the appointments, half the spoiled food, half the nights she called me by your name. Take half the dress I washed three times before breakfast because she kept saying it smelled of somebody else's house. There is a drawer full of things nobody has asked to divide.

**G04 — The peaches**

> I remembered her stealing peaches from the display and putting them in your hood. We were old enough to be appalled. She laughed the whole way home.
>
> It would be easier to send these letters if I could remember only the difficult parts.

**G05 — What I wanted**

> Keep the extra money. I wanted you to offer it before I had to show you the sums.
>
> Now you have offered. I cannot make it feel like the same thing. I know that makes me impossible. I have become quite good at being impossible by myself.

**G06 — Saturday**

> There is one box left in the attic. I could manage it. I am asking you anyway.
>
> Saturday, before lunch. Bring bread. The cupboard is empty and I am tired of arranging visits as though nobody needs to eat.

## H — Small plans

**June to her friend Sal, over several years.** Play, friendship, queer love, pleasure, illness, aging, and an open future. These letters can be generous to a stranger who eventually finds them. They offer a different response to the increasingly possessive correspondence elsewhere.

**H01 — The bicycle**

> I have bought a bicycle. At forty-four I appear to have misplaced the part of the body that knows how to stop.
>
> Come on Sunday. You may laugh if you help me up afterwards. Wear something you can fall over in.

**H02 — Company**

> I kissed her. In the rain, like someone with no imagination.
>
> She asked whether I had wanted to do that for a long time. I said seventeen years. Apparently that is a terrible thing to mention while somebody is trying to find her umbrella.

**H03 — The party**

> We can afford potatoes, one bottle, and six chairs. Everyone can bring the rest.
>

## Sixteen loose notes

These do not share one narrator. Leave them on different kinds of paper: a receipt, an unsent card, a meal wrapper, a prayer book margin, a calendar, an appointment slip. The physical context gives each “you” a plausible recipient before the player begins to feel addressed.

**S01 — Desire, unsent card**

> I wanted to touch you and spent the entire evening asking sensible questions. You answered every one. I went home furious with both of us.

**S02 — Illness, appointment slip**

> I told the doctor I was frightened. He asked where it hurt. I gave him a place because he seemed to need one.

**S03 — Hunger, sandwich wrapper**

> I skipped lunch to save enough for the train. At four I bought something expensive because I was shaking. Being sensible keeps costing more than I expect.

**S04 — Joy, receipt**

> We spent the gas money on a cake. Before you start: the children sang for twenty minutes. There was icing on the dog. For twenty minutes I would have done it again.

**S05 — Boredom, calendar margin**

> I have moved the appointment to Friday. It gives Wednesday something to be about: waiting for Friday. Thursday has always managed on its own.

**S06 — Contempt, torn letter**

> You make a ceremony of being misunderstood. It saves you the embarrassment of being understood and disliked.

**S07 — Possessiveness, envelope flap**

> You say you want me happy. You become very quiet when someone else manages it.

**S08 — Friendship, invitation**

> I was hurt that you didn't come. Then I saw the chairs, the noise, the people asking what you do. Next time we can meet in the park. You won't have to be interesting to anybody else.

**S09 — Parenthood, grocery list**

> The baby slept. I stood in the kitchen and did nothing. When he woke I was angry. When he smiled I was ashamed. Neither feeling helped me put the washing away.

**S10 — Grief, unposted birthday card**

> I can remember the last thing I said. I cannot remember whether you heard it. I keep changing my voice when I try it again.

**S11 — Ambition, manuscript margin**

> You want to be discovered so badly that you never finish anything. A finished thing might receive an ordinary opinion.

**S12 — Aging, mirror note**

> I saw my mother's mouth in the mirror while I was complaining. She would have enjoyed that. I had to sit down before I could enjoy it too.

**S13 — Faith, prayer book margin**

> God, I am not asking you to explain it. I am asking why I have to be polite while I wait.

**S14 — Jealousy, back of a photograph**

> I hated her for knowing the story I was halfway through telling. You laughed before I reached the good part. I wanted you to see how much of it I had kept for you.

**S15 — Domestic irritation, electric bill**

> Stop turning off the light while I am in the room. Saving four pennies does not make it pleasant to live with you in the dark.

**S16 — An apology refused, returned envelope**

> I know you are sorry. You have described it very thoroughly. There is still a broken window, and I am still the person paying to have it fixed.

## Eight notes that can address the actual player

These are optional variants for later implementation. Each condition must be grounded in recorded native play. The writer can interpret an action unfairly; the text must never invent a real-world childhood, relationship, bereavement, diagnosis, or private history. Keep the recipient's original page fixed after its first appearance.

**P01 — Care**

*Condition: the reader has actually cared for an owned companion; use its observed name only if it has one.*

> You stopped for {companion}. I had forgotten anybody still stopped for that sort of thing. I have written this down because the other pages keep making you sound impatient.

**P02 — Returning**

*Condition: the reader has actually returned through a room doorway at least twice.*

> You came back again. I had prepared something sharp to say about it. Then I heard the door and was pleased. It is irritating to be pleased so easily.

**P03 — Taking**

*Condition: this reader personally took supplies from Holloway's belongings. Do not trigger for a peer's action.*

> You took something from Holloway's supplies. I expect you had a use for it. People who take things generally do. It would be awkward to admit you wanted it and left somebody else with the empty place.

**P04 — Leaving a trail**

*Condition: the reader has actually placed more than one valid route marker.*

> You have left marks behind you. Someone coming after you might be grateful. Someone waiting ahead might be grateful too. You seem certain which person you are writing for.

**P05 — Retreating**

*Condition: this reader voluntarily left an unfinished visit and physically returned safely.*

> You found the way back. I spent a long time trying to make that sound contemptible. It is inconvenient how badly I wanted someone to show it to me.

**P06 — Keeping a photograph**

*Condition: the reader has personally collected and retained an original photograph.*

> You kept the photograph. There are people who would call that sentimental. I would like to know what they keep when nobody is watching.

**P07 — Answering**

*Condition: the reader actually submitted a nonblank original reply. Do not claim to understand its contents or echo personal information from them.*

> I have an answer now. I read it twice. The first time I was looking for my own name. The second time I tried to listen.

**P08 — What was taken**

*Condition: the reader has an actual original possession in Mother custody. Insert only its recorded in-game name.*

> {possession} is still entered under your name. Someone has underlined the entry. I think they are trying to make possession sound like affection. I have seen people do that before.

## Order and distribution

- Keep each chain in order for its reader. A later installment appears after the earlier one has been read and the player has moved on; reopening the same letter does not advance the relationship.
- Interleave chains and loose scraps. A romance can be interrupted by a bill or an ugly workplace draft. Avoid successive copies of the same insult or several letters making the same accusation in one hall.
- Give each chain a recognisable hand, signature, and paper habit. Use existing writing styles where appropriate; preserve readable prose. Reserve clawed text for occasional intrusions rather than entire ordinary conversations.
- Keep the maternal chain with its own correspondence context. The mother does not acquire the Mother of Lost Things' identity or rules.
- Place the workplace chain near an archive desk or service area; family expenses near household storage; grief in quieter domestic spaces; pleasure notes near a window, garden, or resting place. These are placement suggestions, not new rooms or encounters.
- Let I–N build a sustained relationship with the reader. Interleave their later installments with the other voices so an affectionate note, a bill, or an ordinary irritation can interrupt the pressure. The optional P notes can ground an intrusion in an actual action. Tender and hostile writers can disagree about that action. Reading supplies no additional Witness credit and creates no obligation to answer.
- Preserve already collected originals and per-reader progress. Expanding the pool should not rewrite someone's old signed pages or replenish papers they removed.
- Keep each escalating writer's claims local to that voice. The host's idea of betrayal, the admirer's jealousy, the confessor's invented complicity, the keeper's correction, the assessor's judgment, and the correspondent's signature are not a single omniscient verdict on the player. A–H retain their different emotional trajectories.
