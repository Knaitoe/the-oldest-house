# Johnny Truant, second slice: the claimant and the collector (0.4.282)

The owner: "Then finish truant." The owner's draft is recorded word for word in docs/TRUANT_RESIDENT_DESIGN_DRAFT.md. The first slice
(docs/TRUANT_0_4_264.md) built his three places, the helpful meetings and the half face. This slice builds everything that slice left
out:
- the claimant and the collector;
- the dangerous favours;
- the hand in the doorway, the covered light and the shove;
- help after harm;
- his papers retelling a reader's account in the first person;
- the multiplayer gate.

Nothing about his places, his look or how he is dealt changes. New builds only.

## What he is at a meeting

Each time one of his places is come into, he is one thing for that meeting. Nobody is told which.

- **Help.** The useful stranger of the first slice: he holds the door, steadies the crossing, makes room at his lamp, asks, remembers.
- **Claim.** He helps as usual, and then the one he has turned to gets the claimant instead of the ordinary talk (below). At his lamp
  the half face leans further out into the passage before it draws back, and says "You skipped something." Then he is at his lamp,
  bent over a page.
- **The hand in the doorway** (the held door only).
- **The covered light** (the lamp only).
- **The shove** (the crossing only).

### Whom he turns to

- He turns to one reader at a time: the one past twenty passes with the longest history with him.
- "Twenty passes" is the deepest the reader has ever been (first slice). Collection is possible only at a meeting after the one at
  which twenty passes were first noted, and never at a first meeting. Twenty opens the possibility; it does not decide the meeting.
- Everyone else in the place meets the man they know. He can help one reader and press another in the same meeting, and everyone
  there sees both.

### How the turn is chosen

- **The first meeting at which he can collect shows it.** At the held door that is the hand in the doorway; elsewhere, a claim.
- **After that:**
  - help 4 in 10;
  - a claim 3 in 10;
  - the place's own hostile form 3 in 10.
- **After he has harmed them:** help 7 in 10 (help after harm), a claim 3 in 10.
- **The shove is rare.** It needs all of these:
  - he once steadied this reader on the crossing;
  - collection began at an earlier meeting;
  - it has been at least five meetings since the last shove.

  Otherwise the crossing's turn is a claim.

### The multiplayer gate

While any reader under twenty passes is in the place with him, nothing hostile happens: no doorway, no covered light, no shove. What
would have been hostile becomes a claim, addressed to the veteran. If an early reader comes into the place partway through, he stops
whatever he was doing.

## The claimant

His panel for the reader he has turned to keeps three things apart.

1. **What he did:** the help bits of the first slice.
2. **What they said:** the promise as they made it, and the places and answers they gave.
3. **What he claims:** his alone, always said in his voice. Each claim is kept in the record with the meeting it was made at.

The narration never agrees with him.

### What he claims, from the record

- **A promise kept:** "You came back and told me about *the place*. You told me how you got out. You didn't tell me what you left
  behind."
- **"I will", not yet kept:** "You said you'd come back." This is what they said.
- **"Maybe", not yet kept:** "You said maybe. Maybe means yes, in here." The reinterpretation is plainly his.
- **"No", or no promise, after help:** "I got you through, didn't I? That's worth something." After a "No" it starts "You said no. I
  heard you."
- **No help at all:** "You keep coming through my places."
- **After he has promised to ask for nothing** (help after harm): at the next claim he first says "I said I wouldn't ask. I know I
  said it."

### His papers

When he has a place this reader told him about and answered his question about, he reads it out from his page:
- at his lamp, or anywhere once he is two meetings past twenty;
- in the first person, built from the answer they actually gave. For example: "I got out of the frozen lake the way I came in. The door
  stayed for me."

What he has written up is kept as his papers. The reader's own account, journal and record are untouched.

### The collector's question

He asks about a place they already told him of:
- "Start before *the place*. You skipped something."
- "Tell me what happened in *the place* after the light went."
- "*The place*. I haven't had the ending."

It is not a memory test. Nothing turns on getting a detail right.

### What the reader can say

- **"That happened to me."** (only when he has read from his papers): "I got you through. Half of it's mine. I'm only getting it
  down."
- **Tell him about a new place.** This works as in the first slice: a new account keeps an earlier promise.
- **Tell him the old place again:** "Not that part. I didn't ask for the part you could tell anybody."
- **"I already told you that."** Usually he finds something left out. The second time, or once he is three meetings past twenty, he
  knows it: "I know. I know you did. Tell it again anyway."
- **"That's all you get."** He answers "You still have something of mine." and lets it end.

After any answer but the last he asks a favour. In the doorway he steps aside instead, and after the covered light he says
"That'll do. For now."

## The dangerous favours

Each favour is fitted to its place. The reader can agree or refuse, and what they said is kept with the meeting.

- **The held door:** "Go back through. Shut it behind you, and tell me what's on the other side when it's shut."
  - Agreed, he will not hold the door for them for the rest of the meeting, so the closer is theirs to get through.
  - It is done when they are back on the near side. He asks "Well? What's on the other side?"
- **The crossing:** "Go back over on your own. I want to watch how you do it."
  - Agreed, he will not steady them or catch a slip for the rest of the meeting.
  - It is done when they reach the other side. He says "Like that. Just like that."
- **The lamp:** "Put the lamp out. I want to see what's past it."
  - Agreed, the reader puts it out. The alcove's only light is gone.
  - He goes past the alcove into the far leg and looks into the dark ("There. Now I can see.").
  - The lamp comes back after a minute, or as soon as the place is empty.
- **Refused:** "You still have something of mine."

No one else's things or promises are ever spent.

## The hand in the doorway

He stands in the doorway he once held, the leer on him, one hand up on the frame, the door open behind him.

- **When they reach him:** once the reader he wants comes within a few blocks on the near side, the claimant's panel opens with "Not
  yet." Others pass him.
- **Told something:** he says "Go on, then.", steps aside to his place by the door and holds it as at any other meeting.
- **Pushed past, the first time:** he shoves them back out of the doorway: "Not yet." It does no damage.
- **Pushed past, the second time:** he gives way: "All right. All right. Go on."
- **Refused:** "Then you can wait." He stays in the doorway.
- **Struck, or with an early reader in the place:** he steps aside.

## The covered light

At his lamp, close to the one he wants:
1. He reaches across the lamp and closes his hand round it. The alcove goes dark, and the reader sees almost nothing (blindness, up to
   four seconds).
2. He steps across their path, between them and the way back to the turn.
3. He takes their wrist and says "You first. Then tell me." For two seconds they can hardly move.
4. He lets go. The lamp is lit again, and the claimant's panel follows.

- **A blow** ends it at once.
- **The lamp is never lost.** It is saved as put out, and lit again when its time is up, when the place empties, or when the server
  stops, even after a crash.

## The shove

He waits in the dark west end of the crossing's near landing.

### The approach

When the reader he wants is a few steps out along the ledge, he comes up behind them. It is brief and it can be heard:
- a shoe scraping as he starts;
- footsteps;
- "Breathing, behind you" close by.

### What stops him

Any of these, and he stops with his hand still up:
- **They turn and look at him.** He says "Easy. I've got you.", what he said the first time.
- **They step off the ledge.**
- **A friend comes within reach of him.**
- **A blow.**
- **An early reader comes into the place.**

Each stop is kept: why for the reader he was coming for, and as an interruption for a friend who came between.

### The push

1. His hand comes to rest where it rested the first time, between their shoulders.
2. A moment later, both hands push: west, off the ledge over the drop, and up off their feet so a crouch cannot hold the edge.

The fall is the crossing's own: seven blocks to the lower landing, injury, separation and a ladder back. A reader who is already hurt
can die of it. Nothing is scripted to soften the landing.

### Afterwards

- He walks to the edge and looks over.
- When they land, or after a moment, he says one word: "Careful."
- He goes back the way he came, into the dark end of the landing.
- He does not follow them.
- Everyone else in the place has it kept as witnessed.
- Talked to while he waits there, he only says "Go on. I'll be behind you."

## Help after harm

After the doorway's shove, the covered light or the shove from the ledge, he can still help. The help is real and asks for nothing.

At the next ordinary meeting he says what he did, in his words, and asks no question and no promise. For example: "The ledge. I don't
know what that was. I've gone over it and I don't know. Sit, if you want. I'm not asking you for anything."

- **The promise is his.** "I'm not asking you for anything" is kept as his promise. His next claim remembers it and breaks it.
- **On the crossing,** after a shove, he keeps his hands to himself, walks two steps back instead of one, and still catches a slip: "I
  won't touch you. I'm right here if you slip."
- **Neither act overwrites the other.** The harm and the forbearance are both kept.

## How he wears

At an ordinary meeting past twenty he adds one line that shows it:
- a question they already answered, asked again;
- their own story told as his;
- the help he gave remembered better than what he said afterwards.

## What is kept

**For each reader**, added to their record:
- the turns he took with them, by meeting;
- each claim, with its meeting;
- their answers to his questions;
- his papers;
- favours, as asked, agreed or refused, and done;
- harm, by kind and meeting (the doorway shove, the wrist, the shove);
- shoves and the meeting of the last;
- witnessed shoves;
- the times he was stopped, and why;
- interruptions;
- help after harm, and his own promise;
- corrections, challenges, retellings, limits and payments.

**Shared:** where he is and his one body (as before), and any lamp of his that is out.

## Defaults for the draft's open decisions (for the owner to change)

- **The addiction:** accounts of the House only, as before.
- **His most serious demand:** the three favours above, and the shove. He never asks for belongings, companions or journals.
- **Mortality:** he cannot die. Struck, he flinches, gives up what he was doing to that reader and keeps his distance for the rest of
  the meeting.
- **His understanding of the debt:** he believes it. He is lucid only when challenged a second time, or once he is three meetings
  past twenty. After harm, he admits what he did.
- **Identity:** unchanged. "Johnny", papers signed J.T.
- **Frequency:** unchanged. The shove is limited as above.

## Tests

`TruantTests.pastTwentyHeClaimsCollectsAndShovesButStillHelps` (exploration, batch `truant_after`) plays with a reader past twenty
with a history with him, and a second reader. In order:

1. **At the lamp, with the second reader under twenty there**, the covered light becomes a claim:
   - the half smile leans out;
   - both get help;
   - the veteran hears their own place in the first person and the claim about what was left behind;
   - the record keeps the claim, the unchanged promise and the papers apart;
   - "That happened to me." leads to a favour, and it is refused;
   - the early reader meets the man they know;
   - the lamp stays lit.
2. **Alone, the covered light:**
   - the lamp goes out, with blindness;
   - he steps across their path and takes the wrist, with slowness and "You first. Then tell me.";
   - the harm is kept;
   - he lets go, the lamp is lit and the effects are gone;
   - the panel follows, and "That's all you get." ends it.
3. **Help after harm:** he says what he did and asks nothing. His forbearance and his promise are kept.
4. **The hand in the doorway:**
   - the leer and the hand on the frame, the door open;
   - "Not yet.", and "I said I wouldn't ask. I know I said it.";
   - the first push past him is shoved back out of the doorway;
   - the second time he gives way to his place by the door.
5. **At the crossing with an early reader there:** no shove, only help.
6. **Alone with a veteran friend on the far landing:**
   - the reader turns to look at him as he comes, and he stops with "Easy. I've got you.";
   - the next time, his hands land, and the push sends them west and up off their feet;
   - the friend has it witnessed;
   - he looks over, says "Careful." and goes back to the dark end of the landing;
   - through all of it, what the reader said stays as said, and he is still there.

The first slice's case passes unchanged beside it.
