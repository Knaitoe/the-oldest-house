# A sort of thing — a proposal (November 15)

The owner, 11 November:

> Looking at, reviewing these, designing these has kind of triggered a bit of derealization ... the idea that everything in the game is just lines. maybe we can build that into a vignette. any classic works that deal with that?

A shortlist was offered, with *Flatland* as the spine. The owner's direction, the same day:

> idk. i think merging through the looking glass and wonderland, while concocting a unique spin on the person becoming more and less of a thing near the sleeping red king - culminating with him either waking or staying asleep - would be best

And on what he is called:

> Yes, the house can name him the king while vignette npcs refer to him using his full name

This is the proposal that came out of it, for review. **Nothing is built, and nothing will be until the owner has read it.** Every number, name and line below is mine unless it is marked as the owner's or as Carroll's.

## In short

- **A country ruled into squares, and a King asleep in the middle of it.** The reader comes in through a looking-glass as a pawn and crosses to the eighth square, as in *Through the Looking-Glass*.
- **The reader is more and less of a thing.** *Wonderland*'s growing and shrinking is kept, but what changes is how much of a thing the reader is: from too much of one, through a thing and a sort of thing, down to a line, and then nowhere.
- **What makes a reader a thing is being taken notice of:** by the King, who dreams clearest what is nearest him; and away from him by his dream, through its rules and its people's looking and naming.
- **Away from him the country itself is only its lines.** Every block of it is drawn twice, filled and ruled, and where the fill ends moves with his breath.
- **It ends at his tree,** with the reader crowned and too much of a thing to stand beside him for long. They say his whole name and he wakes; or they take the crown off and walk away until they are a line, and he sleeps on. Neither settles which of them dreamed the other.

## What the two books bring

Both books are long out of copyright, and so are their first pictures. Even so, only the shape is taken, as with every source. A handful of Carroll's own phrases are said by the dream's people and are marked below. Nothing is taken from any film.

From *Through the Looking-Glass*:

- **The glass.** A drawing-room, a chimney-piece, and a glass that goes soft.
- **The country as a chessboard:** squares, with brooks between the ranks and hedges between the files, seen whole from a hill.
- **The pawn's road:** from the second square to the eighth, the third by railway, and a crown at the end.
- **The King asleep under a tree,** snoring, in a tall night-cap; and two brothers who show him off and say what the reader is: "only a sort of thing in his dream" (Carroll's).
- **The question the book ends on:** which dreamed it.
- **Smaller things:** the Queen whose running gets nowhere, the Guard who looks through three glasses, the Knight who asks to be watched to the turn in the road, the feast where a dish that has been introduced cannot be cut.

From *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*:

- **More and less.** The bottle, the cake, and the mushroom with its two sides.
- **"Who are you?"** (Carroll's), asked of somebody who has been several things since the morning.
- **The Cat** that is there in parts, and the argument over whether a head with no body can be cut off.
- **The court of cards,** which sentences first; and the answer that ends it, "nothing but a pack of cards" (Carroll's).

## The spin: how much of a thing

### Five states

| | The reader is | What it lets | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | **too much of a thing** | Nothing more than a thing can do. | Rooted where they stand: no walking, no jumping. Every sound they make carries. |
| 3 | **a thing** | Hands that hold. A voice that is answered. Being counted on a square. | Bound by the rules: hedges are walls, a brook is crossed only at its crossing, and a pawn does not go back. Can be sentenced. Heard. |
| 2 | **a sort of thing** | Quiet. Hedges give, slowly. Cannot be sentenced. | Holds only light things. Answered every other time. Not counted. |
| 1 | **a line** | Goes through hedges, brooks and people. Not seen, not heard, not bound. | Holds nothing. Is not counted: nothing done as a line is a move. |
| 0 | **nowhere** | | The dream puts them back on the last square that counted them, a sort of thing, unhurt. |

The reader reads their state off themselves, never off a number: their own hands and body (as they are; flat, with no shadow; in outline), and the sound of their own feet.

### What moves it

One sum, the same on every square:

- **The land they stand on.** Filled land makes a thing (3). The band where the fill gives out makes a sort of thing (2). Ruled land makes a line (1).
- **Notice, one step up, and no further than a thing.** However many of these hold, it is one step:
  - being counted: they have stood as a thing on this square's peg (the Queen sets one at the near edge of every square, just past its crossing);
  - being looked at by one of the dream's people who is filled in, or by another reader who is at least a sort of thing;
  - being named (a summons, an introduction);
  - wearing the crown.
- **What they have swallowed, one step either way,** for about twenty seconds.
- **His in-breath, one step up,** beside him and nowhere else.
- **Past a thing** there are only three ways:
  - cake, eaten by a reader who is already a thing;
  - his in-breath, beside him (so a reader who has drunk is only a thing on it);
  - the crown, beside him, which is too much whatever else holds.

A line who drinks, on ruled land with nobody looking, is nowhere.

So near him a reader is a thing whether they like it or not, and too much of one each time he breathes in. Far from him they are a thing only so long as his dream is taking notice of them. Keeping the rules is what makes a thing of you, and being free of the rules is being less.

This is how I have read "more and less of a thing near the sleeping red king": more of a thing the nearer him, and swinging between more and less with his breath when beside him. See the first of the open questions.

### The bottle, the cake and the mushroom

- **A bottle** (less) and **a cake** (more) stand on a glass table on the second square. Each reader takes their own. Three swallows and three bites.
- **The mushroom** on the fifth square refills both: a piece from each side, as often as the reader goes back to it.
- **Too much, by cake:** a thing that eats is too much of one and is rooted till it wears off. This is the picture from *Wonderland* of the arm out of the window.

## The King

He lies under a tree beside the fourth square, in a heap, in a tall night-cap with a tassel. He does not move and is never spoken to. A pawn has no move onto his square except to take something lying there.

### His breath

- **The shared clock.** In for four seconds, out for four. Everybody in the country hears it, louder the nearer.
- **In the country:** the edge of the filled land moves a block out as he breathes in and a block back as he breathes out.
- **Beside him:** a reader is rooted on the in-breath and free on the out-breath. Coming near him is a matter of moving while he breathes out and standing still while he breathes in.

### How deep he sleeps

One number for the whole country, shown only by how far the fill reaches:

| deep | the third square to the fifth | the second and the sixth | the seventh and the eighth |
| lightly | the fourth | the third and the fifth | the rest |
| barely | his own square | the fourth | the rest |
| not at all | nothing: he is awake | | |

- **He stirs** when a thing beside him runs, lands a jump, drops something, or is caught mid-step by the in-breath. Two stirs take him from each line of the table to the next, so six wake him from a deep sleep. The brothers' battle is two.
- **He does not settle by himself.** A country that has drawn in stays drawn in for the rest of the road. The Knight's song, sung beside him, settles him by four stirs, once.
- **Less than a thing cannot stir him,** and a line cannot touch him.

## The country

- **Outdoors, in its own place,** built whole, with its own block family. The generic passes skip it.
- **A strip of the board:** three files wide and eight ranks long. Squares are sixteen blocks; brooks are two wide; hedges are one. Past the strip the squares go on to the horizon as ruled land.
- **Ruled twins.** The art tool draws every block of the country twice at sixteen pixels: filled, and ruled (the same block as its own outline and a little hatching on paper). The ruled twin is derived from the filled one by the tool, so the two cannot drift apart. This is the owner's "just lines", and it is ordinary block art: no filter over the screen.
- **A glass on every square.** A tall looking-glass stands in each field. Stepping into it is the way back to the drawing-room, and coming through the drawing-room's glass again brings a reader out on the farthest square they have reached.

## The pawn's road

Each square is one idea about being taken notice of.

### The glass

A drawing-room off a hall: a fire, a chimney-piece, a glass over it, chessmen on the hearthrug. On the table is the story's first paper, a chess problem with its last move left blank. It is printed backwards and reads forwards only to a reader standing at the glass. The glass gives, and the room beyond is the same room the other way round.

### 2. The hill

- **The Queen** takes the reader's hand and runs. While she has hold of them they are a thing, and the country slides past without their getting anywhere.
- **She lets go** and walks off, and with nobody taking notice of them the reader's hands go flat. This is the first time they see it.
- **Her rule:** a pawn goes one square, straight on, over the crossing, and does not go back.
- **The peg.** She sets a peg and looks back at it from the far side of the field. A reader who stands on it is looked at, so a thing, and counted; and only a thing can lift the bottle and the cake.

### 3. The railway

A short ride. The Guard looks at the reader through one glass after another. While he looks they are a thing, and a thing with no ticket keeps its seat. When he lowers the glass they can get up. The train takes the brook at a jump.

### 4. The wood, and the King

- **The two brothers** point the way with one arm each and take the reader to see him.
- **This is where it is said.** They say what the reader is, and what would happen if he woke. The House says nothing about it.
- **The rattle.** One brother's rattle lies spoiled under the King's tree, and the two of them are dressing for a battle over it, beside him. A battle would stir him twice.
- **Taking it** stops the battle. It lies on his square, and it is the one thing a pawn may step there for. The reader goes in on the out-breaths, stands on the in-breaths, and lifts it as a thing. A swallow from the bottle makes the walk in silent, and then the rattle can be lifted only on an in-breath, when they are a thing again.
- **Not taking it** is allowed. The battle happens, he stirs, a great crow's shadow sends the brothers running, and the road goes on with a smaller country.

### 5. "Who are you?"

- **The Caterpillar** sits where the fill gives out, so the edge of it crosses the mushroom with every breath. It asks. The panel's answers are what the reader is at that moment, and on the out-breath words drop out of them.
- **The mushroom:** one side more, the other less. The Caterpillar looks at whoever it asks, so a reader at the mushroom is never less than a sort of thing and can always break a piece off.
- **The drawn bridge.** The crossing here is a drawing of a bridge. A drawing will not bear a thing: a thing goes through it into the brook and is set back on the bank. So the reader drinks and goes over as less than one.

### 6. The court of cards

- **A summons.** The herald is waiting at the far bank with a scroll, and reads the reader's name from it. Named, they are a thing for as long as the court attends to them, and a thing can be sentenced.
- **Sentence first.** Nothing is ever carried out.
- **Two ways out,** and which the reader takes is remembered:
  - **The Cat's way (less).** Its grin has been in the tree by the lawn since the reader came, and it will talk. Called, its head appears over the court, and the court falls to arguing whether a head can be taken off a thing with no body. While they attend to the Cat nobody attends to the reader, who is a sort of thing again and walks out between the jurors, edge-on. A swallow from the bottle does the same without the Cat.
  - **The cards' way (more).** The reader eats, and as too much of a thing says what the court is. The pack goes up and comes down as cards. The King stirs once, a long way off.

### 7. The forest

All of it is ruled: trees, path and Knight are drawings, and a reader in it is a line.

- **The Knight** rides with them to the last brook. He is a drawing until he is looked at. Then he fills in, and while he is filled in he sees the reader, which makes them a sort of thing. When the reader looks away he thins, and falls off his horse.
- **Each keeps the other.** A reader who walks ahead without looking round is a line with a drawing lying in the path behind them. They go back, look at him till he fills, and he gets on again.
- **His song.** He sings it if he is asked, and the reader can sing it afterwards.
- **The last peg.** He looks at the reader as they stand on it, and a bite of cake does the rest: looked at and fed, they are a thing there, and counted. He keeps a cake in the box on his saddle for a reader who has none.
- **The turn in the road.** He asks to be watched as far as the turn. If the reader waits and watches, he rides out of sight filled in. If they go on, he is a drawing before he gets there.

### 8. The crown

- **The crown comes down on a reader who is counted here.** A line has no head for it.
- **The crown** is real and worn, and it is notice: a crowned reader is never less than a sort of thing anywhere in the country.
- **The feast.** The dishes and the guests are drawings. Whatever the reader looks at is introduced, fills in, and is then somebody, and cannot be cut or sat on. The longer they look about them, the more the table fills with somebodies.
- **The Queen's move.** Eight ruled lines run from the reader's feet across the country, and along a line a Queen is not stopped by hedge or brook. One of them ends beside his tree.

## The last move

Beside him, crowned, the reader is too much of a thing on every breath: rooted, and each in-breath stirs him once. That is check. However lightly he is sleeping by then, they have at least one whole breath to choose in.

### He wakes

- **The reader says his whole name.** It is on the panel, in the dream's people's words, and it is the only time a reader says it. All through the country the dream's notice made a thing of the reader. Here the reader's notice makes a thing of him, and a thing that is noticed cannot go on sleeping.
- **Or they stand there** until the in-breaths wake him.
- **What follows:** the breathing stops in the middle of a breath. It is the first silence since the glass. The fill runs in to him from every side, faster than a reader could, and where it has been there are only lines; then the lines go; then the hall, with the crown on the reader's head.

### He sleeps on

- **The reader takes the crown off.** No longer a Queen, they are only a thing, and can walk on the out-breath. To leave him his sleep, a reader has to stop being a Queen.
- **They may sing first.** He settles, and the country fills out to its edges, for the only time, behind a reader who is leaving it.
- **They walk away from him** and off the board, thinner with every square, until they are a line and then nowhere; and then the hall, with the crown in their hand and the breathing still going on behind the wall for a moment.

### He wakes too soon

If he stirs to nothing before the eighth square, he wakes and the country goes out with the readers in it. That is the story's one failure. They come to at its door, and by 0.4.599 the story is gone from that door and is dealt again later. He is asleep again when it is.

## With more than one reader

- **One dream to a party,** by the scene copies (`SceneInstances`): its own King, sleep and country.
- **Each reader is their own amount of a thing,** and sees the others as they are: filled, flat or in outline.
- **Looking counts.** A reader who is at least a sort of thing holds another by looking at them. In the ruled forest two readers go forward by turns, each looking back to keep the other, with a bite of cake to begin it.
- **His sleep is shared.** More feet beside him make more noise.
- **The last move is whoever makes it.** The first reader to say his name wakes him for everybody still in the country. A reader who would rather he slept has to be off the board first. Each keeps the record of what they themselves did, and a reader who was there when another woke him is told so in their account.

## On your own

Every step that takes a second pair of eyes has somebody in the story for it (the rule of 0.4.598):

- **The hill:** the Queen looks back at the peg.
- **The forest and the last peg:** the Knight is the one who looks.
- **The King's tree:** nobody is needed. The land itself makes a thing of the reader there.
- **The court:** the Cat is the one the court attends to.

## Words

## Afterwards

- **The keepsake: the crown.** Outside the country it is drawn in outline, and it fills in for as long as something is looking at its wearer: another reader, or one of the House's people. It does what the country taught: notice makes a thing of it. In the stories that have a watcher, it tells the wearer they are watched.
- **The country, come back to** (the aftermath rooms, `Aftermath`):
  - **if he woke:** all of it ruled and silent, the night-cap hanging on a bough and nobody under the tree;
  - **if he slept:** all of it filled to its edges, the breathing going on, and the reader a line from one end to the other.
- **The account** is titled for the place, "the ruled country", with the lines above and which way the reader left the court.
- **The Witness line,** one source, resolved once by either ending:

## Open, for the owner

1. **The reading of "more and less of a thing near the sleeping red king".** Built here as both: more of a thing the nearer him, and swinging with his breath beside him. Should it be only the nearness, only the swing, or something else?
2. **Size.** *Wonderland*'s more and less is size. Should too much of a thing also be taller (a quarter, say, so that they brush the boughs of his tree), or is how solid they are enough?
3. **The lines.** Drawn twins of every block, as proposed, or a filter over the reader's whole view by their own state? The twins keep to the art rule and are the same for everybody; a filter would be new to the mod and is the one place the rule on textures would bend.
4. **The hall after he wakes.** Should the House's own hall be lines for a breath when the reader comes to? It would say that the House may be ruled too. It needs the filter, even if the twins are chosen for the country.
5. **Several readers at the tree.** Is it right that the first to say his name decides it for everybody, and that a reader who wants him asleep has to be gone first?
6. **Waking too soon.** A failure that puts the readers out, as proposed, or simply the first ending arrived at early?
7. **One long visit, with the glasses to leave and come back by,** or three visits (to the King; to the forest; the crown)?
8. **The House's names for the others:** "the Queen", "the Knight" and "the court", as in the table?
9. **Carroll's own words.** Four phrases are his. More, fewer, or none?
10. **The keepsake.** The crown that fills in when its wearer is watched: is that the right thing, and should it differ by ending?
11. **Where it is dealt, and how deep.**
12. **The title.** "A sort of thing" for the story and "the ruled country" for the place, or something else.

## Decided (15 November)

The owner, 15 November: "You need to build the sleeping king vignette too", with the open questions left to judgement. Each choice
below can be moved.

1. **More and less near him:** both, as proposed. Nearness sets how much of a thing the country makes of a reader; beside him the
   breath swings it. The swing is the one thing only he does, and nearness gives the road its shape.
2. **Size:** no. How solid a reader is carries it (hands, shadow, feet, what they can hold and pass through). A change of scale would
   fight the hedges, brooks and doorways that the states already change, and the House keeps scale for small readers.
3. **The lines:** drawn twins of every block, as proposed. They keep to the art rule and are the same for every viewer.
4. **The hall after he wakes:** not ruled. It would need the filter that question 3 turns down, and the story's answer is not to
   answer which of them dreamed.
5. **Several readers at the tree:** as proposed. The first to say his name wakes him for everybody still in the country; a reader who
   would rather he slept has to be off the board first, and is told in their account that he woke after they left.
6. **Waking too soon:** the failure, as proposed. It is the one way the road can be lost, and it is what makes his stirring matter.
7. **One long visit,** with a glass on every square to leave by and come back through to the farthest square reached.
8. **The House's names:** the King, the Queen, the Knight, the court, as in the table.
9. **Carroll's words:** the four phrases, no more.
10. **The keepsake:** the crown that fills in while its wearer is watched, the same for either ending. Which way the story ended is in
    the account and the aftermath, not in the crown.
11. **Where it is dealt:** among the understanding stories, from depth twelve, once the quiet rooms are done; not in the dark band.
12. **The title:** "A sort of thing" for the story, "the ruled country" for the place.

It is built in the proposal's eight slices, the fourth square first.
