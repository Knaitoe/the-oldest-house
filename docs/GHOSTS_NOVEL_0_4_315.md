# The Barretts' own conversations (0.4.315)

This is a primary-text dialogue pass on the existing Ghosts Set, prompted by the owner's uploaded *A Head Full of Ghosts*. It is not a claim that every scene of the novel has been recreated. The checked passages in that scanned edition are PDF pages 31–47 (the books, the sisters and ordinary supper), 140–142 (the basement conversation), 150–153 (the child's camera and the crew), 223–228 and 242–247 (the family before and during the exorcism), and 285–298 (the last plan, supper and adult Merry's conflicting recollections). All new speech and papers are original adaptation writing, not quotations.

## Before the horror

The sisters have a shared practice before the show: Marjorie tells stories, Merry writes them into a book, and Merry can demand a better ending. Merry knows the little hidden figure's places and checks them again. Their pictures put them together. The later story that refuses a rescue now has an ordinary, affectionate practice to corrupt.

The six speakers have different lines on each of the seven visits. Merry is interested, argumentative and observant as well as frightened. Marjorie can tease her, enjoy her attention and overstep her trust. John tries to keep a family meal together while defending the decisions he has made. Sarah cares for the girls and is tired, impatient and angry about what the production takes from them. Neither illness nor possession is established as the single explanation.

Merry's pasta without sauce is an old preference. It is present at the early dinner as well as the last. The plain-pasta prop now describes the actual plain meal rather than accidentally taking the sauced-meal branch.

## One click, one line

A family interaction used to call the dialogue cursor twice: once for the action-bar text and once for the speaking animation. With two lines per visit, this skipped every second line. It now consumes one line and uses it for both. The friend's dialogue cursor and original cast stay independent.

Barry uses the conversation panel. Each visit offers a question about that day's work: separate interviews, keeping the table in view, the reconstruction, privacy, Sarah's objection, the drawer or taking the last cases out. Asking does not call a take, record a subject, stand in for putting the camera down or complete a day. Actual framing remains the evidence. Ready/review/leave choices remain available; duplicate replies are rejected by the panel's offer token. The completed cut opens its own paper after the conversation closes.

## The last supper

The old dialogue and meal description incorrectly attributed the sauce to Marjorie. They no longer do. In Merry's later account she was manipulated into adding the powder, and Marjorie's decision to eat too broke the plan Merry believed they shared. The game does not show that later account as an omniscient recording of the preparation.

Merry holds actual written cards before the collapse; her question about her sister eating is written, without a speaking animation. Afterwards she speaks again. The original family bodies, staggered choking, mouth effects and the owner's requested front-door departure remain. Separate readers keep their own living or dead family and their own seven-visit requirements.

The crew leaves before supper. The last visit's papers explicitly identify it as the House's reconstruction after the show ended. The final assembly no longer invents a later broadcast or a new editorial act by an absent Barry. Merry's immediate departure remains the owner's adaptation, not a claim that the novel's child walked out of the house. The account, Witness line, derived advancement text and film keepsake line travel with these changes.
