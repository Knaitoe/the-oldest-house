# Bike night 28: caught in the town: the bike in a later hall (0.4.511)

The proposal (`docs/BIKE_NIGHT_PROPOSAL.md`, "Failure: caught in town"): "A reader who is out walks home. The night is
over for them, and the vignette is never dealt to them again. **The bike** turns up later, in a later hall, chained to
a school fence with its front wheel bent."

Being caught, the bike going off with one of the three, being carried on the Hopper's pegs, being out, and never being
dealt the night again were built (0.4.482, 0.4.508). The bike never turned up.

## What turns up

Two bays of a school yard's fence stand against the wall of a hall, a block and a half high, wire in diamonds between
three posts. The bike the reader lost stands along it on the hall's side, leaning in on the wire, with its front wheel
buckled out of true. A chain goes round its seat tube and through the fence, and there is a padlock on the chain.

It is the reader's own make, in its own paint: not rusted. One of the three had it, and this is where he left it.

It is one reader's. Only they are sent it, so it stands in nobody else's hall, and nothing collides with it: it is in
nobody's way and cannot shut anybody in.

## When

For a reader who is out, each crossing into anywhere is counted. From the third on, the first plain hall they come
into that has room for it has it.

Room is two clear cells along a plain wall, two high, on a whole floor, six to sixteen steps in from the door they
came by, with open floor between them and the way through. It is never put in the way through itself, never across a
door, and never round behind something that stands beside the way. A hall with no such place has nothing, and the
next one is tried.

## Finding it

Coming within five blocks of it and looking at it, the reader hears the chain and is told what it is:

> It is the Rattler. A chain goes round its frame and through the fence, and the front wheel is bent. The padlock is
> the one off the park gate.

(In the park, the open gate's chain "hangs from one leaf, and the padlock is gone.")

A hand on it after that: "The chain is tight, and the padlock is shut." It cannot be taken.

It is found once. Until its reader has looked at it, it is owed: a hall they walk straight through with their back to
it leaves it to turn up in the next that has room. Once they have looked, it is in their record and turns up nowhere
again. The one they found is there for as long as they are near it, and goes ten seconds after they have left.

## How it is drawn

`ChainedBike` is a small entity with a renderer of its own (`ChainedBikeRenderer`): the fence and the chain are two
models from `tools/draw_bike_night_art.py`, and the bike is the make's own, drawn by `BikeLook` with its front wheel
turned out of the bike's plane and tipped. The proof has a page of it against a wall: the Rattler from the hall's
side, and the Wasp from along the wall.

## The case

One new case, 99 in the Bike night namespace. A hall of the case's own, five cells across, and another one cell
across. A reader who is out on the Rattler, and one who is not.

- The night is never dealt again to the one who is out.
- Where it would stand: against the wall, six steps in. In a way one cell across, nowhere. With a door in the wall
  where it would stand, not there.
- Nothing in the first two halls after the night, and nothing in a place that is a scene of its own, however many
  crossings it has been. Then a plain hall: the Rattler, once, at the foot of the wall, facing out of it, in nobody's
  way; sent to its reader and not to the other.
- With their back to it, it is still owed. On into another hall: the one behind them is gone and it is in the next.
- They look at it: they hear the chain, are told what it is, and it is in their record; the other is told nothing.
  A hand on it is told the chain is tight; the other's hand is told nothing.
- Found, it is put nowhere again.
- They leave, and it goes after them.

## Not built, not seen, and left with the owner

- **"A school fence"** in a hall of the House is exactly as strange as it sounds: two bays of chain-link against a
  papered wall. That is what the proposal asks for. If the owner would rather it turned up only in halls of a
  certain kind (stone, or out of doors), it is one condition.
- **Not rusted, and Tully's padlock:** both are readings. The Hollow's bike is the one that rusts.
- **Only the reader sees it.** The proposal does not say. A fence in a shared hall would be in other readers' way, and
  in the way of the halls' own furnishing.
- **It cannot be taken or freed.** There is no key. If the keepsake turns out to be the bike, this is where a reader
  who lost theirs might get it back; that is the owner's call with the keepsake.
- **Numbers:** three crossings; six to sixteen steps in; five blocks to be told; ten seconds after they leave.
- Somebody who is out is told the way home as before ("Nobody is coming back this way. The way home is the way you
  came."), and leaves by the door they came in by. (This line first said that nothing told them. That was wrong, and
  was corrected in 0.4.514.)
- None of it has been seen in a client except the proof's page.
- Still not built: the keepsake (the owner's call).
