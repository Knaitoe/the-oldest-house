# More for the House to read — a proposal (November 25)

The owner, 25 November:

> We should also better design the quiet room vignettes. More read tags, a better developed haven vignette at least. Can you script design docs for this too?

Two documents answer it. This one is the tags: what else the House could learn, where it would learn it, and what each would
change. The other, `docs/PORCH_EVENING_PROPOSAL.md`, is the porch rebuilt as a whole evening, which is where most of the new tags
are read. **Nothing is built, and nothing will be until the owner has read both.**

## Where the read stands

Six traits, one room each (`docs/HOUSE_READ_DESIGN.md`):

| Trait | Values | Room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TONE` | a crayon | the kitchen table |
| `AGE` | `ANALOG`, `TRANSITIONAL`, `DIGITAL` | the waiting room |
| `HAIR` | four routines | the bathroom |
| `SELF` | `MASC`, `FEM`, `ANDROGYNOUS`, `UNSEEN` | the coatroom |
| `DRAWN_TO` | `MEN`, `WOMEN`, `BOTH`, `NO_ONE` | the shelter |
| `HOME` | `HAVEN`, `DANGER`, `RUNAWAY` | the porch |

What is thin about it:

- **It reads what a reader is, and almost nothing of how they live.** Five of six traits are appearance or attraction. Only `HOME`
  is about conduct, and it is a three-way sort made in two hundred ticks.
- **One room, one trait, one moment.** A reader who clicks the nearest thing is filed for good unless a later room disagrees, and
  no later room asks the same question.
- **The payoffs are mostly words.** A line, a song, a prop. Few of them change what a reader does.
- **Nothing a reader nearly did is kept.** A reader who walks to the screen door, stops, and goes round the side is filed the same
  as one who never looked at it.

## Rules the new tags keep

All of the first document's rules stand. Four are worth repeating because the new tags lean on them:

- **Habit only.** No prompt, no menu, no wrong answer. Every tag is read from something a reader does with their hands or feet in
  a room that would work the same if the House were not watching.
- **A tag stops at the habit.** `EATS: NO_MEAT` means this reader left the meat. It is never turned into a belief, a faith, a
  diet or a background, and no tag is used to guess another.
- **Unknown is today's game.** Every caller handles no read, and that branch is what ships now.
- **Private.** Nothing read is shown or said to another reader, and the server options that turn reading off cover every new tag.

## The new tags

Nine are proposed. The first six are the ones I would build; the last three are weaker or more delicate and are here for the
owner to cut.

### 1. `TABLE` — how many a home holds

- **Values:** `ONE`, `TWO`, `FEW` (three or four), `MANY` (five and up).
- **Read by:** setting the table. A stack of plates on the counter and a table with eight chairs; the voice says to set it. The
  reader lays plates until it looks right to them and sits down. The count is the read.
- **Second signal:** the same count of cups, or of chairs pulled out.
- **Why it earns a place:** it is the one fact about a household that every domestic scene can use, and the reader gives it
  without knowing they were asked anything.
- **Changes:**
  - The household rooms off the great staircase lay that many places, hang that many coats, leave that many cups to dry.
  - The Goatman's supper: a `ONE` or `TWO` reader's cousins comment once on the noise; a `MANY` reader is handed the big spoon.
  - Second Person's flat has one toothbrush or several.
  - The voice on the phone in *Until Evening* says "I" or "we", and for `MANY` somebody else is always talking behind it.

### 2. `EATS` — what goes on the plate

- **Values:** `MEAT`, `NO_MEAT`, `LITTLE`.
- **Read by:** serving themselves from four dishes (a roast, greens, bread, something sweet). What is on the plate when they sit.
- **Second signal:** what is left on it when they get up.
- **Changes:**
  - Every later table sets this reader's plate to match: the Goatman's franks or a bun with onions, Sunday lunch, the lake house.
  - Hosts who notice say one line (*"I did you the squash."*).
  - In *Until Evening*, a `NO_MEAT` reader is handed the plate like anyone else. Nothing about the scene softens; the account
    notes that they had not eaten meat in the House before, if that is true.

### 3. `SPEAKS` — whether a question gets an answer

- **Values:** `ANSWERS`, `FEW_WORDS`, `QUIET`.
- **Read by:** the talk panel at supper. Somebody asks how the day was. The panel offers a long reply, a short one, and the option
  it always has: saying nothing until the question passes.
- **Second signal:** a second question later in the meal, answered the same way.
- **Changes:**
  - People in later stories who make small talk make less of it to a `QUIET` reader and stop asking twice.
  - Talkers (Ro at the terminal, Marla at the Last Stop, the Goatman's cousins) pick an `ANSWERS` reader to talk at.
  - *Speak Up* is dealt later to a `QUIET` reader, after they have met more of the House.

### 4. `CARE` — what gets done without being asked

- **Values:** `TENDS`, `WHEN_ASKED`, `LEAVES`.
- **Read by:** the end of supper. Plates on the table, a tap dripping, a dog at the screen door, a small child with a shoe
  untied. Nobody asks for forty seconds. Then somebody asks once.
- **Second signal:** a second undone thing the next morning.
- **Changes:**
  - Rooms with chores (the Pell house, the flat, the Ghosts set's errands) have their people ask a `LEAVES` reader outright and
    thank a `TENDS` reader in passing.
  - In *Until Evening*, the nurse hands the count to a `TENDS` reader on day 10.
  - The House's account says what the reader did for people without being asked, by name, in every story that records one.

### 5. `NIGHT` — how the light is left

- **Values:** `DARK`, `LIGHT_ON`, `DOOR_OPEN`.
- **Read by:** going to bed. A lamp, a night-light in the wall, a door with a hall light beyond it. The reader lies down, and how
  the room is at that moment is the read.
- **Second signal:** whether they get up to change it when the hall light goes off.
- **Changes:**
  - Every bed the House gives this reader is made up that way: the Pell house, the monitor room, the hotel's 217, the lodging.
  - Somebody who checks on a sleeper leaves the door as the reader likes it, which is unsettling the first time it is noticed.
  - Stories that take the light away take the reader's kind of light.

### 6. `KEEPS` — what is worth carrying

- **Values:** `LIKENESS` (a photograph, a drawing), `USE` (a knife, a key, a tin), `PLAY` (a toy, a card, a marble), `NOTHING`.
- **Read by:** a shelf, a drawer or a bag with a dozen small things and room in the hand for one.
- **Second signal:** where they put it: under the pillow, in a pocket, back on the shelf.
- **Changes:**
  - Which of a story's keepsakes is set where the reader will find it first.
  - What the hall stand keeps for the cell.
  - A `NOTHING` reader's accounts end on what they left behind.

### 7. `ANIMAL` — who they go to (weaker)

- **Values:** `DOG`, `CAT`, `NEITHER`.
- **Read by:** a dog under the porch and a cat on the rail, the same distance from the path.
- **Changes:** which stray the House sends first; which animal is at a story's door.
- **Why weaker:** the game already knows the reader's pets. This mostly repeats it.

- **Values:** `AT_ONCE`, `LAST_LIGHT`.
- **Read by:** how long after the whistle the reader comes in.
- **Changes:** whether people in later stories wait for them or start without them.
- **Why weaker:** it reads the player's curiosity about the yard more than a habit.

### 9. `CLOSE` — how near is comfortable (delicate)

- **Values:** `NEAR`, `ARM'S_LENGTH`.
- **Read by:** where the reader stands when somebody opens their arms at the door, and which seat they take beside others.
- **Changes:** whether later people stand close, sit beside the reader, put a hand on their shoulder.
- **Why delicate:** it sets how freely people in a horror game touch the reader. I would build it only with the owner's say.

## Hesitation

A seventh kind of thing to keep, beside the tags: **what the reader started to do and did not.**

- Each choice a room offers has an approach: a step onto the porch stair, a hand that lifts the coat and puts it back, a plate
  taken off the table again.
- An approach that is abandoned is kept as an observation with its own word: *went up two steps and came down*.
- It never sets or changes a tag. It is recited in the account and the book, and it marks a tag as one the House may ask again.

## Asking twice

No tag should rest on one room.

- **A second place for each.** Every tag gets one later, smaller reading in an ordinary story, at an object that belongs there
  anyway: a table to be laid in the Pell house, a bed to be got into at the lodging, a question at the Last Stop.
- **Agreement settles, disagreement unsettles,** by the rule already built. A reader who set one plate at the porch and five at
  the Pell house is `uncertain`, and the next table decides.
- **The House says so.** When a read changes, the next account has one sentence for it: *"It laid one plate for you, once. It
  lays more now."*

## The bar

The first document's test still applies: a tag is worth building only if a reader will notice it. For the new ones:

1. Every tag changes **something the reader does or is given**, and at least one of its changes is more than a line.
2. Two readers who came in together and compare notes after the Pell house **find their tables were laid differently**.
3. The personal account's opening grows by no more than three sentences. The tags show in rooms first and in recital second.

## Cost

- **Writing:** each tag needs today's text plus a variant per value at every place that reads it. Six tags at about five places
  each is roughly a hundred short variants.
- **Code:** six entries in `HouseRead.Trait`, one helper each, the second-read hooks, and the account's sentences.
- **Tests:** the house-read namespace grows by a case per tag and one for hesitation.
- **Order:** `TABLE` and `EATS` first (one scene reads both and every table can use them), then `NIGHT` and `KEEPS`, then
  `SPEAKS` and `CARE`.

## Questions for the owner

1. **Which tags?** All six of the first group, fewer, or any of the last three?
2. **`EATS` and the plate in *Until Evening*:** leave it unsoftened, as written here?
3. **Hesitation in the account:** recited every time, or only when the House later changed its mind?
4. **Asking twice:** is it right for a later story to overturn what a quiet room filed, or should the quiet rooms be final?
5. **Names.** `TABLE`, `EATS`, `SPEAKS`, `CARE`, `NIGHT`, `KEEPS`: keep, or rename?
