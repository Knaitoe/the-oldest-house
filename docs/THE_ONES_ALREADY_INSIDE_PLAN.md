# The Ones Already Inside: the plan and the neutral script

The owner's proposal is `docs/THE_ONES_ALREADY_INSIDE_PROPOSAL.md`, kept as sent. This is the plan it is built to, slice by slice,
and the complete neutral script the proposal asks for before anything else ("Write a complete neutral script first. Then add
personhood fragments to arrival, prepared rooms, Vera's offer, the crisis, and the personal account."). The owner's order, 12
November: "start by finishing each unfinished vignette". After the Pell house this is the next.

Everything below that the proposal does not settle is decided here and listed at the end, so any of it can move.

## The place

A lodging house inside the House: readers come in by the House's door, which is the lodging house's own front door, and stand in
its front hall. Two storeys over a cellar, with passages behind the walls. It keeps its own night: the shutters are screwed shut,
and nothing is outside the windows.

- **Ground floor.** The front hall: coat hooks by the door, the hall table with the house's terms (the first paper), the bench
  across from it (where each reader's account will wait), the hall stand with its glass, and the stair rising north along the
  east wall over a cupboard. The parlor (west) has the fireplace, the place to wait together. The dining room (east) has the long
  table. The back passage runs across the house behind them. Off it: Daniel's room (back west, bolted on the passage side), the
  back stair down to the cellar (middle), the scullery (back east, Vera's kitchen, with the tray for Daniel's hatch).
- **Upstairs.** The bedroom gallery: the upstairs hall, railed round the stairwell, with six guest rooms off it, three a side,
  numbered one to six. Each has a bed, a washstand with its basin and glass, a chair, and a frame set into its outside wall.
  North of the gallery a narrow corridor leads to the workroom (west) and Vera's room (east).
- **The frames.** Each frame is a doorcase set into a room's outside wall with nothing behind it: an opening a person can stand
  in, plastered a hand's depth back. The six guest rooms' frames are empty and waiting. Daniel's is in the back wall of his room,
  and he is held standing in it.
- **The passages.** Behind the side and back walls, on both floors, a passage a body wide, lath on one side and brick on the
  other. Hatches open into the rooms (the food hatch into Daniel's room among them); a papered jib door at the back passage's
  west end, a door from the scullery and a door at the end of the back stair lead in; ladders at the side passages' front ends
  join both floors to the cellar.
- **The cellar.** The frames' shared support: a timber sill laid round three sides of the cellar floor under the room walls, a
  post standing on it under every frame in an iron shoe with its wedge, and in the middle of the back run the ratchet that holds
  the whole sill in tension. In one corner, a post nobody's room stands on any more.

## What the story keeps (the rules)

From the proposal's supernatural facts, as mechanisms. Each indispensable rule has a second source, as the proposal asks.

| Rule | How it is shown | Second source |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Killing one linked body by ordinary violence kills both. | Their health is one health (E4). | Vera says so; the cut showed it. |
| Returning the borrowed body through its frame releases the original and ends the borrowed one. | E1 at Daniel's frame. | Vera's offer (N8); Dan's own fear; the frame's back giving. |

## Who knows what (state)

**Shared, the house's (one per copy):** the chapter (I to IV and the coda); where each of the four is, what each is doing and what
each now intends; Daniel's restraints (held, freed); his door (bolted, drawn, open); who holds the key and where it is (Rae's coat,
the cupboard under the stair, Vera's hand, a reader); the link (whole, severed, ended by E1 or E2); the cut (happened, prevented,
shown by the pin); every frame and the ratchet (set, working, released); the failed bodies; who is taken and where; who has died;
which deliberate resolution is under way and how far.

**Each reader's own:** what they promised and to whom; what they were told, by whom, and what they have told whom (an NPC knows a
thing only if somebody said it to them in the panel, or did it in front of them); what they saw (the cut on both sides, the pin,
the cellar's release, a death); what they did (freed, held, steadied, struck, carried the key, worked the ratchet, stood at the
frame); whether they were taken and how they got out; whether they withdrew and what they knew when they did; and their part at the
end. The account is written from this alone.

## The neutral script

People speak above their heads and, when something is asked of a reader, in the conversation panel (`Talk`). Speech is never chat.
Lines in brackets are the conditions they stand under. Replies are each reader's own; what one reader promises binds only them.
House writing never genders the reader, names a colour or an age, or says how the reader feels.

### Act I: They brought him back

**N1. Rae's request (the front hall).** Rae is in the hall when the first reader comes in, with Dan on the bench in his coat, one
hand in his pocket.

> Rae: Oh, thank God. You're staying here? You must be. Nobody comes in that door who isn't.
> Rae: I need a favour. It's a small one.
> Rae: This is Dan. He isn't well. The woman who keeps this house said she'd see him, and I have to go and find her.
> Rae: Will you stay with him? Only till I'm back. He doesn't want to be on his own with her.
> Dan: I'm fine. (A beat.) I'm not fine. But I can sit in a hall on my own.
> Dan: You don't have to. She asks everybody for things.

Replies: **"I'll stay with him."** (promise: stay with Dan) / **"What's wrong with him?"** / **"What is this place?"** /
**"I'd rather see this Vera first."** / **"It isn't my business."**

> (What's wrong with him?) Rae: His hand, to start with. Show them, Dan.
> Dan takes his hand out of his pocket. The first finger stays bent when the others open.
> Dan: Since the spring. And other things.
> Rae: There was an arrangement here once. Vera promised to put it right if it ever went wrong. It's gone wrong.
> (What is this place?) Dan: A boarding house. I stayed here once, a long time ago. I remember the stairs and not much else.
> (Vera first) Rae: Then come with me. She'll be upstairs, or in the back.
> (Not my business) Rae: No. It isn't. (She turns to the next.) Dan: Good for you.

Rae asks every reader who comes in; one reader's yes is never the party's. When she has asked them all, or after a minute, she goes
up the stair "to find Vera" (she knows the way: a contradiction a reader can notice). Dan stays on the bench, gets up to hold a door
for a reader, and tells a reader who stands apart: "Your friend's still in the hall."

**N2. Your rooms (the gallery).** Vera comes down the stair with Rae behind her.

> Vera: You'll be the new guests. Good. Your rooms are ready.
> Vera: Up the stairs. Your names are on the doors.
> Vera: Nobody touches anything in this house that isn't theirs. You'll find that easy.
> (to Dan) Vera: Let's have a look at you. Not here.

Each reader's room has their name on a card in the door's holder. In the bed, asleep under the blanket, is the reader's body as the
House has it: their hair, their clothes, their face, too smooth, the hands without lines, the eyes moving under the lids. A thing
of the reader's own stands on the washstand (later, from the personhood read).

Replies at the room or to Vera: **"Who is that?"** / **"What is this?"** / **"I'm not staying in here."** / **"Leave it."**

> (Who / what) Vera: That's yours. It isn't finished.
> Vera: It wants a week of looking at you. You'd be surprised how little it needs.
> Vera: When it's ready it can go out in your place for a while, and you can rest. Everybody here was tired once.
> (Not staying) Vera: Nobody makes you. The room is yours either way.
> (Any reader who strikes it) Vera: Don't. It isn't anybody's yet. You'll only spoil the work.

**The first body opens its eyes** while its reader stands near it. It does not look at the reader. It looks for Vera.

> Dan, at a room's door, seeing a body: That's not how it was. That's not how it was for me.
> (A reader alone with Dan, asking what's wrong) Dan: I've stopped sleeping. I don't like what my hands do when I'm not watching
> them.

Vera takes Dan up the corridor to the workroom. Rae goes with them.

**N3. The living original (the back passage).** From his room, when Dan's voice carries (the hall, the back passage, the parlor),
Daniel hammers on his door and shouts.

> Daniel: Is that him? Is that him out there?

A reader at the bolted door, the food hatch, or inside once the bolt is drawn:

> Daniel: You're not her. You're staying here? Listen to me.
> Daniel: There's a key for these. She keeps it, or the woman with her does.
> Daniel: Get me out of this frame before she brings him down. If she brings him down it's finished.

Replies: **"Dan's here."** / **"Who are you?"** / **"I'll get you out."** (promise: free Daniel) / **"I'll come back."** /
**say nothing**.

> (Dan's here) Daniel: I know he's here. I'd know that voice anywhere. It's mine.
> (Who are you?) Daniel: Daniel. This was my room for a week. (He looks at the marked wall.) I stopped counting.
> (I'll get you out) Daniel: Then get the key. Don't let her see you take it.

**N4. The injury.** The first failed body comes through from the passages while Vera is between rooms. It pulls at the door's
frame, then batters the latch. It goes for whoever is nearest. Dan, if he is near, puts himself between it and a reader; glass
breaks (the hall stand's, or the parlor's door light); his hand is cut. In his room at the same moment Daniel's hand opens in the
same place. A reader with Daniel sees it; a reader with Dan sees Dan's.

> Daniel (with a reader): What did you do? What did they do to him?
> Dan: It's nothing. It's a cut.

If the readers keep the body off Dan, nothing is cut, and that success stands. The rule then comes later, deliberately, with both
men's knowledge: Vera, at the frame, a pin. "Watch his hand. Not this one. His."

Act I closes on it: neither man can be treated as an imitation without the other paying for it.

### Act II: What each person wants

**N5. Daniel's account.**

> Daniel: She said a week. I'd been working nights for a year. She said I could sleep a week and something would go out and do my
> days, and when the week was done it would come back, and I'd go out, and nobody would know.
> Daniel: It didn't come back. I asked her where it was. She said it was getting on well.
> Daniel: The food comes through the hatch. I mended the chair. I mended it again.
> Daniel: I want the key. I want my name. I want whatever's left.

Replies include **"He didn't know."** (only if a reader has heard Dan's side), **"Wait."**, and, if a reader has something that cuts
or strikes, **give it to him**.

> (Given a weapon, knowing nothing) Daniel: Good.
> (A reader who saw the cut, giving it or not) "If you hurt him, you hurt yourself. I watched it."
> Daniel: Then he'd better not make me.

If Daniel strikes Dan and nobody stops him, both can die (E4). He is never made to by an unseen score: he acts when he has a
weapon, has been told Dan knew, and finds Dan within reach.

**N6. Dan's account.**

> Dan: There's a woman two doors down from me with a dog that's too old for the stairs. I carry it up for her, morning and night.
> Six years. Rae can tell you. She thinks I'm the nicest man in the street.
> Dan: That happened. I don't care what else happened. That happened.

Told Daniel is still being drained:

> Dan: No. Vera told me he'd gone home. She said he had his own life and didn't want this one.
> Dan: Show me.

Not told, he keeps looking for a repair. Told later, the scene includes who knew and who let him go on.

> (A reader who promised to stand with him) Dan: When it comes to it, what does that mean? Standing with me.

**N7. Rae's confession.** Rae has the release key: she took it off its hook in the workroom when she went "to find Vera", and keeps
it in her coat. A reader can see it in her hand when she thinks nobody is looking, or ask about a contradiction (she said she had
never been here; she knew the way up).

> Rae: I knew. All right? I knew he was alive.
> Rae: If I'd told Dan he'd never have come. He'd have sat in the flat and come apart. I told myself she could save them both.
> Rae: She can't. She told me that upstairs. One of them.

Replies and what Rae does next:

| The reader | Rae next | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gives the key back, promising to protect Dan | Goes to Vera for completion | The party has to face a process Rae helped start. |
| Takes the key and tells Dan what Rae knew | Dan confronts her before the two men meet | Their trust breaks in front of a witness. |
| Uses the key on Daniel without telling Rae | Daniel walks into the parlor | Rae tries to keep him from Dan; it begins physically. |
| Persuades her to give Daniel the key herself | She goes to his room | Daniel may refuse her apology; she can turn on Vera. |

Rae can hide the key (the cupboard under the stair), take it to Vera, or, cornered, admit it and help. One gentle reply does not
undo her fear.

**N8. Vera's offer.** In the workroom, or wherever readers find her.

> Vera: I can mend him. I need help keeping the other one quiet while I do it.
> Vera: It's a matter of making the arrangement stable. It has been loose for years.
> (Challenged with the cut, the pin, Daniel's account, or the cellar) Vera: The other one won't come through it. No.
> Vera: He has been cared for. Dan has a life to go back to. Surely you can see what would be wasted.
> Vera: Or you can send Dan back through the frame. The other walks out. It's possible. It's a waste.

The cost is known before anybody can help complete it.

**N9. The cellar.** By the passages' ladders or the back stair. The sill, the posts, the ratchet. On the dead post in the corner a
failed body is still tied by the wrists.

A reader can knock out that post's wedge. The body begins to come loose at the joins, slowly, and stays awake while it does.

> The failed body: Put it back. Put it back.

From then on that reader knows the third way: release the ratchet and the occupied frame's catch, and every original walks out,
and every borrowed body, Dan among them, starts to come apart.

### Act III: Put them in the same room

**N10. The confrontation.** If Daniel is free, he comes to the parlor. If he is held, somebody brings Dan to him or him out. If one
is dead, nobody comes back to make the scene.

> Daniel: She said a week.
> Dan: She told me you were gone.
> Daniel: I wasn't.
> Rae: He didn't know.
> Daniel: Then why did you?

The last line goes to Rae. A reader can answer, interrupt, or let her face it. Replies here include **"Tell them what Rae said"**
(only for a reader who heard her), **"Tell them what Vera said"**, **"Tell them about the cellar"**, and keeping a promise out loud.

**N11. The first attempt.** One person acts on their plan, in an order the state decides:

- Rae, still for repair, takes Dan's arm toward the stair and Vera.
- Daniel, armed and frightened, goes for Dan.
- Dan, told and supported, asks for time: "Give me a minute. Nobody decides this for me."
- Dan, cornered or lied to, takes hold of Daniel and calls for Vera.
- Vera, exposed, sends a failed body through to scatter the room and goes for the frame.

Each begins visibly and can be stopped.

**N12. A body is taken.** In the disturbance a failed body drags whoever is nearest, a reader or one of the four, toward a frame
or the passages. A taken reader is left in the passages, awake: they can make noise, work a hatch or a latch from inside, and help
from that side. Vera's preparation of the party and her repair of Dan are one activity: if the party backs completion, she brings a
reader's unfinished body down to steady at the frame and asks its original to help; if the party opposes her, she sends for a
reader instead. No reader is ever substituted without their own act.

### Act IV: What the party helped make happen

The two men's fate is the same for everybody in the house. Each reader's part in it is their own.

**E1. Return Daniel.** Dan goes back through the frame; Daniel is released; Dan is not anywhere any more. It differs by how: Dan's
own agreement, the party's force, or Vera using his panic.

> (Final image) Daniel asks Rae what the old dog's name is. Rae tells a reader instead. Daniel hears it.

**E2. Complete Dan.** Daniel is brought to the frame and held. The room grows shallower; the chair is pushed back into a wall with
nothing behind it; Daniel's voice goes on after there is no room for him. Dan lives, and is his own.

> Dan: Rae.
> Under it, from the same mouth: (Daniel's voice, finishing what he was asking.)
> Vera: It settles. It's temporary.
> (Final image) Dan holds his hand over his own mouth. Rae tries to take it away, because she wants him to talk to her.

**E3. Sever the house.** The ratchet released in the cellar, the catch at the occupied frame (in either order, slowly alone,
faster with somebody at the other). Every original walks out; Dan and the failed bodies begin to come apart. One begs for the frame
back; one goes for a freed original to force itself back in; one sits down.

> (Final image) The two men sit in sight of the front door. One is remembering how to stand without the frame. The other holds the
> shape of a hand together under his sleeve. Neither can finish helping the other.

**E4. Neither Daniel.** Ordinary violence after the link is known. Both die, in two rooms or one. Vera starts putting the room
back before anybody has moved. What happens to her and the house after that is still the party's: tell Rae what she did, stop her
preparing the next guest, leave her the house, or go down and break the support.

> (Final image) Two places laid at the dining table for the next arrivals. Rae knocks one chair over and cannot say which place she
> meant.

**Withdrawal and personal endings.** A reader can go out the front door at any time; their account says what they left and what
they knew then. A dead reader comes back by the House's own rule. A taken reader is in the story until rescued, out by their own
hand, gone, or dead. The story never waits for everybody.

## Personhood (later, after the neutral story)

As the proposal's table: AGE (the room's habits and Vera's assumed duty), HAIR (the routine object, which the body tries to use
wrong), SELF (coat, address, the glass), DRAWN_TO (privately, how Vera makes the borrowed life sound), HOME (rest, protection or
freedom), TONE and the House body (the double looks like the reader). Unknown, tentative or opted-out reads get the whole neutral
version. Nothing needed to operate, rescue or finish depends on it.

## Multiplayer

- One house for a party of up to six (six sockets, six guest rooms). A seventh reader is told at the door that the house is full
  and gets a house of their own when one is ready (the scene-copy system, `SceneInstances`). Friends who come through together are
  never split.
- No host. Anybody can do anything their hands reach; conflicting final acts resolve in server order, each revalidating the frame
  and both men first. Nothing is done to a reader's body by somebody else's choice.
- A reader holding the key who leaves, disconnects or dies drops it back where it lives (Rae's coat, or the cupboard if Rae hid
  it). A reader holding a mechanism half-worked leaves it in its ratchet's safe step.
- Late arrival before the crisis: in at the hall, Dan tells them in two lines what is going on, and Rae asks them as she asked the
  others. During the crisis: in by the scullery door, with nothing invented for them.
- Everybody leaves: the house keeps its chapter, the four where they were, the link, the taken, and waits.
- Solo: the same story; the mechanisms take longer and are louder (the ratchet alone, the frame's catch alone).
