# Horrors for the dark halls: proposal, 1 November 2026

The owner, 1 November: dark halls range from depth 16 and are the only ordinary halls from 21, and the staircase is offered from 22
(docs/DARK_HALLS_0_4_203.md). The owner then asked for horror vignettes there: "these horror vignettes should be the most extreme
yet, either psychologically or physically." Earlier the same day the owner picked two of the House of Leaves sequences as
vignettes: Johnny's storeroom and the hallway closing in on the children.

These are designs for the owner to weigh; nothing here is built. They sit in the dark band (depth 16 to 22) and are dealt only
there, one door among several, with the usual spacing.

## What "most extreme" means here

The shipped rooms already scare. Mostly they send the reader back, hurt them a little, or end a visit. These five go further in
three ways the mod can do for real.

- **The body.**
  - Three of them can kill: a real House death, with the Mother's custody of what was carried and the family portrait painted
    on a first death.
  - The others leave real damage the reader carries out.
  - The camera, movement and breath are taken from the reader at the worst moments: forced turns, drags and pins.
- **Loss.** What the House takes is gone: items from the reader's own inventory, crossings of depth, light already placed. It is
  never refunded.
- **The person.** The House already knows the reader: their handwriting, their deaths, their companions' names, their home, what
  they carry, what the porch read in them. These rooms use it. The most frightening thing in them is that they are about the
  reader.

Limits, so it stays horror and not cruelty:
- nothing is gory on screen;
- no harm to a child is ever shown, and what happens to the children is never seen;
- no reader's gender, colour or age is named;
- speech stays off chat;
- every sound is subtitled.

Every death or loss is telegraphed first, and there is always a way through.

## 1. The storeroom (Johnny's, from the book)

**Place.** A dark hall ends in a narrow storeroom, twelve deep and three wide:
- steel shelves, ink, needles in sterile packs, boxes;
- one bulb on a pull chain halfway down;
- a door behind the reader that closes on its own spring.

**The note.** On the door, in the reader's own handwriting (the journal's hand), is a list of three things to fetch.
- Two are the shop's.
- The third is the reader's own: a copy of something from a chest in their real home, from the bled-in home records. It sits on
  the far shelf with their own name on a tag in their own hand.

**Play.**
- While the reader's back is to the door, something gathers behind them:
  - the bulb's chain swings toward them;
  - breath on the subtitles, nearer each time ("Breathing, an arm's length behind you");
  - the screen's edges close;
  - the reader's own heartbeat.
- Turning to face the door holds it back. The bulb dies for a second and comes back with the shelves a block nearer, so every look
  makes the room smaller.
- From the second look the shelves press the reader's sides: real crush damage, a heart every few seconds, while they stand
  between them.
- Breath is spent fast in the pinch, and the caver's choking stage comes back.

**Fail.**
- **The clock runs out:**
  - a hand closes on the reader's shoulder;
  - the camera is turned slowly toward the door, against the mouse;
  - the bulb goes out the instant they would see;
  - a real blow, and the reader comes to on the hall floor outside.
  - One thing from their inventory is gone. The note's third thing, their own, is gone too, and comes back days later, laid on
    their bed in the manor.
- **Too many looks:** the shelves meet and the reader dies in the room. It is a real House death, with the Mother's custody and
  the portrait if it is their first.

**Win.** All three things, and back out without turning. The door opens as the reader backs into it.

**Co-op.**
- Two readers back to back hold the clock between them.
- The room shortens whenever *either* looks.
- The pinch closes on both.

**Literature.** The account is in Johnny's voice: a footnote that runs off the page and turns, partway through, into the reader's
own hand describing the same room.

The Witness line: "I didn't turn round."

Kind: survival.

## 2. The closing hall (the children, from the book)

**Place.** A long dark hall with Chad and Daisy Navidson at the far end, at child scale, holding hands, too frightened to move.

**Play.**
- When the reader reaches them, the hall begins to close in real block moves: the walls step in, the ceiling comes down, and the
  growl sounds under each step.
- The children follow only while held. A reader can lead one and carry the other on their back, at a crawl's speed.
- The hall closes on living bodies: a reader in the wall's path is pushed and crushed, real damage. It never closes on a
  child; it waits for them, so the reader feels it waiting.
- The way out is a squeeze, one block high, still coming down.
- The children go first. If the reader goes first, the children can't follow.

**The choice.** At the squeeze, if the hall is nearly shut and one child is still behind, the reader can:
- go through alone and live;
- go back for them, and be crushed in the hall, a real House death; the child is pushed through ahead of them.

**Fail.**
- **Leaving a child behind:** that child's voice calls the reader's companion's name, or the House's name for the reader, from
  inside the wall for the rest of the visit.
- **On every later visit to the dark halls:** a small handprint is on the inside of the first door they open.
- The visit fails, and the account remembers which child.

**Win.** Both children through. They run into the dark the reader came from, and their voices go on getting further away for
much too long.

**Co-op.** Two readers take a hand each and nobody carries. The hall closes twice as fast, and only one of them can be last.

**Literature.** The account is in Karen's voice, a later interview, about how long the hallway was and who came out last.

The Witness line: "I sent them through first."

Kind: survival.

## 3. The line (Exploration #4, from the book)

**Place.** A dark hall opens on a space with no walls the reader can find. A fishing line is tied to the doorframe and runs off
into the black at hand height.

**Play.**
- Right-click the line to hold it. It guides the reader, drawn only within arm's length. Let go and the dark is total until
  they find it again by walking back.
- Partway in, the line tugs: once, again, then steadily. Something at the other end is pulling.
- Holding still lets it come along the line toward the reader ("Something walking along the line, near"). Its weight drags the
  reader forward across the floor in the dark, as the elk killer's drag does: real scraping damage, the camera dipping, hands
  slipping.
- Holding on is the only way to find the end, and holding on is how it reels them in.

**Fail.** Reeled all the way:
- the line goes slack, and the floor isn't there;
- the reader comes to in an ordinary hall several crossings shallower (real depth lost from their return stack);
- the line is wound tight round their arm: a held item they can't drop until the next sleep;
- one finite thing they carried is missing, and is found, much later, tied to the end of another line.

**Win.** Reach the end before it reaches you. The line is tied to an explorer's pack and a tape recorder. The tape's log is an
original transcript, in the reader's own handwriting, dated days from now, describing this room. The last entry describes the
reader finding the pack.

**Co-op.** One reader holds the line at the door, the other follows it in. The holder feels the tugs first and can pull twice to
warn; the holder is also what the thing in the dark finds first if the follower lets go.

The Witness line: "I held on."

Kind: survival.

## 4. The stillness (the growl, made flesh)

**Place.** A wide, low dark hall with a door at each end.

**Play.**
- Halfway across, the growl comes from the far door and something enormous walks the hall. It is the Minotaur's voice and
  weight, and the reader never sees it.
- Hoofsteps shake the screen; the floor dust jumps.
- It hunts by sound:
  - walking is loud, sprinting louder;
  - crouch-walking is quiet, and standing still is silent;
  - doors, eating and dropped things are loud.
- It finds the still reader by smell and stands over them, twenty to forty seconds:
  - its breath fogs the screen in time with the subtitles counting its breaths;
  - it nudges the reader, small real shoves, to make them move;
  - their heartbeat rises until the screen shakes with it.

**Fail.** Moving while it stands over them is a goring: the arena charge's full damage, which can kill. A shield blocks it
once, and is cracked. Survivors are thrown back through the door they came in by.

**Win.** It moves on, the hoofsteps fade, and the reader reaches the far door.

**Co-op.**
- One sound draws it to everyone.
- A reader can draw it off a friend by making noise, and then has to stand still themselves while it comes.

**Literature.** The account is the reader's own, a count of its breaths, and of how many times it pushed them.

The Witness line: "I let it breathe on me."

Kind: survival.

## 5. Your own light

**Place.** Twin branches running side by side, with gaps in the wall between them.

**Play.**
- Someone walks the other branch with a torch, keeping level with the reader: the reader's own skin, walk and held item.
- It places a torch whenever they do, stops when they stop, and turns its head when they turn theirs. A beat late. Then a beat
  early.
- Halfway, it stops matching. It walks back the way the reader came, taking their torches down one by one with the House's low
  rumble, and goes out through their entry door.

**The cost.** It goes home first. Whether the reader follows or not, the next time they reach the manor:
- their bed has been slept in;
- a chest is rearranged, with nothing taken;
- the lights they left burning are put out;
- a note on their pillow, in their own handwriting, thanks them for the light.
- Until they next sleep, other readers' clients now and then show them a beat behind their own body, the way the double moved
  in the hall.

**Fail.** Following it back finds every torch gone and the entry door shut. The House rumbles, and the reader comes to at the
start of the fork with no light but what is in their hands. Nothing placed comes back, and the manor visit above happens anyway.

**Win.** Going on without looking back. At the far door their torches are set in the wall in the order they were placed, burning.
The manor is still visited, but the note says only "Not yet."

**Co-op.** Each reader's double is theirs alone. A peer sees an empty branch, and later, a friend who moves a beat late.

**Literature.** No papers. The account is the reader's own, about the light they left behind, with the note from the pillow
pasted in.

The Witness line: "I let it take them."

Kind: understanding.

## Across the five

- **Refusals.**
  - Turning round (the storeroom), sending others first (the closing hall), holding on (the line), stillness (the stillness) and
    not looking back (your own light) are five different refusals.
  - None repeats a shipped rule: Stacey's watched rule, the Masque, the motion-light foyer and the Whistlers' shutters.
- **Death.** The storeroom, the closing hall and the stillness can kill: real House deaths, with the Mother's custody and the
  portrait. The line and your own light take things instead: depth, items, light, and the manor's privacy.
- **The Minotaur.** Only the stillness uses its voice and weight. It never appears, and the staircase's own Minotaur, the cell and
  every ending are untouched.
- **Witness.** Each that is built adds one personal resolution to `WitnessAccount.Story`; the quota follows.
- **Suggested order.**
  1. The closing hall and the storeroom, which the owner already liked.
  2. The stillness.
  3. The line.
  4. Your own light, last, since it reaches into the manor and depends on the torch-taking working well.
