# The windows at the Triple Tree

Nell uses the compass to call out a direction in hide-and-seek, and has two places to hide. At the fort the reader can pick a window for their pretend house. Nell speaks from inside the fort and gives the reader time to follow her back down.

The ridge interrupts the game. The chime plays in range of the listener, from the direction of the depot. Nell waits five seconds before naming it, including the full three-and-a-half-second sound and a pause. The fort notes no longer give her explanation away to readers who reach the fort ahead of her.

The children talk about bikes, the knocks on the ridge, and what their families do when somebody disappears. The cuts are already there in childhood. Wren's conversation and the later damage to the bark give those cuts a different meaning on returning.

C.K. Walker's [Borrasca, part one](https://ck-walker.com/2016/04/12/borrasca/) was the reference for the separation between friendship, the carving custom, and what a name can mean on a return visit. Nell, Eli, and Wren are the vignette's own cast.
