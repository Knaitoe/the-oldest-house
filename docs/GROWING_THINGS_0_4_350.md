From the draft:

> *"A back fence opens onto a family yard. Familiar chores come first. Bring in washing and help move the kitchen table when the window frame bows. The action moves inside with Merry and Marjorie. Their father goes out to clear the back steps. By supper, the plants have lifted the kitchen floor enough that dinner has to be carried upstairs. Merry asks whether to bring his plate. Let the player answer by what they carry. The sisters give the vignette a connection to the Ghosts Set."*

The draft links Paul Tremblay on his story and the sisters. Merry and Marjorie are the Ghosts Set's Barrett sisters, and their father is John, as he is there. This is a Saturday years before the filming, with their mother out. Nothing is taken from the published text.

`GROWING_THINGS`, `GrowingThings`. It is a literary place, outside, built whole by `GrowingThingsSite`, in the afternoon (scene clock 16). It is dealt once to each reader, and only to a reader who has crossed one of the House's ordinary yard crossings. Its Witness kind is **understanding**. The story id is `growing_things`.

## The alley and the fence

- **The House's door** opens in the brick backs of a row of garages onto an alley: packed gravel, bins, weeds at the wall foot.
- **The back fences** run along the alley's north side. The neighbours' are boards two high. The Barretts' is boards topped with pickets, so the washing shows over it.
- **The garden gate** is the clear decision. Its latch is held by a loop of twine, and the Saturday list is tied to its post. The list is the story's source, outlined until it is read, in their mother's hand:
  - Merry, the washing in off the line before it rains, all of it;
  - Marjorie, help your sister;
  - John, the table away from that window before it goes, and please the back step;
  - supper's on the stove, don't wait for me;
  - and squeezed in at the bottom in pencil: *they came up in the night again. more by the step. Marjorie says dont pull them.*
- **The way on** is the side door of a garage along the alley, east of the House's door.

Until a reader takes a piece of washing, holds an end of the table or goes into the house, leaving costs nothing.

## The yard

- The washing line, with a sheet, another sheet, a shirt, a towel and a small dress on it.
- A swing on its frame, the shed in the corner, a garden bed of carrots along the east fence.
- **Shoots** come up in the lawn where nothing was planted: none by the fence, more and taller toward the house, thick round the line's posts and under the kitchen window. A reader who comes into the yard is told so.
- **The back step** has shoots coming up round its edges.
- The neighbours' yards and the backs of their houses stand either side; the street runs past the front.

## The house

Two storeys of clapboard. Inside, the walls are painted, the floor and fittings are the Ghosts Set's, and the rooms are a family's:

- **the kitchen** at the back, with its window over the yard (its frame already bowing in), the table by it, counters, the sink, the cooker and the fridge along the west wall, and the counter by the hall door;
- **the hall and the stairs** up the east side;
- **the front room**, with the sofa and the television;
- upstairs, **the girls' room** over the kitchen, two beds, a desk, and a card table set by the window;
- their parents' room at the front.

The front door is locked; their mother has the key.

## The day

One day for the party: one washing line, one table, one supper. It runs on its own clock while anyone is past the gate.

1. **The washing.** When a reader comes into the yard Merry asks for help: "Mom says before it rains." Each piece comes off the line by hand. Merry takes a piece herself every thirteen seconds or so; with help it goes quicker. When it is all in, the basket by the back door is full.
2. **The table.** When a reader comes into the house John asks for a hand: the window is going. It takes a pair of hands on each end.
   - A reader holds an end for ten seconds while they stay by it.
   - Two readers on the two ends lift it together.
   - A reader alone gets John on the other end.
   - If nobody has helped after ninety seconds, John drags it away himself, and Merry finishes the washing.
3. **The afternoon** begins when both are done.
   - Marjorie stands at the kitchen window, watching the yard. Merry talks.
   - At ten seconds John takes a spade out to clear the back step. The girls go to the window.
   - He digs at the step, and the shoots round it come up faster than he can turn them.
   - At twenty seconds the window frame splits, with shoots through the pane.
   - From thirty seconds, John is gone the first moment nobody can see him. His spade is left standing in the shoots, and the step goes under them. Marjorie says, "He's out with them."
   - If someone is still watching him forty-five seconds into the afternoon, he leaves the spade standing and walks round the side of the house, and is gone there when nobody is looking.
   - A reader who later sees the spade is told so.
4. **The floor.** At twenty, forty and sixty seconds the kitchen floor lifts: a ridge, then a tent with shoots in the gaps, then broken boards and shoots. Nobody is left standing inside the boards. Readers in the house feel each stage.
5. **Supper.** When the floor has broken, Merry says they can't eat down there; their mother said they could eat upstairs if they had to. Then, to everyone past the gate: *"Should we bring Dad's plate?"*
   - The girls carry their own plates upstairs and sit at the card table.
   - The pot, the loaf and Dad's plate (his name scratched into the rim, like the others') wait on the counter by the hall door. A hand on the counter takes them one at a time.
   - A hand on the card table sets down whatever the reader carries. His plate goes at the place by the window.

## Endings

All three resolve one source, `growing_things`:

| --- | --- |
| The pot, the loaf and his plate | `carried_his_plate_upstairs`: the girls sit with it, and Marjorie says he will come up for it |

| Nothing: the reader went back out through the gate after committing and before supper was up | `went_back_through_the_fence`: their own ending, at once |

- When the pot and the loaf are both up, the girls wait five seconds for his plate, or fifteen while a reader still has it.
- If nobody carries anything for ninety seconds, Merry fetches the pot and the loaf herself, and leaves his plate.

**Roles.**

- His plate up: whoever set it down is the actor.
- His plate left: whoever carried the pot or the loaf is the actor. If nobody did, everyone there is.
- Everyone else past the gate is a witness.
- A reader who came in once the floor was lifting is partway.

**The yield.** Marjorie puts a dry seed head off the kitchen sill in each reader's hand. A reader who went back through the gate finds one caught in their sleeve.

## The House's read

- **HOME = HAVEN.** When the girls sit down, Merry pats the place beside her for that reader: "You can sit here." The account says so.
- **HOME = DANGER.** When the window frame splits, Marjorie steps between that reader and the window. The account says so.

## The accounts

"The account: growing things", in the reader's hand, built only from their own record:

- the gate, and the shoots in the lawn;
- how many pieces of washing they took off the line;
- whose hands were on the table with theirs (John's, or another reader's), or that John dragged it alone;
- Merry keeping a place, or Marjorie at the window;
- John going out with the spade, walking round the side of the house if they watched, and not coming back in;
- his spade standing in the shoots;
- the window splitting, and Marjorie stepping between them and it;
- the floor coming up;
- Merry's question;
- what they carried, and who carried his plate if they didn't;
- the girls with his plate, or his plate on the counter and Merry looking down the stairs;
- or that they went back out through the gate, and the seed head in their sleeve.

It ends, for those at supper, with the seed head in their hand and the yard seen from the girls' window. Nothing names the reader's gender, a colour or an age.

The Witness line is "I helped a family with their Saturday chores while something came up through their yard. Their father went out to clear the back step and didn't come back in, and at supper his daughter asked whether to bring his plate up."

## The Ghosts Set, years later

For a reader who finished Growing Things, in the Ghosts Set:

- **A child's drawing** is held by a magnet to the Barretts' fridge: a house, a yard of tall strokes up to the windows, and a figure standing in them with its arms out. It is sent to that reader alone, so nobody else sees it and the world itself is unchanged.
- **Merry looks at them** once, for a moment, as if she remembers them from somewhere.

## The seed head, carried

Carried, it leans the dealing a little toward the House's places where things grow: the storm field, the windbreak, the Marsh farm, the tall grass and the Sphinx's field crossing. Pressed into earth or grass, it goes in, and nothing comes of it.

## Reset

Once the yard has been empty for ten seconds, it stands as it was that morning: the washing on the line, the table by the window, the floor flat, the step bare, no spade, and the family home.
