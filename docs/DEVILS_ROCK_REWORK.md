# Disappearance at Devil's Rock, reworked

> "Devil's Rock: complete rework." The owner sent Paul Tremblay's novel ("for when you're designing that"), left the open
> choices to me, and then asked: "I'd really like to incorporate the maddened chase sequence from devil's rock story - we
> chased each other until we forgot which was prey and which was predator?"

The old room was a plaster box with a bed, a desk, two pressure plates, a tripwire and a sculk sensor. Eight loose pages
turned up on its floor over three visits. That mechanic was sound, but nothing around it came from the book. This rebuild
puts the pages back in the Sandersons' house and puts the park behind the house.

## What the story gives

- Tommy Sanderson, fourteen, goes missing one night in Borderland State Park. He was at the split boulder his friends
  Josh and Luis call Devil's Rock.
- At home his mother Elizabeth and his sister Kate find pages from his diary on the floors, in rooms they had just
  walked through. Elizabeth sees him crouched in the empty space between her plush chair and the little end table Kate
  painted with flowers. She sees him only out of the corner of her eye.
- Elizabeth sets up a camera in the house, and later another one at the rock.
- Arnold Rooney, a man in his twenties who called himself a seer, befriended the boys over that summer.
- The last night, in the three statements: at the rock Tommy runs off the trail, sees his own double come out of the
  trees and run away ("It looked dead. Did you see the eyes? There was something over its eyes"), tells the others
  "Don't follow me", and charges down the trail. Rooney runs. Then: "it flipped, you know, it flipped, me and Tommy
  switching spots. Tommy wasn't chasing me anymore. I was chasing him. We never said anything. No names." Tommy runs
  into a pond, swims to an island and is found there between two rocks pushed almost together, crouched as if hiding,
  with the old pennies Rooney took from his bureau.
- Afterwards Josh looks into the split: "I thought I saw him sitting there all hunched up, like he was hiding. But he
  wasn't there."

## Decisions

- **No Rooney on screen and no body.** The House never shows the man or the boy's death. What the reader meets is the
  double: Tommy's shape, with two pennies over its eyes.
- **The chase is the climax, and it never uses words.** Nothing is written on screen during it, no names, only feet,
  breath and branches.
- **Prey and predator trade places.** The reader chases the double until it nearly closes the distance or loses sight
  of it; then it is behind them. Run from it until it catches up, or turn and run at it, and it is ahead again. The swaps
  come faster as the chase goes on, and the double never says which way round it is.
- **The pennies tie the house to the island.** They sit in a dish on Tommy's bureau. On the last night the dish is empty
  for that reader, and the pennies lie between the two rocks on the island.
- **The way on is Tommy's closet,** "open just a crack", as on the book's last page.

## The place

An outdoor scene at midnight (scene clock 18000):

- **The front walk and the house.** A one-storey ranch house: entry, living room (couch, television, the camera on its
  tripod facing the couch), kitchen (the mother's watch log on the table, a missing poster on the fridge), the hall,
  Tommy's room (blue walls, bed, bureau with the dish of pennies, desk, shelf, closet), Kate's room, and Elizabeth's
  room (bed, plush chair, the painted end table, and the gap between them).
- **The back yard,** a deck and a fence with a gate, and beyond it the park.
- **The woods.** Mixed trees, undergrowth and old stone walls, with a trail from the gate to the split rock.
- **Devil's Rock.** A boulder split clean through, the trail running through the split, the devil's footprint worn
  into its top and a tree beside it. A trail camera is strapped to a trunk facing it.
- **The pond,** murky and weedy, with an island; two rocks on the island stand almost pushed together.

## Visits

Pages keep their old rule: three appear each visit, unseen, in the house's rooms (eight over three visits, read in order,
each one where it lies). In addition:

1. **The first night.** A shape crouches in the gap between the chair and the end table in Elizabeth's room, there only
   at the edge of the reader's sight. The trail camera has no motion on it.
2. **The second night.** The shape crouches at the end of the hall by the back door. The trail camera's print has a
   frame of something crouched on top of the rock.
3. **The last night.** The shape is in the split of the rock. The dish on the bureau is empty. Once the eighth page has
   been read, the double comes out of the trees beside the rock and runs. The chase goes round the woods and swaps
   until it breaks for the pond. The double swims to the island and crouches between the two rocks. Looked at
   straight, it is not there, and the pennies are on the ground between the rocks. The account lies on the island.

If the reader leaves the park mid-chase, the chase ends; it starts again at the rock. Being caught costs nothing but a
moment of dark and the swap. Every part of this is private: each reader's pages, shape, double and chase are their own.
Readers in the house and the park together each see only their own figures.

## Resolution

Still the memory source "The eight loose pages". It is now ready when the reader reaches the island after the chase,
rather than as soon as the eighth page is read, with the outcome `followed_him_to_the_island`. The Witness line and the
account tell the chase and the island. The last page's keepsake ("tell him the path was still there") is unchanged.
