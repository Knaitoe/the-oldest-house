## The morning

At dawn Vera comes in from the gauge room to her porch. Once she is there and the morning has run (fifteen seconds), Day Two begins: "Slate first."

Whatever was done or not done to prime the intake on Day One, the surge filled it; the pump has its load this morning.

## The slate and the far end

Vera walks into the office to the work slate and reads it: "Ellis is still up there. Marked present, the night of the last crossing. I never rubbed it out." Everyone in the office remembers hearing it.

She lays the cable tools on the desk, rolled in canvas, and asks: "Cable tools are on the desk. The drum wants lining up on the witness marks at the far end before tonight's pull. Who's taking the far end?" Then she goes back to her porch.

Whoever takes the tools off the desk has the far end. The tools can be handed to another of the crew like the lamp and the wrench, and anything of the station's carried off it goes back where it belongs: the tools go back on the desk.

## The winch

The design's puzzle, in three places in the cable house:

- **The witness plate** on the cable bed at the far end, by the last roller. It is greased over until someone scrapes it with the cable tools. Once it is clean, a hand on it reads how far the stripe of paint on the cable sits from the mark cut in the bed: "two hand-widths short of", "three past", or "square on". The reading can be called aloud, said over the reader's head for everyone near: "Two short!", "Three past!", or "Marks meet! Set the catch!"
- **The brake** by the drum. Eased, the drum pays out a notch; leaned on crouching, it is cranked in a notch. Each notch ratchets aloud.
- **The parking catch** beside the brake.

The drum starts three or four notches short of the marks, or two or three past them. Left alone once anyone has touched it, the drum creeps out a notch every ten seconds under the river's pull ("Nobody on the brake: the drum creeps out a notch."). A reader staying at the brake holds it there. So one reader stays on the brake while another reads the marks; the two can talk, call aloud, or ring the work bell by the roller bed.

The parking catch is for a reader working alone. Dropped into the drum's teeth, it stops the brake from being worked until it is knocked out again. Set square on the marks, it holds there for the pull. Set anywhere else, it slips after twenty seconds and the drum runs out a notch. Set on the marks, it lines the drum up: everyone who had a hand in it (on the brake, the plate or the catch) remembers lining it up, and with whom. If Vera is within earshot she says "That's it. Leave it there."

The pull itself is Night Two's.

## The branches

Two service branches can be flooded, each by a bypass wheel on a riser out of the floor with a tin tag wired to it:

- **The ramp branch:** the low tunnel from the sluice pit under the ramp and the spillway to the cable house's flooded passage. Its bypass stands in the intake, beside the grate into the pit. Its grates are into the pit and into the passage.
- **The winch branch:** a short spur off the passage, north under the cable house floor, coming up under a floor grate behind the brake. Its bypass stands in the cable house by that grate. Its sluice stands between it and the passage until the bypass is opened.

Opening a bypass floods its branch. Nothing records who turned it except the wheel itself, and anyone who saw it turned: they remember whose hand it was ("turns a bypass wheel open").

What an open bypass leaves for the crew to notice:

- **The wheel**, turned, and its tag turned with it.
- **The grates**, wet while the branch runs, then damp for a minute after it is shut, then dry. A reader close to one, or standing over it, remembers.
- **Running water** behind the grates, heard close to them, and water dripping through them.
- **A stain** on the boards under the fairlead, spreading while the winch branch runs and drying for a minute after.
- **The gauge.** Once the intake is primed the needle dips every thirty seconds, as on Day One. With a bypass open, the same dip comes every ten. A reader at the gauge notices: "The needle dips again, sooner than it did yesterday."

Shut again, a branch drains. Its sluice will not drop on a body in its way.

## The Call by day

From the design: *"During daylight, an infected reader crouching beside a wet grate can draw one nearby hostile mob to it."*

A carrier crouching at a wet grate (over the winch branch, or the ramp branch's grate into the pit) for three seconds calls:

- Something scrapes an answer through the pipes. The carrier is told "Down in the branch, something scrapes an answer, and comes." Readers in the intake, the cable house or the pit, or near a speaking pipe or one of the grates, hear "Something scrapes, deep in the pipes, and stops."
- Three seconds later one of the things from the water comes up through that grate, in broad daylight, if the branch is still wet. If the bypass has been shut meanwhile, it only taps once under the iron.
- What comes up stays half a minute, then goes back into the water.
- A carrier can call once every forty-five seconds.

**The things pass a carrier by.** From now on, Night One's as well, the Undertow go for whoever else is nearest and turn on a carrier only if the carrier hurts them. When one is close to a carrier and going for someone else, the carrier knows, and whoever sees it remembers whom it passed by.

**The plate.** Within four blocks of the plate, standing, hung or carried, the Call catches in the throat and nothing answers.

## Vera

- **Day Two's panel.** "What needs doing?" explains the drum, the far end, the brake, the spillway and the gauge. "Did anybody ever answer for Ellis?" She admits it: "Once. The night the ferry went over, I called the roll down the intake pipe, like a fool. Something answered. I took my ear off the pipe and I didn't put it back." The carrier can still tell her "It got a claw in me." aloud.
- **Before dusk** (five sixths of the way through the day): "Leave the brake set for the first pull. Whatever it's lined up on at dark, that's what we pull on." Then: "You'll want to know what it sounded like. It was Ellis's voice. Coming up the pipe a breath late, like it had been listening first." Everyone within reach of her voice remembers it.
- **The second dusk:** "That's the light going. Get to your places." Everyone on the roll who is there has worked Day Two.

The carrier's second breath keeps coming through the day, every forty-five seconds, heard by nobody else.

## Reset

Both bypasses shut and their branches drained, the grates dry, the witness plate greased over, the catch off, no stain, the tools put away and the cable house's brake set; the day count back to one.
