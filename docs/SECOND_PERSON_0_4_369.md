# Second Person, winter (0.4.369)

The proposal is `docs/SECOND_PERSON_PROPOSAL.md`; the first slice, summer and autumn and the last morning, is `docs/SECOND_PERSON_0_4_274.md`. This slice puts the winter between autumn and the last morning:

> *"This is the season the reader is seen, and the season the House begins to edit."*

- **Seasons:** summer, autumn, **winter**, the last morning. The cupboard after autumn now opens on the winter; the cupboard after the winter opens on the last morning (E1), as before.
- **Witness:** unchanged, 81 / 61 / two kinds / three endings. Still one understanding source, resolved by reading the account.
- **Layout:** 122 (the hall table and chair). **Protocol:** 162 (three new fittings).
- **Neutral and HAVEN only.** The DANGER and RUNAWAY winter beats, spring and the other endings are later slices.

## The funeral

> *"While you were in the cupboard: Iris died. They went to the funeral in a borrowed coat. You were not there."*

- **The coat:** borrowed only if theirs was lost (hidden under the bed or carried off). Otherwise they wore the coat from the hook.
- **What was under the bed:** anything the reader pushed under the bed in the summer turns up now: "looking for their good shoes". It goes back where it lives.
- **The tin:** they come in from the landing with Iris's sewing tin and set it on the kitchen counter. "She left me this." At the table they read her note aloud: *"For you, and for your lodger."* They turn it over. "What lodger?"

## Iris's tin

> *"Inside are needles, buttons and a half-finished sleeve. The reader can put the sleeve on the armchair arm, where the resident will find it and finish it, or leave the tin shut."*

- **Open it:** needles, buttons, a card of thread, and a sleeve half-finished on its needles.
- **The sleeve:** taken out, it can be laid on the arm of the armchair. That evening they find it: "...Iris's." Then: "She never finished anything. Look at this." By the end of the winter it is finished and folded on the seat.
- **Changing your mind:** before they find it, the sleeve can be taken back off the arm or put back in the tin.

## The care scene

> *"In grief the resident stops keeping their routine. The reader can lay the object out each morning."*

- **Three mornings at the basin.** Each night their routine (the bathroom object the quiet rooms kept) goes into the drawer under the basin. A routine the reader hid somewhere else stays hidden.
- **Laying it out:** take it from the drawer and set it on the shelf over the basin, or anywhere in the bathroom, before they give up on the morning (400 ticks).
- **What follows** depends on how many mornings it has been laid out:
  - the first: they look at it a long time and leave it;
  - the second: they use it, and cry, and keep going;
  - the third: "All right. All right."
- **Not laid out:** they stand at the basin, do nothing, and go back to bed for an hour.

## The ticket

> *"HAVEN: the parent goes into hospital. A train ticket is on the hall table. The reader can put it on the mat where it cannot be missed, hide it, or leave it."*

- **The post:** on the first winter day it comes through the door, and they put it on the new hall table unopened. "Later."
- **What is in it:**
  - HAVEN: a letter from home with a train ticket, and on the envelope: *They're in hospital. Come when you can.*
  - Neutral: a card from the old friend with a ticket for New Year.
- **The second day** reads where the ticket is:
  - **On the mat by the door:** they find it ("...Oh."), pack and go. The flat is the reader's for a while, and nothing moves unless the reader moves it. They come back changed:
    - HAVEN: "They knew me. Not the first day. Then they did."
    - Neutral: "I'd forgotten I liked people."
  - **Left on the table, or out somewhere:** they put it in a pocket. "Next week."
  - **Hidden:** they never know it came.
- **Not going:** at the end of the winter the phone rings, and they do not answer it. The reader can lift it.
  - HAVEN: the ward.
  - Neutral: the old friend, who waited for both trains.

## The sleepwalk

> *"Some nights the resident gets up asleep and does, in the flat, what the reader did in the quiet rooms, in the order the reader did it."*

- **When:** the first winter night. They get up with their eyes open, asleep.
- **Where they go:** to each quiet room the reader went through, in the House's order. The reader's House read records them on first arrival (`Snap.Walk`).
  - **The kitchen table** (if the reader hung a drawing there): they draw at the table without a light on. In the morning a face is on the table, the one the reader drew.
  - **The waiting room:** they sit on the hall chair. "Is it me next?"
  - **The bathroom:** they do their routine twice in the dark, with nothing in their hands if it is not on its shelf.
  - **The coatroom:** they put on the coat.
  - **The shelter:** they stand at the street window. "It'll stop in a minute."
- **Habits:**
  > *"The reader's own habits in the flat join the sleepwalk."*

  The reader's most repeated act in the flat joins the walk if it was done more than once: the lamp on, the tap run, the glass turned, or a knock.
- **Last of all, the latch:** they go to the front door and open it. HAVEN: "Is that you? I've left it open for you."
- **Bringing them back:** at any point, the lamp turned on, a knock or a hand. They wake: "...How did I get here?" and go back to bed.
- **Let be:** they wake in the open doorway, cold. In the morning: "I woke up at the door."
- **No read:** with the read off, the walk is only their habit, if they have one, and the latch.

## Being seen

> *"Once this winter the resident sees the reader. It happens in the bathroom mirror if it faces the room, otherwise in the black window, otherwise in the television."*

- **Where:** the flat's mirror faces the room, so it is the bathroom mirror on a morning at the basin. Failing that, it is the dark television, from the armchair of an evening.
- **How:** the reader stands behind them, in the same room, where the glass would show them. "...That's my face." Then:
  - **Stay still:** they stare, then ask "Are you me?". A YES or NO written on glass soon after is their answer.
  - **Step out of the glass:** "...Gone." Only the room.
  - **Touch them:** what it means depends on the year so far.
    - Gentle: "It's you. It's been you."
    - Otherwise: "Don't. Don't touch me." They back away until the wall stops them.
  - **Write a word on the glass:** they read it at once. From winter the glass also holds YES and NO.
- **Once only.**

## Smaller things

- **Iris's wall:** knocked on in the winter, nobody knocks back.
- **Cold glass:** the street window is cold all winter and holds a word at any hour. The mirror steams when they run the hot tap of a morning.
- **The street:** in winter the tree is bare and snow lies on both pavements. The snow is gone by the last morning.

## When you are not looking

> *"From winter on, the book holds pages the reader did not turn."*

- **When:** the reader goes into the cupboard at the winter's end, and the House carries on in their name. Following the proposal's crueller choice, the book does not warn first.
- **Two pages,** following the reader's own pattern (`SecondPersonStory.gentle` against `fright`):
  - **Gentle:** *"74. You sat on the end of their bed while they slept..."* and the kettle on before they were up.
  - **Frightening, and a little worse than the reader:** *"74. You stood at the end of their bed all night. They lay very still so you would think they were asleep."* and every door opened, one after another.
  - **Neither:** the street window most nights, and once seen there from the street.
- **The resident** speaks of it on the last morning as the reader's doing: "You were there all night. At the end of the bed."
- **Kept apart:**
  - the book's latest page lists them as *the pages you did not turn*;
  - the account says they are not the reader's pages;
  - the keepsake prints them last, in a different type.

## The decision, the account

- **Stay or go** now also reads the winter:
  - towards staying: the mornings laid out, the sleeve finished, going to the ticket, being brought back, a kind look in the glass;
  - towards going: the hidden ticket, the walk let be to the door, stepping out of the glass, the House's worse pages.
- **The account** gains the winter, in order:
  - Iris and the tin, and what was under the bed;
  - the sleeve;
  - the mornings;
  - the night they walked, in the reader's own order;
  - the ticket and the phone;
  - the glass;
  - Iris's wall;
  - then, apart, the House's pages.

## Multiplayer

The flat is each reader's own copy (SceneInstances), so the winter is too. Two readers who went in together have two different winters, and two different sets of pages the House turned for them.
