# Bike night 24: the specters (the witch, and being caught, 0.4.499; the ghosts, 0.4.500; the werewolf, 0.4.501; the headless rider, 0.4.502)

The proposal (`docs/BIKE_NIGHT_PROPOSAL.md`, "Act three"): "**Specters chase**", and "Being caught by any of them is
the Hollow's failure." As answered on November 11: the Hollow is one shared course, and each specter is private to
whoever it is after.

This is the first of the four, and what happens to a reader one of them catches. The werewolf, the ghosts and the
headless rider are not built.

## The witch

"**witches** swoop low on brooms across the path, to be ducked or dodged", and "ducking a witch is the hop's crouch, so
a witch just before a gap is a choice."

**Who she is.** A long face the colour of old leaves, hair down her back, all in black; a pointed hat with a band and
a buckle; a broom. Her skin is painted with the rest of the cast (`literary_hollow_witch`, stranger phase 187). Her
hat and broom are their own small model and sheet, drawn in code (`tools/draw_calder_specters_art.py`), on the cast's
own renderer. On her broom she leans along it with her hands on the stick and her legs forward.

**Where.** She crosses the plain way on three rows: two and a half blocks short of the gap of three, where the hop
has to be held anyway; on the vine's top, where it is two blocks wide and swinging; and on the top, two and a half
blocks short of the gap to the last stretch.

**For one reader.** A witch is there for one reader and nobody else sees or hears her. She comes for them when they
are within ten blocks of her row on that part of the way, from one side or the other, twelve blocks out and five up,
and they hear her cackle. She drops to a rider's head height as she crosses the way and climbs away on the other side.

**Timing.** Until they are within four blocks of her row she times herself to cross as they reach it, at whatever
speed they are making. After that she does not change. So a rider who keeps on meets her; one who brakes hard in the
last four blocks has her pass in front of them; one who is already past is not hers.

**Ducked or caught.** Whoever is in her row and in her reach as she crosses, and is down over their bars, has her go
over them. Down over the bars is the hop held: a third of the way into its crouch, which is four ticks of holding.
(On foot, sneaking.) Letting the hop go afterwards is a full hop. Whoever is in her row and reach and is sitting up is
caught. Each row is crossed once for each reader in a night.

One sound, hers: "A witch cackles" (41 cues).

## Caught

"**The moment:** a specter catches the reader. The screen goes to mist. **Afterwards:** they come round at dawn outside
the graveyard's front gate, alive, the bike gone."

- The mist is over everything for four seconds (as much as their comfort setting lets through, and nothing with that
  at nothing), and what is in it is heard.
- They are on the pavement outside the cemetery's front gate, on foot, facing it, unhurt, and told: "You come round on
  the pavement outside the cemetery gate. The bike is gone."
- The bike is gone from under them, or from where it lay, and is nobody's for the rest of the night. It does not go
  back to the rack.
- It is in their record, with what caught them. They take no other bike that night: a hand on one is told what a
  reader the three have robbed is told.
- Whoever was on their Hopper's pegs is left where they were, over whatever was under them, and is set down on their
  own island by the checkpoint's rule, on foot.
- "In co-op the others ride on and see it happen": they see mist where the caught reader was.

## Cases

One (87 in the namespace). It passed the first time it was run.

- Her three rows are on the plain way at its height, the first and last within three blocks of a brink.
- **Standing half a block short of her first row, down over the bars**: a witch comes from one side and goes to the
  other, keeping to her row, as low as a rider's head over the way and no lower; she is her reader's alone (two others
  on the hill are not told of her and do not hear her); she goes over, and they are still riding. Standing in that
  row again, nothing comes.
- **Standing short of her second row, sitting up**: caught. They are outside the front gate on foot, facing it,
  unhurt, with room to stand; the Lamp is gone from under them, is not back in the rack and is nowhere on the course;
  it is in their record; a hand on another bike does not put them on it; they are told, with the mist over them for
  the four seconds. The others ride on.
- **Flat out up the fences from some nineteen blocks short of her first row, sitting up**: she has them at her row.
- **The same, down over the bars from six blocks short**: she goes over them at her row and they ride on.

**Found by the full gate.** The case that rides the whole of the plain way sits up all the way. The first time the
gate ran with her in it, she caught its rider at her first row. That is her doing what she is for. The course's own
cases now keep the specters away, as they keep Gus home and the three away; her own case does not.

A page of the client proof shows her as the game draws her: on her broom from the side, the front, and behind and
above, and standing (25 pages).

## 0.4.500: the ghosts

"**ghosts** drift through the course and through the riders, chilling their view."

**Who they are.** Three of the hill's dead in what they were buried in (a long dress, a coat, a shirt and a beard),
every part of them the colour of the mist, with hollow faces. They are painted with the rest of the cast (stranger
phases 188 to 190) and drawn half there. All three are grown: no child is among them.

**Where.** Three stretches of the plain way are haunted: the three graves, the grave past the lids, and the stairs.

**For one reader.** While a reader is on a haunted stretch, ghosts come for them one after another, two and a half
seconds apart, never more than two at once, and nobody else sees them. Each comes from one side or the other, nine
blocks off and five to eight blocks ahead of where its reader is, a little over the ground where it will cross the
way, and drifts across and on out the other side. It thins in as it comes and thins out as it goes. It hangs in the
air with its feet behind it and its head down.

**They catch nobody.** One that passes within a little over a block of its reader goes through them, once: some of
the mist is over their view for a second and a half (three fifths of what going down gives, and as much of that as
their comfort setting lets through), and they hear it go by. That is all it does. A rider who is chilled a few
blocks short of a gap cannot see the gap well.

One sound: "A ghost goes through you" (42 cues). The mist's message also says how much of it is over them: protocol
185.

### Cases

One (88 in the namespace). It passed the first time it was run. A reader standing on the foot of the stairs has a
ghost come from nine blocks to one side, five to eight ahead, a little over the stairs where it will cross them, thin
as it comes. Over the next sixteen seconds they come one after another, two at the most at once, each its reader's
alone, never more than half there, crossing from one side to the other; nothing happens to the reader, and none
comes for a second reader on the hill. Moved into one's way as it crosses, the reader has it go through them: they
hear it and some of the mist is over them; kept inside it for a second, they are chilled the once; they are not caught
or hurt and are still riding. Off the haunted stretch, the ones that are out go and no more come.

The case first said that nothing chilled the reader again in the two seconds after. The full gate's run with the
werewolf showed that it could: with two ghosts out, the other one went through them too. The case now keeps its
reader inside one ghost and counts.

The proof's page of the specters has the three of them under the witch, over a dark band, so that what shows
through them is seen. I have looked at it: they are pale and alike, and read as three people more than as three
different people.

## 0.4.501: the werewolf

"a **werewolf** runs alongside on a parallel island and leaps across at gaps", and "The werewolf leaps at gaps, so a
gap is timed."

**Who he is.** Fur where skin would be and on his face, yellow eyes, what is left of a flannel shirt and a pair of
jeans; a muzzle, two ears and a tail. His skin is painted with the rest of the cast (stranger phase 191); the muzzle,
ears and tail are their own small model and sheet, drawn in code. He goes on four legs: running, gathered to leap,
and stretched out in the air.

**His ground.** He has two of the plain way's gaps: from the first grave to the second, and the one in the last
stretch. Beside each there is a strip of sod he runs up, a perch at its end level with the gap, and a perch across the
way from it. It is real ground, but none of it is within two blocks of ground anybody rides, and none of it is an
island.

**For one reader.** He comes for a reader who is within fourteen blocks of one of his gaps, at the far end of his
run, and runs up it keeping six blocks ahead of them as far as his perch. They hear him howl when he takes it.

**The rhythm.** From his perch he leaps across the way, over the middle of the gap, to his other perch; and back; and
so on. He is on a perch for thirty-six ticks, the last ten of them gathered, and in the air for fourteen, a little over
two blocks high at the top: two and a half seconds from one leap to the next.

**Caught.** Whoever is over the gap, between its brinks, within a block and a third of him as he crosses, is caught,
as a reader the witch has is. Whoever is on the ground is not his, however near he passes. So the gap is crossed just
after he lands: there is nearly two seconds before he is in the air again.

**He goes** when his reader is four blocks past the gap, or well back from it. He is at each gap once for each reader
in a night: somebody who goes down at his gap and comes back to it finds it empty.

One sound: "A werewolf howls" (43 cues).

### Cases

- **A reader standing a block short of the brink**: he comes at the far end of his run, runs up it, and leaps from

The proof's page of the specters has him between the witch and the ghosts: running from the side and from the front

## 0.4.502: the headless rider

"a **headless rider on a headless horse** comes up behind on the last stretch", and "The headless rider on the last
stretch is a check on pace."

**Who he is.** All in one dark cloth to the wrists, gloves and boots, and no head; nothing is drawn where it would be.
His horse is the colour of slate, with a saddle on a cloth and the stump of a neck that ends in nothing with a pale
rim. No wound is drawn on either of them. His skin is painted with the rest of the cast (stranger phase 192); the
horse is its own model and sheet, drawn in code, with legs that go in pairs. He sits it leaning into the gallop.

**Where and when.** He comes for a reader who rides onto the last stretch, ten blocks behind them and no further
back than the top, and rides after them up the middle of the way. His horse does not mind the gap.

**The pace.** He makes 0.22 of a block a tick. A bike is pedalled at 0.32. So somebody who keeps going draws away
from him; somebody who stops, or waits too long at the gap, or goes down and is set back, has him come up. They hear
the horse, louder as he closes.

**Caught.** Whoever he comes within a block and a fifth of is caught, as the witch's and the werewolf's are. He pulls
up and is gone when his reader is on the far side's ground. He comes once for each reader in a night.

**With the werewolf.** The gap in the last stretch is one of the werewolf's. A rider who reaches it at speed has the
rider some thirteen blocks behind, which he covers in about three seconds; the werewolf's cycle is two and a half.
So there is time to wait for the werewolf to land once, and not twice. (This is arithmetic. No case rides it with
both of them there.)

One sound: "Hooves come up behind" (44 cues).

Each specter's case now runs with only its own specter coming (`BikeNightSpecters.only`), so that the rider's case is
not also the werewolf's.

### Cases

One (90 in the namespace). It passed the first time it was run.

- On the top, nobody is behind anybody.
- **A reader who rides onto the last stretch and stops** has him come ten blocks behind them on the ground that is
  there; he comes on and never falls back; he is their reader's alone, who hears the horse; and he has them in about
  two seconds, at his own pace. It is in their record that it was him. A second reader on the top is not his.
- **A reader who rides the last stretch without stopping**, with a tap at its gap, is on the far side's ground with

The proof has a page of him: from the side at two moments of the horse's stride, from the front quarter, and from

at night, and had the pale front of a shirt at his collar with a dark line down it, which read as a wound; the horse
is slate now and he is one dark cloth.

## Not built, not seen, and left with the owner

- **The ghouls and goblins that jump out.**
- The rider's numbers are first guesses: ten blocks, 0.22, a block and a fifth. Whether three seconds at the last gap
  with the werewolf across it is fair for a failure that is for good is the owner's to say.
- The werewolf's numbers are first guesses: thirty-six ticks, fourteen in the air, a block and a third. A rider who
  does not look and rides straight at the gap is over it for about a third of a second, and he is in the air for
  seven tenths of a second in every two and a half; whether that is the right chance of being caught for good is the
  owner's to say.
- **"At dawn"** is not built: it is still night outside the gate. **The headstone** with the reader's name, the
  rusting bike, and **"the vignette is never dealt to them again"** are the endings' part and are not built; the House
  does not deal the night to anybody yet.
- She crosses only the plain way. Nothing crosses the high way or the long way.
- Her look is a first one, seen only on the proof's page and not in a client in the world. Whether she reads from a
  saddle at night, coming in from twelve blocks out, is the owner's to see.
- The numbers are first guesses the case passes with: ten blocks, four blocks, twelve out and five up, a reach of
  nine tenths of a block, a third of the crouch. Whether four ticks of warning in the crouch is fair, and whether a
  second and a half of cackle is enough to find her by, are the owner's to try.
- A reader on the pegs has the pedaller's crouch: if the one pedalling ducks, both are down.
