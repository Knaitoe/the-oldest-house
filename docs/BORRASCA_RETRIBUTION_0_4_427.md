# Borrasca: retribution against the Sheriff (0.4.427)

The owner, November 9: "Ensure the player can enact violent retribution against the sheriff too, when they're an adult."

## Before

The Sheriff could be struck only on the last night. After two blows he hit back, and after three he yielded ("All right. Go.") and
could never be hurt again. He could not die, and a child's blows did nothing.

## Now

On both grown visits (the third, at the tape by the second marker, and the last night, in the depot yard) the reader can fight him,
beat him and kill him. A child still cannot hurt him.

- **The fight.** A blow lands for three to eight points of his forty, whatever it is struck with, and he never dies from it outright.
  From the first blow he turns on the reader: he comes at them and swings his flashlight for a heart and a half about once a second.
  He breaks off the blockade, the escort or his patrol to do it. A reader who runs more than twenty-four blocks off is let go, for now.
- **Beaten.** At fourteen or less he goes down in the dirt, sitting with his legs out and his hands up, the flashlight gone. He
  says "Wait. Wait. You don't know what this town is." Talked to, he pleads, in a line that changes each time: it was never his idea,
  there was a Sheriff before him, there's a waiting list for the badge, the Prescotts own the ground. A reader can walk away; he is on
  his feet again, healed, on the next visit.
- **Killed.** A blow after he has gone down (not in the same moment) kills him. "He doesn't get up." He lies face down where he
  fell and takes no more blows. The body stays there. Nobody comes for it: if he dies on the third visit it is still lying by the
  tape on the last night, where he fell. There is no patrol, no blockade, no escort and no one to hand the compass to.

## What the town knows

On the visit after he died:

- **Dutch:** "Sheriff's dead. He's still up by the tape. Nobody's gone for him." And: "Prescott says leave him till the county
  comes. So he's still lying up there."
- **Merle:** "Somebody's going to have to write it up. It won't be me. Not this time."
- **Gus:** "Drinks are on the house tonight. Don't ask me why."
- **Earl,** in the cell: "Somebody finally did it. Don't tell me who." And: "Leave the door open on your way out. Nobody's coming to
  lock it."
- **The payphone.** The night he dies, his office says he isn't answering his radio and asks who is calling. After that night it says
  the Sheriff is dead and to stay on the line. On the last night, 911 has nobody up on the ridge to send.

## The account

The account gains one sentence before "I stopped the machine". If he was killed: "I killed the Sheriff on the ridge. He talked until
he couldn't, and none of it was sorry." If he was beaten and left alive: "I beat the Sheriff down into the dirt and left him there,
still talking." The Witness line adds "The Sheriff never came down off the ridge." The old "He lowered his hand and told me to go" is
gone with the yielding.

The vignette's resolution is unchanged (stopping the machine and carrying out her name); killing him is not required and grants
nothing by itself.
