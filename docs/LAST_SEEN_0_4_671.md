`docs/THREE_VIGNETTES_PROPOSAL_2026_11_17.md`, which the owner approved on 17 November:

Part 4 (`docs/LAST_SEEN_0_4_670.md`) was the night. Part 5 is the day after:

- the park by day and empty, the fence down and the notices torn;
- Nora's drawer unlocked;
- in it, the reader's own folder: their face, last seen at a door, on the day the House first took them in;

*Last Seen* is now a Witness source, of the understanding kind. The pool is 93 and the quota 70, across two kinds; there are still three
endings.

## After

A reader who has had the night (their own `Days` at least three) comes back to the day after. With two or more readers it is the
furthest any of them has reached, as before.

- **The park.** By day, with nobody in it: none of the eight, no families, no Ocampo, no men.
  - The gate stands open.
  - The city's fence round the playground lies flat in the grass, a panel at a time, along the line it stood on. A reader can walk over
    it.
  - Every notice on the lamp posts is torn: two corners and the tape are left.
  - The lamps are dark.
- **The office.** Nora, Patricia and Luz are at their places, and the front door is open. On the long table, the folders of the ones
  who were gone at dawn lie open at their first page, with a gap on each one's shelf: the ones gone in the nights of whoever is here.
- **Nora's drawer** is unlocked.
- **The hour** is mid-morning, and the day stands still there.
- **Being told.** Each reader is told once what the morning is: the park empty, or, in the office, nobody at the shelves and the drawer
  unlocked.
- **The three speak:**
  - Nora: "My drawer's unlocked. Go on."; once the reader has opened it, "It stays open. They all do.";
  - Patricia: "Nobody's come in yet. They will, after work.";
  - Luz: "There's nobody out there. Not one of them."

### The drawer

A hand on Nora's desk pulls the drawer out, and the folder screen opens on what is in it.

- **A reader who has had the night is shown their own folder first.** It is the same manila card as the children's, with their own face
  clipped to it. The face is taken from their skin: the House body where they wear one, otherwise their own, face and hat layer, as
  the game draws a face in the player list. The form says:
  - **NAME:** theirs;
  - **LAST SEEN:** at a door;
  - **ON:** the House's own record of the day it first took them in. This is the part of the day and the House's count of days, as "the
    evening of day four". A reader who came in before the House kept that record is "not written in";
  - **the heading,** which follows the House's read of their age. It is the minors' file (MISSING CHILDREN) for a reader the House
    reads as young, and the plain one (MISSING PERSONS) otherwise. No age is named;
  - **Nora's pencil note,** which follows the House's read of home:

    | The House's read of home | Nora's note |
    | --- | --- |
    | a haven | "Somebody is still looking." |
    | a danger | "Nobody has asked." |
    | run from | "Not the first time." |
    | not read | "Folder stays open." |

- **The first time,** Nora says "Yours came in on the first night. I keep it with the others." The reader is ready
  (`found_their_own_folder`).
- **Every folder in the drawer.** The drawer holds a folder for every reader who has had the night here, whether or not they are here
  now. The folder screen pages through them with the book's page buttons, the reader's own first and the rest by name. Friends find
  each other's faces among the missing. Each folder's face is that reader's own.
- **The account.** Once the folder has been shown that visit, the reader's next hand on the drawer takes out the account, which is under
  the folder. Each visit the folder comes first. The ending book floats over the desk while the account is waiting.
- **A reader who has not had the night** is shown the others' folders, if there are any, and told "There is no folder with your face in
  it." Nothing counts for them.
- **Spectators** open nothing and are in no drawer: a spectator is never counted at dawn, so has no folder.

### The Corner's girl

This happens once, to a reader who has finished *The House on the Corner*, and without a word.

- From ten seconds into the day after, the first moment nobody sees the fountain or the far pavement, two people appear:
  - the girl with one arm, at the fountain's rim where the first of them stood, facing the avenue;
  - across the avenue on the far pavement, by the bus shelter, a man facing the park. It is her brother, grown. He does not cross.
- Each reader who has finished the Corner and sees her is marked as having seen her (`SawRomina`).
- A hand on her: she turns her head to the reader, and says nothing (`RominaTurned`, for a reader who finished the Corner). A hand on
  him: "He stays on the far pavement, facing the park. He does not cross."
- Once every reader there who finished the Corner has seen her, she and he are gone the first moment nobody sees. She does not come
  again that day.
- A reader who has not finished the Corner can see her and put a hand on her. Nothing is kept for them.

### The account

1. The office and the archive, and Nora's rule if she told it to them.
2. The first one at the fountain, and whether this reader brought her folder or saw someone else bring it.
3. The second day:
   - the children they matched, by name;
   - the fence and the notices;
   - the man from the city, and Nora who would not close the files.
4. The night:
   - the chained gates;
   - whose hands they took, by name, one at a time;
   - whom they brought in by the back door, each to their shelf;
   - whether they kept the door and it held, or the men came over the railing and the lamps went out.
5. The morning: the ones gone from the shelves, by name, their folders open on the long table.
6. After:
   - the park empty, the fence flat, the notices torn, the drawer unlocked;
   - their own folder with their own face, last seen at a door, and the day;
   - Nora's note.
7. The others' folders under theirs, by name.
8. The girl with one arm and the man across the avenue, if they saw her, and her turned head if they put a hand on her.
9. The last line: "She keeps it with the others. A folder stays open."
