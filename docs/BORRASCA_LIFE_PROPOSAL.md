# Borrasca: more life in the town

The owner's notes of 10 November, quoted whole, and a plan worked from them. Nothing here changes the four visits, the ending, or
the Witness count. Almost all of it is optional: a reader who walks straight up the trail loses nothing they need.

## The owner's notes

> I've got some notes too, for adding more life to borrasca, once you've finished with the bike vignette.
>
> You should start dev with Kelly's fort window. The player already pick a window in her pretend house. Let her tuck a borrowed
> comic into that sill for you. Years later, when you find her in Room 6, she remembers which window you chose and asks if you ever
> finished it. That gives the two of you an ordinary thing to talk about before she asks you to carry her name back up.
>
> * Give Patrick and Kendra a turn at the arcade. Patrick watches your first run of Deep Seam and asks for a rematch if you beat
>   him. Kendra knows a trick he keeps getting wrong. On the next visit, she reacts differently depending on whose initials are at
>   the top. Patrick's score would remind you of an afternoon you actually spent with him.
> * Carry a letter for Dorsey. He already mentions the Garritys' mailbox going untouched. On the second visit, he could hand you a
>   letter to leave under their porch door. Years later, the box is still on its post, with the family name barely legible under
>   cloudy tape. You remember where it is because you've been to that porch.
> * Put on the church supper from the noticeboard. Let the player help set out plates, take a slice of pie, and hear the adults talk
>   about work and their kids. Prescott's truck brings the folding tables, and somebody thanks the driver for fixing their porch
>   steps. Seeing that same company name at the depot later would give you a better sense of how much the town relies on him.
>
> The arcade rematch is the one I'd pair with the window. It gives Patrick and Kendra more room to be funny and competitive while
> the four of you are still together.
>
> Most of these should be optional. I'd also add more active play - hide and seek, tag, maybe the kids find an animal in the woods.

## What ties them together

Each of these puts an ordinary thing in the reader's hands on the first or second visit and lets the later visits find it again,
changed. The reader is never told to remember anything. The thing is there, and it is where they left it.

| On the early visits | Years later |
|---|---|
| A comic in the window the reader picked | It is still in the sill, bleached, or in the reader's pack. Kelly asks about it in room 6. |
| Patrick's and the reader's initials on DEEP SEAM | The table still shows through the dust on the unplugged cabinet. |
| A letter left under the Garritys' porch door | The mailbox is still on its post, the name under cloudy tape. |
| Folding tables carried off Prescott's truck | The same company name on the depot's receipt. |

## 1. The window and the comic (first)

**Built in 0.4.445.** What is said, word for word, and how it is made: [BORRASCA_COMIC_0_4_445.md](BORRASCA_COMIC_0_4_445.md).

**The window.** In the fort Kelly already says "Pick a window", and answers west or east. The choice is now kept. A reader who
picks neither gets hers, the west one.

**The comic.** Once the window is settled she walks to its sill and tucks a comic into it: *Kit & Caboose* No. 31, a space comic
from the drugstore's spinner, some years old. It is Patrick's; she borrowed it and is halfway through. The comic is a real object
in the sill, drawn leaning on the glass.

- Taking it is the reader's choice. Taken, it is a thing they carry, like the compass, and it opens as a comic: a cover, six pages
  of story and a back cover, drawn in the tool, turned with the arrow keys. The story stops on "CONTINUED IN No. 32".
- The back cover has a subscription coupon that Patrick started to fill in, in pencil, and did not finish.
- Patrick, asked while the reader has it: it is his, and they can keep it till they are done. Kendra, on the second visit, with
  Patrick gone: it is Patrick's, and somebody should hold on to it.
- Left in the sill, it stays there. Years later the window has bleached it. A grown reader can still take it, and it is the
  bleached one they carry and read.

**Room 6.** After her first line Kelly says which window the reader took, and asks whether they ever finished it. What follows is
what the reader really did:

| What the reader did | What is said |
|---|---|
| Read it to the last page | The reader tells her where it stops: a knock, and continued. She gave it a better ending herself; she had the time. |
| Took it and did not finish | Then neither of them knows, and she does not want it found out for her. |
| Left it in the sill | Then it is where she said it would be. Somebody else will pick that window. |
| Has it with them now | She sees it. It can be left with her (she knows her page) or kept. |

Then her lines go on as they do now, to the name.

**The account** says what happened to the comic, in a sentence.

## 2. Patrick and Kendra at the arcade (with the window)

**Built in 0.4.446.** What is said, word for word, and how it is made: [BORRASCA_ARCADE_FRIENDS_0_4_446.md](BORRASCA_ARCADE_FRIENDS_0_4_446.md).
Kendra takes BIG CAT at once, not only if the reader stays, and Kelly comes in to the comics.

On the first visit, when the reader puts a quarter in DEEP SEAM, Patrick and Kendra come into the drugstore and stand at the
reader's shoulder (they come in while the reader is looking at the screen). Patrick's is the top score.

- **Under his score:** he says so, at length.
- **Over his score:** he wants the cabinet. He plays, and the reader can watch his game over his shoulder: his own run, played
  from his own keys. He hops the fallen timbers, which cannot be hopped, and Kendra says so every time. That is the trick he keeps
  getting wrong, and it is a true one for the reader's own game.
- The reader can tell him, or not. Told, he goes round the next timber, and his next game is a good one.
- Kendra takes a turn at BIG CAT if the reader stays.

On the second visit Patrick is gone, and Kendra's words by the cabinets follow whose initials are at the top: the reader's, kept
over his; or his, because the reader told him how; or his, because nobody ever beat him. Years later the dusty table says the
same thing it said that afternoon.

## 3. The letter (later)

On the second visit Dorsey has a letter for the Garritys and nobody at their door. He hands it to the reader to push under the
porch door. The porch is a real one, with the box on its post at the gate. Years later the box is still there, GARRITY under tape
gone cloudy, and the letter is where the reader put it or it is not.

## 4. The church supper (later)

The noticeboard's supper, held: on the first visit, on the green by the chapel, late in the day. Prescott's truck is backed up
with the folding tables. The reader can carry a table, set plates, take a slice of pie, and stand near the grown-ups while they
talk about shifts and their children. Somebody thanks the driver for their porch steps. On the second visit the supper is
cancelled for a storm that does not come.

## 5. More play (later)

- **Tag and hide and seek** with all four, in the trees behind the store, started by any of them and dropped whenever the reader
  walks off.
- **The animal in the woods:** the noticeboard already has "Lost: brown dog, answers to Biscuit." The children find the dog below
  the first marker. It can be led home on a length of rope. The notice comes down on the next visit.

## Order

1. The window and the comic, and room 6.
2. The arcade.
3. The letter. 4. The supper. 5. More play.
