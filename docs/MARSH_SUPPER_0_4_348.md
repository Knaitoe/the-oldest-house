The first of the yard's four stories, from the owner's draft "The House Has a Yard" (4 November), built as `docs/YARD_STORIES_PROPOSAL.md` describes. Both of the proposal's slices ship together, so the story is playable from its first release, and its Witness source is added now. Layout 109 appends slot 209. Protocol 143 adds the farmhouse's blocks, items and sounds. Nothing standing is rebuilt.

From the draft:

> *"The corn maze opens into a farmhouse clearing. You see the upstairs windows long before you find the porch. A fallen tree blocks the only drive, and you squeeze around it on foot. 'Wipe your boots' is the first thing you hear inside."*
>
> *"Give the first meal time to feel ordinary. A guest keeps checking the porch light for a ride. When their chair is gone and the host says the ride came, the drive gives the reader reason to doubt it. A brief view into the kitchen gives them proof before the meat is served. If they say the guest's name, one diner admits they heard them calling from the kitchen. Another says they thought the voice came from the corn. `MARSH_SUPPER`, `MarshSupper`. It is a literary place, outside, built whole by `MarshSupperSite`, at dusk (scene clock 3). It is dealt once to each reader, and only to a reader who has crossed one of the House's ordinary yard crossings. Its Witness kind is **understanding**.

The story id is `marsh_supper`, not `the_supper` as the proposal had it, because a Witness story's id is its place's id.

## The approach

- **The card.** At the mouth of the maze, a card on a post in Ida's hand: supper at six or at the bell, follow the cut rows toward the windows, mind the corn, come round by the porch and not the back, wipe your boots. It is the story's source, outlined until it is read.
- **The corn maze.** It starts at the House's door. Its paths are cut through corn two stalks high, over a reader's head, and the field round it stands three high.
  - The maze is thirteen cells by nineteen, three blocks to a cell. It is generated from a fixed seed, so every farm has the same maze: a winding tree with seven extra cuts, so it has loops and is never one long corridor.
  - The farmhouse's upstairs windows show over the tassels from the first turn.
  - The corn holds a body back and bends under a reader pushing through it, the yard's stalks as the storm field has them.
- **The clearing.** The maze opens onto a mown clearing inside a fence: a yard light on a pole, a pump, the dinner bell on its post. Ringing the bell carries out over the corn, and faintly it rings back.
- **The drive.** The only drive runs east to a gate onto the county road. An oak lies across it, its trunk mossy and split from an old storm and its roots standing up out of the ditch. A reader squeezes round on foot between the root plate and the water. The first time a reader sees it, they are told nothing on wheels has come up this drive in a long while.
- **The porch.** It wraps round the front and the east side. The doormat lies at the top of the steps under the porch light.
  - Stepping onto the porch, the reader hears Ida from inside: "Wipe your boots."
  - Standing on the mat wipes them, with a scrape.
  - A reader who turns back here simply leaves; nothing has started for them.

## The house

Two storeys under a gable roof with a chimney. Downstairs:

- **The dining room** has wallpaper, plank floors, a long table with a lamp on it, lanterns over it and chests of drawers along the wall. Five of its cane chairs are the household's and the guest's; five more are free for readers.
- **The kitchen** lies through a swing door. It has linoleum, a sink, a cooker and counters, the butcher's block, a serving hatch onto the dining room, a window onto the east porch, and a back door out to it.
- **The coat hook** is by the front door. Corinne's coat hangs on it.

Upstairs is not entered. Its windows, and the attic's in each gable, are lit behind their curtains. They and the porch light are the farmhouse's own fittings, lit until something puts them out.

## The household

Five people, on stranger phases 110 to 114, painted by the character painter:

- **Ida Marsh**, who does the talking and keeps her kitchen;
- **Royce Marsh**, who carves and says little;
- **Del**, their son, friendly, who says too much;
- **Nana**, who says she is deaf;
- **Corinne**, a guest whose car quit on the county road. She walked up through the corn to the only light, and her brother is coming for her.

They speak over their heads and in the action bar of anyone near enough to hear. Right-clicked, Ida, Del, Nana and Corinne talk through the conversation panel; Royce nods. A reader who tries a chair someone is sitting in is told so.

## The supper

One supper for the whole party: one table, one Corinne, one meal. Its stages run on one clock while anyone is at the farm.

1. **Arrival.** The meal starts when a reader sits down, or after twenty seconds in the house with nobody sat. Ida says to sit wherever there's a plate.
2. **The soup.** It lasts fifty seconds and nothing hurries it.
   - Ida asks each seated reader where they have come from: the road, a house a long way back, or they'd rather not say. She answers each, and then talks about the farm, Nana, the supper, Corinne and the drive for as long as the reader keeps asking.
   - Ida fills each seated reader's glass from the jug without asking.
   - Corinne goes to the front window twice to look at the porch light. Del, Nana and Ida talk.
   - A reader who leaves now leaves. Nobody stops them, and nothing is resolved.
3. **Corinne goes out.** At the end of the soup she says she'll just look at the porch light. She takes her coat from the hook ("Take your coat, it's turned cold"), goes out of the front door, along the porch and round the east side, and in at the back door. The household lays clean plates. Del goes through to the kitchen and back.
   - When nobody can see her, she is gone. A reader on the east porch who watches her go round the side of the house, and not down the steps, has that in their record.
   - When nobody can see the kitchen side, her keys and coat are there.
   - When nobody can see her place, her chair and her setting are taken away and the places closed up.
   - If readers keep watching, all three happen anyway after forty-five seconds.
4. **The chair.** When a reader is back in the dining room, Ida says, "Her ride came." Every reader in the house has heard it. A reader who saw the oak is reminded of it: nothing has come up the drive in a long while. Del goes through to the kitchen again.
5. **Her name.** For forty-five seconds a reader in the dining room may say "Where's Corinne?" or "Corinne's still here" through the panel, talking with Ida, Del or Nana.
   - Del says he heard her calling, from the kitchen.
   - Nana says she thought it came from the corn.
   - Ida says the meat is ready.
   - If nobody says it, Royce says he'll see to it, and the meat comes anyway.
6. **The meat.** Royce goes to the kitchen and comes back. The roast arrives under its cover and is carved at the table. Everyone sitting is served, and a reader who sits down later is served too.

## The kitchen

There is one brief look before the meat, and the proof is what is on the side by the butcher's block: her car keys on their fob, and her coat over the back of a chair. It is never more than that. A reader sees it when the keys are within fourteen blocks, before their eyes and in clear sight:

- through the swing door when it opens: the household opens it going through, and it swings shut again after two seconds;
- through the serving hatch;
- from the east porch, in at the kitchen window;
- or by going in.

Ida keeps her kitchen. From the dining side, the swing door turns a reader back ("Not in my kitchen"), unless another reader is keeping her talking in the panel. Then she doesn't look up and the reader slips through, and the one talking has covered for them. From inside the kitchen the door opens freely. The back door is latched from outside until Corinne is gone.

The keys can be taken. They pass from hand to hand like any item. If carried out of the farm, they are gone from the carrier's pocket and back on the side.

## The three endings

When the meat has come, each reader does one of three things. Each resolves the one source.

| What they do | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Take their own plate, once the meat is on it, and eat | `ate_at_the_marsh_table` |
| Get up and leave, once her chair is gone: back into the corn, out past the oak, or through either door | `left_before_the_meat` |
| Lay her keys on the table in front of Ida (right-click Ida, or near her place, holding them) | `put_her_keys_on_the_table` |

- **Eating.** The reader eats what was served. It is good. They wake in the House with the taste of it still in their mouth.
- **Leaving.** The farmhouse windows go out behind them one at a time, for them alone, and then the porch light. A reader going out past the oak hears the porch light still burning behind them. The way on is the gate onto the county road, the onward door; the House's door is where they left it.
- **The keys.** The meal ends for everyone. The household goes still and the porch light goes out. Every other reader in the house resolves with the same outcome, as a witness, or partway if they came in after the chair was gone.

A reader who arrived after the chair was gone is partway, whichever ending is theirs.

## Together

- **The chair is gone for everyone,** and only when no reader is looking.
- **Keeping Ida talking.** While one reader is in the panel with Ida, another can go through the kitchen door. The talker's account says they kept her talking while the other went through.
- **The keys travel.** One reader can take them in the kitchen and pass them to another at the table. Either may lay them down.
- **Her name** said by one reader is heard by everyone in the house, and the answers are the same for all.
- **The co-op line.** "%s {was} at the table with me" (`CoopAccounts`).
- **Five places for readers** at the table. A sixth can still say her name, see the kitchen, lay the keys or leave, but the soup, the jug and the meat go to those sitting down, and eating needs a plate.

## The House's read

- **AGE.** The jug is tea for ANALOG and TRANSITIONAL readers, milk otherwise. The account says which was poured. No age is named.
- **HOME = HAVEN.** On a later visit (a reader who left before the chair was gone and came back), Ida says she kept their place, and the account says she had.
- **HOME = DANGER.** Royce carves standing behind that reader's chair, and the account says they heard the knife, and not him.

## The accounts

"The account: the supper", in the reader's hand, built only from their own record:

- the corn, and the oak across the drive if they saw it;
- "Wipe your boots", and whether they did;
- the five at the table, or that they came in late to a gap where a chair had been;
- what Ida asked and what they answered, and what she poured;
- what Corinne told them, if they spoke with her;
- the chair gone and "her ride came", and that they had seen the drive;
- that they saw her go round the side of the house;
- being turned back from the kitchen door, once or more than once, or keeping Ida talking while someone went through;
- how they saw into the kitchen, and the keys and coat;
- taking her keys;
- whether they said her name or who did, and the answers;
- where Royce stood;
- what they did: ate, left by the corn or the drive, or laid the keys, or watched someone else lay them (and whether they had carried them out of the kitchen themselves).

Every account ends with the dinner bell on its post, which had lost its clapper, and the clapper in the reader's pocket. Nothing names the reader's gender, a colour or an age.

The Witness line is "I sat down to supper at a farmhouse at the end of a corn maze. The guest went out to look for her ride, and when I came back to the table her chair was gone and they said her ride had come."

## The yield

**The Marsh dinner bell's clapper**, given with the account. While carried, the House's doors to outside places come up a little more often, as the reflector does. Its advancement is under understanding.

## Reset

When the farm has been empty for ten seconds, the next reader finds it as it was: the corn standing, the chairs back, the table laid, the coat on its hook, the keys put away, every light on and the doors shut.
