# The list 1a: the middle of town (0.4.699)

This is the first part of *The list*, the vignette after Stephen Graham Jones's *I Was a Teenage Slasher* (`docs/THE_LIST_PROPOSAL.md`). The owner's decisions of 17 November govern it: a high school cast; real slasher kills; asymmetric multiplayer; four nights; longer set-piece finishes; the book's own names and only its names. Everything said in it is written new. The plan for every part is `docs/THE_LIST_PLAN.md`.

This part is the place alone: the middle of Lamesa, Texas, at midnight, through the back door of the reader's own house. Nobody is out yet; the town's people come in part 1b. The House does not deal the place until night one exists (part 3), and it is not a Witness source until the last part.

## The place

It is outdoors, under the outside sky, at midnight (`SceneClock` mode 9). It stands at its own height (`TheListSite.BASE_Y`, 96), as the other tall places outside do, and the House finds a reader in it by its whole bounds, so the water tower's catwalk is in it. Every position is relative to the base, the ground just inside the House's door; north is -z. The scene runs from x -88 to 96 and from z 24 to -176.

- **The reader's house** is at the base: a low ranch house with brick along the foot of its walls and lap siding above, under a half-pitch roof of shingle slabs with siding in the gables. A swamp cooler sits on the back slope with its duct, and an aerial and a vent stack stand on the front slope. A carport stands to the west, and the slab under it runs out to the street.
  - **The kitchen** is at the back, and the House's door is its back door. It has linoleum, counters along the back wall with the sink under the window, the range, cabinets over the counters, the refrigerator by the back door, a dinette table with four chairs and the light over it, and the telephone on the west wall. The telephone is where the first paper is.
  - **The reader's room** is beside the kitchen, behind its own door. It has the bed against the east wall, the nightstand with the clock radio, the desk with the tape deck (where the account will be), the dresser, the closet's folding doors, a low bookcase and posters.
  - **The hall** runs across the middle of the house. Off it are the parents' room (two beds and nightstands, a dresser, a framed picture), the bathroom (tub, toilet, vanity, mirror) and the front room. The front room is panelled, with the couch, the recliner, the console television, the end table and its lamp, the coffee table, the picture window and the front door.
- **The back yard** has chain-link round it, the clothesline, the burn barrel, the faucet on the back wall and the porch light. The House's own hall stands in it as a steel shop, its painted panels rusted along the foot and trimmed in galvanized iron at the corners and along the top, with a roll-up door, a window and a light over its door.
- **The neighborhood.** South Ninth and South Seventh are caliche from the cotton's edge to the highway, and the alley behind Main Street is too. Lots of thin, worn lawn run between them, with back fences down the middle of each block and weeds along them. Seven houses stand on them, each its own: lap siding over a brick course, face brick to the eaves, or stucco.
  - Each has its windows dark or lit, a front door and, if anyone is home, a porch light. Panes and front doors are set in the wall's outer face, so no reveal shows to the street to catch the light of the rooms behind.
  - Each has a low roof and a mailbox at the curb, and a walk from its door to the street.
  - Some have a carport whose slab runs to the curb, and some have chain-link across the front yard short of the drive.
  - Every neighbor's door is locked.
  - The house across South Ninth from the reader's is empty and dark, with no light on its porch. Its front door is the way on.
- **Avenue K** is the highway, paved the whole length with a dashed line and caliche shoulders where it runs between the cotton. Street signs, a stop sign for each street and a street light stand at the corners. Utility poles run along the backs of the lots and up the highway's east side, their wires sagging between them, with a transformer on one and a street light on two. No pole stands in a road.
- **Main Street** is paved from edge to edge, with its line, curbs and sidewalks, and street lights along both curbs; none stands in the highway or on the tracks.
  - On the south side: **the feed store**, galvanized iron, with sacks, a mineral block, a seed bin and the counter, the bus bench and the bus stop's sign at the curb, and a loading dock at the side. **Dawson Drug**, in face brick with its display windows lit, an awning and a glass door; its aisles, the counter and register before the pharmacist's shelves along the back wall, two fountain stools, the magazine rack and the card spinner. **The sheriff's substation**, stucco, with its name hung low over the door and no awning under it; desks, the radio desk, filing cabinets, a bench, the bulletin board, the water cooler, and the flag on its pole out front.
  - On the north side: **Reel Time Video**, a room deep, with its name lit. Inside are the horror wall along the west, new releases along the east, a double shelf down the middle, the counter and register by the door, a cardboard standee, and the television on its bracket on the east wall. The return slot is in a brick pier beside the door, opening onto the sidewalk.
  - Older fronts stand between the stores, each its own building six to nine blocks wide: one storey or two, in face brick, stucco or painted block. Some are storefronts gone dark under their transoms, with brick piers at their ends; the rest have a pair of small windows. The two-storey ones have windows upstairs, and the brick ones a capped parapet. None of them opens, and none is lit inside. The open stores' display glass and glass doors sit in the wall's outer face too.
- **The Caprock Drive-In** is on the north side east of the highway. Its lot is paved to the street. A canopy runs along the lot's east side on posts, its lights along the edge, with a menu and speaker at each stall under it. The stand has an order window facing the lot, the fryer and flat-top behind it and picnic tables before it. The sign stands at the corner on its pole, three boards across and two down, lit, with its arrow pointing in.
- **East of the highway.**
  - **The tracks** run north and south, a rail on ballast. They are paved where Main Street crosses them and caliche where the gin road crosses, with a crossbuck either side of each crossing.
  - **The gin** is a long building of galvanized iron, rusted along its foot. Inside are the module feeder at the yard door, the gin stands in a row, the augers in their troughs, the lint cleaner and the press at the far end; the suction pipe goes up out of the roof. Wrapped, tagged bales stand by the press door. To the south, modules lie in rows under tarps on the caliche yard.
  - **The seed house** is a tall shed of galvanized bins, its sliding door under a lamp, a ladder up its side and a chute out of its top. Inside are the bins' walls, cottonseed piled against them and the auger in its trench.
  - **The water tower** stands on four braced legs, with the riser down the middle. A ladder climbs a leg's face and goes on through the catwalk where its rail leaves a gap. The catwalk runs round the bowl, and over it is the tank, with LAMESA on the faces toward the town and the highway, read left to right. On top are its roof, cap and beacon.
  - **The playa** is north of the gin, a dry lake bed with a ragged edge. Main Street runs on across it.
- **Mesquite** stands along the tracks, at the backs of the lots, by the gin and round the playa, clear of the roads, the fences and every building.
- **Cotton** is everywhere else, in rows with furrows between, open: a short plant here, one leaning at a row's end, picked stalks every so often. It has no body.
- **The haze.** A dark haze with a little of the street lights' warmth in it closes in from about fifteen blocks and is whole by fifty-two (`TheListClient`). It draws in closer as a reader nears the edge, so no edge is seen. There is no haze under a roof (the house, a store, the gin, the seed house).

## Single and multiplayer

The place is recurring and shared: every reader sees the same town.

- A hand on anything answers that reader alone, in a grey line over the hotbar, and changes nothing. A neighbor's door, either half, says it is locked and stays shut. The reader's own doors open as doors do. The empty house's door is the House's way on.
- Each reader is counted once for each time they come in by the back door (`Visits` on their own record).
- The town's night is one, begun by the first reader in and forgotten when nobody has been there for 100 ticks. Spectators are not readers.
- Its sounds are played within reach of whoever they are for:
  - crickets near a reader who is outside;
  - the wind going by;
  - a dog behind the back fence nearest the reader;
  - the drive-in's nearest speaker post, to anyone on that stretch of Main Street;
  - the loose tin on whichever wall of the gin is nearest anyone close to it;
  - the tower ticking over anyone under it;
  - every so often a train out past the playa, which each reader hears from that way, however far away they are.
