# Quiet rooms: something to read two ways (0.4.765)

This follows the porch evening (0.4.764). Each quiet room gets someone just out of full view whose attention could mean
welcome or something worse, plus things in the room that draw the reader away from them. No line says which reading is
right. A different sense carries the presence in each room, so the porch's shape in the window doesn't repeat. The House's
read is unchanged in every room: nothing below sets or settles a trait.

## The shelter

- One of the two strangers, chosen per reader, looks up as the reader comes in and looks away about two seconds later.
- The first time the reader comes within five and a half blocks of either stranger, that stranger slides along the rail
  toward the lake, making room.
- The first time each stranger is looked at during the song, they look out at the rain for a second and a half before
  they come.
- Neither ever speaks.
- Once the song is on, the jukebox gives the reader something to do with their hands: its strips, read one at a time.
  If the jukebox carries the room's card, it still opens the card.
- If the reader catches the eye of the one who looked up while that one is still looking, it goes in the account as an
  action.

## The coatroom

- Thirty ticks in, a voice through the curtain: "Is that you? Put something dry on. We've got company."
- Talking through the curtain comes and goes after that. It stops for three seconds each time a garment goes on, and stops
  for good when the curtain parts. Nobody comes through.
- Sitting on the bench under the window, out of sight of the curtain, goes in the account as an action.

## The bathroom

- Two seconds in, a knock on the door behind the reader, then "Take your time." Nobody tries the door.
- Picking something up from the counter brings a board shifting out in the hall, at most once every three seconds.
- When the light goes out, by itself or at the switch, steps go away down the hall.

## The waiting room

- Behind the frosted glass, a shape sits at the receptionist's desk, head down. It raises its head toward the reader at
  the counter and never gets up or slides the glass open. It is the reader's own.
- The first time the reader uses the counter, it shows the sign-in sheet: "The sign-in sheet. The last line already has
  your name on it, in someone else's handwriting." After that the counter goes back to the calendar beyond it.
- Low voices come from behind the office door until the reader is called through it.

## Left out

- **The kitchen table's uncolored figure.** The fridge art was deliberately drawn with "none of them people", most likely
  so the skin-tone read is left alone. A family drawing with one figure left uncolored would cut across that, so it waits
  for the owner.
- **Sounds.** The rooms reuse the existing "voices through a door" recording, the House's far knock and the ordinary
  wooden step. Nothing new was recorded, since the owner rejected synthesized voices.

## Checks

- The house-read cases (20) pass: the shelter's look-up, the room on the rail and the first look away; the shape behind
  the glass and the sign-in sheet; the knock and the steps going away.
- The bathroom test finishes its routine before the knock is due. There, the check confirms instead that no knock comes
  once the routine is over. That makes the knock itself untested in that case.
