# The Pell house 11: a week in (0.4.533)

The owner, 12 November: "start by finishing each unfinished vignette". The Pell house (`docs/PELL_HOUSE_PROPOSAL.md`) has its first
visit whole, from the car at the curb to Gail's call (`docs/PELL_HOUSE_CALL_0_4_532.md`). This is its eleventh slice, the start of the
proposal's "2. A week in":

> The pool: the cover is off. June swims lengths at dawn. Tilly: June starts calling her Nessa "by mistake".

The knife and Gail's marks, June's hand, the tapes, the freezer and the journal come after; this slice is the visit itself, the house as
June has made it by then, the swim, and the first of the slips. The House still does not deal the house.

## Which visit

- **Each reader's own record keeps which visits they have had all of**: "FirstVisit" and "SecondVisit". A visit is had once Gail has
  rung and June has hung up with the reader at the house.
- **When the house begins again it plays the earliest visit that a reader at it has not had**, so nobody misses the first. A reader who
  arrives at a visit already begun has the house as it is.
- **There is no third visit yet.** A reader who has had both has the second again.

## A week in

- **No Gail and no car.** June is at her stove, Tilly is in Nessa's room with the door open, and Callum is at the end of the hall. The
  front door, the sliding door and the garage's walk door are shut, and a towel has been pushed under the garage's door from the yard.
- **The late afternoon holds until a reader first comes into the house.** June calls hello from the kitchen: "That you, love? Dinner's
  at six." A reader in the house too far off to hear her is told: "June, calling from the kitchen: 'That you, love? Dinner's at six.'"
  Then the hour goes on.
- **A hand on June** a week in gets, in turn: "There you are, love. Dinner's at six." / "I go in the pool first thing, every morning.
  Clears the head." / "The pool gate stays locked, mind. Same as ever." / "Inside the line after dark, love. You know that by now." /
  "Tilly's settling in lovely. She's sleeping right through." The pool gate and the line still count as June's rules for a reader who
  hears them, so a reader who comes in on a visit already begun can still have them.
- **A hand on Tilly**: "June brushes my hair every night now." / "Is the pool open? I can hear the water." / "She calls me love.
  Mostly." / "Callum's hair drips. I can hear it." / "I'm keeping my hat on."

## The house a week on

- **The pool.** The cover is off and the water is to the brim. One tile of the band along the waterline is missing ("The grout round the
  gap is new"); another, on the deep end's far wall, does not match. In the deep end's floor is the drain, and under its grate a child's
  swimming goggles, the strap knotted where it broke. A lane rope crosses the water at the step between the shallow end and the deep, its
  floats at the waterline; one length has lost a float.
- **The line** round the lot has been gone over again.
- **The kitchen and Callum's room.** One slot of the knife block is empty, and there is a knife in Callum's drawer of cutlery.
- **The garage.** A second knife's outline on the pegboard is empty.
- **The shed.** Its calendar has more nights crossed off, nearer the circled night.
- **The fridge** has two new photographs, of the doorstep on the day of the placement.

## The slips

- **At dinner** June serves Tilly first, as always: "There you go, Nessa. Oh. Tilly. Sorry, love."
- **At bedtime** the brushing is how it has been every night. Tilly: "You do it the same way every night." June: "That's how she liked
  it." and, when she has brushed long enough, "There. It's coming on. Nearly to your shoulders." She puts the brush away: "Night night,
  Nessa." Tilly, still sitting up: "It's Tilly." June: "Tilly. I know, love. Night night." Then Tilly lies down.
- **At breakfast** June: "Nessa, love, where did you put your goggles?" Tilly: "I haven't got any goggles." June: "Tilly. Sorry, love.
  I'm all over the place this morning." Tilly: "That's all right." (On the first visit, breakfast is still the singing in the wall.)

Each is heard by the readers near enough to June when she says it.

## First light

The night is as it was the first night. Then, at first light, a week in:

- **June comes out of her room** in her swimming things and her towelling robe, lets herself out by the sliding door, and locks it
  behind her once nobody is in the house or in the doorway (she asks anyone in the house back to bed).
- **At the pool's gate** she takes the padlock off, goes in and shuts the gate behind her. The padlock hangs open on the hasp while she
  swims.
- **At the chair** she takes the robe off and drops it over the chair ("A towelling robe has been dropped over the pool chair. It is
  still warm."), and gets in at the shallow end.
- **She swims six lengths** down the middle, under the lane rope and back, a splash at each wall, without stopping. A hand on her: "June
  doesn't stop. She turns at the wall and pushes off again."
- **The hour holds at half past five** while she is out of her room.
- **Out**, she puts the robe on, comes out of the gate and padlocks it behind her once nobody is inside the pool's fence or in the
  gateway ("Out you come, love. I'm locking it."), comes in by the sliding door and locks it behind her once nobody has come into the
  house after her, and goes to her room and shuts its door. A while later, at six, she gets up for the day, dressed, as on the first
  morning.
- **While she swims the gate is only shut.** A hand opens it ("The padlock hangs open on the hasp. The gate swings in."), and a reader
  can go in, to the water, the band and the drain.
- **A hand on June in her robe**, on her way down or back: "Morning, love. You're up early." / "I go in first thing. Every morning.
  Clears the head." / "Back to bed, love. It's not six yet."
- **While every reader at the house is asleep**, the night hurries as before, and she is back in her room at once.

## The week's call

Gail rings after breakfast as she did on the first morning, and June has her first:

- June: "Settling in lovely, the pair of them." (If she met a reader in the night, or one spoke to her or stepped in her paint:
  "Settling in. One of them's still up and about at night, but I see them back to bed.") / "Tilly's eating well. She's a different
  girl." / "And Callum's been good as gold." / "Hang on, I'll put them on."
- Gail, to a reader on the line: "Hello, love. It's Gail." / "I've just been talking to June. She says it's all going well. A week in,
  now. How are you finding it?"
- The answers are the first call's, two of them as they are a week in: "It's fine." and "I'm not sleeping much."
- Gail, a week in:

  | Told | Gail |
  |---|---|
  | It's fine. | "Good. That's good, love." / "June says you're a help to her. You're in good hands." |
  | I'm not sleeping much. | "Still? That's hard, love." / "It can take a while. It does get easier." (If June told her somebody was still up: "June said somebody's still up at night." / "It can take a while. It does get easier, love.") |
  | I don't want to stay here. | "Oh, love. I know." / "It's only been a week. Give it a bit longer. Give June a bit longer. That's all I'm asking." |

  The rest answer as on the first call. Then: "That's the first week done. I'll ring again next week. Put June back on for me, would
  you?"
- June's goodbye is the week's: "Next week, then. Bye now, love."

With the receiver hung up, each reader at the house has had the second visit.

## How it is made

- **`PellWeek`** has the visits: `next(record)` (the first visit a reader has not had), `visitFor(readers)` (the earliest any reader at
  the house is on, `LAST` = 2), `done(record, visit)` at the call's hang-up, `weekIn` (the people and doors a week in), `dress` (the house
  as the visit has it, both ways, so a house that begins again at the first visit is put back), `welcome`, the lane rope, and the pool's
  details for a hand.
- **The household** keeps the visit it is playing (`visit`) and whether June has called hello (`welcomed`). `PellVisit.restart` takes the
  visit, and a week in `PellPeople.ensure` makes no Gail. The clock holds the afternoon a week in until `welcomed`.
- **`PellSwim`** has the swim: three parts (down, in the water, home), each a list of waypoints like June's night rounds, and the same
  rule for every door or gate she shuts behind her. The hour holds at `HALF_FIVE` while `out`; she dresses `DRESS` (100) ticks after;
  `LENGTHS` (6) of `LENGTH` (110) ticks, at `IN_WATER` below the surface and `UNDER` lower under the rope.
- **The pool's gate** (`PellBlock` `POOL_GATE`, stages 0 shut, 1 padlocked, 2 open): `PellSwim.gateHand` opens and shuts it while it is
  not padlocked.
- **New materials** for the pool, drawn by `tools/draw_pell_house.py`: `pool_band_missing`, `pool_band_odd`, `pool_drain`, set by
  `PellSite.pool` at `PellSite.BAND_MISSING`, `BAND_ODD` and `DRAIN`. Under the cover on the first visit nobody can reach them.
- **The line**: `PellSite.repaint` sets the whole line to one stage, keeping each cell's shape and ground.
- **The things a week on** are stages the things already had (the knife block, the cutlery, the pegboard's bit 2, the shed's
  calendar, the fridge top), and the towel is the `TOWEL` thing at `PellWeek.TOWEL`. The robe over the pool chair is the chair's stage 1,
  a new model with the robe over its back and seat (`tools/draw_pell_things.py`, `robe`).
- **The lane rope** is nine item displays, one to each block across the water, `pell_lane` and (at `GAP_X`) `pell_lane_gap`, drawn by
  `tools/draw_pell_people.py`: a rope with a pale and a blue float to each length, the blue gone from one.
- **June's two new skins**, painted by `tools/paint_character_skins.py`: `literary_pell_june_robe` (the towelling robe on the coat layer,
  open down the front over the one-piece, its belt tied with the ends hanging, the sleeves to the wrist with their cuffs turned back) and
  `literary_pell_june_swim` (a one-piece and a bathing cap, the coat layer clear). Both are in the character catalogue. The renderer
  picks the skin by June's gesture (`PellPeople.dressed`): `ROBE` and `SWIM`.
- **Swimming** (`SWIM`): she is laid along the water face down, as the last swimmer is, and swims a crawl: each arm in turn reaches
  ahead, pulls down through the water to her hip and comes back over above the surface, swung wide; her head turns to one side to
  breathe while her right arm comes over; her legs kick. A stroke every thirty ticks. Her back lies just under the surface, her head
  out of it.
- **What a reader sees**: the house's sight (`PellNight.sees`) now looks through the pool fence's bars and its gate, as through clear
  glass.
- **For tests**, the fixtures are those of the first visit (`PellVisit.dined`, `PellNight.lockedUp`, `PellMorning.smallHours`,
  `PellCall.afterBreakfast`); a case gives its readers the first visit by setting "FirstVisit" in their records before the house begins.

## Decided here

The proposal says the cover is off, June swims lengths at dawn and starts calling Tilly Nessa. These are mine, and any of them can be
moved:

- **Which visit** is each reader's own, and the house plays the earliest one a reader there has not had.
- **A week in begins in the late afternoon with nobody arriving**: no car, no tour, the household where it is by then. The rules are not
  given again except by a hand on June.
- **The house a week on**: the pool open, the lane rope with a float gone, the band's missing and odd tiles, the goggles under the drain,
  a knife moved from the block to Callum's drawer, a second knife's outline empty, the calendar on, the doorstep photographs, the line
  fresher, the towel under the garage's door. Nothing is explained.
- **The slips** are once each, at dinner, bedtime and breakfast, and June corrects herself each time.
- **The swim** is six lengths at first light, before six, with the gate shut but not padlocked while she is in the water. A reader can
  go in; she waits for them to come out before she locks it.
- **The week's call** is the first call's, a week on, and Gail's goodbye says she will ring next week.
- **There is no third visit yet**; the second plays again.

## Found while building it

- **The pool's bars were walls.** The house's sight stopped at the pool fence, so a reader on the patio could not see June in the water
  behind it. It now looks through the bars and the gate, as it looks through clear glass. Nothing seen through them before mattered.
- **Tilly lay down before she said her name.** Written first with the first visit's order (Tilly lies down, then goodnight), she said
  "It's Tilly." lying in bed with the house already counting her asleep. A week in she now says it sitting up, and lies down after June's
  goodnight.
- **The first stroke was not a crawl.** A local render of the swim, from both sides and from above against the pool's surface, showed
  one arm always ahead and the other always pulling down: she paddled. Each arm now goes all the way round in turn. The same render
  showed the robe and her cap as drawn.
- **The week's answer would have overwritten the first.** What a reader told Gail on the week's call went into the first call's
  "ToldGail". It has keys of its own.
