# Second Person's street, as a British terrace (0.4.283)

The owner: "Consider actual regional landscaping and architecture." Second Person's flat is above a closed launderette on a terraced
street in Britain. Inside, the flat read well. Outside, the street was a box:
- the terrace the reader comes out of was one flat brick wall with a parapet;
- both ends of the road were blank brick;
- there was bare stone either side of the launderette;
- the street tree stood in a plain square of earth with a cube of leaves on top.

This pass gives it the look of a real terraced street. Everything stays inside the scene's room. No story position, door, window or
walking surface of the street or the flat moves. New builds only.

## The street (`SecondPersonStreet`)

- **The terrace behind the reader** reads as a row of houses:
  - a slate eave along the top in place of the parapet;
  - chimney stacks at the party walls, each with two pots;
  - a stone head over every window and door;
  - brick between the floors, so the first-floor windows are two panes high.

  The room ends at its front wall, so its roof can't slope away behind it; the eave and the chimneys stand for the roof.
- **The opposite row.** The launderette's parade has a two-storey house on each side, filling what was bare stone:
  - each house has stock brick, a front door with a fanlight, dark windows with stone heads, a slate gable roof and a chimney stack;
  - behind each house is a walled back yard, flagged, with a bin;
  - the flat's kitchen and bedroom windows look out over the west yard.
- **The launderette** keeps its flat roof behind the parapet, as a parade shop with a flat over it does. Two chimney stacks with pots now
  stand above it.
- **Both ends of the road** meet the front of a house across a T-junction:
  - a kerb runs across the end of the road;
  - each house has a door and fanlight, windows with stone heads, a slate eave and a chimney stack;
  - beyond the yards, the walls are the backs of the next street's houses, with back windows over the yards.
- **The street tree** stays: the year still shows in it through the window. It now has an iron grille round its foot, and its crown is
  rounded, with the cube's corners left open.

## What is kept

- The paving, the kerbs and the road.

The terrace's doors are still locked iron doors with nothing in front of them: the architecture pass allows nothing to climb in a
