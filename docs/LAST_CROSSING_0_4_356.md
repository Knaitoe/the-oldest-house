## The third dusk

At the end of Day Three the light goes, and Vera says: "That's the last light. The rise comes tonight. Get to your places." She walks down to keep the night at the ramp. The night runs five minutes on the station's clock.

Night Two's workings run again, without the pull and without a second slot. What the second night left stays: tears, wet prints and the pinned floor grate. Those workings are:

- the turned reader's water, claws, call and broken speech;
- the floor grate behind the brake;
- the things from the water coming over the spillway's banks and up any branch left open by day;
- the grapple.

## The last turns

A moment after dusk the Night One carrier turns again where they stand. If a crew of six filled the second slot on Night Two, that carrier turns too, for the first time: "The light goes, and the breath that's been under yours for a day comes up and takes it." Anyone with them in view knows whose face it was, as on Night Two.

Their speech is broken as on Night Two, a little worse. It chokes them near the plate, and while they hold the sluice wheel before the pin is in or the feed gate before it is barred.

## The roll

Vera calls the last roll at the ramp: "Last roll. Same order as the first day. Answer up." There is no cable door to open tonight.

- The dead are called too, once each, and nobody can answer for them.
- Anyone else is called, and again if the name goes unanswered.
- As on Night Two, only a reader who can hear her can answer. A reader below ground while she is above, or shut in behind a door of another building, doesn't hear their name.
- A name still unanswered is noted absent by everyone who heard it go unanswered.

Asked "What do we do?", she says: "Wheel below, pin above, and the rope when she's off the sill. Or the gate in the gallery, and the cutoff. Nobody goes anywhere alone."

## The final rise

Fifteen seconds in, the rise comes: "Here it comes. That's the rise." The sluice pit fills to just below its deck, and the ladder stands in the water. A turned reader who knows what the master bypass will do (from Day Three) hears: "The water's high. Down at the foot of the pit, the master wheel will turn now."

## The launch

From the design: *"Hold the sluice closed until the pin seats, then release once the water clears the sill."*

- **The wheel.** One reader holds the sluice wheel shut at the bottom of the flooded pit. At night it stays held as long as they stay on it, with no timeout.
- **The wheel and the turned.** A turned reader can reach the wheel from below before anyone clean gets there. Holding it, their throat closes even under water, until the pin seats: "The pin drops home above you, and your throat opens on the water again." They can hold on, let go into standing water, or go and open the master bypass instead.
- **The pin.** Another reader drops the staged pin from the deck above: "Pin's in! Let go the wheel!" Tonight the pin stays in.
- **The sill.** Ten seconds after the pin seats the ferry floats off her sill: "She's off the sill! Whoever's on the rope, when you're ready." Before that the rope won't let her go ("She's still on her sill. Not yet."), and without the pin she won't float at all.
- **The rope.** Anyone can pull the launch rope at its bollard. Vera goes aboard, and everyone standing on the ferry's deck crosses with her. That can include a turned reader, and the crew can also sail with a turned reader still on the ramp:
  - those aboard hear "The rope slips the bollard and the deck moves under you. The station starts to slide away.", and the station fades out behind them;
  - those of the crew left on the station hear "The ferry moves off the landing without you.";
  - each reader aboard remembers who was aboard with them;
  - readers on the shore remember any turned reader they saw go aboard.
- **The crossing.** The crossing takes ten seconds. Whoever goes over the side is no longer aboard. Then the ferry comes in at the far landing and those aboard step off.

If one of them is clean, the crossing is the crew's. If nobody aboard is clean, she comes in anyway and the night goes on.

## The gallery

From the design: *"The feed gate and the cutoff expose the source while its last defenders are still mobile."*

- **The gate.** One reader throws their weight on the feed gate. Holding it, a clean reader hears the feed hammer behind it; a turned reader feels their throat close as on the wheel. The gate kicks the holder off if they step away.
- **The bar.** Another reader drops the locking bar across it, staged by day or carried down now: "You drop the bar across the gate. It takes the feed's weight with a clang." The holder and the one who barred it remember each other.
- **The cutoff.** With the gate barred, the upstream cutoff in its alcove winds shut. Before that it "won't turn against the feed".
- **The drain.** Over five seconds the chamber drains, a layer at a time, the weed going with the water. Then the gate draws up into its housing. On the source "things are clinging, holding it": two of the things from the water, and one more for every two of the crew. They keep within a few blocks of the source and fight whoever comes in.
- **The source.** While anything covers it, blows don't land: "They're over it, covering it. Get them off it first." Once they are fought off, six blows kill it.

When the source dies:

- every turned reader is let go at once, wherever they are: "Something in your chest lets go. The second breath is gone. You're only yourself.";
- the things in the night go back into the water;
- if a clean reader of the crew is still standing, the station is cleared: "Listen. The water's gone still."

The station bell then calls the muster: "That's the bell. Out of the gallery, all of you, where I can see you." If nobody rings it, Vera does after twenty seconds. Everyone who was down in the gallery or the chamber for the fight and is still alive is marked as having come out of it with her. Ten seconds later it is morning.

## Siding with the source

From the design: *"An infected ally ... can ... deliberately open the master bypass. The wheel and the bypass are separate actions. Letting go to save yourself leaves the job unfinished and keeps the bargain open."*

- **The master bypass.** After the rise, the master bypass at the foot of the pit turns for a turned reader. For anyone else it stays rusted fast. Opened, it lets water run back into the ramp channel the wrong way, and anyone with it in view remembers who opened it.
- **Holding it.** The bypass stays open only while its holder stays on it. Let go before the bargain is done, it swings shut.
- **What it does while open:**
  - the pipes hammer, heard at any speaking-pipe mouth and on the ramp;
  - the source's choke on the turned lets go;
  - every other thing from the water comes up onto the landing by the ramp;
  - halfway through the hold, water comes up over the ramp's approach.
- **The bargain.** Held open for twenty seconds, the bargain is done and the holder has sided with the source. A carrier the plate exposed takes the Other Breath: "In the flood channel something lets go of your throat for good. You'll stay under longer now. Wherever you are." Anyone else who sided feels the water run through and nothing more.
- **The Other Breath.** It is three more seconds of breath under water, permanently. It stays through death, through the End, through logging out and back in, and in ordinary play. It is earned once per reader, and is kept whatever ending follows.
- **The downstream sluice.** Wound shut while the bypass is open and the bargain not yet done, it denies it:
  - the water goes slack, the master shuts and won't open again tonight;
  - the holder knows who denied them: "The water goes slack in the channel. Whatever was opening in your throat closes again.";
  - wound shut after the bargain, it only drains the landing, and the change is kept.

## The source takes it

From the design: *"The source wins when the last clean reader is taken before a crew victory, or when the final night ends with neither crew victory secured. A ferry already in transit gets to finish its short trip before the closing result is settled."*

- The check runs once a second while no ferry is in transit.
- If nobody clean of the crew is still standing at the station (or already across), the source has the crossing.
- A clean reader who has just dropped out still counts for thirty seconds (the design's short grace window).
- At the end of the night with neither the ferry across nor the source dead, the source has it.

## The endings

Each reader of the crew still at the station keeps which ending they saw: the ferry, the gallery or the source.

- **The ferry crosses.** Vera calls the roll on the far bank once those aboard have had a moment to see who is there: "Roll. Answer up, all of you." Each reader on the far bank can answer "Here." A name that goes unanswered (the dead, those left on the station) is heard unanswered by the others. After the last name she calls Ellis and waits on it. A long breath later, from the far end of the boat, something says "Here." Then morning: "That's the light. Whoever's here, you're across."
- **The station cleared.** The muster, as above, then morning: "That's the light. Water's still. We'll take her across in the morning, all of us that are left."
- **The source takes the crossing.**
  - Everyone still at the station is brought over to the far landing, and Vera with them: "The ferry's coming in. Listen."
  - The ferry comes in by itself, and someone aboard calls the roll in Vera's order.
  - Every name gets an answer from the far end of the boat, the dead and Ellis's included. Everyone on the shore hears whose names were answered.
  - Then morning: "That's the light. Every name answered. I don't know who answered them."

In the morning everyone is brought across to the far landing. The things go back into the water, the turned come back to their own shape, and everyone of the crew has worked Night Three. Vera, talked to, says "You're across."
